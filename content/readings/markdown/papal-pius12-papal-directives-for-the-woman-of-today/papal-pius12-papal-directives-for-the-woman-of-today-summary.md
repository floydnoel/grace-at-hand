## Summary

A 1947 address by Pope Pius XII to the International Union of Catholic Women’s Leagues, urging Catholic women to take an active, courageous role in social and political life to defend Christian values — particularly the dignity of women, the sanctity of marriage and the family — against the secularizing and materialist forces unleashed or intensified by World War II.

## Historical Context

The address was delivered in Rome in 1947, just two years after the end of World War II. Europe was devastated, families were shattered, and the Church confronted atheistic communism in the East and rising secularism in the West. Pius XII was responding to a crisis he saw as unprecedented: women had been forced into "more than manly virtues" during the war, yet were now exposed to false doctrines on marriage, divorce, euthanasia, and materialism. The speech complements his earlier 1945 address to Italian Catholic women and seeks to mobilize the global laity for what he viewed as a spiritual counter-offensive.

## Key Themes

- **A lively, supernatural faith** — not merely nominal Catholicism but an absolute, fearless faith expressed daily in prayer, humility, and sacrifice.
- **Rejection of a false "spirituality"** that would confine the Church to the sacristy; Catholics must be present in public life, especially education, legislation, and social order.
- **Fidelity to the Church's social doctrine** — including equitable distribution of wealth and equal pay for equal work between men and women.
- **Women's active political role** — not all are called to political careers, but those with time and preparation should serve as representatives; all should vote and form public opinion.
- **The dangers of misplaced sentimentality** — false pity that justifies euthanasia, divorce, or seducing the poor into atheistic materialism.
- **Unity in diversity** — the Union's federated structure preserves each member organization's autonomy while enabling common action.

## Significance

The address is one of the most substantial papal statements on the role of women in public life before the Second Vatican Council. It affirms women's political rights and duties while grounding them firmly in a traditional Catholic vision of womanhood. It also anticipates themes that would be developed by Paul VI and John Paul II, particularly the call to build a "civilization of love" against the forces of secularization and the emphasis on the laity's responsibility in the temporal order. The speech remains a key reference point for Catholic social teaching on gender, family, and citizenship.
