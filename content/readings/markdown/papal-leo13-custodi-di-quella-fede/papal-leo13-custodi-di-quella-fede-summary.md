## Summary

*Custodi Di Quella Fede* ("Guardians of That Faith") is a papal encyclical issued by Pope Leo XIII in 1892, addressed to the Italian people, condemning Freemasonry as an implacable enemy of the Catholic Church and of Italy itself. The document warns that Masonic societies have infiltrated Italian political and social life, promoting naturalism, secularism, and materialism while persecuting the Church, and calls Catholics to resist through separation from Masonic associations, vigilance in family and education, and active Catholic organizing in press, schools, and charitable works.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written on December 8, 1892, in the aftermath of Italian unification (the *Risorgimento*), which had stripped the Papal States of their territory and left the pope as a self-described "prisoner in the Vatican." Anti-clerical liberal governments, often influenced by Masonic lodges, pursued policies of secularization — expropriating Church property, suppressing religious orders, introducing civil marriage, and laicizing education. Leo XIII builds on his earlier encyclical *Humanum genus* (1884) and a 1890 letter on the same subject, now focusing specifically on the damage he saw Masonry inflicting on Italy in just over three decades since unification.

## Key Themes

- **Irreconcilability of Catholicism and Freemasonry** — joining one is to abandon the other; no compromise or "universal tolerance" is possible.
- **Masonry as a dual enemy** — it attacks both faith (the Church) and homeland (Italy), substituting naturalism for Christianity and revolution for Gospel law.
- **Concrete social harms** — breakdown of paternal authority, rise of divorce, class conflict, spread of vice, and growth of socialism, communism, and anarchy.
- **Laicization as a Masonic project** — the sect aims to "remove the mark of Christianity" from schools, charities, and public institutions.
- **Duty of separation** — Catholics must leave Masonic lodges under pain of sin; parents and teachers must guard those in their care.
- **Positive Catholic action** — Catholics should counter Masonic institutions with their own: Catholic press, schools, clubs, credit unions, and charitable associations.
- **Unity around the papacy** — Catholics should gather around the See of Peter and their bishops to defend Church and nation.

## Significance

The encyclical represents the high-water mark of 19th-century papal opposition to Freemasonry, applying the broad theological condemnation of *Humanum genus* to the specific political situation of post-unification Italy. It reinforced the Church's absolute prohibition on Catholic membership in Masonic lodges — a discipline that remained in force until the 1983 Code of Canon Law. The document also exemplifies Leo XIII's broader strategy of encouraging lay Catholic mobilization ("Catholic Action") as a response to secular liberalism, a theme that would deeply influence Catholic social and political engagement into the 20th century.
