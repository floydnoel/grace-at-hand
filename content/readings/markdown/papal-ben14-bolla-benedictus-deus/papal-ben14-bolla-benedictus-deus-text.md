# Benedictus Deus

**La Santa Sede**

---

> *Note: This document was translated from the original Italian into English. Translation provided by DeepL (July 2026).*

BULL OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV BENEDICTUS DEUS Bishop Benedict, servant of the servants of God. To all the faithful Christians who will read this Letter, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. Blessed be the Lord, Father of mercy and God of all consolation, who has granted Us to spend the Jubilee Year joyfully and fruitfully in every good thing, according to the desire of Our heart. We have been filled with consolation and overflowing joy as we have witnessed throughout the year the faith, piety, and devotion of Our Beloved children of the Church in Christ. We have seen countless Christians of every language, people, nation, social class, age, and gender arrive in this City of Ours, bearing witness by their very arrival to the fervor of their faith and the greatness of the Kingdom of God. We have seen them, as they vied with one another to visit the sacred basilicas of this city, with a contrite and humble spirit, seeking the purification of their souls, and—like olive shoots around the Lord’s Table, the nourishment of Christian unity—receiving the Sacrament of Peace. They also moved with zeal and on a religious pilgrimage from one basilica to another; at times invoking the mercy of Our Savior in the Constantinian Lateran Basilica, through the merits of the Blessed Forerunner and of the beloved Disciple and Evangelist of the same name; now invoking the help and aid of the Glorious Virgin Mother of God in the Liberian Basilica on the Esquiline Hill; now the sacred patronage of the Prince of the Apostles in the Vatican; now venerating, with kisses and copious tears, the martyrdom of the Apostle to the Gentiles on the Via Ostiense and raising fervent and unanimous prayers for the peace and glorification of the Catholic Church, and for the safety and salvation of all believers. With what joy of spirit have We heard, with Our own ears, the streets, squares, open spaces, and hills resound with devout prayers, sweet songs, and praises of the Divine Name! How many times have We been moved to the depths of Our being upon seeing the great crowds of the faithful who, at Our feet, venerated in Our humble person the authority of the Vicariate of Christ and the apostolic succession of Blessed Peter (whose dignity is not diminished in his unworthy heir) with eloquent signs of filial reverence! A shining example of this fervent commitment on the part of the peoples were the Sacred Shepherds, who—in accordance with tradition—have come in great numbers this year to honor the memory of the Most Blessed Prince of the Church himself; We, surrounded by their splendid assembly, celebrated the sacred gatherings with the utmost dignity and to the edification of the universal brotherhood. What shall we say of Our Venerable Brother Cardinals of the Holy Roman Church and the other Dignitaries of Our Curia who, distinguishing themselves from others by the assiduity of their pious works and, at the same time, by humbling themselves through the exercise of Christian humility below all their other brothers—even those of the lowest station—have become the sweet fragrance of Christ to the entire citizenry and to all who have stayed there? What, finally, shall we say of the devotion of the entire clergy of this most beloved City and of the people, whose examples of piety and works of charity toward foreigners and pilgrims—remarkable this year for their number and fervor—we have no doubt must be made known to the whole world? Blessed are Our Sons, the Citizens and Inhabitants of the City of Rome, who have performed such works of mercy for their brothers. We, too, will trust in the Lord, for He is gracious, and His consolations have gladdened Our soul. 1. But We also rightly conceived the hope that the Most Clement Lord Himself would show Us the mercy We implored in Our prayers and would grant this to His Church, since He permitted it to be asked of Him with such unanimous and fervent prayers; may He exalt the Catholic Church itself—the Kingdom of His Son—throughout the whole earth, in holiness and in scope; may He purify the world from all error; may He strengthen harmony and peace among Christian princes; and, by defending all the people of Christ from every worldly adversity, help them reach the harbor of eternal salvation. To ask this with the greatest confidence of the Most High God, having heard the counsel of Our aforementioned Brother Cardinals of the Holy Roman Church, we have decreed that all the faithful followers of Christ, who in every place and among every people are united in the communion of the one Faith, be included in the bond of these prayers and, with the treasury of Indulgences very generously opened, may effectively participate in them, in the well-founded hope that, by striving toward this end, having their sins blotted out through penance, they will conduct themselves appropriately and, filled with pious devotion—and thereby manifesting the reverence due to the Apostolic See—will together perform works of piety and worship, so that each may obtain for himself what he asks for others, and those prayers and supplications offered up for their brothers—as they themselves spread every good—will rightly return to their innocence and charity. 2. Therefore, by the mercy of Almighty God, trusting in the authority of the Blessed Apostles Peter and Paul, and by virtue of the supreme power to bind and loose, which the Lord has granted to Us, though We are unworthy, We have granted permission to all the faithful followers of Christ of both sexes, wherever in the world they may dwell—provided they remain faithful and obedient to the Apostolic See—and also to those who happen to be in Rome after the Year has ended and who, here or elsewhere, for whatever reason, have desired this Jubilee granted by Us—provided they are truly repentant, have gone to confession, and have received Holy Communion—within six months from the date of publication of this Letter in each diocese, provided they have devoutly visited the diocesan cathedral or the most important church, and three other churches in the same city or locality—including those in the surrounding area, to be designated by the local Ordinaries, their Vicars, or others appointed by them—at least once a day for fifteen consecutive days, or with natural or ecclesiastical intervals, from First Vespers of one day until the conclusion of Vespers of the following day, and there have offered devout prayers to God for the exaltation of Holy Mother Church, for the eradication of heresies, for the harmony of Catholic Princes, for the salvation and peace of the Christian people, and to obtain a plenary indulgence, the forgiveness and remission of all their own sins, as if they had visited the four Basilicas or the churches designated by Us—both within and outside Rome —to fulfill the Jubilee, on days personally chosen for this purpose and having fulfilled the other required obligations: we mercifully grant and bestow in the Lord. 3. Sailors and those who are traveling, if they return home at the end of the prescribed months, or remain in a particular place after the times already indicated have elapsed, having visited the cathedral, or the most important church, or the parish church of their place of residence or of their stopover, alternatively, may obtain the same Indulgence with the same efficacy: likewise, the Ordinaries of places not mentioned above shall make provisions for nuns, the Oblates, other young women, or women—whether in cloistered monasteries, other congregations, pious houses, or living in communities—as well as for anchorites, hermits, and other persons of this kind, whether lay or ecclesiastical, whether Secular or Regular, for those living in prison or who are prisoners, since they cannot make the visits already indicated, or due to illness or other impediments; and also for children who have not yet been admitted to First Communion, with the prescribed dispensation from Communion, and for all those living in communities and individuals, whether voluntarily or through the instructions of prelates, or Superiors, or prudent Assistants, other works of piety, charity, and worship must be assigned to be performed in place of the prescribed visits or the sacramental Communion already mentioned; and furthermore, to Chapters, Secular and Regular Congregations, Sodalities, Confraternities, Universities, and Colleges of every kind that visit the designated churches in procession, we graciously grant permission to reduce the number of visits, according to their own prudent judgment and particular needs. 4. Furthermore, we grant to the aforementioned nuns and their novices, to all Christians considered individually of both sexes—whether laypeople or clergy— whether Secular or Regular members of any Order, Congregation, or Institute—to be specified separately—the license and authority to choose any priest as their confessor, whether secular or religious, from any Order or Institute, approved by the current Ordinaries in whose cities, dioceses, and territories where confessions are to be heard, provided that said confessor is authorized to hear the confessions of the laity; for the nuns, the confessor must have specific authorization. We refer to all those, men and women, who, within the aforementioned period of one semester, have sincerely and seriously resolved to take advantage of this Jubilee with the intention of gaining the indulgence by performing the required works and who are preparing to go to confession before them. To these confessors we graciously grant, on this occasion and within the exclusive scope of their conscience, the authority to lift sentences and censures of excommunication, suspension, and the like, issued by virtue of law or as a penalty, or even those falling under the jurisdiction of the local Ordinaries, as well as Ours and that of the Apostolic See—whether it be the Bull customarily read on Holy Thursday or any other apostolic provision or condemnation under the Sacred Canons; as well as from all sins and excesses, however grave and enormous, even those reserved as above to the same Ordinaries, to Us, and to the Apostolic See, after a salutary penance and whatever must be obligatorily added have been imposed upon the penitents; we further grant permission to commute vows of every kind, even those made under oath and reserved to the Apostolic See (except, as always, vows of chastity, religious vows, and those pertaining to a specific state of life that have been publicly made, as well as those that cause harm to others, and those measures that help to avoid sin, unless the commutation is judged to be equivalent to or superior to the subject matter of the previous vow) into other pious and salutary works. In the case of penitents who have received Holy Orders, including those in religious orders, we grant the authority to dispense from the censure incurred due to some hidden failure to observe the rule of their Order or the directives of their superiors: we grant this graciously by virtue of the same Apostolic authority and benevolence. 5. We do not intend by this Bull to grant a dispensation regarding any other public or secret irregularity, or fault, or other incapacity or inability incurred in any way, nor to confer any authority—beyond those already mentioned—to dispense, rehabilitate, or restore to the previous state, even within the exclusive sphere of their conscience, nor do we intend to grant any confessor the power to absolve an accomplice in any dishonorable sin against the Sixth Commandment, nor to grant the accomplice the possibility of choosing a confessor with the powers granted by this Bull, as was already amply specified in another of Our previous Bulls beginning with \*Sacramentum Poenitentiae\* of June 1, 1741, the first year of Our Pontificate; nor, finally, do We intend that this Bull may or should in any way benefit those who have been declared excommunicated, suspended, interdicted, or otherwise subject to other sentences or censures, or who have been publicly denounced, unless within the established six-month period they have made amends and reached an agreement with the other party, where applicable. 6. Furthermore, if some, after initially intending to gain the benefits of this Jubilee, have been unable to complete the complex works prescribed and the required number of visits, We, wishing benevolently to support their pious and resolute intention, decree that they—if truly repentant, having confessed and received Communion—shall share in the aforementioned Indulgence and remission of sins, as if they had visited the aforementioned churches on the established days. As for those who, after having obtained by virtue of this document the absolution from censures, the commutation of vows, or the aforementioned dispensations, have demonstrated the required serious and sincere intention and fulfilled the other necessary requirements to gain the Indulgence, even if they can scarcely be considered free from guilt, we nevertheless hold and affirm that the absolutions, commutations, and dispensations obtained by them under the aforementioned provision remain valid. 7. To achieve the desired effect of this decision of Ours, taken for the salvation and sanctification of the entire Christian people, We place Our utmost trust first in God and then in the cooperation and commitment of Our Venerable Brother Bishops of the Church of God. Therefore, by virtue of Apostolic Authority, we exhort, pray, and urge all Patriarchs, Primates, Archbishops, Bishops, and other local Ordinary Prelates—or those who legitimately exercise jurisdiction in various places where sees are vacant—and who are in full communion with the Apostolic See, in the name of Our Lord, the Prince of Shepherds, Jesus Christ, for the love of the Church, His bride, so that, as soon as they receive a copy of this Letter, not only solemnly make it public and give it precedence over all other provisions, but also strive in every way to ensure that the Faithful entrusted to their care joyfully embrace the opportunity offered to them for conversion, reconciliation, and sanctification; and, by showing themselves worthy of the grace and favors of the Apostolic See, may they transform these into most abundant fruits for their souls and for the whole Church. We have preferred that the Antistites themselves take charge of preparing the faithful entrusted to them for the universal Jubilee; to them We have communicated in writing the directives of this Apostolic See. We believe they have been informed that We Ourselves have undertaken this task in this City of Ours. We believe that no corner of the earth is unaware of those fruits of piety and that example of devotion with which, here in Rome, by God’s blessing, the Jubilee has been solemnly celebrated. We therefore rightly ask and expect that the Shepherds of the Church themselves, as those who draw their inspiration from the Rock from which they derive, will take care to manifest in this as well their communion and unity with the Church of Rome, so as to achieve, each in their own cities and dioceses, what Mother Church herself, for the benefit and growth of the entire Catholic world, desires for the purification and salvation of the Christian people—and which she confirms and teaches through illustrious examples. Let them therefore direct all their pastoral concerns toward this end, so that the peoples entrusted to them may be united in the purity of the same discipline and in the fervor of Christian piety, and so that the single splendor of good works may clearly shine forth in all the Catholic Churches; so that the Almighty Father, invoked by the united prayers of all the faithful, may purify His Church from recurring errors, defend her, and enlarge her; may He shower heavenly blessings upon the Supreme Princes, who are such by His will, and may He guide all believers—through the gift of His grace—along the path of peace and the ways of His commandments toward the eternal salvation that His Only-Begotten Son has won for us. Only then will Our joy be complete, and the Shepherds of the Church themselves will rejoice with Us at the improvement of the sacred flock. 8. Finally, we have no doubt that our most beloved Sons in Christ—the Emperor-elect, the Kings, and all the Catholic Princes, the majority of whom have expressed the desire to extend this Jubilee to their Kingdoms and Dominions—have sufficiently understood thatthe implementation of this salutary work in their domains, through the intensification of preaching, the commitment of the subject peoples to sincere obedience toward them will be strengthened, and divine help, peace, and prosperity for the Kings and Princes themselves will be very effectively implored; therefore, we exhort and recommend that all, considered individually, provide every possible assistance to the aforementioned Prelates of the Church and contribute to promoting this pious practice by the most suitable means, in order to achieve the desired end. 9. We further hold and desire that this Letter of Ours be valid and effective in every part, and that it may achieve and produce its full effects wherever it is made public and put into effect by the aforementioned Ordinary Bishops of the Church; that all Christians —bound by gratitude and obedience to the Apostolic See—who live in these places or have taken refuge there after a voyage or journey; and that the restrictive directives and provisions—regarding the granting of indulgences—issued by Universal and Provincial Apostolic Councils and Synods, as well as by Constitutions and general and special ordinances concerning absolution, reductions, and dispensations contained—among other things—in the aforementioned Bull of Holy Thursday; likewise, the Statutes, Laws, deeply rooted customs and traditions, the privileges themselves, the indults, and the Apostolic Letters granted to the Mendicant and Military Orders, to the Congregations, to the pious Societies, to Institutes, including the Society of Jesus, as well as every form of Constitution or rule pertaining to them, even if validated by apostolic confirmation, by oath, or by any other authoritative assent, with particular regard to provisions prohibiting the confession of one’s sins outside one’s own institute; to all—even taken individually—members of these religious families, even if a valid exemption were to require a specific and personal mention or some other appropriate formulation to maintain the validity of every rule and provision mentioned above: to all of them—even taken individually—we grant a broad exemption, solely on this occasion and for the purposes mentioned, rejecting any other contrary exception. 10. We further decree that all copies of this document, including printed ones, provided they are signed by a public notary and bear the seal of a person holding ecclesiastical office, shall be accorded the same trust as if the original were presented or shown. 11. No one shall be permitted, in any way, to tamper with this document of Our extension, exhortation, commission, concession, exemption, decree, and will, nor to oppose it with reckless audacity. If anyone should dare to do so, let him know that he will incur the wrath of Almighty God and of the Blessed Apostles Peter and Paul. Given at Rome, at Santa Maria Maggiore, on December 25, in the year 1750 of the Incarnation, the eleventh year of Our Pontificate.
