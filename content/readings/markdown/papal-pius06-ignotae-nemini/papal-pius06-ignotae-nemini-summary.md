# Ignotae nemini · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VI appeals to the German hierarchy and religious communities to sustain French clergy driven from their homes because of their fidelity to the Church. He praises the rulers and peoples who have already received them, expressly including non-Catholic benefactors and particularly the king and nation of Great Britain. Renewed French military advances in Savoy and Nice have forced some refugees to flee again, increasing the need for shelter and support.

The pope describes assistance organized in Rome and the Papal States and answers a request from exiled French bishops near Constance for a wider appeal to German churches. He grounds hospitality in Scripture, patristic teaching, monastic rules, and conciliar obligations. Bishops should lead by example and exhortation; abbots, abbesses, chapters, and other holders of ecclesiastical resources should contribute according to their means. Hospitality is presented as receiving Christ in the stranger and as a work of mercy to accompany public prayer for peace.

## Key themes

- Material support for displaced French clergy
- Gratitude to Catholic and non-Catholic hosts
- Episcopal and monastic obligations of hospitality
- Charity and prayer amid renewed displacement
