# L'olio oggetto · English editorial summary

## Summary

Leo XII reforms Rome's wholesale oil trade to counter monopoly, false measurement, adulteration, and abusive brokerage. He identifies oil as a necessity, especially under Catholic fasting customs, and links fair trading with the welfare of farmers, merchants, and consumers. Wholesale sales are henceforth to use weight instead of the former system of measures, with officially verified scales.

The decree establishes a government-appointed controller, names Antonio Mazio to the office, and requires facilities, staff, equipment, and registers financed through the fees for services rendered. Recorded weights and brokered contracts are to be available for inspection and have legal evidentiary value. Parties remain free to contract directly, but mediation and weighing by unauthorized intermediaries are prohibited. The responsible food-supply authority must issue further regulations to implement the system. Leo presents these controls as safeguards for commerce rather than barriers to direct exchange.

## Key themes

- Replacing wholesale volume measures with verified weights
- A controller and public records to check fraud
- Protection of producers and consumers while permitting direct contracts
