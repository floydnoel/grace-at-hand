# Dilectissima Nobis · English editorial summary

## Summary

*Dilectissima Nobis* (1933) is an encyclical of Pope Pius XI protesting the persecution of the Catholic Church in Spain under the newly established Second Spanish Republic. It condemns the Republic's laws on religious confessions and congregations, which dissolved religious orders, confiscated Church property, banned religious teaching, and imposed a hostile separation of Church and State. The Pope defends the Church's rights while affirming that the Church is not opposed to republican government as such.

## Historical Context

Written on June 3, 1933, the encyclical responds to the anti-clerical turn of the Second Spanish Republic, proclaimed in 1931. The Republic's 1931 Constitution and subsequent legislation (the "Law of Religious Confessions and Congregations") disestablished Catholicism, dissolved the Society of Jesus, nationalized Church property, prohibited religious orders from teaching, and introduced civil divorce. This was part of a broader wave of secularization across Europe, echoed by the ongoing persecution of the Church in Mexico and the Soviet Union, which Pius XI also condemned in earlier encyclicals.

## Key Themes

- **Separation of Church and State is an error** — especially in a Catholic nation, and constitutes a form of "laicism" or societal apostasy.
- **The Church is not bound to any political form** — she can adapt to monarchy or republic; the real issue is whether divine rights and Christian consciences are respected.
- **Spain's anti-clerical laws violate both justice and liberty** — they deprive the Church of property, tax what they have seized, restrict worship, and ban religious education while claiming to guarantee freedom.
- **Religious orders are unjustly targeted** — especially the Jesuits, for their loyalty to the Holy See; their schools are proven by history to be effective and beloved by families.
- **Education is the central battleground** — the laws aim to root out Catholic faith from the next generation by imposing godless, laic schooling.
- **Catholic Action as a non-political response** — the laity should organize to defend the faith above party lines.

## Significance

*Dilectissima Nobis* is a key witness to the Church's experience of persecution in 1930s Europe and foreshadows the far greater violence of the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939), in which thousands of clergy and religious were martyred. It also develops Pius XI's teaching on the social reign of Christ the King (from *Quas Primas*) and on laicism as the great error of the modern state. The encyclical's insistence that religious liberty cannot justify the suppression of the Church remains a touchstone for Catholic social teaching on Church–State relations.
