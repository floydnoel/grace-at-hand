# Ci è stato · English editorial summary

## Summary

Gregory XVI authorizes the diversion of the Aniene River through Mount Catillo to protect Tivoli from renewed disaster. He reviews the protective wall built after the 1826 breach and later expert investigations, which found continuing instability around the river's existing course and natural caves. The approved project seeks to secure the town, preserve the benefits of its mills and workshops, and end recurring expenditure on inadequate defenses.

The chirograph orders construction of a pair of tunnels and entrusts implementation to Cardinal Agostino Rivarola and his successors in the Congregation of Waters. Clemente Folchi is named director, with an executing engineer to be appointed. Gregory confirms the cost distribution among property owners throughout the state, Tivoli, and the public treasury, anticipating expenditure beyond the original estimate. He grants authority to approve necessary modifications, arrange payments against engineering certificates, keep separate accounts, and overcome legal obstacles to execution.

## Key themes

- Engineering works to address persistent flood danger
- Delegated supervision and adaptation during construction
- Shared financing, certified payments, and separate accounts
