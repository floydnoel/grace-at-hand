# Non Ambigimus

**La Santa Sede**

---

> *Note: This document was translated from the original Italian into English. Translation provided by DeepL (July 2026).*

BRIEF OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV NON AMBIGIMUS To the Venerable Brothers, Patriarchs, Primates, Metropolitans, Archbishops, and Bishops who are in peace and communion with the Apostolic See. Pope Benedict XIV. Venerable Brothers, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. We have no doubt, Venerable Brothers, that all who adhere to the Catholic Faith know how the entire Church, spread throughout the Christian world, considers Lenten fasting to be one of the principal cornerstones of sound doctrine. First outlined in the Law and the Prophets, almost consecrated by the example of Our Lord Jesus Christ, handed down by the Apostles, and prescribed everywhere by the Sacred Canons, it has been accepted and observed by the entire Church since its earliest days. Certainly, as the ancient Fathers taught, through the institution of this common remedy for us who sin daily, we too, united to the Cross of Christ, can participate in what He Himself has provided for us. At the same time, purified by fasting in body and soul, we prepare ourselves to commemorate more worthily the sacred Mysteries of our Redemption through the remembrance of the Passion and the Resurrection, celebrated with greater solemnity especially during the season of Lent. Through fasting, which is almost a badge of our military service, we are distinguished from the enemies of the Church; we ward off the thunderbolts of divine vengeance; and, with divine help, we are protected day by day from the Princes of Darkness. Failure to observe fasting results in no small harm to the glory of God, a significant disgrace to the Catholic Faith, and a certain danger to the faithful; for it is certain that the misfortunes of peoples, and mortal calamities—both public and private—have no other origin. How far removed, how dissimilar, how contradictory is the current behavior of those who fast, compared to the conviction and reverence for the most holy Lent and for the other days dedicated to fasting, which are deeply rooted in the hearts of all Catholics; how far it deviates from the authentic doctrine of fasting and from the practice observed always, everywhere, and by all. You, Venerable Brothers, who are well acquainted with the customs and traditions of the peoples entrusted to your care, perceive all this more clearly than anyone else, thanks to your particular insight. Certainly, since news from the peoples is reported to Us—who are placed in this eminent vantage point of the Apostolic Government—We cannot but lament that the most sacred observance of Lenten fasting, due to the excessive ease with which dispensations are granted everywhere, indiscriminately, for trivial and non-urgent reasons, has been almost completely eliminated—to such an extent that it provokes the just complaints of those who follow the orthodox Religion, while the followers of heresies mock and exult. We are deeply distressed that, in addition to this pernicious corruption of many, there is a license that has taken such a firm hold, without due regard for the apostolic teachings and sacred decrees, to promote banquets with impunity during times of fasting and in public, and to host forbidden feasts in an unseemly manner. Moved, therefore, by sincere and pressing concern, We turn to you, Venerable Brothers: it is impossible for Us, given the lofty task of the most holy apostolate entrusted to Us, not to appeal to your ardent zeal to find a remedy against these evils and to devise laws suitable for rooting out these abuses. Therefore, Venerable Brothers, Our joy and Our crown, considering together that there is nothing more pleasing to God, nothing more fitting for Our pastoral ministry, and nothing more useful for the flock entrusted to Our care—serving as leaders by word and example— let us kindle in the hearts of the faithful the desire to resume with greater conviction such a salutary practice of penance and devotion, to remain constantly faithful to it, and to carry it out according to the established provisions. Let us strive with every care and all our zeal to ensure that the people remain faithful before God through a more austere observance of fasting, as is proper during the Easter season itself. Therefore, the proper exercise of your paternal care and charity requires that you make it known to all that no one is permitted to grant a dispensation without just cause and the advice of two physicians. A dispensation from Lenten fasting for an entire population, a city, or a category of people without distinction must be requested—unless for an urgent and most grave necessity and with due respect for this Apostolic See— whenever it is necessary to grant it, without presumptuously and resolutely taking it upon oneself, nor demanding it from the Church in a haughty and arrogant manner, as we know is the custom in certain places. Although there is no need to explain to you what constitutes such a grave necessity, we wish you to know clearly that in such a situation one must, above all, limit oneself to a single meal. Here in Rome as well, having ourselves granted a dispensation this year for urgent reasons, we have expressly decreed that no banquets—whether lawful or prohibited—may take place without due discernment. Therefore, just as we are convinced that the granting of indulgences must be approached with the utmost caution—and it could not be otherwise, since we will have to give an account of this to the Supreme Judge—so too in this case we believe that the responsibility must rest upon your conscience. At the same time, we ask your brotherhoods and implore you in the Lord that, for those who cannot observe the penitential discipline common to all the faithful, may you invite them so that, as each person’s own devotion may suggest, they may not neglect to atone for their sins and ask God for forgiveness through other works of piety. With true fervor, may they seek to discover the best way to heal the wounds that open in fragile human nature and, if they are unable to heal them through purifying fasting, may they atone for the sins committed due to human frailty through works of piety, the intercession of prayers, and the giving of alms. As we await relief and consolation for Our heavy affliction from Your pastoral solicitude and charity—which You will not fail to extend to Us—We wholeheartedly bestow upon You, Venerable Brothers, the Apostolic Blessing, rich in abundant heavenly favors, to be extended to your flocks. We further decree that copies of this document, including printed copies, signed by a public notary and bearing the seal of an ecclesiastical authority, shall retain the same authority as the original and be recognized wherever they are made public. Given in Rome, at Saint Mary Major, under the Fisherman’s Ring, on May 30, 1741, in the first year of Our Pontificate.
