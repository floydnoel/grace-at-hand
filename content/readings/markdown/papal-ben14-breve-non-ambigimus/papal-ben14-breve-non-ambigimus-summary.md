## Summary

*Non Ambigimus* (May 30, 1741) is a papal brief reaffirming fasting as essential to Catholic life. The pope laments that lax granting of dispensations had eroded fast observance, warns that this neglect brings divine punishment and moral decay, and instructs bishops to restrict dispensations to genuine necessity. The document also codified a major disciplinary change: it permitted meat on fasting days while forbidding the consumption of both fish and meat at the same meal, effectively introducing what later canon law would call "partial abstinence."

## Historical Context

Benedict XIV (Prospero Lambertini, r. 1740–1758) reigned during the Enlightenment, when Catholic practice faced growing criticism from secular and Protestant thinkers. Across Europe, absolutist monarchs were asserting control over church affairs, and devotional disciplines like fasting were widely relaxed or ignored. The pope — a renowned scholar and former archbishop of Bologna — sought to balance pastoral realism with doctrinal fidelity. By 1741, indiscriminate dispensations from Lenten observance had become common, and *Non Ambigimus* was his attempt to reassert traditional discipline while accommodating practical necessities in a changing world.

## Key Themes

- **Fasting as a badge of Catholic identity**: Benedict calls the Lenten fast a "mark of our militia" that distinguishes Catholics from the Church's enemies and from heretics.
- **Moral consequences of neglect**: Failure to fast properly is tied directly to public calamities, private woe, and collective moral decline — not merely a matter of private devotion.
- **Regulation of dispensations**: Bishops are ordered not to grant dispensations without serious cause and the advice of two doctors; wholesale dispensations for a city or class of persons require explicit recourse to the Holy See.
- **Introduction of mitigated abstinence**: The brief permits meat on fasting days but strictly forbids consuming fish and flesh meat at the same meal — the first formal step toward the modern "partial abstinence" rule.
- **Works of piety as substitution**: Those genuinely unable to fast are exhorted to substitute prayer, almsgiving, and other penitential works.
- **Pastoral authority and accountability**: Benedict insists bishops must answer to the Supreme Judge for their stewardship of fast discipline.

## Significance

*Non Ambigimus* is a landmark in the history of Catholic penitential discipline. It marks the first official relaxation of the ancient requirement of complete abstinence from meat throughout Lent, a concession that would be extended and refined by later popes (notably Leo XIII) and eventually codified in the 1917 Code of Canon Law. At the same time, the brief is a forceful rearguard action against the erosion of traditional piety — Benedict XIV's eloquent defence of fasting as "one of the principal bulwarks of sound doctrine" remains one of the strongest papal statements on the subject. The document vividly captures the tension, still familiar today, between pastoral accommodation and the preservation of demanding ascetical practices.
