# Novae hae litterae · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VI reviews the mixed reception of his 1791 condemnation of the French Civil Constitution of the Clergy. He praises bishops, clergy, religious, and laity who have remained faithful through persecution, and welcomes those who have retracted the civic oath and returned to their legitimate pastors. Mere resignation or flight by unauthorized bishops, he explains, does not substitute for repentance and satisfaction. He laments the widening establishment of a constitutional hierarchy and rejects its claims to communion with Rome.

The letter answers attacks on the authenticity and binding force of papal warnings, including the claim that they require civil authorization. It condemns writings defending the constitutional settlement, particularly Accord des vrais principes. Pius nevertheless postpones excommunication to allow further repentance, while maintaining the suspensions already imposed. He issues a renewed canonical admonition, allowing sixty days for the second warning and another sixty for the third, and specifies the consecrators, unauthorized bishops and ministers, oath-taking prelates, and other supporters concerned. He urges lawful pastors to combine firm instruction with assistance to the fallen, and the faithful to avoid unauthorized religious ministry and pray for reconciliation.

## Key themes

- Fidelity, public retraction, and conditions of reconciliation
- Rejection of the constitutional hierarchy's asserted legitimacy
- Renewed warning periods before threatened excommunication
- Papal letters' authority and condemnation of defensive publications
