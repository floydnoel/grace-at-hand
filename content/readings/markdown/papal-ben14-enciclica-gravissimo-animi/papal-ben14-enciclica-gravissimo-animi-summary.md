## Summary

*Gravissimo Animi* (1749) is a disciplinary encyclical in which Pope Benedict XIV rebukes civil governors and prelates of the Papal States who visit monasteries of nuns and speak with them without first obtaining permission from the local bishop. Drawing on previous canon law — from Boniface VIII's *Periculoso* to the Council of Trent — Benedict reaffirms that the custody of nuns' cloister belongs to the local Ordinary, and no one, including visiting bishops, vicar-generals, or city governors, is exempt from seeking the bishop's license before entering a convent.

## Historical Context

Benedict XIV (reigned 1740–1758) was a scholar-pope who wrote extensively on canon law, beatification, and diocesan synods. The 18th-century Papal States were administratively complex, with overlapping civil and ecclesiastical jurisdictions. City governors (often laymen or non-episcopal prelates) increasingly asserted the right to visit convents within or outside their territories without episcopal leave, eroding the bishop's authority over monastic discipline. Benedict had personally witnessed this abuse as Bishop of Ancona and Archbishop of Bologna — and found that even his vehement admonitions went unheeded. *Gravissimo Animi* was issued on 31 October 1749 from Rome to close this jurisdictional loophole.

## Key Themes

- **Episcopal jurisdiction over cloister** — The Council of Trent (Session 25, Chapter 5) and Boniface VIII's *Periculoso* entrust enforcement of nuns' enclosure to the local bishop, not to civil or exempt authorities.
- **No exemptions from the permission requirement** — Even bishops visiting another diocese, vicar-generals, and city governors must obtain the local Ordinary's license before approaching or speaking with nuns.
- **Exemption from penalties, not from obedience** — Regulars and other exempt persons may not be subject to episcopal censures, but they must still observe the prohibition; violations are to be reported to the Pope or the Congregation for Bishops and Regulars for remedy.
- **Continuity with prior law** — Benedict explicitly anchors his ruling in a tradition stretching from Clement III and John XXII through Eugene IV, and cites contemporary canonists (Pellizzario, Graziano, Tamburino, Ferraris).
- **Practical remedy** — The Pope orders that offenders be reported directly to Rome, ensuring episcopal authority is vindicated where local coercive power cannot reach.

## Significance

*Gravissimo Animi* is a minor but precise canonical intervention that illustrates Benedict XIV's characteristic approach: rigorous scholarship, respect for precedent, and practical enforcement. It closed a jurisdictional gap that had allowed civil officials to bypass episcopal oversight of convents. While it does not break new theological ground, it is frequently cited in collections of pontifical documents on cloister discipline and remains relevant to Catholic canon law on the enclosure of nuns. The encyclical also offers a vivid snapshot of the tension between episcopal authority and civil exemption in the early modern Papal States.
