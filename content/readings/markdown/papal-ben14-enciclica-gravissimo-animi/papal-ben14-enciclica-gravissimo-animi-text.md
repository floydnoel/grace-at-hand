ENCYCLICAL GRAVISSIMO ANIMI OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV To the Archbishops, Bishops, and Ordinaries of the Papal States. Pope Benedict XIV. Venerable Brother, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. It is not without grave distress of heart that We have been informed by trustworthy sources that at times certain local prelates, governors of the cities of Our State, visit the monasteries of nuns and speak freely with them without having requested and obtained theappropriate permission from the local bishops and ordinaries, thereby contravening the synodal decrees and episcopal constitutions, which prescribe—under penalty of the prescribed sanctions—that it is forbidden to approach the convents of nuns and speak with them without the appropriate permissions. The same is also practiced by prelates and governors who, being governors of a city, for some reason of their own pass through another city, go in the same manner to the convents of nuns, claiming to act as they do on the grounds that they are exempt from the specific constitutions of the bishops and ordinaries that prohibit access to nuns without the proper licenses. We had no difficulty in giving credence to the reports and complaints made to us, having unfortunately witnessed—to Our great dismay—when We were Bishop of Ancona and Archbishop of Bologna —while standing there, witnessed this happening right before Our eyes, with Our admonitions carrying little or no weight, even though they were made with some vehemence. 1. It cannot be denied that there would be little, if anything, to be done if the resolutions issued by these courts of ours—and approved by our predecessors, who had foreseen everything in times past and had wisely provided for all matters in other times—were kept in mind. But since, for the most part, these decrees are unknown, or if they are known, people act on a whim, it falls to those currently in power to bring them back to people’s attention and to ensure their observance. 2. In times past, the Sacred Congregations, with the approval of Our Predecessors, the Supreme Pontiffs, established what was necessary regarding the access of Regulars who, on the basis of their exemption, claimed the right to visit the monasteries of nuns subject to their jurisdiction without the proper license from the bishop, as can be seen in our treatise \*De Synodo\* (Book 7, Chapter 41, No. 7), where the aforementioned resolutions are indicated and cited. But they also considered and made provisions not only for the case of prelates or governors, but also for that of bishops who either are passing through or happen to be staying in some other city or place subject to another bishop: they may not visit the monasteries of nuns and speak with them without first obtaining the consent of the local Ordinary. These specific provisions are also cited by modern authors, as can be seen in Pellizzario (De Monialibus, chap. 5, sect. 5, n. 210), in Graziano (Discept. 392, n. 1), in Tamburino (De jure Abbatissarum disput., 25, quest. 2), and in Ferraris’s Bibliotheca (under the entry “Moniales,” art. 4, n. 34). 3. The enforcement of the cloister of nuns is entrusted by canonical provisions to the ordinary jurisdiction of the local bishop in the monasteries subject to him, as was established by Our Predecessor Pope Boniface VIII in the sixth chapter of Periculoso, De statu Monachorum, sixth, which was renewed by the Sacred Council of Trent (chap. 5, sess. 25, De Regularibus et Monialibus). A consequence of this custody of the cloister is the prohibition against anyone approaching the nuns’ monasteries and speaking with them without the proper permission of the Custodian. It follows that everyone must request and obtain such permission from the Bishop; all must be subject to the binding authority of this prohibition, as reported by the aforementioned authors and as can be inferred from the previous decrees of the Congregations. 4. These are fully approved by Us, and, should it be necessary—which we do not believe—we hereby enact a new law stipulating that Bishops, when outside their own diocese, may not visit the monasteries of nuns located in another diocese, nor may they speak with the nuns without the necessary authorization from the local Ordinary. Nor may Vicar-Generals or local Governors—even if they are prelates—enter convents of nuns located within their jurisdiction without the aforementioned authorization, much less convents located outside their jurisdiction, in other places where they may reside or through which they are obliged to pass while traveling. 5. It has just been stated that all these persons are subject to the regulatory authority of the Bishops’ Constitutions, which prohibit access to nuns’ monasteries without the proper license, because, since the aforementioned are exempt from their ordinary jurisdiction, they are exempt from the coercive power of that same jurisdiction. This is tantamount to saying that they are exempt from the penalties imposed on transgressors, in accordance with the response of Our Predecessor Clement III (in the chapter “A Nobis,” 21, “De sententia excommunicationis”). So that no inconvenience may arise from this exemption, in the case at hand, we order that if in the future (which we do not believe will happen) any of the aforementioned persons were to go to the convents of nuns and speak with them without the proper license, contrary to what has been established in the Episcopal Synods, the Constitutions, or the Episcopal Proclamations, they should report this to Us or to the Congregation for Bishops and Regulars, which will not fail to take the necessary measures to vindicate the despised episcopal authority; and We are all the more willing to adhere to this decision because We see that it was adopted and established (in the event that the Ordinaries were to take action against those exempt and immediately subject to the Apostolic See) by Our Predecessor John XXII (chap. Cum Matthaeus, \*De hereticis\*, in the \*Stravaganti comuni\*), and by Our other Predecessor Eugene IV (chap. \*Divina\*, \*de privilegiis\*, also among the \*Stravaganti comuni\*). 6. This is what we have deemed necessary to do. It now remains for you, Venerable Brother, to diligently preserve this Letter of Ours in your Chancery and to notify its contents in the manner you deem most appropriate to whomever it is to be notified. Embracing you fraternally in the Lord, We bestow upon you and the flock entrusted to you the Apostolic Blessing. Given at Rome, at Saint Mary Major, on October 31, 1749, the tenth year of Our Pontificate.
