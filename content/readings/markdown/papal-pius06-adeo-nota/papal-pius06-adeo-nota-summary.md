# Adeo nota · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VI condemns the revolutionary transformation of Avignon and the Comtat Venaissin, describing violence, removal of papal officials, confiscation of Church goods, and the imposition of the French Civil Constitution of the Clergy. A central case is the coerced election of Benedetto Francesco Malierio as capitular vicar while the lawful archbishop remained alive and in office. The pope rejects the purported vacancy, the election's freedom and voting sufficiency, and Malierio's subsequent jurisdictional acts.

The letter extends the French ecclesiastical censures to these territories, suspends Malierio and a cooperating priest from ministry, and orders the faithful to remain with their legitimate pastors even when persecution forces them away. Pius separately addresses civil government in his capacity as territorial sovereign. He expressly distinguishes this intervention from his treatment of French civil legislation, then annuls attempts to transfer allegiance to France, establish a republic, or displace papal administration. He reiterates willingness to remedy genuine abuses through consultation, while calling for an end to factions and a return to lawful order, charity, and peace. Both strands defend the authority he regards as canonically and civilly legitimate.

## Key themes

- Coercion and invalidity in the Avignon capitular election
- Continuing jurisdiction of displaced legitimate pastors
- Distinction between ecclesiastical discipline and territorial sovereignty
- Papal rejection of annexation and unauthorized constitutional change
