# Inter Omnigenas

**La Santa Sede**

ENCYCLICAL INTER OMNIGENAS OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV To the Venerable Brothers, Beloved Sons, Archbishops, Bishops, Parish Priests, and to the entire Clergy and People of the Kingdom of Serbia and the other neighboring regions. Pope Benedict XIV. Venerable Brothers, Beloved Sons, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. Among the calamities of every kind by which the children of the Church living under the rule of the infidels are oppressed on every side—and for all of which we feel compassion with paternal charity— those that most trouble and weigh upon our hearts are those from which we fear an occasion of perdition may arise for the souls redeemed by the Blood of Christ, with the consequence that harm may be caused to the integrity of the Catholic Faith and discipline. Among these calamities—which you, Venerable Brothers and Beloved Sons, have long endured in the Kingdom of Serbia under the harshest yoke of the Turks, and which have at other times reached Our ears from many quarters— We were struck with incredible sorrow by the very latest news, which was explained to Us in greater detail and almost laid bare before Our eyes by Our Venerable Brother John the Baptist, whom We have elected and appointed Archbishop of Skopje. Indeed, even though We have had to praise—and indeed admire—the assiduous vigilance and care for their flock shown by the shepherds of that Kingdom, as well as the steadfast perseverance of the people in the Faith and in piety, amid the most grievous oppressions and persecutions inflicted by the cruelty of the infidels and the hatred of the schismatics, We were nevertheless deeply pained both by the inconsistent, wavering, or even arbitrary behavior in matters of the utmost importance, on the part of some of them, as well as the corruption of morals and discipline brought about in the majority of the faithful by the company of foreigners; but above all, the shameful concealment of the Christian profession—resembling infidelity—which many in these regions are seen to practice out of fear of material harm. 1. In truth, those things which are said to have been introduced among the faithful of these Churches against the purity of the faith and morals should, for the most part, have been prevented or corrected and remedied by virtue of the well-known sanctions of pontifical and canon law, of the decrees of the Apostolic See issued most often through the agency of Our Venerable Brothers, the Cardinals of the Holy Roman Church, entrusted with the affairs of “Propaganda Fide,” but especially by virtue of what was established at the Albanian Council, which was convened and held under Our predecessor of blessed memory, Clement XI, by the Primate of that Kingdom, for all the clergy and people of Albania and Serbia. Therefore, reminding all of you of all the aforementioned laws of Ecclesiastical Law and of the Apostolic See, and strongly urging you to study and observe them, we command that the aforementioned Albanian Council, adapted especially to your situation and to the needs of the times, be upheld in every respect and observed everywhere, desiring that all Archbishops, Bishops, parish priests, missionaries, and others who have the care of souls in this Kingdom to keep a copy of that Council with them and to strive to regulate and conform their conduct and that of their faithful according to those norms. 2. However, in order that the most serious abuses concerning the integrity of the Faith, morals, and rites—which have come to Our attention from those regions—may be completely removed and eliminated forever, after having considered and examined them ourselves with care and diligence, We have decided, with the counsel of Our Venerable Brothers, to communicate to you and proclaim the following Decrees, whose provisions We trust in the Lord will be willingly embraced by all of you to whom it falls, as useful and necessary; nevertheless, We order with Apostolic authority that they be most strictly carried out and observed. 3. Beginning, therefore, with matters of the Faith—without which it is impossible to please God—we strictly order and command all the faithful of this Kingdom, one and all, who wish to maintain communion with the Catholic Church, to refrain from doing or admitting anything contrary to the precepts and norms of the Gospel, in order to conceal their adherence to the Christian religion—even though this may sometimes be lawful and necessary—especially those things that imply an affirmation of the Muslim sect. Therefore, if they have undergone circumcision, let them know that Christ will be of no benefit to them whatsoever, according to the word of the Apostle. They must completely avoid taking Turkish names, which they should not even utter; from frequenting the abominable temples of the infidels, called mosques; and from desecrating the days of ecclesiastical fasting by eating meat: all this in order to be believed to be Muslims. For all these things, even if the Christian faith is preserved in the heart, cannot be done without feigning the errors of Muhammad, which is contrary to Christian sincerity; such pretense contains a lie regarding a most serious matter and entails a virtual denial of the Faith, constituting a most grave offense against God and a scandal to one’s neighbor. 4. But even more so, should they be questioned by public authorities, they must know that it is not lawful for them to profess themselves followers of the Mohammedan sect, but let them remember that this is the time when—wielding the shield of the Faith—they must not only believe in His righteousness with their hearts, but also confess Christ with their mouths for salvation; otherwise, if they have dared to deny Him before men, He too will deny them before His Father. 5. Equally impious and unlawful is the misconduct of those Christians in Serbia who, nearing death, permit or arrange for their bodies to be handed over for burial by the Turks, with the assistance of the latter and through the use of Muslim rites; for, if they are not to be ashamed of Christ at all while they live, they must at least not be ashamed of Him at the moment when they are about to appear before His terrible judgment, so that He may not be ashamed of them before His Eternal Father. 6. It shall therefore be the duty of bishops, parish priests, and missionaries to seriously instruct and admonish those Christians who impiously dare to do the aforementioned things, which constitute a grave offense against the Faith; they boast in vain of their guardianship, their zeal for the Christian law, and the upbringing of their children in that same law, for if they fail in even one of these points, they are guilty of all of them. Therefore, let them declare openly to them that whoever, out of fear of any authority or out of fear of losing material possessions, betrays his faith, brings God’s wrath upon himself and excludes himself from all hope of salvation, unless he repents, for he fears man more than God and prefers to preserve the fleeting things of this earth rather than acquire eternal realities. If, however, some wish to persist obstinately on this path of impiety, they shall be deprived of the sacraments during their lifetime and, if they die unrepentant, of prayers for the dead after their death; no minister of the Church shall dare to admit them; otherwise, he shall be punished by his own bishop with canonical penalties, as also prescribed by the aforementioned Albanian Council. 7. Likewise, those women who, having been brought into the Turkish camp under the guise of wives while concealing their Christian faith, lead a life there far removed from any practice of religion, the pastors must make it clear to them that they should not place their trust for eternal salvation in that faith which, being dead without works, they believe they can usefully preserve only in their hearts. 8. As for the children of these women, who are presented to the parish priests for baptism, if their lives appear to be in danger, the aforementioned parish priests should not hesitate to baptize them, admonishing the mothers that, if the children recover, they must commit to raising them in the Christian faith. As for those of sound and robust constitution, who are presented for Baptism by the aforementioned mothers without superstitious motives, but with the sole purpose of obtaining salvation—since it is impossible to examine the individual circumstances that might indicate whether they will persevere in the observance of the Gospel law and the Faith, or whether, deprived of a Christian upbringing by mothers of that sort, they will follow the impiety of their Muslim father—considering also the dangers of infancy, due to which, as they say, about one-third of children die before reaching the age of 10—we do not deem it necessary to issue any specific command. We merely exhort the ecclesiastical ministers that, after having earnestly implored the light of the Holy Spirit, they act in accordance with His guidance and the dictates of His prudence. If, then, they deem it appropriate to admit them to Baptism, let them not fail to impress upon the mothers the strict obligation they are bound to fulfill: to make known the truth of God to these children of the Church, should they reach the age of reason, and to educate them in the discipline and law of the Lord. 9. It has also come to Our attention the grave and most regrettable news that the Decrees of the Council of Trent concerning the Sacrament of Matrimony are not being observed by some in these regions, where—as the Albanian Council itself attests—they were duly published in their time. Therefore, declaring that all the faithful in these regions are bound by the aforementioned Decrees, We declare completely invalid and null those so-called marriages that are contracted before the Turkish judge alone, known as the “cadi,” or even without him, by the spouses alone, and not in accordance with the prescriptions of the aforementioned Council of Trent. We command that those who have entered into such null and clandestine marriages and, having done so, live together, shall—as persons living in illicit concubinage—be barred from receiving the sacraments, unless they do penance for their past and are united in a marriage valid according to the Church. 10. But when a marriage has been contracted according to the rite of the faithful, we do not permit them at all—not even to protect their wives from abduction by the Turks—to renew it before the Qadi through proxies according to the Turkish rite, unless the Muslim wedding rite is purely civil and contains no invocation of Muhammad or any other form of superstition. In fact, although they do this not in person but through proxies, they must never be considered innocent of the crime committed by their authority or mandate. 11. Regarding the banns established by the Council of Trent, although it is said that in Serbia they are not at all confirmed by custom, since they are nevertheless prescribed for parish priests, including those in Serbia, in the aforementioned Albanian Council—with the authority to grant dispensations removed except for reasons of urgent necessity—we command that this be observed in every respect, as far as possible. 12. Furthermore, if the wife of any believer flees to the Turks and dares to enter into an illicit marriage with one of them, it is not lawful for the husband to marry another woman in her place, since marriage—which is indissoluble by divine right as long as the spouses live— is in no way dissolved by the misdeed of such a woman. Therefore, if a man in such a situation marries another woman, he commits adultery; and if he does not completely separate from her, he must be kept from the sacraments. 13. And it is clear to all what must be said regarding the salvation of such women, unless they do penance. With regard to Christian women who were forcibly abducted by the Turks and married against their will or as children—and who, without being bound by any sacramental bond of faith, persist in illicit cohabitation with infidels—we establish in all respects the same ruling that was decreed at the aforementioned Albanian Council: they are to be denied the sacraments of the Church, taking no account whatsoever of their alleged perseverance in the Christian faith, nor of the violence inflicted upon them by the Turks in childhood, nor even of the fact that they are considered by the Turks to be their sole, best, or rightful wife. These circumstances do not confer any right upon those living in concubinage or fornication to receive the sacraments, nor do they grant priests any authority to administer them to those who are unworthy. 14. Regarding marital dispensations, the bishops and missionaries of Serbia are to be careful not to use the powers conferred upon them by this Holy See indiscriminately or toward those who are unworthy, and not to exceed the limits of their authority. We have therefore decreed that no dispensation is to be granted to those secret Christians, mentioned above, who pretend to follow the rites of Islam; for since they are ashamed of Christ, they render themselves unworthy of the graces of the Church, which is the bride of Christ. Furthermore, they must not grant any dispensation in cases where they foresee that marriages will not be celebrated validly and sanctified according to the rite of the Catholic Church, as stated above; for in such cases they would not be dispensations, but rather encouragements to immorality and incitements to licentiousness, from which the faithful and prudent minister of Christ must keep himself far removed in every way. 15. Above all, they should consider that, among the other powers conferred upon them, there is none to dispense from the impediment of public decency arising from a previous marriage that took place between one of the parties and a first-degree blood relative of the other party, even if that marriage was dissolved before consummation—whether by death or by some other legitimate cause. In fact, this impediment is stronger than that arising from betrothal; therefore, they must refrain from granting a dispensation of this kind. 16. When celebrating weddings, the times prescribed by the Catholic Church must be observed. If, however, Muslims, in celebrating their weddings during prohibited times, have invited any Catholic on account of his office—since the precepts of the Church do not in the least concern those outside it—Catholics in Serbia are not prohibited from participating, provided they conduct themselves with Christian modesty, provided that this can be done without offending the Creator, the faithful, or the Church of God, and provided that there is no invocation of Muhammad at the Turks’ weddings and no superstitious rites in which the invited Christians are required to participate or consent to, either verbally or by their actions. However, if they strive, as much as they can, to avoid those gatherings of infidels and those profane feasts, they will avoid many dangers to their souls. 17. With regard to spiritual kinship, we command that the most wise Decrees of the Council of Trent be observed in their entirety in Serbia, notwithstanding any contrary custom. Therefore, we do not permit that spiritual kinship arising from the sacraments of Baptism and Confirmation to be extended beyond the persons and degrees defined by the Council itself, and we expressly declare that no spiritual kinship arises from any other cause—especially not from attending a wedding, even at the invitation of the bride and groom; nor even among those who cut children’s hair for the first time. Indeed, the rationale behind the aforementioned conciliar decree is important: to prevent, due to excessive prohibitions, situations where marriages are contracted in prohibited cases without the parties’ knowledge, or where they persist in sin, or where such marriages must be dissolved to the scandal of the faithful. What was wisely established regarding those cases of spiritual consanguinity that had already been accepted into the Church must, with even greater reason, apply to other similar cases which, unknown in the Catholic Church, have an origin tainted by schismatics—for whom it is typical to impose upon people heavy burdens that are impossible to bear, without lifting a finger to help. Therefore, the scandal caused by these people is certainly to be despised, should they learn of the observance of this decree among the faithful. 18. As for the sacred rites, in which the Churches of these regions—taking this Roman Church, Mother and Teacher of all others, as their mirror and example—demonstrate that they use nothing other than the Missal, the Roman Ritual, and the Roman Ceremonial, we urge our Venerable Brothers, the Archbishops and Bishops, not to change anything in this sound and praiseworthy custom; whether in the celebration of the Holy Mysteries and the administration of the Sacraments, or in Blessings and Exorcisms, they must not permit, under any pretext, the introduction of any other rites, ceremonies, or prayers taken from elsewhere. 19. They should also ensure that the reasons for concern sometimes cited for omitting the ceremonies prescribed by the Roman Ritual in the administration of Baptism are not groundless or trivial; and, should they be neglected for genuine and serious reasons, they shall also see to it that they are performed as soon as possible. Indeed, rites of such great importance and such antiquity—and which are supremely necessary for fostering reverence for the Sacrament—must not be neglected without committing a grave sin. 20. Care must also be taken to ensure that, except in cases of necessity or just fear inspired by non-believers, ordinary, natural water—or even water blessed for purification—is not used in the administration of Baptism; nor should one recklessly omit the use of holy water for this specific purpose, as prescribed by the Roman Ritual. In fact, it can hardly happen—except through the utmost negligence and groundless fear (even according to the understanding of the Albanian Council)—that in parish churches, where they exist, the baptismal fonts are not blessed at the established times and according to the rites, or that there is not a sufficient quantity of Sacred Oils for this purpose. 21. In fulfilling their pastoral duty, they should also ensure that the faithful residing in any place have access to Catholic priests who can administer the Most Holy Eucharist to them on the Solemnity of Easter, both so that the Decree of the Lateran Council may be observed for all the faithful of both sexes, and so that, in the common joy of the whole Church for the Resurrection of the Lord, the children of the Church may be nourished and strengthened by this life-giving pasture, which is also a symbol of unity. And if it should happen that, due to the unfavorable circumstances of place and time, this cannot in any way be done within the two weeks between Palm Sunday and Low Sunday, in that case, in accordance with this Letter, we grant and permit the people of those regions of Serbia to fulfill that precept either during Lent or on the Solemnity of Pentecost and the days preceding it, according to the counsel of their own priest. 22. It is with great sorrow that we have learned that the churches in these regions are so neglected and dilapidated, and that the fury of the infidels is so brazen, that it is not possible to preserve the Most Holy Eucharist in a decent and secure manner, as is proper. Consequently, most of the sick faithful pass away without receiving the Viaticum of salvation. In the future, this most grave evil must be remedied and addressed as far as possible; therefore, parish priests must strive with eager diligence to learn of the sick, not only to purify them through the Sacrament of Penance, and to help and uplift them with Christian exhortations and spiritual comfort, but also so that they may be nourished with the Most Holy Body of Christ and strengthened to face the final battle. Therefore, when they see that the danger of death looms over any of the faithful, they must administer the aforementioned Sacrament of the Eucharist to them as soon as possible; and, if they can do so without danger, having taken it from the church—if there is one—they must bring it to the sick person’s home, since it is not permissible to celebrate Mass at the bedside of the sick in an unconsecrated place, except in cases of extreme necessity. 23. Furthermore, while the priest brings such a great Sacrament to the sick, he must strictly observe the decrees promulgated at the Council of Albania, which command that, having put on the surplice and placed the stole over his shoulders, with at least one candle in front of him, and reciting hymns and psalms in a low voice, devoutly carry the Sacrament in the Sacred Pyx or in a clean Chalice, holding it before his chest with both hands. But when the tyranny and iniquity of the Turks are at their worst (as is added in the same passage), the priest shall always keep the stole covered by his vestments, conceal the ciborium in a small bag or pouch—which, suspended from the neck by cords, he shall hold against his chest—and shall never go alone, but shall be accompanied by at least one of the faithful, in the absence of a cleric. 24. Finally, regarding the burial of the bodies of the faithful, all the vain superstitions of the Turks are to be avoided, from which, in truth, certain superstitious rites have their impure origin—such as washings performed with incense and the recitation of certain prayers that are disapproved of by the Catholic Church. Therefore, by refraining as much as possible from any negative appearances and from imitating the infidels, the people of these regions should learn that there is nothing in such rites that is necessary for the salvation and intercession of the deceased, and they should attach no importance either to the rumors and mockery of the Turks or to the vain discourse of the schismatics. 25. In assessing the dangers in the face of which we have declared that the rigor of ecclesiastical discipline may be mitigated under the circumstances set forth above, we admonish and implore all the faithful in these regions—and especially the shepherds of souls—that, by lifting up despondent spirits with Christian fortitude, they may consider what is truly to be feared and what is to be despised; that they may observe the precepts of God and the Church not out of anxiety or fear of earthly authorities, but with the generosity of charity and the ardor of love that drives out fear; and that they may administer the care of souls. And if they deem the mere fear of insults from the Turks or the danger of minor inconveniences to be a just cause for transgressing the precepts of the Christian faith or for neglecting the care of the souls entrusted to them, then it may truly be said of them: “They trembled with fear where there was nothing to fear.” Therefore, we exhort in the Lord and implore our Venerable Brothers, the Archbishops and Bishops, whom the Holy Spirit has appointed to govern these Churches oppressed by a host of most grievous calamities, to drive these vain fears from the hearts of the lower ministers of the Church and of all the faithful, and to lift them up and encourage them so that, trampling underfoot both the allurements and the terrors of the world, they may follow Christ with constancy along the arduous and narrow path, the Head of the Church, who calls them to the summit of sanctification. 26. Finally, let them recognize the singular mercy shown to them by our God, who, while by terrible judgment He permitted the Christian religion to be completely trampled underfoot and extinguished in other regions subject to the dominion of the infidels, willed instead that in this Kingdom of Serbia the light of His truth should shine, so that, by beholding it, those who find themselves in distress and tribulation might receive consolation in this life and be led to attain the better and more blessed life to come. 27. Therefore, Venerable Brothers, Beloved Sons, reflect once more, and take care that, through the fault of an ungrateful heart, the flow of divine mercy toward you does not dry up and the Kingdom of God be taken from you, since you will have disdained to obey its laws and to preserve the customs rightly established. 28. If, then, the decrees set forth above—to which we wish to call your attention in order to uphold the purity of the most holy laws of the Church, and which we hereby declare with Apostolic authority in this Letter of ours must be fully carried out by you— seem burdensome and impossible to bear to you, take care not to attribute—in your own judgment—to the light burden of Christ and the gentle yoke of His law that heaviness and distress which stem either from excessive concern for preserving temporal goods or from the greed to acquire them. If you reject these things and hold that the servitude of the world cannot be reconciled with the servitude you have pledged to Christ, everything will truly seem light and easy to you in the observance of Christian law. Moreover, God is faithful and will not allow you to be persecuted by the unbelievers nor to be tempted beyond your strength; rather, He will turn temptation to your benefit and will richly reward the brief moments of your tribulations with an eternal store of glory. Wishing this for you from the heart, through the Father of mercies and the God of all consolation, we affectionately impart the Apostolic Blessing to all of you. Given in Rome, at Saint Mary Major, on February 2, 1744, the fourth year of Our Pontificate.
