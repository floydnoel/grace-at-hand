## Summary

*Inter Omnigenas* (Latin: "Among the Calamities of Every Kind") is an encyclical issued by Pope Benedict XIV on February 2, 1744, addressed to the bishops, clergy, and faithful of Serbia and neighboring regions under Ottoman rule. It addresses the pastoral crisis facing Catholics living under Turkish domination: the concealment of Christian faith (crypto-Christianity), the corruption of morals through contact with Muslims, and the widespread abuse of the sacrament of marriage — including clandestine marriages contracted before Turkish civil judges, divorce and remarriage when spouses were abducted, and the granting of dispensations to those who outwardly simulated Islamic rites.

## Historical Context

In the mid-18th century, Serbia and the surrounding Balkans were under Ottoman Turkish rule. Catholics formed a minority, pressed between a Muslim ruling class and a majority Eastern Orthodox population (whom the document calls "schismatics"). Many Catholics, facing persecution and social marginalization, adopted the practice of outwardly conforming to Islam — sometimes called "Nikodemism" or crypto-Christianity — while secretly maintaining Christian belief. The encyclical was prompted by a report from John the Baptist (Giovanni Battista), the newly appointed Archbishop of Skopje, who brought detailed accounts of these abuses to the Pope's attention. Benedict XIV, a renowned canon lawyer and one of the great scholar-popes of the 18th century, was responding to a concrete pastoral emergency at the intersection of Catholic sacramental theology, Islamic law, and Ottoman social reality.

## Key Themes

- **Prohibition of crypto-Christianity**: The Pope condemns those who conceal or deny their Christian faith to avoid persecution, declaring that those who "simulate the Moslem rites" are "ashamed of Christ" and unworthy of the Church's favors, including matrimonial dispensations.
- **Marriage and the Council of Trent**: The encyclical reaffirms that the Tridentine form of marriage is binding in these regions; marriages contracted before a Turkish judge (*caid*) or without observing proper canonical form are declared null and void.
- **Indissolubility of marriage**: Even if a Catholic wife is abducted by Turks and enters a union with one of them, her husband may not remarry — the bond of marriage remains valid and indissoluble by divine law while both spouses live. Any man who remarries in such circumstances commits adultery and must be refused the sacraments.
- **Discipline of the sacraments**: Those who contract clandestine or invalid marriages and cohabit are to be excluded from the sacraments unless they separate and regularize their union. Pastors are warned against granting dispensations rashly or exceeding their faculties.
- **Pastoral vigilance**: Bishops and missionaries are instructed to guard against laxity in granting dispensations and to ensure that marriages are celebrated validly and "in a holy manner according to the rites of the Catholic Church."

## Significance

*Inter Omnigenas* is a significant document in the history of Catholic sacramental discipline, particularly regarding marriage under conditions of religious persecution. It demonstrates Benedict XIV's characteristic approach: combining a canonist's precision with pastoral compassion, while drawing a firm line against practices that compromised the integrity of the faith or the sacraments. The encyclical is also an important historical witness to the phenomenon of crypto-Christianity under Ottoman rule, the challenges facing the Catholic Church in Muslim-majority societies, and the development of papal teaching on marital indissolubility as a matter of divine law — themes that would resonate in later magisterial documents on marriage. Benedict XIV himself later referred to *Inter Omnigenas* as an "encyclical" in his subsequent letter *Quod Provinciale* (1754), confirming its place in the corpus of papal teaching.
