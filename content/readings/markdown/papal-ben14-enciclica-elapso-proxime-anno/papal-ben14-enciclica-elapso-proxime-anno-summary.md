## Summary

*Elapso Proxime Anno* (1751) is an encyclical of Pope Benedict XIV addressed to the Inquisitors of the Holy Office. It clarifies that the crime of heresy is an explicit exception to the local immunity of churches (the right of sanctuary). While previous constitutions had established rules for removing common criminals from immune places — requiring episcopal authorization, judicial torture to establish sufficient grounds, and safeguards for their return if evidence later collapsed — heretics are categorically excluded from this protection and may be removed from church asylum without the usual procedural guarantees.

## Historical Context

The encyclical builds on Benedict XIV's own constitution *Officii Nostri* (March 15, 1750), which itself codified and tightened earlier rulings by Gregory XIV, Benedict XIII, and Clement XII on church immunity. The 18th-century Church faced ongoing challenges from Protestant reform movements, Jansenist controversies, and the growing power of secular states. The Inquisition remained active in parts of Europe (notably the Papal States, Spain, and Portugal), and questions of jurisdictional boundaries between ecclesiastical and civil authority — including the right of sanctuary — were fiercely contested. This document reaffirms that the fight against heresy overrides traditional immunities.

## Key Themes

- **Exception of heresy from church asylum:** Heretics do not enjoy the privilege of local immunity, even under the most favorable constitutions — the crime is so grave that sanctuary does not apply.
- **Hierarchical control of removal:** Only bishops or higher prelates may authorize the extraction of criminals from immune places; lower-ranking ordinaries are excluded.
- **Procedural safeguards for ordinary criminals:** For non-heresy cases, removal requires torture-established evidence, episcopal authorization, clerical supervision, and a promise of return to the Church if evidence collapses.
- **Papal centralization:** Benedict XIV is systematically consolidating and clarifying disparate precedents into a single, uniform discipline.
- **Cooperation with secular authority:** The Church regulates how and when criminals may be handed over to civil courts, asserting ongoing ecclesiastical jurisdiction even after extradition.

## Significance

*Elapso Proxime Anno* represents a notable moment in the Church's long retreat from the medieval right of sanctuary. By narrowing immunity for the sake of combating heresy, Benedict XIV prioritised doctrinal enforcement over traditional privileges. The encyclical also illustrates the 18th-century papacy's effort to rationalise and centralise canon law, a project that would culminate in the 1917 Code of Canon Law. It remains a reference point in Catholic teaching on the limits of ecclesiastical immunity and the Church's cooperation with civil authority in suppressing grave threats to the faith.
