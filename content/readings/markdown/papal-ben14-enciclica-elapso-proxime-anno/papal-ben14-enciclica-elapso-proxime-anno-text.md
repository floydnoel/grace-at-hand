# Elapso Proxime Anno

**La Santa Sede**

---

> *Note: This document was translated from the original Italian into English. Translation provided by DeepL (July 2026).*

ENCYCLICAL ELAPSO PROXIME ANNO OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV To the Inquisitors of the Holy Office delegated to combat the perverse heresy. Pope Benedict XIV. Beloved Son, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. On March 15 of last year, 1750, We published a Constitution beginning with the words “Officii nostri” concerning the local immunity of churches. In it, in accordance with the Constitutions of Our Predecessors Gregory XIV, Benedict XIII, and Clement XII, after having eliminated certain quibbles and subterfuges that impeded their enforcement, We declared that – should any criminal, with the exception of those granted immunity, take refuge in an immune place – he must be removed from there whenever there were sufficient grounds, established by torture, to prove the crime. We also declared that the extraction should take place only with the authorization of the Bishop and with the assistance of a clerical official to be designated by him; and that, upon delivery to the secular court, the court should be warned of the penalties provided for should the person extracted not be returned to the Church, whenever, in the course of the proceedings, the evidence against him were to be removed. And since Our aforementioned Predecessors had established that removal from a place of immunity could be carried out only by Bishops or by Prelates superior to them, excluding those of lower rank, whether they were Ordinaries or nullius dioecesis and had a separate territory—in which case the removal was to be entrusted to the neighboring bishop—We, too, in Our aforementioned Constitution, established the same rule. 1. As you well know, the crime of heresy is an exception, and the offender does not enjoy asylum within the Church, even under the Constitution of Gregory XIV governing local immunity. Since, during the session of the Congregation of the Holy Inquisition held, as usual, before Us on Thursday, January 28, of this year 1751, the question was raised as to how it should be carried out, and how the removal of a person guilty of heresy should be carried out from the church where he had taken refuge—either to avoid imprisonment or having fled after arrest— or who had been sentenced to galley service, to forced labor, or to another form of custodial punishment, We reserved the right—having issued the aforementioned Constitution the previous year—to rule on the matter. This is Our decision. 2. Either the offense is heresy, which is the primary matter within the jurisdiction of the Tribunal of the Holy Office; or it concerns other crimes not specifically exempted that do not enjoy immunity; or other crimes not specifically exempted that do enjoy immunity but fall under the Tribunal’s jurisdiction, having been committed by someone who, being subject to its jurisdiction, must be tried by the Tribunal itself. 3. If it is a crime of heresy, as was already determined by Our Predecessor John XXI, known as John XXII, in his Constitution beginning with \*Ex parte vestra\* (in the \*Bollario Romano\*, Volume 1), that heretics, or those suspected of heresy, as well as Jews converted to the Catholic Faith who subsequently fall into apostasy, if they take refuge in churches, are to be immediately removed from them by the Inquisitor; we do not intend to derogate from the aforementioned Constitution. On the contrary, we prescribe its observance, but with the following addition: when carrying out the removal from the church, care must be taken to do so with due respect for the House of God; and since it is in no way practicable to communicate to the Bishop, prior to the removal, the evidence held against the offender, as this is prohibited by the law of Secrecy; and since, even if this were possible, it would be useless, given that the Holy Tribunal does not proceed with the arrest unless it has reliable evidence of the crime, nevertheless, do not fail—whether before or after the arrest—to notify the Bishop of this, both out of due deference to his Dignity and to fulfill, as best as possible, the provisions set forth in the Constitutions of Gregory, Benedict, Clement, and Ours. This is also ordered by Us, since the case was brought before the Congregation of the Holy Office convened before Our Predecessor Urban VIII on Thursday, June 10, 1638, and the following question was debated: “Whether a person accused of heresy who has taken refuge in a church must be removed by the Bishop or the Inquisitor,” to which the Pontiff, “having heard the arguments, replied: The accused may be removed by the Inquisitor, provided that the Ordinary is notified, either before or after.” 4. As for other crimes that are exempted but do not constitute heresy—even if they fall under the jurisdiction of the Sacred Tribunal, either because they were committed by someone subject to it or on some other charge— we declare that the offenders in cases not exempted shall enjoy immunity, and that for offenders in exempted cases—when they are not guilty of heresy—since they must be removed from the Church, the provisions set forth in Our Constitution and in previous ones shall be strictly observed: sufficient evidence to justify torture shall be communicated to the Bishop, without the law of secrecy being an obstacle in this regard; expulsion shall not be carried out except with the Bishop’s authorization and with the personal involvement of a cleric to be designated by him, and in compliance with all that is prescribed in the aforementioned Constitutions. 5. What has been stated thus far must also be applied to those who escape from prisons, galleys, or their place of exile and take refuge in a church or other sanctuary; for if they are guilty or convicted of the crime of heresy, they must be removed by the Inquisitor, and the Bishop must be notified of this, sooner or later. If they are offenders or those convicted of another crime not specifically exempted—and not for the crime of heresy, or for another crime not specifically exempted—as for the former, the removal shall be carried out with the Bishop’s authorization and with the assistance of a cleric to be designated by the Bishop; as for the latter, they are to be left in the asylum to which they have fled, even if they have escaped from prison in chains, provided they are not being sought by the officials of said prisons or by other officials of justice, unless the Bishop has received an indult from the Apostolic See authorizing him to remove from the churches those who have escaped from prison. In this case, one may appeal to him to regain custody of the fugitive, and one may also appeal to the Apostolic See when the Bishop does not have the aforementioned indulgence, since the Holy See does not refuse, in particular cases, to grant Bishops—when the crime so requires— the authority to remove from the churches those who have escaped from prisons and galleys, even if they are guilty of any crime whatsoever. This is what we wished to convey to the Inquisitors, and in the meantime we impart our Apostolic Blessing to each of them. Given in Rome, at Santa Maria Maggiore, on February 20, 1751, the eleventh year of Our Pontificate.
