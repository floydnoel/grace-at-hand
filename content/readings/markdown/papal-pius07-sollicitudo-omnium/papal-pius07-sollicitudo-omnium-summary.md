# Sollicitudo omnium · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VII restores the Society of Jesus throughout the world by extending the faculties previously granted in Russia and the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies. He recounts the Russian petition, supported by Emperor Paul I, that led to the 1801 authorization of a Jesuit body under Ignatius of Loyola's rule, and the extension requested by Ferdinand for the Two Sicilies in 1804. Reports of its educational and pastoral work, widespread requests, and the damage recent upheavals have inflicted on religious life now support a universal restoration.

The constitution authorizes the superior general and his delegates to admit members and organize houses, colleges, and provinces. Jesuits may operate seminaries and schools and, with local ordinaries' approval, preach, hear confessions, and administer sacraments. Pius places the restored society under immediate papal protection and obedience, reserving future regulation and correction. He exhorts members to fidelity to their founder and asks rulers and bishops to receive them favorably. Clement XIV's suppressing brief and other contrary provisions are expressly derogated from for the purposes of this restoration.

## Key themes

- Extension of earlier regional restorations to the whole Church
- Jesuit education, preaching, and sacramental ministry
- Ignatian rule and immediate papal oversight
- Derogation from the 1773 suppression
