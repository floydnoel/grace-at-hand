## Summary

*Laborem Exercens* ("Through Work") is Pope John Paul II's 1981 encyclical on the dignity and meaning of human work. Written on the 90th anniversary of Leo XIII's *Rerum Novarum*, the document presents a comprehensive Christian theology of work — arguing that work is not merely an economic commodity but a fundamental dimension of human existence through which man participates in God's creative activity, achieves personal fulfilment, and contributes to the common good.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was issued on 14 September 1981, though dated to 15 May (the 90th anniversary of *Rerum Novarum*). It was written during a period of intense Cold War tension, widespread industrial unrest, and growing awareness of global inequality between developed and developing nations. John Paul II had survived an assassination attempt earlier that year and completed the document during his hospital convalescence. The encyclical engages directly with the ideological conflict between liberal capitalism and Marxist communism, while extending the Church's social teaching tradition from Leo XIII through Pius XI, John XXIII, Paul VI, and the Second Vatican Council.

## Key Themes

- **The priority of labour over capital**: Work is the primary efficient cause in production; capital is merely an instrument. This principle corrects both capitalism and collectivism.
- **Subjective versus objective dimensions of work**: The personal dignity of the worker (subjective) takes precedence over the type or output of work (objective) — work is *for* man, not man *for* work.
- **Work as participation in God's creation**: Drawing on Genesis, human labour is a sharing in the creative activity of God, and through Christ the carpenter, work is sanctified.
- **The "Gospel of Work"**: Jesus himself was a worker, and the Paschal Mystery (Cross and Resurrection) gives redemptive meaning to the toil and suffering inherent in labour.
- **Rights of workers**: Just wages, the right to rest, safe working conditions, freedom of association and the right to form unions, and the legitimacy of the strike as a last resort.
- **The "indirect employer"**: States, international organizations, and transnational corporations bear moral responsibility for labour conditions alongside the direct employer.
- **Special care for the vulnerable**: Agricultural workers, disabled persons, immigrants, women, and the unemployed receive particular attention and defence of their rights.
- **Critique of economism and materialism**: Reducing labour to a mere economic factor or commodity is a fundamental ethical error that subordinates the person to material production.

## Significance

*Laborem Exercens* is a landmark in Catholic social teaching, offering the most developed theological reflection on work in the Church's magisterium. It introduced the foundational distinction between the subjective and objective dimensions of work and enshrined the "priority of labour over capital" as a key principle of social ethics. The encyclical avoids endorsing either liberal capitalism or Marxist collectivism, instead proposing a "personalist" third way centred on human dignity. Its spirituality of work — rooted in creation, the Incarnation, and the Paschal Mystery — continues to shape Catholic thinking on labour, economic justice, and the rights of workers globally.
