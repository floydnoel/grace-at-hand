## Summary

*Arcanum* (subtitled *On Christian Marriage*) is an 1880 encyclical by Pope Leo XIII affirming the divine origin, sacramental nature, unity, and indissolubility of Christian marriage. It defends the Church's sole authority over matrimony against the rising tide of civil marriage, divorce laws, and secular philosophies that sought to reduce marriage to a mere civil contract.

## Historical Context

Written in the wake of European unification movements, rising secularism, and the spread of liberal political ideologies, the encyclical responds to the proliferation of civil marriage laws and divorce legislation across Europe and America. The 19th century saw governments increasingly assert control over marriage, treating it as a purely civil matter — a trend Leo XIII squarely confronts. The document also reflects the Church's ongoing battle with Enlightenment rationalism, socialist thought, and Protestant doctrines that had loosened the traditional understanding of marriage.

## Key Themes

- **Marriage is divinely instituted** — not a human invention, but ordained by God from creation with unity and perpetuity as its essential properties.
- **Christ raised marriage to a sacrament**, making the marriage bond inseparable from the sacrament itself; there is no valid Christian marriage that is not also a sacrament.
- **The Church alone has authority over Christian marriage** — civil rulers have no power over the sacramental bond, only over its civil effects.
- **Divorce is a grave evil** that destroys families, harms children, degrades women, and undermines society; no civil law can dissolve a ratified and consummated Christian marriage.
- **The husband is the head of the family**, but the wife is a companion, not a servant; both are bound to mutual love, fidelity, and help.
- **Mixed marriages** are strongly discouraged due to risks to faith, religious unity, and children's education.

## Significance

*Arcanum* is a foundational document in modern Catholic teaching on marriage, anticipating and shaping later encyclicals like *Casti Connubii* (1930) and *Humanae Vitae* (1968). It articulated the Church's uncompromising stance on marriage's sacramental dignity and indissolubility during a period of aggressive secularization, and its principles continue to underpin Catholic canon law and pastoral practice regarding matrimony today.
