## Summary

*Ubi Lutetiam* is a letter from Pope Pius VI (1792) addressing French bishops who had received an apostolic indult granting them extraordinary powers during the crisis of the French Revolution. The bishops realized that the indult omitted the authority to absolve clergy from other dioceses, and they sought clarification on whether it also covered "intruding clergy" — those who had taken the civil oath and joined the schism, thereby occupying benefices illegally. The pope praises the bishops' scrupulous care not to exceed their granted authority and affirms his intention to remove all uncertainty.

## Historical Context

The document was written in 1792, at the height of the French Revolution. The Revolution had enacted the Civil Constitution of the Clergy (1790), which required all French clergy to swear an oath of loyalty to the state. Pope Pius VI condemned this in his April 1791 letter *Apostolicae Sedis*. By 1792, the Church in France was deeply divided between "juring" clergy (who took the oath) and "non-juring" clergy (who refused). Many sees were occupied by intruders — clergy who took the oath and were installed by revolutionary authorities in place of legitimate bishops. Pius VI had granted emergency faculties on March 19, 1792, but the rapidly deteriorating situation required further clarification.

## Key Themes

- **Papal authority and episcopal obedience**: The document commends the bishops for their careful restraint in not exceeding their delegated powers, reinforcing the principle that bishops exercise authority only in communion with and under the authority of the Roman Pontiff.
- **Absolution from censures**: The central question is who may be absolved — particularly whether the faculty extends to intruding clergy whose crime (usurping ecclesiastical office) is graver than that of those who merely took the civil oath.
- **Pastoral mercy amid crisis**: Despite the gravity of the schism, the pope recognizes that granting absolution to intruders could be of great benefit to religion, balancing discipline with the need for reconciliation.
- **Clarity in canon law**: The letter seeks to resolve ambiguity in the earlier indult, illustrating the importance of precise legal formulation in ecclesiastical governance.

## Significance

*Ubi Lutetiam* is a minor but revealing document from a pivotal moment: the French Revolution's assault on the Church's institutional structure. It shows Pius VI attempting to navigate a pastoral emergency while maintaining canonical order. The document exemplifies the papacy's struggle to respond to a revolutionary state that sought to control the Church from within. Its careful attention to the gradations of ecclesiastical crime (civil oath vs. intrusion) reflects the casuistic precision of 18th-century canon law. It also foreshadows the more extensive persecution Pius VI would endure — he was eventually arrested by French forces and died in exile in 1799.
