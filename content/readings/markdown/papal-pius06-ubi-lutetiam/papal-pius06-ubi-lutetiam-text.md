# Ubi Lutetiam

**Italian**

---

> *Note: This document was translated from the original Italian into English. Translation provided by DeepL (July 2026).*

1\. When the apostolic indult regarding certain extraordinary powers—which We granted on March 19 of this year to all archbishops, bishops, and diocesan administrators in France—reached Paris, your confreres residing in that city—who had obtained that indult on behalf of the entire episcopal college—immediately realized that the power to absolve clergy from other dioceses had been omitted from the powers granted to them; and, believing that this specific omission was due to an oversight, they turned to Us once more to ask that We include this faculty as well in a new formula. 2. Immediately afterward, your confreres stated that they did not know whether the authority included in the first article of the same indulgence—namely, the authority to absolve anyone from all censures, whether laypeople, secular clergy, or religious of either sex— and even those who had joined the schism and who had taken a civil oath, persisting in it for more than the forty days set forth in the Apostolic Letter of April 13 of last year so as not to incur suspension a divinis—whether the power to absolve intruding clergy was also included, whose crime is graver than that of those who bound themselves only by the civil oath and who were specifically mentioned in that article. 3. In this uncertain situation, the bishops, with devout and scrupulous care, declared that they did not in any way intend to exceed the limits of the powers granted to them; however, turning once more to Us to eliminate every cause of uncertainty, they made it clear to Us that they ardently desired that the Bishops be granted the authority to restore the grace of the Church to the intruders and to absolve them from all condemnation, since this authority would not only pose no danger but could, on the contrary, be of great benefit to the religion. 4. This conduct of yours, Our beloved sons and Venerable Brothers, is certainly worthy of the highest appreciation, which We express as We have already done on another occasion. Therefore, ready and willing to grant your petitions in all matters We deem beneficial to the Lord, We have resolved to confer upon you a new and broader authority that corresponds to your praiseworthy intentions and at the same time complies with the canonical rules and customs of the Church. 5. It is necessary, however, first and foremost, to consider that, as you have been clearly informed by Us on the first and second occasions, it is evident that among the powers contained in the general indult, We did not in any way intend to include that of absolving intruding clergy. The omission of this authority cannot be attributed to an oversight. In fact, upon personally reviewing Our letter of April 13, 1791, in which We not only mentioned the crime of taking a civil oath but also referred to the other crime committed by those who had invaded the episcopal and parish sees, the aspects pertaining to both the first and the second crime could not have escaped Our notice. If, in the first article cited, We clearly mentioned the former and passed over the latter in silence, it may well be inferred that the concession concerned the case of which We had spoken and that the one passed over in silence was reserved solely for Us. 6. On the other hand, We will never cede this to others, but will reserve for Ourselves alone the power to absolve the intruders; the main reason, without a doubt, is that between these two infamies lies a gulf that has been noted by your own confreres and is evident to all. In reality, however grave the crime attributed to those who, invoking the bond of Religion, bound themselves to observe a constitution which—according to the opinion of the entire French episcopate, as well as Our solemn declaration—is partly heretical and partly schismatic, nevertheless, a far greater and far more serious infamy is perpetrated by those who, deliberately and knowingly, set out to carry out what they had promised under oath (that is, sacrilegiously or consecrated according to the rite, if a bishop; sacrilegiously or ordained according to the rite, if a priest) and thus exercises an illegitimate mission and authority and invades episcopal or parish churches; having separated himself to such an extent from this Apostolic See and from the legitimate bishops, he accumulates day by day, with unprecedented speed, as many sins as there are acts he performs under illegitimate jurisdiction; he also profanes the highest Sacraments; he wretchedly leads the people into error; he brings into the kingdom a new constitutional church distinct from the Church of Jesus Christ, both in substance and in laws and in name; finally, he paves the way for schism with his own hand. To remedy this state of affairs, it was right and proper to appeal to the Holy See, as the entity that suffers injustice more than any other, and which can also employ appropriate measures of indulgence that could not be easily and properly applied if the power to absolve were entrusted to the arbitrary judgment of many. 7. Surely it is not unknown to you, Venerable Brothers, how severely the Church dealt with those responsible for such crimes. While always taking into account the difference between those who, through no fault of their own, were born to heretical or schismatic parents and those who, born to Catholic parents, voluntarily sided with the heretics, when faced with those born into heresy and schism and those who had become heretics and schismatics, she always treated the latter much more harshly, as they were considered more accountable. This severity of conduct was directed even more strictly against clergy, so much so that we know of the threats made against them: namely, that if anyone intentionally turned to a heretic and received ordination from him, he would be expelled from the Church. 8. This most severe rule of the Church is recalled by Saint Innocent I. Writing to Rufus and the other bishops of Macedonia, he teaches that a bishop or cleric ordained while in heresy or schism—or even one who later fell into it—may enjoy only lay communion, in accordance with the ancient rules handed down by the Apostles and their successors, which the Roman Church preserves. Although the Fathers of the First Council of Nicaea, showing a certain degree of leniency toward the Novatians, made an exception to the eighth canon by establishing that if the clerics returned to the Church, they would continue to be part of the clergy, and whoever was considered a bishop in his community would retain the office of priest—provided that the true bishop did not wish to confirm him in that office—they nevertheless established that, first and foremost, they would have to refute their error with more than convincing arguments; furthermore, they decreed that certain rules must be observed by them, namely, first: to recognize, accept, and follow all the precepts and dogmas of the Catholic and Apostolic Church; second: with regard to those who, during the persecution, continued to fall into error, the prescribed stages and established timeframes for proving their repentance must be observed—namely, those four degrees of penance which, according to the norms then in force, were to precede reconciliation and readmission to the sacraments; finally, that they abandon the occupied churches and leave them to the legitimate bishops. It is indeed certain, as the aforementioned Fathers stated, that only the titular bishop is to be recognized as holding episcopal dignity and that two bishops cannot coexist in the same city. 9. When Rufus, Bishop of Thessalonica, wished to receive—by virtue of this canon of Nicaea—the clerics ordained by the Photinian bishops and, in this regard, sought counsel from Saint Innocent I, the Pope replied: “I can say that this provision applies only to the Novatians and cannot concern other heretical clerics. In fact, if they had intended to include everyone, they would have added the other repentant heretics to the Novatians in order to have them readmitted to their order.”Undoubtedly, on this occasion, the Holy Pontiff established that a one-time amnesty cannot be presented in general terms, and it is quite evident that it was almost an administrative act “as a remedy and necessity of the moment.” 10. St. Jerome’s opinion was no different. In fact, speaking at the Synod of Rimini about bishops who had fallen into error, he declared that they, stripped of their episcopal prerogatives, should be restored to the lay state to mourn their crime for all eternity. However, the Holy Doctor observed that this severity could be moderated somewhat. To ensure that such clemency was accompanied by precise rules, at the Synod of Alexandria—attended by St. Athanasius, St. Hilary of Poitiers, and St. Eusebius of Vercelli, it was decided that, regarding the most serious crimes and those responsible for them—whose error could not be excused—the ancient rule should remain intact, while other sincere penitents could be readmitted to the Church. The entire Western Roman Church gave its consent, as St. Jerome himself confirms. 11. We shall not deviate in the slightest from the equity and indulgence subsequently accepted by the Church if, in responding to your general inquiry regarding the authority to absolve intruders, we distinguish between priests and other ecclesiastics of lower rank and archbishops and bishops of higher rank. To priests and other clergy—insofar as they fall within the scope of the fourth and fifth classes of the Apostolic Letter of March 19 of this year, as defined by Us—We grant, for one year, to each of you, Our beloved sons and Venerable Brothers, the authority to absolve—either directly or through priests appointed by you—all those who, whether ordained unlawfully or lawfully, occupied an entire parish or even a single part thereof and, having been delegated by intruder bishops, performed official acts there, and to bring them back into the bosom of the Church so that, in accordance with the indulgence granted by the aforementioned eighth canon of the Council of Nicaea, they may remain in the clergy. 12. And so that such absolutions are not granted rashly or are not inconsistent with one another, we shall conform to the aforementioned Council of Nicaea and to the most favorable principles of ecclesiastical discipline: we ordain that none of the intruders be absolved unless they have first renounced in writing the civic oath and the errors contained in the Civil Constitution of the French Clergy and have specifically declared that the ordinations received or conferred by the intruders are sacrilegious, that theauthority conferred by them is invalid, that the intrusion is unjust and null and void, as are the acts derived therefrom, and has not promised by oath to obey this Apostolic See and the legitimate Bishops, and finally that he has actually renounced the parish or the part of it that he occupied; such renunciation and abdication must be public, just as the offense was public, requiring each of them “according to what the sentiment and wisdom—as the Tridentine Fathers say—of salutary satisfactions suitable to the nature of the crime and the condition of the penitents may suggest,” in place of the degrees of public penance which, in force at the time of the Council of Nicaea, were later mitigated by the Church’s benevolence. We reserve to Ourselves the authority to permit those who are absolved to retain the benefices and parishes they have illegally occupied and possessed. 13. While we grant you this broader authority to absolve even clergy of lower rank from the infamous sacrilege of intrusion, we cannot, in truth, fail to address you and those clergy once more in a fatherly manner. To you, we repeat, we urge that you exercise the granted authority with caution for the sake of edification and scrupulously observe the conditions that have been prescribed for you; to the repentant clergy, that they may avail themselves of our indulgence with a grateful heart, without any pretense, since it would be of no benefit to them but would drive them toward greater ruin. For, though absolved before the Church, they would not be so before God at all, and they would turn the remedy into poison. If those who deny themselves do not possess the Holy Spirit, surely those who are falsely present in the Church cannot receive Him, for it is written: “The Holy Spirit flees from the false.” Therefore, whoever wishes to possess the Holy Spirit must guard against entering the Church under false pretenses; but if they have already entered in this way, they must avoid persisting in such pretense if they wish to remain closely united to the tree of life. 14. Furthermore, in order to remove any ambiguity, we add that the powers included in the aforementioned general indulgence, as well as those contained in this letter, are to be understood as granted both to French archbishops and bishops and to foreign archbishops and bishops whose populations and dioceses extend into the Kingdom of France 15. Likewise, as regards the archbishops and bishops of the higher ecclesiastical order—whether they be consecrators or assistants, or whether they themselves are intruders or merely bound by the civic oath—they all fall under the first, second, and third classes of Our letter of March 19 of this year, and We deem it appropriate to reserve to Ourselves and Our successors alone the authority to absolve them. For their defection, in fact, incurs a judgment that is far more severe than that of others and far surpasses it, since some of them are the leaders of this entire infamous situation; all can truly be considered the architects of the fatal schism that is ravaging this entire kingdom, all the more so since, according to the aforementioned canons, they deserve to be dealt with more severely. However, we certainly do not wish for this reservation to cause their spirits to be crushed and broken, but we ardently desire that their confidence be restored so that they may turn to our common Mother and take refuge promptly in her bosom. If, then, their repentance is sincere and they are willing to atone for their misdeeds through convincing acts of reparation and to renounce the occupied churches, we—as we have already declared with Pope Saint Leo and likewise declare once more—will welcome them with open arms and call upon them to enjoy, in harmony, peace and Our communion. Our intentions, like yours, aim at nothing other than to bring the straying back into the fold and to put an end once and for all to the schism, as we ceaselessly implore the Most High God with tears. 16. After all this, if there were ever a need, We shall inform you of a certain document that has just been forwarded to Us. It is a document that the schismatics—no longer hoping to defend their authority, which is despised by all and now utterly destroyed—have had the audacity to circulate in Our name under the title of “Breve,” written in French and German and, moreover, as if it had been issued “in Rome, at Santa Maria Maggiore, under the Fisherman’s Ring, on April 2, 1792,” that is, fourteen days after Our last letter, written on March 19 of this year. This false Brief, which begins with “Our paternal heart,” with unheard-of audacity declares all the apostolic letters We have written and published against the Civil Constitution of the French Clergy to be false; its authors and supporters strip this Holy See of its jurisdictional primacy, continue to extol the entire Constitution with endless praise, and urge the people to obey the constitutional bishops and parish priests. 17. Oh, ill-fated cunning! As if the false and slanderous tone of this writing were not evident to all, whether one considers the place from which it purports to have been issued —On April 2, we were residing, as indeed we still are, not at Santa Maria Maggiore, but at St. Peter’s—or by considering the entire context and the coherence of the argument as it was conceived. In fact, using the usual deceptive language so familiar to them, they add no argument that has not been refuted and rejected a hundred times; and, just as rightly, one can say that there are as many errors in that text as there are words. Nevertheless, so that the naive may not be deceived, We, consistent with what We stated in Our last Letter against such perverse documents, declare that writing to be false, fictitious, slanderous, heretical, and schismatic; therefore, We reject it, repudiate it, and condemn it. The greater the deceptions of Our adversaries, the greater must be Our vigilance and yours. We urge this upon you more than ever, and for the time being, to you, Our beloved sons and Venerable Brothers, and to the flocks entrusted to your care, We impart with great affection the Apostolic Blessing. Given in Rome, at St. Peter’s, on June 13, 1792, the eighteenth year of Our Pontificate.
