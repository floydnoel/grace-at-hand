## Summary

*Licet Multa* is an apostolic letter from Pope Leo XIII (1881) addressed to Cardinal Deschamps and the Belgian bishops. The Pope praises Belgian Catholics for their fidelity but expresses deep concern over internal divisions among Catholic citizens — especially disputes about whether civil institutions should conform to Catholic doctrine under "modern law." He calls for unity, charity in public debate, respect for ecclesiastical authority, and fidelity to the teaching of St. Thomas Aquinas in Catholic education.

## Historical Context

Written in 1881, during Leo XIII's pontificate and the wider *Kulturkampf*-era tensions between the Church and secular liberal states across Europe. In Belgium specifically, Catholics were sharply divided between those who advocated engagement with constitutional/constitutional monarchy ("modern law") and those who insisted on a restoration of confessional government. Leo XIII was simultaneously trying to defend Catholic social teaching (as in *Rerum Novarum* a decade later) while maintaining Church unity in a politically polarized environment.

## Key Themes

- **Unity of Catholics** — The Pope urgently warns against factionalism, which plays into the hands of the Church's enemies.
- **Church and State / "Modern Law"** — Addresses the controversy over accommodating Catholic principles to liberal constitutional orders; the Church must defend its doctrines but may prudently tolerate certain arrangements.
- **Charity in Controversy** — Forbids rash accusations against fellow Catholics, especially bishops and cardinals; defends episcopal authority and the right of the Holy See to judge doctrinal matters.
- **Thomistic Revival** — Renews the call for philosophy and theology to follow St. Thomas Aquinas (echoing *Aeterni Patris*, 1879); urges the expansion of Thomistic teaching at the University of Louvain.
- **Education** — Commends the bishops' work in religious education at all levels and insists on doctrinal unity among teachers.

## Significance

*Licet Multa* is an important document for understanding Leo XIII's strategy of *ralliement* — the policy of encouraging Catholics to accept and work within modern constitutional states without abandoning Catholic principles. It is also a key text in the Thomistic revival, reinforcing *Aeterni Patris* and shaping the intellectual direction of Catholic universities. Past orally, it stands as a warning against internal Catholic division and a defense of episcopal authority against ideological factionalism.
