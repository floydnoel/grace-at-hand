# Tam multa · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VII asks the French bishops in communion with Rome to resign their sees freely so that the Church in France can be reorganized and its unity restored. He first honors their fidelity and sacrifices during persecution, then presents relinquishing office as a further and greater sacrifice for the faithful whom they have served. Their earlier willingness to resign if religion required it strengthens his appeal.

Using Gregory Nazianzen and Augustine's account of African bishops confronting schism, the pope argues that episcopal office exists for the people rather than its holder. A resignation that gathers the flock can therefore serve the episcopal vocation more fruitfully than retaining a see amid division. He requires a complete written response within ten days, warning that delay will be treated as refusal and that other necessary measures will follow if obstacles remain. The letter nevertheless expresses continuing paternal regard and promises to seek appropriate provision for those making the requested sacrifice.

## Key themes

- Voluntary episcopal resignation for the Church's reorganization
- Pastoral office understood as service to the faithful
- Patristic precedents for sacrificing office to preserve unity
- A ten-day response requirement and warning of further measures
