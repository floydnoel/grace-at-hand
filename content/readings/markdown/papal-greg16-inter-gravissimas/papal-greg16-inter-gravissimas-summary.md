# Inter gravissimas · English editorial summary

## Summary

Gregory XVI protests a Tuscan government circular forbidding foreign priests to preach within the grand duchy, even when invited by local bishops. He treats the restriction as an encroachment on the Church's authority and the bishops' principal duty of proclaiming the Gospel. Earlier appeals to Grand Duke Leopold have, he says, failed to secure the necessary freedom.

The letter argues that the Church's mission crosses political boundaries: the apostles themselves preached abroad, and foreign clergy have continued to serve both missionary territories and established Catholic communities. Gregory asks the bishops to seek reconsideration of the prohibition in his name and their own. At the same time, he directs them to act prudently and demonstrate faithful obedience to civil authority in its proper sphere. Their resistance is to concern religious jurisdiction, which he regards as entrusted to the Church by Christ.

## Key themes

- Civil restriction of bishops’ choice of preachers
- The international character of the Church’s mission
- Respect for civil authority alongside defense of religious jurisdiction
