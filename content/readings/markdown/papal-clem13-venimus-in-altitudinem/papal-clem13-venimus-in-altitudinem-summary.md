# Venimus in altitudinem · English editorial summary

## Summary

At the beginning of his pontificate, Clement XIII acknowledges the burdens of his office and the injuries he sees afflicting Catholic faith, discipline, and ecclesiastical rights. He directs Christians toward divine mercy through prayer, fasting, repentance, and practical generosity to the poor, announcing a jubilee to accompany this appeal.

The brief specifies a Roman procession and church visits, together with confession, Communion, fasting, and almsgiving. Outside Rome, local authorities are to designate churches and publish the observances. Travelers may fulfill them after returning home, while confessors may adapt the works for cloistered religious, prisoners, the sick, and others genuinely impeded. The pope grants exceptional faculties for absolution and commutation of certain vows, while excluding a general dispensation from canonical disabilities and retaining requirements for publicly censured persons. Bishops and other local pastors must circulate the letter promptly and designate the churches to be visited.

## Key themes

- Prayer and penance at the start of Clement’s pontificate
- Jubilee procession, church visits, and sacramental participation
- Accommodations and carefully delimited confessional faculties
