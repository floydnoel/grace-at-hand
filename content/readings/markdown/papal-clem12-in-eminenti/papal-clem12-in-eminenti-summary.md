## Summary

In eminenti (1738) is a papal bull that formally condemns and prohibits Freemasonry (the *Liberi Muratori* or *Francs Massons*). The document forbids Catholics from joining, promoting, or harboring Masonic lodges, and imposes the penalty of automatic excommunication (*latae sententiae*) on any transgressor, with absolution reserved solely to the Pope except at the point of death. It also directs bishops, ordinaries, and inquisitors to investigate and punish violators as "most suspect of heresy," and authorizes them to call upon the secular arm if needed.

## Historical Context

The bull was issued during the pontificate of Clement XII (r. 1730–1740), a period when Freemasonry was spreading rapidly across Europe, particularly in England, France, and Italy. Originating in the late 17th and early 18th centuries, Masonic lodges emphasized secrecy, natural religion, and brotherhood across religious lines — principles that the Catholic Church saw as a direct threat to its doctrinal authority and the unity of the faith. Several European civil authorities had already banned Masonic societies as threats to public security. Clement XII, writing in the wake of the Protestant Reformation and the ongoing wars of religion, viewed the movement's secrecy, interfaith membership, and oath-bound structure as inherently subversive of both Church and state.

## Key Themes

- **Secret societies as intrinsically suspect:** The bull argues that the very secrecy of Masonic lodges proves their evil character — "if they were not doing evil they would not have so great a hatred of the light."
- **Threat to both spiritual and temporal peace:** Freemasonry is condemned not only for endangering souls but also for undermining civil order, echoing earlier Church concerns about oath-bound societies.
- **Automatic excommunication:** The penalty for joining or supporting Masonry is incurred *ipso facto* (by the deed itself), with no need for a formal declaration, and absolution is reserved to the Pope alone.
- **Heresy suspicion:** Members are to be treated as "most suspect of heresy," linking Freemasonry to doctrinal error, not merely to disciplinary disobedience.
- **Pastoral responsibility of bishops:** Local ordinaries and inquisitors are commanded to actively investigate and punish violators, with the backing of the secular arm.

## Significance

*In eminenti* is the founding document of the Catholic Church's longstanding opposition to Freemasonry. It established the framework for a series of later papal condemnations — most notably by Benedict XIV, Leo XIII, and Pius IX — and remains the doctrinal basis for the Church's position today. The bull set a pattern: every subsequent pope who addressed the question reaffirmed or expanded Clement XII's prohibition. The ban on Catholic membership in Masonic lodges was still in force in the 1983 *Code of Canon Law* (Canon 1374), and while the 1983 code softened the language, the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith confirmed in 1983 that the prohibition had not changed. In *In eminenti* can be seen the early modern Church's broader anxiety about the Enlightenment, religious indifferentism, and the rise of voluntary associations that operated outside ecclesiastical and civil authority.
