## Summary

*Exiit qui seminat* (1279) is a papal bull by Pope Nicholas III that definitively confirms and clarifies the Rule of the Friars Minor, the Franciscan rule instituted by St. Francis of Assisi. It affirms that the Franciscan profession of absolute poverty — owning nothing individually or in common — is licit, meritorious, and observable, and settles disputed questions about the rule's interpretation, including the use of money, property, work, preaching, and the authority of St. Francis's Testament.

## Historical Context

The Franciscan order had grown explosively since St. Francis's death in 1226, but internal and external controversies erupted over the rigor of its poverty. Some critics called the rule "illicit, unobservable, and divisive"; others within the order (the "Spirituals") demanded ever-stricter observance. Earlier popes — Gregory IX, Innocent IV, and Gregory X (at the Second Council of Lyon in 1274) — had issued declarations, but disputes persisted. Nicholas III, who as Cardinal Protector of the Franciscans knew the order intimately, wrote *Exiit qui seminat* from Soriano to settle all doubts with full apostolic authority.

## Key Themes

- **Absolute poverty**: The friars may hold nothing individually *or* in common; dominion of all their goods resides with the Holy See, while they retain only *usus facti* (de facto use).
- **Usus pauper**: Use of goods must be moderate, without superfluity or abundance, preserving the appearance and reality of poverty.
- **Money prohibitions**: Friars may not receive or handle money themselves, but may rely on spiritual friends (nuntii) to handle necessary transactions.
- **Obedience and governance**: Clarifies elections, admission of novices, delegation of authority by ministers, and the role of provincial chapters.
- **Work and study**: Manual labor is not obligatory for those engaged in spiritual labors (study, preaching, divine office); idleness alone is forbidden.
- **Testament of St. Francis**: Declares that Francis's Testament does not bind under obedience, because it was made without the consent of the friars and ministers.
- **Finality**: The constitution is to be read faithfully to the letter, without glosses or contradictory interpretations, under pain of excommunication.

## Significance

*Exiit qui seminat* is the definitive medieval magisterial statement on Franciscan poverty. It remained the benchmark for all subsequent poverty disputes, including the fierce controversies of the early 14th century that culminated in Pope John XXII's reversal in *Cum inter nonnullos* (1323). Together with later bulls, it shaped the legal framework for mendicant life and influenced Catholic social teaching on property, use, and the relationship between evangelical counsel and Church law.
