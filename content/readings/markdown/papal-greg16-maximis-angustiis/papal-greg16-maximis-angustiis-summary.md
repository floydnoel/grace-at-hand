# Maximis angustiis · English editorial summary

## Summary

Gregory XVI addresses the archbishop of Florence about a restored Tuscan law requiring episcopal writings to undergo examination by a civil magistrate before printing. He regards the rule as an injury to the Church's freedom and the bishops' religious authority. Recalling earlier resistance under Pius VII, he encourages persistence even when previous representations have failed.

The pope praises the circulation of handwritten episcopal documents and recommends it where practicable. In urgent cases he tolerates recourse to printers, provided bishops remain merely passive toward the government's imposed restrictions rather than treating them as rightful authority. He asks the ordinaries to continue respectful petitions to the grand duke and defend the Church's rights. The letter combines this temporary practical accommodation with hope that the ruler will reject harmful official advice. The archbishop is instructed to communicate the entire letter to the province's ordinaries.

## Key themes

- Government examination of episcopal publications
- Handwritten circulation and narrowly tolerated printing
- Persistent, respectful defense of religious authority
