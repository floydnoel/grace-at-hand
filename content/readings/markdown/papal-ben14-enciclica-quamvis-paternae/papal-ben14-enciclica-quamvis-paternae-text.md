ENCYCLICAL QUAMVIS PATERNAE OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV To the Venerable Brothers, Patriarchs, Primates, Archbishops, and Bishops. Pope Benedict XIV. Venerable Brothers, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. Although it has long been known to Our paternal vigilance—by virtue of which We must be diligent in rendering full and prudent justice to all—that, for the convenience of the litigants, in order to settle and resolve their cases as is fitting, cases are entrusted outside Our Roman Curia to certain individuals (as is said) who lack both adequate knowledge of the laws and the safeguard of integrity and the Faith, we have nevertheless been struck by an even deeper distress because this abuse, having become public knowledge, is openly confirmed by the numerous complaints forwarded to the Apostolic See and by the ever-increasing number of letters sent to Us. The primary reasons for this abuse, as evident from many of these letters, concern above all the high number of Protonotaries lacking competence. To them, by virtue of their ecclesiastical office, the aforementioned cases are delegated for the duration of their tenure, without their merits and qualifications having been subjected to a prior careful examination, and without careful consideration of whether they possess the necessary legal expertise and the ability to steer the course of justice. 1. Sincere concern and deep sorrow have taken hold of Our heart upon learning that the affairs and activities of others are being endangered and thrown into uncertainty due to the ignorance and wickedness of the judges: this can be fully understood by those who are thoroughly acquainted with the profound zeal that animates Us in the administration of justice. But since Our paternal charity—having been entrusted with the salvation of peoples—can no longer remain silent, We are compelled to take the necessary measures to eliminate corruption and restore integrity to the courts. We know that these complaints are not recent but long-standing: this is not, therefore, a disease that is only now taking its first steps, but a well-established evil. It is not necessary, therefore, to devise new remedies to eliminate it, but to put into effect those that have long since been established. The origin of this evil cannot be attributed to our Roman Curia, but to those who unjustly complain about the Curia’s practices. 2. Indeed, if we look back in time, anyone can easily discover that such complaints were presented to Pope Boniface VIII, of blessed memory, and the Pontiff himself, in fulfilling his office of apostolic guidance, rightly established by his decree (Statute of the Rescripts, Chapter 6), rightly decreed that the Apostolic See and its Legates should delegate cases only to ecclesiastical dignitaries who had attained an office or a canonry in a cathedral church, and that the trials, furthermore, be conducted not in an obscure town, but in a city of prestige and with a large population, where a sufficient number of competent judges could be found. It is reasonable to believe that a petition regarding the issue of judges’ lack of expertise was also presented at the Council of Trent. In fact, after noting that cases were not always delegated by the Apostolic See to suitable judges—and that this occurred sometimes due to the malicious interference of the powerful, and sometimes due to the remoteness of the locations, from which it was impossible to obtain information about the individuals to whom the cases were delegated— “the Council itself decreed that in all provincial or diocesan synods, certain persons possessing the qualifications set forth in the Constitution of Boniface VIII—and suitable to perform this office—should be designated, to whom cases would be entrusted in the future. If, in the meantime, any of the chosen persons were to die, the local Ordinary, after consulting the Chapter, was to designate a replacement until the next provincial or diocesan synod. In this way, every diocese had at its disposal four or even more competent and qualified persons to whom these cases could be entrusted. Once the designation was made—and was to be immediately communicated to the Roman Pontiff—any delegation to judges other than those designated would be considered surreptitious” (Council of Trent, sess. 25, chap. 10, De reform.). 3. But since the convening of provincial and diocesan synods—which, according to the prescriptions of the Council of Trent, were to be held every three years and every year, respectively—had been postponed due to adverse circumstances, the bishops, having no well-founded hope that, once the obstacles were removed, they would be able to convene such synods in order to appoint synodal judges to replace those who had died, frequently submitted inquiries on this matter to the Congregation of the Council of Trent. The Congregation replied that the appointment of replacement judges was to be made by the bishops after consulting the chapter. 4. Those prudent and time-honored provisions clearly reveal that the wounds inflicted on this specific discipline are ancient and are becoming even deeper—not because of a lack of care, but because the canonical provisions regarding the appointment of synodal judges are being disregarded. For if timely notification had been given to the Roman Pontiff, the cases would have been entrusted solely to them, thereby removing any cause for complaint. 5. Even as we attend to in this Curia, to minor offices, we direct our attention above all to that of the Secretariat of the Congregation of the Council, and nothing has ever been more important than instilling in the bishops and other prelates of the Church this conviction, and exhorting them to uphold ecclesiastical laws intact by every means. Now, however, having been called by the inscrutable will of God to the supreme heights of the apostolate—albeit undeservedly—the nature of our office compels us to establish in this letter a course of action valid for the future, in order to clear the field of doubts and complaints. We therefore decree that in those dioceses where prudent Bishops, in compliance with the provisions of the Council of Trent, have proceeded to elect judges to both provincial and diocesan councils, they shall communicate the names as soon as possible, and if it should happen that one or more of the elected judges should pass away before the next convocation, they be replaced by new judges, chosen by the Bishop after consulting the Chapter Council. These shall remain in office until the day of the Synod, and their names shall be immediately communicated to Us. Where, on the other hand, the provincial and synodal councils have not been convened for a long time, and there is no news of the election of judges, in view of the concern of all the Churches for Our humble person, We earnestly exhort in the Lord and implore the Archbishops and Bishops, should this not yet have been done, to remove the obstacles and convene them as soon as possible. In the meantime, however, with the consent of their Chapters, they are to proceed with the election of judges and, after recording the names of those elected in a register, have it sent to Us. Should anyone die, he is to be replaced after consulting the Chapter, and the names of the replacements are to be promptly communicated to Us. Regarding the number of judges to be elected, although the Decree of Trent stipulated that up to four judges must be elected in each diocese, We wish that a greater number be elected if the size of the diocese or some other special circumstance justifiably requires it. 6. We therefore wish that the local Ordinaries be aware, in accordance with the directives of the aforementioned Decree of Boniface VIII—to which the provisions specified by the Council of Trent are also aligned—that the power to judge must be conferred upon persons holding ecclesiastical dignity, such as a parish priesthood or a canonry, in the cathedral church; their qualifications must be verified so that the person appointed to this office possesses, above all, those indispensable qualities of doctrine and suitability that constitute the essence of judicial activity. The judges designated and elected in accordance with established practice shall be personally notified to Our Secretariat of Accusations by the Ordinaries through their respective procurators residing in Rome. It shall be Our express duty to ensure that the officials of the Curia entrusted with this task do not, in the future, delegate cases to others rather than to the aforementioned judges. While We wish these provisions to be brought to your attention, Venerable Brothers, through this Apostolic Letter, as a wish for your happiness and a pledge of Our paternal benevolence, We impart the Apostolic Blessing. Given in Rome, at Saint Mary Major, on August 26, 1741, in the second year of Our Pontificate.
