# dogmatica Pastor Aeternus · English editorial summary

## Summary

Promulgated by Pius IX with the First Vatican Council’s approval, Pastor aeternus teaches that Christ gave Peter a primacy of jurisdiction, continuing in the bishops of Rome, to serve the Church’s unity in faith and communion. It describes papal jurisdiction as full, supreme, ordinary, and immediate over the whole Church, including discipline and government. At the same time, it affirms the bishops’ own ordinary and immediate pastoral authority, which papal primacy is to strengthen and defend. It also asserts free communication between the pope and the Church and rejects civil control of ecclesiastical judgments.

The final chapter defines papal infallibility under specified conditions: the pope must act as supreme shepherd and teacher of all Christians, exercise his supreme apostolic authority, and definitively teach a doctrine of faith or morals to be held by the whole Church. Such ex cathedra definitions, through promised divine assistance, are irreformable of themselves rather than through subsequent ecclesial consent. The constitution connects this assistance to faithfully guarding and expounding the apostolic deposit of faith, not to receiving new revelation. Its definition therefore concerns this particular exercise of doctrinal office rather than every papal statement.

## Key themes

- Petrine primacy continued in the Roman pontiffs
- Universal papal jurisdiction alongside genuine episcopal authority
- Specified conditions of ex cathedra infallibility
- Faithful preservation of the apostolic deposit
