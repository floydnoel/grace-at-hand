# Super soliditate Petrae · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VI defends the Roman pontiff's primacy against Eybel's pamphlet What Is the Pope?, whose circulation and translation have prompted formal examination. He rejects a model in which the pope is merely an honorary president or occasional corrective within a republic of equally independent bishops. The brief argues that Christ entrusted Peter and his successors with real supreme authority over the whole Church and that this primacy sustains ecclesial unity and legitimate episcopal government.

An extensive appeal to Scripture, the Fathers, councils, and French theological authorities supports the pope's account of ordinary universal jurisdiction, reserved cases, appeals, and the hierarchical assignment of particular flocks to bishops. Pius also accuses the pamphlet of selectively quoting the Fathers. After theological and cardinalitial review, he condemns the work for propositions carrying various specified censures, ranging from false and scandalous to heretical. Reading, retaining, or printing it is prohibited; ecclesiastics face suspension and laypeople major excommunication, with remission reserved as stated. Copies must be surrendered to local ordinaries. The disciplinary decree is presented as protection of the faithful and the Church's unity.

## Key themes

- Papal primacy as jurisdiction rather than honorary presidency
- Patristic and conciliar testimony to ecclesial unity
- Papal authority and the government of particular churches
- Formal censorship of Eybel's pamphlet and specified penalties
