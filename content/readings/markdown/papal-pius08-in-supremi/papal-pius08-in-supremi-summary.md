# In supremi · English editorial summary

## Summary

At the beginning of his pontificate, Pius VIII asks the entire Church to pray for divine assistance in his new responsibilities. Following his predecessors, he proclaims a jubilee indulgence to encourage that prayer and the faithful's spiritual preparation. In Rome the observance is set for two weeks from June 28 to July 12, 1829; elsewhere local ecclesiastical authorities are to designate the period and churches.

The brief prescribes church visits, prayer, fasting, confession, Communion, and almsgiving. It provides accommodations for travelers, enclosed religious, prisoners, the sick, and children not yet admitted to Communion. Approved confessors receive limited faculties concerning reserved sins, censures, and certain vows. These do not extend to canonical irregularities or remove all existing restrictions, and public ecclesiastical penalties require the specified satisfaction or agreement. Bishops and other pastors must publish the grant promptly and explain its local observance. The jubilee joins prayer for the pope to penitence and reconciliation throughout the Church.

## Key themes

- Prayer for a newly elected pope
- A worldwide jubilee and locally designated observances
- Accommodation of impeded participants
- Defined limits on exceptional confessional faculties
