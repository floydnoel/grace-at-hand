## Summary

This apostolic brief (a type of papal letter less formal than a bull) extends a previously proclaimed Jubilee indulgence — originally granted for Italy and the adjacent islands amid widespread calamities — to the Kingdom of France. The immediate occasion is thanksgiving for the recovery of King Louis XV from a grave illness. The Pope presents prayer and fasting as the sole refuge in an age when "the charity of many is waning" and "the malice of men so often abounds."

## Historical Context

The document was issued on **February 18, 1745**, the fifth year of Benedict XIV's pontificate (1740–1758). It references an earlier brief, *In Suprema Catholicae Ecclesiae administratione* (November 20, 1744), which had proclaimed a Jubilee for Italy and the adjacent islands. Europe was in a period of dynastic warfare (the War of the Austrian Succession, 1740–1748) and instability. King Louis XV of France had recently recovered from a life-threatening illness, and Benedict XIV — known for his diplomatic acumen and pastoral reform — extends the spiritual benefits of the Jubilee to France as an act of gratitude and intercession.

## Key Themes

- **Prayer and Fasting as Refuge:** In morally dark times, prayer and fasting are presented as the divinely ordained means to implore mercy, grounded in both divine and human law and the witness of the Prophets and Apostles.
- **Jubilee as a Wellspring of Grace:** The Jubilee indulgence is described as "abundant springs of Divine mercy" that the Pope can direct to different kingdoms for their spiritual and temporal needs.
- **Intercession for the Civil Ruler:** The faithful are called to pray for King Louis XV — that God would hold the king's heart in His hand, guide him to act according to God's commandments, and grant him lasting hope in the Lord's mercy.
- **Unity of Spiritual and Temporal Welfare:** The king's health and the "enduring stability of the Kingdom" are linked to the spiritual disposition of the people and their recourse to divine grace.

## Significance

*Breve Cum Multorum Charitate* exemplifies Benedict XIV's pastoral approach to the papacy — using traditional devotional instruments (indulgences, Jubilees) to address concrete political and social crises. It also illustrates the close relationship between the 18th-century papacy and the French monarchy, reflecting the Gallican context in which the Pope both asserts his spiritual authority and accommodates the interests of the Most Christian King. The document is a window into how the universal Church adapted its ministry of grace to the particular needs of a kingdom in a time of war, illness, and moral anxiety.
