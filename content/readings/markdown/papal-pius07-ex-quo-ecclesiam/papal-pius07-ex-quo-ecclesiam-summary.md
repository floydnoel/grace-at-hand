# Ex quo Ecclesiam · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VII calls the faithful to sincere and lasting conversion amid continuing calamities. Drawing on the prophets, he joins repentance to justice, humility, fasting, and generosity toward the poor, and invokes Mary's intercession. Warfare and political disruption prevent the gathering in Rome that he would have welcomed, so he opens a jubilee-like opportunity throughout the Church instead.

The letter grants a plenary indulgence during locally announced two-week periods, with designated church visits, prayer for the Church and peace among Christian rulers, fasting, confession, Communion, and almsgiving. It accommodates travelers and people prevented by enclosure, imprisonment, illness, or other obstacles. Approved confessors receive exceptional but bounded faculties, including certain reserved cases and, under stated conditions, reconciliation from externally manifested heresy. Canonical irregularities remain outside the grant, and existing public penalties require satisfaction. Pastors must publish the observance promptly. The closing exclusion of opponents of the Apostolic See reinforces the document's connection between penitential renewal and ecclesial communion.

## Key themes

- Enduring conversion expressed through justice and charity
- A dispersed jubilee during warfare and upheaval
- Exceptional confessional faculties with explicit restrictions
- Penitence and communion with the Apostolic See
