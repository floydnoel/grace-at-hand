# Quod Provinciale

**La Santa Sede**

> *Note: This document was translated from the original Italian into English. Translation provided by DeepL (July 2026).*

ENCYCLICAL QUOD PROVINCIALE OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV To the Venerable Brothers, Archbishops and Bishops, and to the Beloved Sons, Parish Priests and Missionaries of the Province of Albania. Pope Benedict XIV. Venerable Brothers, Beloved Sons, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. The Provincial Council of your Province of Albania, Venerable Brothers, Beloved Sons, held in the year 1703 under Pope Clement XI of blessed memory, our Predecessor, had most sacredly established, among other things, in the third canon, that in Baptism neither children nor adults should be given Turkish or Muslim names, and that Christians should not tolerate being called by Turkish or Muslim names that had never been imposed upon them, whether for any exemption from taxes or immunities, or for facilitation in trading freely, or to avoid penalties. Recommending these same things ourselves, We confirmed them and commanded that they be observed in Our Encyclical Letter beginning with the words \*Inter omnigenas\*, issued for the Kingdom of Serbia and neighboring regions, on various points of religion and discipline, on February 11, 1744, the fourth year of Our Pontificate. \[In fact, as indicated in the \*Bullarium\*, the date is February 2, 1744\]. What was established with wisdom and piety by your predecessors was truly providential and salutary, a shining example of the Catholic Faith and of your sincere Christian piety, to be held up as an example to others and prescribed by Us to be strictly observed, for the greater glory and prestige of your Province and for the greater benefit in achieving the eternal salvation of souls: so much so that if, by any chance, it were to be neglected, it would bring greater dishonor upon your own Province and cause open harm to souls. 1. Therefore, We—who in Our aforementioned Letter proclaimed that abuse to be a shameful concealment of the Christian Faith, akin to infidelity— have learned, to the greatest sorrow of our Pontifical heart, that a great many in this Province, having neglected the thought of eternal salvation, continue to use the same Turkish or Muslim names, not only to be considered exempt and free from the taxes and burdens imposed on Christians, but also so that it may not be believed that they themselves or their relatives have apostatized from the Muslim religion, and so that they may not be punished with the penalties imposed in such cases. In fact, all these things—even if the Christian faith is preserved in the heart—cannot be done without feigning the errors of Muhammad, which is contrary to Christian sincerity; this pretense involves a lie on a most serious matter and amounts to a virtual denial of the Faith, constituting a grave offense against God and a scandal to one’s neighbor: thereby offering the Turks themselves a convenient opportunity to regard all Christians as hypocrites and deceivers, deserving to be rightly and justly persecuted. 2. Furthermore, adding ever more to our distress and sorrow is the fact that some of you yourselves, Venerable Brothers, and even some of you, beloved parish priests and missionaries, paying no heed whatsoever to such a wicked and detestable pretense, but rather turning a blind eye—and driven by motives that are insufficient to excuse their sins—do not hesitate to admit to participation in the sacraments, without any pangs of conscience and to the public offense of good Christians, those faithful entrusted to your care who assume the aforementioned Turkish or Muslim names and seek to be called by them. 3. It follows that We, who (by virtue of the care for all the Churches entrusted to Us, and by the supreme oversight of the Most Holy Apostolate), are obliged to lead all Christians back onto the path of salvation and to present them to God pure, sincere, walking in spirit and truth, and without blemish, having heard on this matter our Venerable Brother Cardinals of the Holy Roman Church, the General Inquisitors against heretical wickedness, on their advice, first renewing the praiseworthy Canon of the Albanian Council of your Province, we hereby confirm it with our Apostolic authority, in accordance with the terms of this Letter, and command that it be strictly observed. By the same authority and in the same manner, we also extend to your Province the decrees of our aforementioned Letter and command that they be observed in the same way. Therefore, we strictly forbid any Christian, for any reason or pretext or under any conceivable circumstance, from daring to assume the same Turkish or Muslim names in order to pass himself off as a Muslim. 4. Furthermore, Venerable Brothers, Beloved Sons, we beg and exhort you in the Lord so that, seriously considering your ministry and the strict account you will have to render to the Supreme Prince of Shepherds and Eternal Judge, Jesus Christ, regarding the sheep entrusted to each of you, you yourselves take care to secure your election through your good works, and do not fail—which cannot happen without your most grievous fault of carelessness and negligence—to rebuke, implore, and admonish with all patience and instruction the Christians of your province so that, by behaving well among the pagans, they may in every way serve as an example of good works, so that their adversaries may be put to shame, having nothing bad to say about them, as if they were evildoers—those who, for the sake of shameful gain, speak differently from what they think. If, however, some do not obey your exhortations and our orders, in accordance with the norms of Apostolic discipline, they must be compelled by forceful means: the sanctions and penalties of your Albanian Synod and of our aforementioned Letter must be fully applied to them, and it must be made clear to them that they will not be able to receive the sacraments during their lifetime, nor, after death—if they die without repentance—the prayers of the faithful. We hereby renew and reimpose these penalties, as necessary, and we command that you ensure their proper enforcement. This, however, must not seem odious to any of you, Venerable Brothers, Beloved Sons, for if your justice does not surpass that of the Schismatics and Heretics—none of whom dares to take a Muslim name—you will not enter the Kingdom of Heaven. 5. Finally, those who have converted to Christianity from Islam, or who are the children of converts, in the event that they doubt their own steadfastness in the Faith and fear incurring the punishments of their rulers if they abandon their Turkish names, and are afraid of suffering them, earnestly urge them to secretly leave those regions and come to take refuge in Christian lands, where they will lack neither God, who provides food for every living creature, nor the charity of the faithful—especially if they are equipped with letters of recommendation from the bishops. Meanwhile, to you, Venerable Brothers and Beloved Sons, we affectionately bestow the Apostolic Blessing, which we wish to be given in Our name to Christians of sound faith by every Venerable Brother Bishop in his diocese. Given in Rome, at Saint Mary Major, on August 1, 1754, the fourteenth year of Our Pontificate.
