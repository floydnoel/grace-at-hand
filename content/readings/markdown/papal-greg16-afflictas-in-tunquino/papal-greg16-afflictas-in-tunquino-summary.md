# Afflictas in tunquino · English editorial summary

## Summary

Addressing the cardinals, Gregory XVI recounts reports of Christians who endured imprisonment, torture, and death in Tonkin, Cochinchina, and neighboring regions. His survey moves through events from 1835 onward, honoring missionaries, indigenous priests, bishops, catechists, laypeople, and soldiers. Among those named are Marchand, Cornay, Jaccard, Delgado, and Henares. The examples emphasize perseverance under coercion, long years of pastoral service, and the shared witness of local Christians and foreign missionaries.

The pope gives particular attention to soldiers falsely represented as apostates after being drugged and placed upon an image of Christ. He praises their subsequent public reaffirmation of faith and the deaths of Nicholas and Augustine. Although he speaks admiringly of these witnesses as martyrs, he also expresses the wish for adequate investigation so that the Holy See can judge their cases according to its established procedures. The address ends with hope for relief from persecution and renewed growth of the Church.

## Key themes

- Perseverance under persecution in Tonkin and Cochinchina
- Indigenous and missionary participation in Christian witness
- Public repudiation of a falsely alleged apostasy
- Investigation before formal ecclesiastical veneration
