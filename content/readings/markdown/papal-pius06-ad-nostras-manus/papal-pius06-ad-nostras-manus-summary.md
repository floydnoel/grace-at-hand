# Ad nostras manus · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VI warns the French Church about Guillot de Folleville, who was presenting himself as bishop of Agra and apostolic vicar at Saint-Laurent-sur-Sèvre. A manifesto attributed to commanders of the Christian and royal army had directed displaced priests to seek his guidance. The pope explicitly leaves the manifesto's authenticity unresolved but states that no such bishop exists and that he has never conferred the claimed apostolic vicariate. He therefore rejects both titles and declares the man's purported official acts null and sacrilegious.

The letter particularly charges bishops near Poitou and Brittany with warning their people and ordering the pretender to stop exercising the usurped offices and seek ecclesiastical pardon. Pius recalls the extraordinary powers already given to legitimate bishops to provide for their dioceses during exile. If persecution defeats those arrangements, assistance must be sought from the Apostolic See. Restoration of Catholic life, he insists, requires legitimate ecclesiastical authority as well as religious zeal.

## Key themes

- Rejection of Guillot de Folleville's claimed episcopal authority
- Episcopal warnings against unauthorized pastoral leadership
- Extraordinary provisions for dioceses affected by persecution
