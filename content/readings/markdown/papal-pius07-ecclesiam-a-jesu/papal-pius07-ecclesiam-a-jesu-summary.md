# Ecclesiam a Jesu · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VII formally condemns the Carbonari and the secret associations operating under their various names. He argues that their professions of Christian devotion conceal teachings and practices hostile to Catholic faith, ecclesiastical authority, and civil government. Citing their documents and former members' testimony, he attributes to the society religious indifferentism, profanation of Christian mysteries, secrecy enforced by oaths, and encouragement of rebellion. These are the document's grounds for condemnation, presented in the pope's own polemical assessment.

Building on earlier papal prohibitions of Freemasonry and his own governmental edicts, Pius expressly prohibits membership and every form of organization, recruitment, shelter, financial assistance, or other support. The constitution attaches immediate excommunication, with absolution reserved to the pope except at the point of death. It also requires denunciation of known participants to competent ecclesiastical authorities and prohibits the society's catechisms, statutes, and defensive writings, ordering their surrender. The solemn act is meant to remove the Carbonari's contention that earlier condemnations of secret societies did not apply to them.

## Key themes

- Papal allegations concerning Carbonari doctrine and rituals
- Secret oaths and opposition to ecclesiastical and civil authority
- Prohibition of membership and material assistance
- Reserved excommunication and surrender of prohibited writings
