# Pastoris Aeterni · English editorial summary

## Summary

Leo XII appeals to Catholics associated with the Petite Église to return to communion with the pope and the bishops united with him. He distinguishes followers whom he considers deceived from the priests directing the separation, recalling the followers' earlier fidelity during persecution. Their resistance is linked to rejection of Pius VII's agreements with the French government in 1801 and 1817.

The exhortation argues that one cannot retain communion with the Apostolic See while refusing communion with its occupant and the recognized episcopate. Leo challenges the group's claim to preserve the true Church against the rest of Catholicism, drawing repeated comparisons with the Donatists. He points to bishops who relinquished office for unity and to priests who have already returned as examples to follow.

A substantial defense of Pius VII distinguishes immutable religious principles from disciplinary arrangements that a pope may prudently adapt to grave circumstances. Leo denies that the French settlements changed Catholic faith or exceeded papal authority. He asks both leaders and followers to accept reconciliation, invokes the patient efforts of their bishops, and presents return to unity as the path to sharing in the jubilee's mercy.

## Key themes

- Communion with the pope and recognized bishops as a condition of unity
- Defense of Pius VII’s French ecclesiastical settlements
- Distinction between enduring doctrine and adaptable discipline
- A paternal invitation to reconciliation during the jubilee
