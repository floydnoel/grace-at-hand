# Cum Nos superiori · English editorial summary

## Summary

Writing from forced residence near Florence, Pius VI enlarges the electoral provisions issued the previous year because the expulsion, imprisonment, and dispersal of cardinals now threaten a free papal succession. For the next election, and immediate subsequent elections if the same emergency persists without new legislation, he dispenses from ceremonies and procedural customs that do not belong to the substance of canonical election.

Cardinals may discuss practical arrangements during his lifetime, including place, timing, enclosure, and personnel, but may not agree on the person to be elected before his death. Essential safeguards remain: a secure location, free electors, a two-thirds majority of those present, and exclusion of simony and corrupt canvassing. If he dies away from the Roman Curia, the election is to gather around the largest concentration of cardinals in a Catholic prince's territory, with all others invited to join. Those assembled may proceed after the customary interval without awaiting every absentee. The constitution seeks to prevent rival assemblies and schism while requiring cardinals to place the Church's peace above private interests.

## Key themes

- Extraordinary electoral provisions for a dispersed College of Cardinals
- Procedural discussion without advance agreement on candidates
- Preserved freedom, voting threshold, and prohibition of corruption
- A single assembly to prevent competing elections
