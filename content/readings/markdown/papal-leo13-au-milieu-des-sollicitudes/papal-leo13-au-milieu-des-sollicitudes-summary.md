# Au Milieu Des Sollicitudes · English editorial summary

## Summary

This encyclical addresses the relationship between the Catholic Church and the French Republic, urging French Catholics to accept the republican form of government while resisting anti-Christian legislation. Pope Leo XIII distinguishes between accepting a legitimate political authority (which is morally obligatory for the sake of public order) and approving unjust laws passed by that authority (which must be opposed). He calls for unity among Catholics, setting aside partisan divisions to defend the Church's rights and France's moral integrity.

## Historical Context

Written in 1892, the encyclical responds to the turbulent political situation in France following the establishment of the Third Republic (1870). Anti-clerical forces had gained strength, pursuing secularization policies that included the expulsion of religious orders, restrictions on Catholic education, and calls to abrogate the Concordat of 1801 or even separate Church and State entirely. Many French Catholics remained monarchist in sympathy, creating a rift between "legitimist" Catholics loyal to the old monarchy and the republican government. Leo XIII sought to reconcile Catholics to the Republic ("ralliement") so they could defend their religious interests within its institutions rather than remain politically isolated.

## Key Themes

- **Legitimacy of diverse political forms**: No form of government (monarchy, empire, republic) is intrinsically contrary to Catholic doctrine; Catholics may prefer any form that serves the common good.
- **Duty to accept established authority**: Once a government is constituted and maintains public order, citizens are morally bound to accept it — rebellion and civil war are graver evils.
- **Distinction between political power and legislation**: Citizens may accept a government while opposing unjust laws passed by it; obedience to God takes precedence over obedience to the State.
- **Religion as the foundation of society**: True morality depends on recognition of God; without religion, society degenerates. The Catholic faith is the historical source of France's greatness.
- **Rejection of Church-State separation**: Separation would reduce the Church to the status of a private association and deny God's place in public life; it is a veil for persecution.
- **Calumny of political ambition**: The Church does not seek political domination over the State — this is an ancient lie first used against Christ Himself.
- **The Concordat must be respected as a bilateral compact**: The Holy See alone is competent to negotiate its terms; Catholics should not agitate for its unilateral abolition.

## Significance

"Au Milieu Des Sollicitudes" is a landmark text on Catholic political theology and one of the most important statements of Leo XIII's magisterium on Church-State relations. It introduced the policy of *ralliement*, calling French Catholics to end their monarchist intransigence and work within the Republic. Though the policy met with mixed success — many Catholics remained unreconciled — the encyclical's principles influenced later Catholic social teaching, particularly on the legitimacy of democratic governance and the distinction between accepting political authority and resisting unjust laws. It remains a key reference for understanding how the Church navigates political change without compromising her divine mission.
