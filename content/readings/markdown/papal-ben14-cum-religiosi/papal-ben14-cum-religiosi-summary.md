## Summary

*Cum Religiosi* (1754) is a papal encyclical by Benedict XIV addressed to the bishops of Italy concerning the urgent need for thorough catechesis. The pope was prompted by reports that people who had traveled to Rome to obtain marriage dispensations were frequently found to be ignorant of the basic mysteries of the faith — even those truths necessary for salvation — and thus unable to receive the sacraments worthily. The pope absolves the bishops of blame, attributing the problem to the obstinacy or negligence of the faithful, but he nevertheless calls on them to redouble their efforts in teaching Christian doctrine.

## Historical Context

Written in 1754, near the end of Benedict XIV's learned and reform-minded pontificate (1740–1758), the encyclical reflects the post-Tridentine Church's ongoing project of implementing the reforms of the Council of Trent (1545–1563). The pope explicitly invokes the example of St. Charles Borromeo, the great reforming archbishop of Milan, who had faced similar difficulties with religious ignorance in his own diocese a century and a half earlier. The document also reveals a practical pastoral reality: the Roman Chancery required penitential pilgrimages and sacramental reception before issuing marriage dispensations, and the system was breaking down because the pilgrims lacked basic doctrinal knowledge.

## Key Themes

- **Necessity of knowledge for salvation**: The pope stresses that there are truths which must be known *necessitate medii* (by necessity of means) — one cannot be saved without knowing them.
- **Pastoral persistence**: Drawing on St. Charles Borromeo, the pope urges bishops not to lose heart even when their efforts seem fruitless, but to add cares to cares.
- **Shared responsibility**: Bishops are not at fault if they have already provided for catechesis; the ignorance stems from obstinate or negligent subjects. Nevertheless, pastors must keep trying.
- **Comprehensive catechetical program**: The encyclical lists concrete measures: schoolmasters and mistresses teaching doctrine on fixed days, confessors instructing the ignorant at the tribunal, pre-marital instruction, fathers of families teaching their households, the recitation of acts of Faith, Hope, and Charity at Mass, and Gospel explanations on feast days.
- **Adaptation to the learner**: The pope recommends St. Augustine's method of friendly questioning in catechetical instruction.

## Significance

*Cum Religiosi* is a landmark in the history of Catholic catechesis. It stands in the line of Tridentine pastoral reform and anticipates the later emphasis on popular religious education that would culminate in such documents as the *Catechism of the Catholic Church* (1992). The encyclical is also notable for the pope's pastoral realism — his recognition that well-intentioned episcopal legislation does not automatically produce a faithful laity, and that the work of teaching must be undertaken again and again, without despair. Its insistence that Christian doctrine be taught to the ignorant *before* the sacraments reinforces the Church's perennial conviction that faith and understanding are prerequisites for worthy reception of the Eucharist and Penance.
