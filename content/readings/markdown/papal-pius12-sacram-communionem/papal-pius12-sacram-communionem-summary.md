## Summary

*Sacram Communionem* is a 1957 motu proprio of Pope Pius XII that further relaxed the Eucharistic fast and permanently extended permission for afternoon Mass. Building on the 1953 apostolic constitution *Christus Dominus*, it reduced the fast to three hours for solid foods and alcoholic drinks, and one hour for non-alcoholic liquids, before Mass or Communion — regardless of the time of day — and allowed local ordinaries to permit daily evening Mass whenever it served the spiritual good of a significant number of the faithful.

## Historical Context

The document was issued in the midst of sweeping social and economic change after World War II. Industrialized work schedules, longer commutes, and shifts away from agrarian rhythms had made it increasingly difficult for the faithful to attend morning Mass or observe the traditional midnight-to- Communion fast. Bishops around the world petitioned Rome for relief, and Pius XII responded by universalizing and simplifying concessions first granted experimentally in 1953. The rapid effective date (March 25, 1957, only six days after promulgation) underscored the pastoral urgency.

## Key Themes

- **Pastoral adaptation without doctrinal change** — The Church is "inflexible where doctrinal principles are concerned" but can harmonize discipline with changing circumstances.
- **Universal application** — The new rules are no longer case-by-case permissions but a law binding all the faithful everywhere, removing the need to consult a confessor.
- **The common good as the criterion** — Afternoon Mass may be permitted whenever a "notable portion of the faithful" would benefit, the same standard used for bination.
- **Preferential option for the poor and working classes** — The relaxed rules especially help those bound by "poverty, hard labor, and the difficulties of life."
- **Special solicitude for the sick** — The infirm may take medicine (even with alcohol) and non-alcoholic liquids at any time before Communion.
- **Exhortation, not obligation** — The faithful are encouraged but not required to use the new concessions; the old, stricter fast remains a freely chosen path of greater merit.
- **Water never breaks the fast** — Clarified to include mineral, carbonated, and purified water.

## Significance

*Sacram Communionem* marked a decisive shift in the Church's discipline on the Eucharist, moving from a uniform, rigorous rule to one calibrated to modern working life. It anticipated the broader liturgical reforms of the Second Vatican Council by centering pastoral need and the accessibility of the sacraments. The document's principles — a simplified fast, allowance for evening Mass, and care for the sick and laboring classes — remain substantially in force today, making it one of the most practically impactful disciplinary acts of Pius XII's pontificate.
