## Summary

_Redemptoris Missio_ (1990) is Pope John Paul II's encyclical reaffirming the Church's permanent duty to proclaim Christ to all peoples. Written to counter a growing indifference toward missionary activity and to clarify that interreligious dialogue and human development complement rather than replace evangelization, it insists that missionary work _ad gentes_ remains essential and never-ending.

## Historical Context

The encyclical appeared 25 years after Vatican II's decree _Ad Gentes_ and 15 years after Paul VI's _Evangelii Nuntiandi_, at a time when the Church faced internal doubts about whether mission work was still relevant. Some theologians questioned whether salvation was possible outside Christianity, whether interreligious dialogue had superseded proclamation, and whether the Church's mission should focus on socio-economic liberation rather than conversion. Externally, the collapse of the Soviet bloc was opening new frontiers, while de-Christianization in the West, the rise of religious relativism, and a decline in missionary vocations created fresh challenges.

## Key Themes

- **Christ the sole Savior** — No one comes to the Father except through Christ; his unique mediation is the foundation of mission.
- **The Kingdom of God cannot be separated from Christ or the Church** — The Kingdom is not a purely human or secular project, but is identical with the person of Jesus and inseparable from the Church, which is its seed, sign, and instrument.
- **The Holy Spirit as the principal agent of mission** — The Spirit is at work both in the Church and in the wider world, preparing hearts for the Gospel, but this universal action always points to Christ.
- **Mission _ad gentes_ remains urgent** — Three distinct situations exist: initial mission to non-Christians, pastoral care of established churches, and new evangelization of formerly Christian societies.
- **Inculturation and interreligious dialogue belong within mission** — Both are authentic expressions of evangelization, but they neither replace nor diminish the call to conversion and baptism.
- **Every baptized person is a missionary** — The laity, consecrated religious, diocesan priests, and catechists all share responsibility for the Church's universal mission.
- **Missionary spirituality** — Holiness, docility to the Spirit, contemplation, and apostolic charity are indispensable for credible witness.

## Significance

_Redemptoris Missio_ stopped a drift toward theological relativism by firmly restating the Church's missionary nature after Vatican II. It reconciled interreligious dialogue, inculturation, and human development with the obligation to proclaim Christ, providing the theological framework that guided Catholic missionary activity into the third millennium. It remains the most authoritative modern statement on why the Church cannot stop proposing Christ to the world.
