## Summary

*Christi Nomen* (1894) is Pope Leo XIII's encyclical on the propagation of the faith and the Eastern Churches. It urges Catholics to increase their financial and spiritual support for the Society for the Propagation of the Faith, directing those resources toward reunion with Eastern Christians who have separated from the Catholic Church. The Pope calls for the establishment of schools and seminaries suited to Eastern rites and culture, and frames missionary work and ecumenical outreach as a single, unified duty.

## Historical Context

Written in the later years of Leo XIII's long pontificate (1878–1903), the encyclical follows his 1894 apostolic letter *Praeclara*, which invited all Christians to unity, and his letters on safeguarding Eastern Rites. The 1890s saw growing Catholic missionary activity worldwide, as well as heightened papal attention to the Eastern Catholic Churches (churches in communion with Rome that retain their own liturgical traditions). Leo XIII sought to balance respect for Eastern traditions with the goal of drawing Orthodox Christians into full communion, a project that required money, clergy, and cultural sensitivity.

## Key Themes

- **Mission and reunion are one work** — spreading the faith and bringing separated Eastern Christians back to the Church are not separate enterprises.
- **Respect for Eastern rites** — Catholic teaching and life must be imparted "in such a fashion that it will fit their national character congenially"; the Eastern Churches should not be Latinized.
- **The need for clergy and education** — reunion requires well-formed, pious ministers and schools that serve Eastern Catholic youth in their own traditions.
- **Reliance on lay generosity** — the Society for the Propagation of the Faith is the primary vehicle for funding this work; the faithful are urged to give more.
- **Prayer and human effort** — divine aid must be joined with concrete, well-funded action.

## Significance

*Christi Nomen* is a landmark in modern Catholic ecumenism toward the East. It reaffirms the importance of Eastern Catholic Churches as a bridge to Orthodox Christians and insists that reunion must respect liturgical and cultural heritage—decades before the Second Vatican Council's *Orientalium Ecclesiarum* and *Unitatis Redintegratio*. It also illustrates Leo XIII's characteristic strategy: a global vision carried out through local bishops, traditional piety, and practical organization.
