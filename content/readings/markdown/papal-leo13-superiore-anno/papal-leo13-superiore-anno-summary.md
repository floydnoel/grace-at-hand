## Summary

*Superiore Anno* (1884) is Pope Leo XIII's follow-up encyclical renewing his call from the previous year for the universal recitation of the Rosary throughout October. It urges perseverance in Marian prayer amid ongoing trials facing the Church, particularly the threat of an Asiatic cholera outbreak spreading into Italy, and extends the same devotions, indulgences, and liturgical practices he had prescribed in 1883.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was issued on August 30, 1884, during the seventh year of Leo XIII's pontificate. The Church faced multiple simultaneous crises: the ongoing loss of temporal power (the "Roman Question" following the capture of Rome in 1870), the rise of secularism and anti-clerical movements across Europe, and a literal public health emergency as Asiatic cholera spread from a French port into neighboring Italian territories. Leo frames the Rosary campaign as a response to both spiritual and physical threats.

## Key Themes

- **Perseverance in prayer** — Leo insists that because enemies of Christianity are stubborn, defenders must be equally steadfast; heavenly help is "usually the fruits of our perseverance"
- **Mary as intercessor and dispenser of grace** — She is "the dispenser of all heavenly graces," a "type of Judith," and the one to whom the Church must fly in times of plague and trouble
- **The Rosary as catechetical prayer** — Its structure, recalling the mysteries of salvation in sequence, is "eminently fitted to foster the spirit of piety"
- **Unity of spiritual and temporal aid** — The same devotion that seeks the Church's freedom and the Pope's liberty also petitions for protection from cholera
- **Indulgences and popular piety** — Generous grants of partial and plenary indulgences are offered to encourage participation, with pastoral flexibility for rural laborers

## Significance

*Superiore Anno* cemented Leo XIII's legacy as the "Rosary Pope" — between 1883 and 1898 he issued eleven encyclicals on the Rosary, and this document demonstrates his conviction that persistent, organized Marian prayer was the Church's primary weapon against modern secularism. Its concern for practical pastoral accommodations (delaying devotions for rural workers) also reflects a characteristic Leo XIII attention to the concrete circumstances of the faithful. The document remains a touchstone for the October Rosary tradition in the Catholic Church.
