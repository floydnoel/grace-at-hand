## Summary

*Tribus Circiter* (1906) is a papal encyclical by Pope Pius X addressed to the bishops of Poland, condemning the Mariavite movement — a breakaway group of priests who had formed an unauthorized pseudo-monastic society centered on a woman they proclaimed to be divinely enlightened. The pope recounts the Holy See's prior efforts to suppress the movement, describes the Mariavites' continued disobedience and rebellion against local bishops, and bitterly notes that even after two of their leaders came to Rome, professed submission, and were pardoned, they returned to Poland and resumed their schismatic activity.

## Historical Context

The Mariavite movement emerged in early 20th-century Poland (then under Russian partition) among younger clergy who sought a more intense devotional life focused on Eucharistic adoration and frequent communion. However, they placed themselves under the spiritual authority of a Polish mystic, Maria Franciszka Kozłowska, claimed direct divine revelations, and began denouncing bishops who questioned them. By 1904 the Vatican had formally suppressed the society, but the movement persisted, and by April 1906 — when this encyclical was issued — it had grown into a full-blown schism, with Mariavite priests rejecting episcopal authority and alleging the Church had been abandoned by the Holy Spirit.

## Key Themes

- **Episcopal authority and ecclesial obedience**: The central pastoral concern is the duty of priests and faithful to obey their bishops, whom the Holy Ghost has placed to rule the Church. Resistance to bishops constitutes self-exclusion from the Church.
- **Private revelation vs. public doctrine**: The pope warns against claims of private revelation that bypass or contradict the Church's ordinary magisterium and hierarchy, citing Ezekiel on false prophets who "see vain things and foretell lies."
- **The primacy of the Bishop of Rome**: Only the pope (and superiors in authority) may judge or rebuke bishops; private individuals may bring grave complaints to Rome, but not clamorously or abusively.
- **Pastoral mercy balanced with discipline**: The pope urges bishops to embrace repentant Mariavites with paternal charity, but warns that persistent contumacy will be met with severe measures.
- **Legitimate devotional practices**: The encyclical affirms that Eucharistic adoration and frequent communion are commendable — when carried out under proper ecclesiastical authority, not as a pretext for insubordination.

## Significance

*Tribus Circiter* is a landmark disciplinary document in the early 20th-century Church's struggle against movements that combined genuine devotional fervor with rejection of hierarchical authority. The Mariavite schism eventually led to the formation of a separate Mariavite Church in Poland, which exists to this day. The encyclical is frequently cited in discussions of the limits of private revelation, the necessity of episcopal obedience, and the proper relationship between charismatic piety and institutional Church authority. It reflects Pius X's broader concern with "modernist" and schismatic threats to ecclesial unity — a theme that would culminate two years later in his sweeping encyclical *Pascendi Dominici Gregis*.
