## Summary

*Gravissimum Supremi* (September 8, 1745) is an encyclical by Pope Benedict XIV addressed to the bishops of the Kingdom of Naples, calling for a widespread renewal of moral and religious life through "Sacred Missions" — teams of itinerant missionary preachers to be dispatched into remote villages and mountain regions where faith had grown lax and morals corrupt.

## Historical Context

Benedict XIV wrote this encyclical midway through his pontificate (1740–1758), a period of relative stability for the Church but also one in which rural and mountainous areas of Italy had become spiritually neglected. The Kingdom of Naples was then ruled by the Bourbon King Charles VII (later Charles III of Spain), a Catholic monarch with whom the pope had generally good relations. Benedict draws on his own extensive pastoral experience — as former Promoter of the Faith, Secretary of the Congregation of the Council of Trent, and Bishop of Ancona and Bologna — to diagnose the problem and propose remedies. The document reflects the post-Tridentine emphasis on episcopal vigilance, clerical reform, and popular mission preaching as tools for re-evangelization.

## Key Themes

- **Twofold duty of the apostolate**: spreading the faith to those who lack it and preserving it where it already exists, with emphasis on *living* the faith, not merely holding it.
- **Moral corruption in rural areas**: a particular concern for the mountainous, hard-to-reach parts of Neapolitan dioceses where ignorance and vice were rampant.
- **Sacred Missions as the primary remedy**: an ancient and proven tool — teams of preachers sent into neglected territories to preach, catechize, and hear confessions.
- **Qualities of good missionaries**: doctrinal learning, personal holiness, freedom from avarice and vainglory — modeled on the Apostles, St. Vincent de Paul, St. John Francis Regis, and St. Charles Borromeo.
- **Episcopal coordination**: all missions are to be organized through Cardinal Spinello, Archbishop of Naples, who receives special faculties from the pope to deputize preachers.
- **Role of religious orders**: Dominicans and Jesuits are called upon to supply missionaries for the Samnites and Calabrians, at no cost to the local clergy or public treasuries.
- **King Charles as ally**: the pope urges bishops to enlist the civil authority of the King of the Two Sicilies to ensure the missions run smoothly.
- **Personal example of bishops**: bishops themselves should travel their dioceses and teach the people, following the biblical model of King Jehoshaphat.

## Significance

*Gravissimum Supremi* is a pastoral encyclical that codifies the "popular mission" as a standard instrument of Catholic reform, building on the legacy of St. Charles Borromeo and St. Vincent de Paul. It demonstrates Benedict XIV's characteristic blend of erudition, administrative acumen, and practical pastoral concern. While its immediate scope was the Kingdom of Naples, the principles it articulated — especially the bishop's responsibility to reach the peripheries and the value of coordinated mission teams — anticipated later missionary and evangelization movements. The encyclical is also a window into 18th-century Catholic pastoral strategy, showing how the Church sought to address rural de-Christianization long before the revolutions of the late century.
