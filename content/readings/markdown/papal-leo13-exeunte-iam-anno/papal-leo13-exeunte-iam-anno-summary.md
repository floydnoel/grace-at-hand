## Summary

*Exeunte Iam Anno* (1888) is an encyclical by Pope Leo XIII on the right ordering of Christian life, written at the close of his jubilee year celebrating the fiftieth anniversary of his priesthood. The document calls the faithful to authentic Christian living, warns against the moral corruption of the age, and insists that personal holiness — rooted in prayer, self-denial, and fidelity to the Gospel — is the only true remedy for the social and spiritual ills afflicting the modern world.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was published on Christmas Day 1888, at the end of Leo XIII's jubilee year. The late 19th century saw the Church grappling with the aftermath of the Italian unification (the "Roman Question"), the rise of secular nation-states, and the spread of rationalism, materialism, socialism, communism, and nihilism across Europe. Industrial capitalism was widening inequality, and the intellectual climate was increasingly hostile to religious authority. Leo XIII, who would later be known for *Rerum Novarum* (1891), was already deeply concerned about the disconnect between modern society and Christian morality.

## Key Themes

- **The primacy of Christian virtue** — mere profession of faith is insufficient; a holy life, shaped by the imitation of Christ and self-mortification, is a duty for every Christian, not just religious.
- **The diagnosis of modern ills** — the world is dominated by "the concupiscence of the flesh, the concupiscence of the eyes, and the pride of life"; these manifest in greed, hedonism, disregard for the poor, contempt for authority, and the corruption of art, theatre, and education.
- **The insufficiency of secular remedies** — law and force alone cannot cure moral decay; only a return to Jesus Christ and the Church can restore both personal virtue and social order.
- **The centrality of prayer** — God gives grace abundantly to those who ask; prayer humbles the soul, moves God to mercy, and is the indispensable source of faith and strength.
- **The exemplary role of the clergy** — priests must be learned and holy, shining as lights and mirrors of virtue for the laity.
- **The link between personal morality and the common good** — individual corruption leads to social breakdown; repentance and reform are necessary for the peace and prosperity of the human family.

## Significance

*Exeunte Iam Anno* is a transitional encyclical that bridges Leo XIII's earlier emphasis on doctrinal defense and his later development of Catholic social teaching. It articulates a comprehensive spiritual diagnosis of modernity that would influence subsequent papal teaching on the relationship between personal holiness and social renewal. While less cited than *Rerum Novarum* or *Immortale Dei*, it remains a clear statement of the late 19th-century papacy's insistence that the crisis of the age was fundamentally moral and spiritual, not merely political or economic.
