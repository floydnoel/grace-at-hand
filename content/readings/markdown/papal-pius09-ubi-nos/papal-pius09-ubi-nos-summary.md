## Summary

*Ubi Nos* is an encyclical of Pope Pius IX, issued on 15 May 1871, lamenting the seizure of the Papal States by the Piedmontese (Italian) government and rejecting the so-called "Law of Guarantees" as a hollow and unacceptable substitute for the Pope's temporal sovereignty. Pius insists that without independent civil rule, the Roman Pontiff cannot freely exercise his supreme spiritual authority, and he calls on the world's bishops and faithful to unite in prayer and protest.

## Historical Context

The document was written in the immediate aftermath of the capture of Rome on 20 September 1870, which completed Italian unification (the *Risorgimento*) and ended the Papal States' eleven-hundred-year existence. In May 1871, the Italian parliament was debating the Law of Guarantees (*Legge delle Guarentigie*), which offered the Pope extraterritorial privileges, a stipend, and freedom of communication in exchange for renouncing temporal power. Pius IX had already condemned the law in a previous letter (2 March 1871) and here reiterates that refusal, framing the loss of civil rule not as a political inconvenience but as a threat to the Church's divinely ordained liberty.

## Key Themes

- **Necessity of papal temporal sovereignty** — Civil rule is not a worldly accretion but a providential safeguard of the Pope's spiritual independence; without it, a hostile ruler could control the Church.
- **Rejection of the "Guarantees"** — The offered immunities are a mockery; any arrangement that diminishes the Church's God-given rights is unacceptable, regardless of its form.
- **Unity of the faithful in suffering** — Pius draws comfort from the worldwide outpouring of prayer and loyalty from Catholics, whom he sees as one heart and one soul with the Apostolic See.
- **The Church's superiority over civil power** — Rulers are sons, not lords, of the Church; citing St. Anselm and St. Bernard, Pius insists that temporal authorities must defend, not dominate, ecclesiastical liberty.
- **Appeal to prayer and divine intervention** — Since human means have failed, the Pope urges intensified prayer that God may convert the impious, unite Christian rulers, and restore justice.

## Significance

*Ubi Nos* is a key document of the "Roman Question" — the six-decade standoff between the Holy See and the Italian state that lasted until the Lateran Treaty of 1929. It established the non-negotiable character of Pius IX's claim to temporal sovereignty and set the terms of Catholic opposition to the Italian government for generations. The encyclical also exemplifies the nineteenth-century papacy's increasingly intransigent posture toward liberalism and secular state power, a posture that culminated in the First Vatican Council (1869–70) and the definition of papal infallibility.
