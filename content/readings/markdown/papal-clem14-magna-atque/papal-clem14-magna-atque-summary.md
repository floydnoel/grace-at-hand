# Magna atque · English editorial summary

## Summary

Clement XIV asks the bishops of the Papal States to prepare their people for Easter by preaching Christ's redemption, mercy, and Eucharistic love. He urges pastors and faithful to imitate Christ's humility and charity and to pass from their former lives to renewed life with him. This spiritual appeal introduces a special grant of a plenary indulgence during the period from Holy Thursday through the Sunday after Easter.

The brief specifies visits to designated churches, prayer, confession, Communion, and almsgiving as its conditions. It allows confessors to adapt observances for people prevented by enclosure, imprisonment, illness, or another obstacle. Approved confessors receive exceptional faculties for reserved sins and censures and for certain vows, while religious and chastity vows remain excepted. Clement explicitly excludes dispensations from canonical irregularities and requires satisfaction and reconciliation in cases involving existing ecclesiastical penalties. The grant thus combines an Easter invitation to conversion with carefully delimited jubilee faculties.

## Key themes

- Easter conversion through Christ's Passion and the Eucharist
- Jubilee indulgence and its specified devotional conditions
- Adaptations for people unable to perform the prescribed works
- Exceptional confessional faculties and their canonical limits
