## Summary

*Exultavit Cor Nostrum* ("Our Heart Has Rejoiced") is an apostolic letter issued by Pope Pius IX on November 21, 1851, expressing gratitude for the spiritual fruits of the recently concluded Holy Year (Jubilee) of 1851. The Pope celebrates the conversions, confessions, and fervent prayer he has witnessed across the dioceses, but quickly pivots to a lament over the state of the Church and civil society, which he describes as besieged by false doctrines, moral corruption, and revolutionary movements. He calls for continued public prayers, grants a new Jubilee indulgence to spur devotion, and urges bishops to lead their flocks in penance and supplication for God's mercy.

## Historical Context

The document was written in the aftermath of the 1848 Revolutions, a wave of republican uprisings that had swept across Europe and forced Pius IX himself into temporary exile from Rome. The Pope had returned in 1850 under the protection of French troops, deeply shaken by the experience. His earlier liberal sympathies had soured, and the 1850s marked a sharp turn toward the defense of papal authority and condemnation of modern liberties. The 1851 Jubilee was thus both a religious observance and a political statement — a call for spiritual renewal in the face of what Pius saw as a concerted assault on the Church and the traditional social order by liberalism, secularism, and secret societies.

## Key Themes

- **Jubilee as spiritual medicine** — The Holy Year is celebrated as a genuine source of conversion and grace, but it is also deployed as a response to a civilizational crisis.
- **Sharp critique of modern errors** — Pius IX catalogs the ills of the age: indifferentism, unbridled liberty of thought and action, contempt for authority, corruption of youth, bad books and pamphlets, impious conspiracies, and the mockery of sacred and divine rights.
- **Exclusive reliance on God** — In a time of crisis, all hope must be placed in God alone through persistent prayer; human remedies are insufficient.
- **Marian intercession** — The faithful are urged to turn to the Blessed Virgin Mary, whose intercession is described as uniquely powerful and efficacious.
- **Papal unity and authority** — The Pope positions himself as the center of Catholic resistance, calling on all bishops to unite in public prayer and obedience to the Apostolic See.
- **The Church versus the Revolution** — The document frames the struggle as a cosmic battle between faith and impiety, order and revolution, truth and the "deadly virus" of false doctrine.

## Significance

*Exultavit Cor Nostrum* is an early instance of the combative, restorationist tone that would define Pius IX's long pontificate and culminate in the *Syllabus of Errors* (1864) and the definition of Papal Infallibility at Vatican I (1870). It shows the Pope pivoting from the conciliatory hopes of his early reign to a posture of unyielding opposition to modern liberalism. The letter also illustrates how the 19th-century papacy used Jubilee indulgences and devotional piety as instruments of pastoral governance and political resistance. While not one of Pius IX's most famous documents, it is a revealing snapshot of the Catholic Church's self-understanding in the midst of the revolutionary upheavals of the mid-19th century.
