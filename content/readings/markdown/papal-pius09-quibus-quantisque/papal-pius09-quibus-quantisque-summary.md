# Quibus, quantisque · English editorial summary

## Summary

From exile, Pius IX gives an extended account of the political crisis that displaced him from Rome. He recalls his amnesty, consultative institutions, constitutional statute, and efforts to maintain public order, arguing that organized agitators exploited these concessions to pursue the destruction of papal rule. He defends his refusal to join the Italian war or preside over an Italian republic, linking that refusal to his responsibility for all nations. Violence at the Quirinal, imposed ministries, and the constituent assembly’s deposition of the papacy form the culmination of his narrative.

Pius distinguishes the revolutionary leaders from the majority of his subjects, whom he regards as loyal. He reports religious oppression, confiscations, financial disorder, and restrictions on communication, and rejects the idea that losing temporal sovereignty would make the Church freer. Independence from another ruler, he argues, is necessary for confidence in his universal ministry.

The allocution explains appeals to Austria, France, Spain, and the Two Sicilies for assistance. It also renews condemnation of secret societies and revolutionary doctrines, warns against justifying wrongdoing through patriotism, and urges bishops to teach, correct error, foster repentance, and pray for peace. Its account consistently presents the crisis from the displaced pope’s perspective.

## Key themes

- Papal explanation of reforms and the revolutionary crisis
- Universal pastoral office and refusal of nationalist war
- Temporal independence and requests for foreign assistance
- Moral limits of political action and episcopal teaching
