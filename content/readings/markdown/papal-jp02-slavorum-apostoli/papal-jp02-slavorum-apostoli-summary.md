## Summary

*Slavorum Apostoli* (1985) is Pope John Paul II's encyclical marking the eleventh centenary of the death of Saint Methodius, celebrating the missionary work of Saints Cyril and Methodius — the "Apostles to the Slavs." The document presents them as evangelizers who inculturated the Gospel into Slavic cultures, created a new alphabet (Glagolitic/Cyrillic), translated Scripture and liturgy into Old Slavonic, and built up the Church among Slavic peoples while maintaining full communion with both Rome and Constantinople. John Paul II holds them up as models of missionary method, ecumenism, and the proper relationship between Gospel and culture.

## Historical Context

Published on 2 June 1985, the encyclical coincides with the 1100th anniversary of Methodius's death (885). It follows John Paul II's 1980 apostolic letter *Egregiae Virtutis*, which named Cyril and Methodius co-patrons of Europe alongside Benedict. The document sits within the pontiff's broader project of re-evangelizing Europe and healing the East-West divide, written while the Cold War still divided the continent and during the early years of Catholic-Orthodox theological dialogue (begun in 1980 at Patmos). John Paul II — the first Slav pope — writes with a personal stake in celebrating the Slavic peoples' Christian roots.

## Key Themes

- **Inculturation**: Cyril and Methodius translated Scripture and liturgy into the vernacular, a model for how the Gospel can take root in any culture without imposing foreign forms.
- **Catholicity**: The brothers defended the legitimacy of Slavonic liturgy against the "three-language heresy" (the claim that only Hebrew, Greek, and Latin could be used in worship), arguing that the Church's catholicity demands diversity in unity.
- **Ecumenism**: The encyclical presents Cyril and Methodius as "precursors of ecumenism" who preserved communion between East and West, and whose example summons divided Christians — especially Catholic and Orthodox — to visible unity.
- **Evangelization and culture**: The brothers did not merely preach; they created an alphabet and literary language, thereby laying the foundation of Slavic literature and identity. Gospel and culture are not opposed but mutually enriching.
- **Unity of the Church**: Methodius's fidelity to both the Roman Pontiff and the Patriarch of Constantinople illustrates a model of communion that respects legitimate diversity while maintaining apostolic unity.
- **Europe's Christian roots**: The encyclical argues that the Slavic peoples' entry into Christendom through Cyril and Methodius is integral to Europe's identity and indispensable for any renewal of European unity.

## Significance

*Slavorum Apostoli* is a landmark document in Catholic teaching on inculturation, anticipating themes later developed in *Redemptoris Missio* and the teaching of Pope Francis. It remains one of the most developed magisterial treatments of the relationship between evangelization and culture, and is a key reference for Catholic-Orthodox ecumenical dialogue, especially regarding the principle of unity in diversity. By naming Cyril and Methodius co-patrons of Europe, John Paul II asserted that Europe's Christian heritage includes not only the Latin West but also the Byzantine and Slavic East — a prophetic gesture given the fall of the Iron Curtain only four years later.
