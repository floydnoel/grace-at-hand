## Summary

*Allatae Sunt* (1755) is an encyclical by Pope Benedict XIV addressing a missionary's questions about whether Oriental Catholics (Armenians and Syrians) in Basra should abandon their traditional rites — including their calendar for fixing Easter and their fasting practices — in favor of Latin practices. The Pope responds with a firm "no innovations are to be made," reaffirming a long-standing policy that Eastern Catholic rites approved by the Holy See must be preserved intact. Missionaries are instructed to focus on correcting errors of faith in schismatics returning to the Church, not on replacing their liturgical traditions with the Latin rite.

## Historical Context

Published in the mid-18th century, the letter reflects centuries of tension between Latin missionaries and Eastern Christians. Since the Great Schism (1054) and through reunion councils at Lyons (1274) and Florence (1439), Rome had consistently promised to respect Eastern rites. In practice, Latin missionaries often pressured converts to adopt Latin customs, creating friction. The document responds to a concrete case from the Ottoman Empire, where Eastern Catholics lacked their own churches and used Latin churches for their liturgies. The Gregorian calendar reform (1582) had also created a lasting divergence between Western and Eastern dating of Easter and feasts.

## Key Themes

- **Preservation of Eastern rites**: The Apostolic See has always insisted that returning schismatics keep their ancient rites, only requiring them to abandon doctrinal errors. The goal is "that all may be Catholic rather than all become Latin."
- **Forbidden mixing of rites**: While Eastern priests may celebrate in Latin churches when they lack their own, priests must never celebrate Mass in both rites, nor may Latins receive from leavened bread or Easterners from unleavened.
- **Transfer between rites**: Transfer from Latin to Greek rite is strictly forbidden. Transfer from Greek to Latin is not encouraged and generally requires permission from the Apostolic See. Missionaries must not pressure converts to change rites.
- **No blanket dispensations**: The request for a general faculty to dispense Oriental Catholics from fish abstinence is denied — fasting discipline belongs to each rite and must be maintained.
- **Calendar tolerance**: Where enforcing the Gregorian calendar would cause unrest, the old calendar may be tolerated, as was done for Armenians in Livorno.
- **Papal authority over rites**: Only the Roman Pontiff may approve changes to or removals of specific rites; individual missionaries or bishops may not.

## Significance

*Allatae Sunt* is one of the most authoritative papal statements on the integrity of Eastern Catholic traditions. It codified the principle — later reaffirmed at Vatican II (Orientalium Ecclesiarum, 1964) — that Eastern Catholic Churches should preserve their liturgical, theological, and disciplinary heritage. It served as a corrective to Latinization by missionaries and remains a key reference point for Catholic teaching on ecclesial diversity within unity.
