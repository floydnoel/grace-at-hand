# Melchitarum catholicorum · English editorial summary

## Summary

Gregory XVI condemns the acts and book of the Melkite synod held at Carcafe in 1806. He explains that complaints from Eastern bishops and pastors led to examination of an authenticated Italian translation by theologians and cardinals. Before judgment, the elected patriarch Maximos Mazlum was consulted; Gregory praises his submission to Rome, suspension of the book's use, and rejection of disputed opinions associated with Germanos Adam.

The pope identifies teachings he considers related to the condemned Synod of Pistoia, Baius, and Jansenism, together with errors concerning sacraments, indulgences, ecclesiastical authority, appeals to Rome, and discipline. He also rejects a supposed approval attributed to an earlier apostolic delegate as insufficient and unrecognized. The brief declares the synod's acts invalid and prohibits the book's use and future printing. Eastern patriarchs and bishops are directed instead to follow approved councils as standards of doctrine and conduct.

## Key themes

- Examination of the Carcafe synod and consultation with its patriarch
- Papal objections to doctrine and ecclesiastical discipline
- Historical prohibition of the synodal book and its acts
