## Summary

This encyclical, issued on October 15, 1890, is Pope Leo XIII's dire warning to the bishops, clergy, and people of Italy about the rise of Freemasonry and its systematic assault on the Catholic Church in Italian public life. The Pope argues that Masonic sects, animated by hatred of Christ and His Church, have infiltrated the Italian government and are orchestrating a coordinated campaign — through confiscation of Church property, suppression of religious orders, secularization of education, imposition of civil marriage, and exclusion of Catholics from public office — to strip Italy of its Catholic faith entirely.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written in the wake of Italian unification (the *Risorgimento*), which had stripped the Papal States of their territory and left the Pope politically isolated in Vatican City after the Capture of Rome in 1870. Leo XIII reigned during an era of intense Church–State conflict across Europe, as liberal and nationalist movements sought to limit ecclesiastical power. In Italy specifically, the anticlerical governing class was deeply influenced by Freemasonry, and a series of laws had been passed dissolving religious orders, confiscating Church property, and imposing secular education. The encyclical reflects the Papacy's sense of siege and its struggle to maintain moral and spiritual authority in a rapidly secularizing state.

## Key Themes

- **Freemasonry as Satanic Instrument**: The Masons are described as "possessed by the spirit of Satan," burning with "deadly and implacable hatred of Jesus Christ," waging a systematic war to destroy the Church.
- **Separation of Faith from Public Life**: The Pope condemns the removal of religion from legislation, education, and official life, arguing this creates a moral vacuum that destroys society.
- **Religion as Foundation of Society**: Leo XIII insists that authentic morality, social order, family stability, and national prosperity are impossible without the Catholic religion.
- **Duty of Lay Catholics**: The laity are called to openly profess their faith, resist the sects, obey their bishops and the Pope, and recognize that the salvation of their souls is at stake.
- **False Patriotism vs. True Love of Country**: The Pope refutes the charge that Catholics are enemies of Italy, arguing instead that fidelity to the Church is Italy's only path to genuine greatness and stability.
- **The Pope's Imprisonment**: The loss of the Pope's temporal sovereignty is presented not merely as a political grievance but as an assault on the Church's freedom to govern the universal flock.

## Significance

*Dall'alto Dell'apostolico Seggio* stands as one of the most forceful papal condemnations of Freemasonry in the modern period and a landmark document on Church–State relations in post-unification Italy. It crystallizes the combative posture of the late-19th-century Papacy toward liberal secularism and anticipates the broader Catholic social teaching that would flourish under Leo XIII (notably *Rerum Novarum*, the following year). The encyclical also reinforced the "Roman Question" — the breach between the Holy See and the Italian state — which would remain unresolved until the Lateran Treaty of 1929. Its apocalyptic tone and call to Catholic resistance shaped Italian Catholic political identity for generations.
