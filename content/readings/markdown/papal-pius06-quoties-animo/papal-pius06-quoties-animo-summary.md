# Quoties animo · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VI considers King Charles of Spain's request to sell property belonging to hospitals, hospices, orphanages, confraternities, chaplaincies, and similar pious foundations in order to reduce the public debt. The proposal promises the affected institutions an annual three-percent payment from the royal treasury in place of the property sold.

The pope entrusts approval to the bishops and other ordinaries throughout the king's dominions, including overseas territories. Their authorization is restricted to property whose sale clearly benefits the particular pious or ecclesiastical institution, and canonical forms of alienation must still be observed. They must also secure the promised interest so firmly that payment will not cease or be obstructed. Pius makes each ordinary responsible in conscience for these safeguards and explicitly declares the concession exceptional and unavailable as a precedent. Contrary foundation terms and regulations are displaced only to the extent required for this specific grant.

## Key themes

- Ecclesiastical property and the Spanish public debt
- Protection of hospitals and other pious foundations
- Guaranteed three-percent treasury income
- Local scrutiny and an explicitly exceptional concession
