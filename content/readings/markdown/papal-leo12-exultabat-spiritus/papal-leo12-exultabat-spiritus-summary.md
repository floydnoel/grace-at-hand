# Exultabat Spiritus · English editorial summary

## Summary

Leo XII reviews the completed Roman jubilee with gratitude for pilgrimage, sacramental reconciliation, hospitality, and works of mercy. He explains that Saint Mary in Trastevere replaced the fire-damaged basilica of Saint Paul and attributes reduced pilgrim numbers to difficult circumstances rather than diminished devotion. The jubilee's desired fruits include personal conversion, stronger Catholic faith, charity, and public concord.

The bull extends its benefits throughout the Catholic world. Within a locally published six-month period, eligible faithful may obtain the plenary indulgence through confession, Communion, prescribed visits to designated churches over fifteen days, and prayer for the stated intentions. Leo provides accommodations for travelers, enclosed religious, prisoners, the sick, and children, and grants exceptional confessional faculties subject to detailed reservations. He also addresses those who die before completing the visits and the continuing validity of specified absolutions and dispensations.

Bishops are to announce the jubilee, instruct their people, and oppose publications and teachings the pope considers destructive of faith and morals. Leo calls for cooperation between pastors and Catholic rulers, presenting religious fidelity and civil authority as mutually supporting. He finally urges all the faithful to examine their lives, repent sincerely, reject harmful instruction, and use the offered mercy for lasting reform.

## Key themes

- Thanksgiving for the Roman jubilee and its charitable works
- Worldwide extension with local observances and accommodations
- Exceptional confessional faculties and explicit legal limits
- Conversion, doctrinal vigilance, and cooperation with Catholic rulers
