## Summary

A papal bull issued by Pope Paul III that affirms the full humanity of indigenous peoples of the Americas, declares their capacity to receive the Catholic faith, and prohibits their enslavement or deprivation of property, even if they are not Christian.

## Historical Context

The document was written during the early Spanish colonization of the Americas, a period in which conquistadors and colonists frequently enslaved indigenous populations under the pretext that they were "natural slaves" or subhuman. Theological and legal debates in Spain — notably the Valladolid Controversy — had raised the question of whether indigenous peoples possessed rational souls. Pope Paul III intervened to settle the matter on the side of their full humanity, directly countering arguments that they could be treated as "dumb brutes."

## Key Themes

- The intrinsic human dignity of all people, rooted in creation by God and the capacity for eternal life.
- The universal scope of the Great Commission ("Go ye and teach all nations") — no people are excluded from the call to evangelization.
- The rational capacity of indigenous peoples to understand and receive the Christian faith.
- A forceful condemnation of the theory that non-Christians may be enslaved or dispossessed.
- The principle that evangelization must proceed by preaching and good example, not by force or coercion.
- The legal nullity of any enslavement or property seizure that contradicts this teaching.

## Significance

*Sublimis Deus* is one of the earliest and most explicit papal statements on human rights and the dignity of indigenous peoples. It laid a theological foundation for later Catholic teaching on universal human rights, influenced the development of international law concerning the treatment of native populations, and is frequently cited in modern Catholic social teaching as a landmark repudiation of racism and colonial exploitation.
