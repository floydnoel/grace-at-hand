## Summary

*Rerum Novarum* (Latin for "Of Revolutionary Change") is a landmark encyclical by Pope Leo XIII on the condition of the working classes. It addresses the social upheaval caused by the Industrial Revolution — the vast inequality between capital and labor, the exploitation of workers, and the rise of socialist solutions — and offers a Catholic alternative grounded in natural law, private property, just wages, and the right of workers to form associations.

## Historical Context

Published May 15, 1891, the encyclical was written during the Industrial Revolution's peak, when rapid industrialization had created immense wealth for a few and crushing poverty for the many. Worker guilds had been abolished; labor was treated as a commodity; child labor, excessive hours, and unsafe conditions were rampant. Socialism was gaining ground as a proposed remedy. The Church faced pressure to address the "social question" while resisting both laissez-faire capitalism and collectivist socialism — a tension Leo XIII navigated by grounding his teaching in Thomistic natural law and papal authority.

## Key Themes

- **Private property is a natural right** — rooted in human reason, labor, and the need to provide for one's family; socialism's call for common ownership is unjust and impractical.
- **Class harmony, not class conflict** — capital and labor need each other; Christian charity and mutual obligation, not class struggle, are the path to justice.
- **The just wage** — wages must be sufficient to support a frugal and well-behaved wage-earner and his family; free consent alone does not make a wage just.
- **The role of the State** — the State must protect the common good and the vulnerable, enforce justice, and safeguard private property, but must not absorb the family or individuals (subsidiarity).
- **Right to form associations** — workers have a natural right to form unions and associations; these should be grounded in Christian principles.
- **The Church's indispensable role** — religion alone can provide the moral foundation for a just social order; legislation without virtue is insufficient.
- **Sunday rest and humane working conditions** — labor must not degrade body or soul; workers need time for worship, family, and rest.

## Significance

*Rerum Novarum* is the founding document of modern Catholic social teaching. It established a third way between socialism and unrestrained capitalism, articulated principles (subsidiarity, the just wage, the dignity of labor, the universal destination of goods) that would be developed by every subsequent pope. It inspired the Christian democratic and Catholic labor movements worldwide and directly influenced later social encyclicals such as Pius XI's *Quadragesimo Anno* (1931) and John Paul II's *Centesimus Annus* (1991). Its defense of workers' rights while affirming private property gave the Church a durable framework for engaging with modern economic life.
