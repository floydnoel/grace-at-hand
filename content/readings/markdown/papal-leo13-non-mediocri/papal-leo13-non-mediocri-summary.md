# Non Mediocri · English editorial summary

## Summary

Leo XIII establishes a firmer basis for the Spanish college in Rome as a response to weakened opportunities for advanced ecclesiastical study in Spain. He recalls the achievements of Spanish theologians and universities, then describes the damage political upheavals, institutional losses, inadequate resources, and a shortage of teachers have caused to seminaries. A Roman college is intended to help selected clerics acquire learning and spiritual formation for the eventual benefit of their own dioceses.

The pope grants the Spanish bishops use of the Altemps building for housing their students, with temporary accommodation elsewhere while preparations are completed. He appoints the archbishops of Toledo and Seville to handle major affairs with the Holy See and requires annual financial, disciplinary, and student reports. The encyclical asks the Spanish episcopate to support and implement this undertaking promptly, presenting intellectual formation, ecclesiastical oversight, and the prudent use of shared institutions as complementary responsibilities.

## Key themes

- Recovery of advanced studies for Spanish clergy
- Roman formation directed toward service in home dioceses
- College premises, episcopal responsibility, and annual accountability
