# Dominus ac Redemptor · English editorial summary

## Summary

Clement XIV suppresses the Society of Jesus, presenting the decision as a measure to restore the Church's peace. He reviews papal precedents for suppressing religious institutes, the Jesuits' founding aims and extensive approvals, longstanding internal and external disputes, unsuccessful remedies, and the requests of several Catholic monarchs. He proceeds through supreme administrative authority rather than an ordinary judicial trial of the society's alleged offenses.

The brief abolishes the society's offices, institutions, government, privileges, and corporate existence, transferring authority to local ordinaries under specified conditions. Novices must leave; simply professed members not in sacred orders are released from their vows and must depart within an allotted period not exceeding a year. Ordained members may enter another approved institute or live as secular clergy, with provision for support. Those unable reasonably to leave may remain under diocesan oversight and secular administration. The decree also applies to expelled members and reserves further arrangements for missions to the pope.

Former Jesuits living as secular clergy require written episcopal permission to preach or hear confessions; those remaining in former society houses may not exercise these ministries for outsiders. Teaching is permitted only under stated conditions, without former administrative control. Clement bars obstruction of the suppression and unauthorized controversy, while also prohibiting insults or mistreatment of members. The concluding appeal insists that implementing this severe measure must serve charity and peace.

## Key themes

- Suppression justified through ecclesial peace and administrative precedent
- Abolition of corporate government with transfer to local ordinaries
- Distinct provisions for novices, professed members, and ordained clergy
- Regulated ministry, material support, and protection against abuse
