## Summary

*Omnem Sollicitudinem* (1874) is a papal encyclical by Pope Pius IX addressed to Ruthenian (Ukrainian Greek Catholic) bishops, condemning unauthorized liturgical innovations in the dioceses of Lwow and Chelm. It rejects the actions of a "pseudo-administrator" who had introduced schismatic liturgical practices under the pretext of "purifying" the Ruthenian rite, and reaffirms the principle that no changes to Oriental liturgies may be made without the approval of the Apostolic See.

## Historical Context

The document was written during a period of intense Russification in the partitioned territories of the former Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth. After the failed January Uprising (1863–64), the Russian Empire systematically repressed the Greek Catholic Church in the Chełm region, seeking to bring it under the authority of the Russian Orthodox Church. The "pseudo-administrator" Pius IX refers to was likely acting under imperial pressure to replace Catholic liturgical practices with Orthodox ones. This encyclical is part of a longer struggle — Pius IX mentions earlier interventions by Gregory XVI (1830s) when some 300,000 Ruthenians were forced into schism, and the Synod of Zamosc (1720) whose liturgical norms had been approved by Rome.

## Key Themes

- **Liturgical integrity and papal authority:** No Oriental rite may be altered without consultation with the Holy See; all innovations are null and void without papal approval.
- **Protection of Eastern Catholic identity:** The Ruthenian rite is legitimate and must be preserved intact, but was always subject to papal oversight to keep it free from heresy or schism.
- **Rejection of "restoration" as a pretext:** Claims to be "purifying" or "restoring" the liturgy to its pristine form are a disguise for introducing schismatic practices.
- **Unity of faith and worship:** Liturgical practice is closely bound to dogmatic truth; changes to rite can endanger the faith of the faithful.
- **Heroic witness of the laity:** Pius IX praises the Ruthenian faithful of Chełm who resisted the imposed innovations, preferring danger to their lives over abandoning their Catholic faith.

## Significance

The encyclical is a landmark document in the history of Eastern Catholic Churches, reaffirming the Vatican's longstanding policy that Eastern liturgies should be preserved but remain subject to Roman approval — a principle earlier articulated by Benedict XIV and Gregory XVI. It also foreshadows the eventual suppression of the Chełm eparchy (1875), when most of its faithful were forcibly absorbed into the Russian Orthodox Church — a tragedy that would not be fully undone until the revival of the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church after 1989. The document is frequently cited in Catholic discussions of liturgical reform and the rights of Eastern Catholic Churches.
