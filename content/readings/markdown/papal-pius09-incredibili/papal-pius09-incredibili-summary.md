## Summary

*Incredibili* is a papal encyclical issued by Pope Pius IX on September 17, 1863, condemning the anti-Catholic laws and persecutions carried out by the government of New Granada (modern-day Colombia). The Pope decries the confiscation of Church property, the expulsion of religious orders, the suppression of papal communications, the imposition of a loyalty oath on clergy, and the establishment of religious liberty for non-Catholic sects. He praises the bishops, clergy, nuns, and laity who resisted, abrogates the unjust laws, and calls on the offending clerics who cooperated with the government to repent.

## Historical Context

In the mid-19th century, liberal and anti-clerical movements swept across Latin America, seeking to reduce the Church's political and economic power. New Granada's government, influenced by these currents, enacted a series of secularizing reforms between 1861 and 1863 — confiscating Church properties, suppressing religious orders, subordinating clergy to civil authority, and disestablishing Catholicism as the exclusive state religion. This encyclical belongs to Pius IX's broader campaign against what he saw as the "errors" of modernity, including secularism, liberalism, and the separation of Church and state — themes he had recently crystallized in the *Syllabus of Errors* (1864).

## Key Themes

- **Church vs. State overreach**: The Pope asserts the Church's exclusive jurisdiction over its own doctrine, worship, ministry, and property, rejecting government interference as sacrilegious.
- **Persecution as suffering for the faith**: The encyclical frames the government's actions as a form of martyrdom, praising bishops exiled, priests imprisoned, and nuns expelled from their convents for their fidelity.
- **Condemnation of religious liberty**: The allowance of non-Catholic sects is presented not as a positive good but as an attack on the Church's unique claims.
- **The integrity of clerical office**: Clerics who took the government's oath are called to repentance; those who resisted are praised as models of fidelity.
- **The Pope as defender of the universal Church**: Pius IX exercises his apostolic authority to declare the offending laws null and void from the outset (*ab initio*).

## Significance

*Incredibili* is a forceful example of 19th-century papal resistance to the liberal state in Latin America. It encapsulates the confrontational ecclesiology of Pius IX's pontificate — the Church as a perfect society, independent of and superior to civil authority — that would dominate Catholic teaching until the Second Vatican Council. The letter also foreshadows the long conflict between the Catholic Church and anti-clerical governments in Colombia and elsewhere in the region, a tension that would persist well into the 20th century.
