## Summary

*Quartus Supra* (1873) is a papal encyclical by Pope Pius IX addressed to the Armenian Catholic Church, condemning a schism that had arisen among Armenians in Constantinople and the Ottoman Empire. The Pope defends his constitution *Reversurus* (1867), which reformed the election of bishops and patriarchs and regulated church property, against accusations that it violated Armenian national rights and imperial law. He argues that the schismatics, by rejecting papal authority in disciplinary matters, have placed themselves outside the Catholic Church, and he calls on the faithful to remain loyal to the Apostolic See.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written during a period of intense conflict over authority in the Armenian Catholic Church. In 1870, a schism broke out in Constantinople after Pius IX issued *Reversurus* (1867), which transferred the primatial see of Constantinople to the patriarchal see of Cilicia, gave the pope final say in episcopal elections, and restricted alienation of church property. Dissidents elected a rival patriarch, seized churches by force, and drove the legitimate patriarch into exile. Pius IX had already condemned the movement in several earlier letters (1870–1871). The encyclical also reflects the broader nineteenth-century context: the Vatican Council's recent definition of papal infallibility and primacy (1870), Pius IX's long-running effort to centralize authority in the face of Gallicanism, Jansenism, and Eastern nationalist movements, and the delicate position of Catholic communities living under Ottoman rule.

## Key Themes

- **Papal Primacy and Jurisdiction** — The pope has full and immediate authority over all churches, including Eastern patriarchates, in both doctrine and discipline. Rejecting this authority is ipso facto schismatic.
- **Unity with the Apostolic See as the Test of Catholicism** — No one can claim to be Catholic while refusing obedience to the Roman See, which is the touchstone of orthodoxy and communion.
- **Schism and Its Consequences** — Schism is not a mere disciplinary disagreement but a grave sin that severs one from the Church, often leading to heresy. The document draws heavily on the Fathers (Cyprian, Jerome, Gelasius, Leo) to argue this.
- **Episcopal Elections Belong to the Church, Not the State or People** — The laity and civil authorities have no right to elect bishops; this is a spiritual matter reserved to bishops under the pope's supreme authority.
- **Church Property Is Subject to Ecclesiastical, Not Civil, Control** — The pope's regulation of alienation of church goods is a legitimate exercise of his pastoral office, not an infringement on imperial rights.
- **Distinction Between Spiritual and Temporal Power** — The Church renders to Caesar what is Caesar's, but civil rulers may not interfere in ecclesiastical affairs.
- **Continuity with Eastern Tradition** — Pius IX argues that his reforms are consistent with ancient practice (e.g., Agapetus, Martin I, Chalcedon) and with the Armenian tradition itself, which traces its origins to Pope St. Gregory the Illuminator's confirmation from Rome.

## Significance

*Quartus Supra* is one of the most forceful nineteenth-century papal statements on the primacy of the Roman See over Eastern Catholic churches. It directly applies the ultramontane ecclesiology of the First Vatican Council to a concrete crisis, asserting that the pope's disciplinary authority extends to the ancient patriarchal churches of the East, a point long contested. The encyclical also illustrates the tension between Catholic unity and national/ethnic identity, a recurring challenge in the Eastern Catholic churches that continues to resonate in ecumenical discussions today.
