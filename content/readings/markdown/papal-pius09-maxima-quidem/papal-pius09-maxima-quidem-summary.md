# Maxima quidem · English editorial summary

## Summary

Addressing bishops gathered after the canonization of twenty-seven saints, Pius IX contrasts that celebration with doctrines and political actions he regards as threats to Church and society. He condemns the reduction of revelation to human speculation, the dismissal of biblical miracles and mysteries as myths, and theories identifying God with the changing world. He also rejects accounts of morality and law detached from God and natural justice, and political claims making material force the measure of right or the state the source of every right.

The allocution defends the Church’s independent authority, free communication with Rome, and ecclesiastical property, while protesting interference with bishops and religious communities. Pius repeats his argument that papal temporal sovereignty safeguards freedom in governing the universal Church. He asks bishops to answer the condemned positions through teaching and suitable writings, oversee education, and form clergy in learning and holiness. He concludes with gratitude for episcopal solidarity and prayer for the conversion of opponents and restoration of peace.

## Key themes

- Critique of rationalism, pantheism, and moral autonomy
- Church authority and rights independent of civil permission
- Episcopal teaching, clerical formation, and supervision of education
