## Summary

"Quae In Patriarchatu" is an apostolic letter from Pope Pius IX (1872) addressed to the Chaldean Catholic Patriarchate of Babylonia, concerning the church in Chaldea (modern-day Iraq). It recounts the long struggle to suppress the Nestorian heresy in the region, restore ecclesiastical discipline, and address the insubordination of Patriarch Joseph Audo, who overstepped his jurisdiction by interfering in the Malabar Church (India) and resisted papal authority, including refusing to submit to the decrees of the First Vatican Council.

## Historical Context

The document was written in the aftermath of the First Vatican Council (1869–1870), which defined papal primacy and infallibility. Pius IX had spent decades trying to reform the Chaldean Church, which had been weakened by the Nestorian heresy and by corrupt practices such as the hereditary succession of patriarchs. The patriarch at the time, Joseph Audo, had been elevated by Pius IX in 1848 with hopes he would lead a revival, but he repeatedly defied Rome — sending a bishop to the Malabar coast without authorization, refusing to consecrate bishops chosen by the pope, and associating with neo-schismatic Armenian groups in Constantinople.

## Key Themes

- **Papal Primacy** — The pope's authority over all patriarchs and particular churches is asserted throughout, especially in requiring Audo to submit to Vatican I's decrees on the Church of Christ.
- **Ecclesiastical Discipline** — The restoration of proper episcopal elections (via the constitution *Cum ecclesiastica disciplina*, 1869) and the abolition of hereditary succession are central.
- **Unity vs. Heresy** — The document traces the damage of Nestorianism and the ongoing need to guard against schism and doctrinal error.
- **Missionary Jurisdiction** — The pope decisively rejects Audo's claim of jurisdiction over the Malabar Christians in India, reserving that authority to the Holy See and its delegates.
- **Obedience and Scandal** — Audo's defiance (refusing consecrations, consorting with schismatics, ignoring papal legates) is presented as a grave scandal requiring correction.

## Significance

The letter is a concrete example of the post–Vatican I assertion of universal papal jurisdiction over Eastern Catholic patriarchates. It demonstrates how Pius IX applied the council's teaching on papal primacy to a real crisis of authority, insisting that even ancient patriarchal rights are subordinate to the Roman pontiff. It also illustrates the tension between Latin and Eastern Catholic communities in the 19th century, particularly over mission territories like India, which would remain a source of friction for decades.
