# Ancorché antichissimo · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VI approves a formal protest by Giacomo Borsari, commissioner general of the Apostolic Camera, against the French National Assembly's decree incorporating Avignon and the Comtat Venaissin into France. The embedded protest reviews earlier papal objections, disputed local votes, and the Assembly's changing decisions. It argues from treaties, purchase, longstanding possession, and former French recognition that sovereignty remains with the Holy See, while challenging the freedom and representativeness of the votes used to justify annexation.

The pope adopts the protest and declares the September 14, 1791 decree unjust, violent, and without effect upon papal rights. He rejects the implication that compensation could legitimize surrender of the territories and confirms his intention to preserve their sovereignty intact. The camerlengo is ordered to register and safeguard the instrument with the related protests in the Camera's archive. Special provisions give the chirograph legal force through the papal signature and protect it against procedural challenges.

## Key themes

- Formal challenge to the French annexation decree
- Documentary claims of title and contested popular consent
- Rejection of compensation as a substitute for sovereignty
- Archival preservation of the papal protest
