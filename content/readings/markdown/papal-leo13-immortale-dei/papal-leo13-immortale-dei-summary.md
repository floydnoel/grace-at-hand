## Summary

*Immortale Dei* ("The Immortal God") is an encyclical by Pope Leo XIII, issued in 1885, on the Christian constitution of States. It argues that civil authority derives from God, not the people, and that the State has a moral duty to publicly profess and protect the true religion — the Catholic Church. Leo XIII rejects the emerging liberal principles of popular sovereignty, separation of Church and State, religious indifferentism, and unrestricted freedom of conscience and speech, while affirming that the Church and State are two perfect societies, each supreme in its own sphere, that ought to work in harmony.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written in the wake of the *Risorgimento* — the unification of Italy — which had stripped the Papal States of their territory in 1870, leaving the Pope a "prisoner in the Vatican." Across Europe, the latter half of the 19th century saw the rise of liberal, secular, and often anti-clerical governments that sought to subordinate the Church to civil authority. Leo XIII's predecessor, Pius IX, had already condemned many of these ideas in the *Syllabus of Errors* (1864). *Immortale Dei* is a sustained, positive restatement of the Catholic position on Church–State relations in response to this new political order.

## Key Themes

- **Divine origin of authority** — all political power comes from God, not from the people or social contract.
- **Church as a perfect society** — the Church is a sovereign, independent institution with its own laws, governance, and jurisdiction, not a subject of the State.
- **Two powers** — Church and State are distinct, each supreme in its own sphere (sacred vs. temporal), but must coexist harmoniously, like soul and body.
- **Duty of the State toward religion** — the State must publicly worship God and favor the true religion; religious indifferentism (treating all religions as equal) leads to atheism.
- **Rejection of modern liberties** — unlimited freedom of thought, speech, conscience, and worship are condemned as license, not true liberty.
- **Legitimacy of diverse governments** — no single form of government is condemned; monarchy, aristocracy, or democracy can all be valid if they respect God's law.
- **Catholic political participation** — Catholics are encouraged to engage in public life to infuse society with Christian values, while remaining loyal to the Church's teaching.

## Significance

*Immortale Dei* is one of the foundational documents of modern Catholic social teaching on Church and State. It set the framework for Catholic political engagement in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, defending the Church's independence against secular liberal states while cautiously allowing Catholics to work within those states. Its teaching was later developed by Leo XIII himself in *Rerum Novarum* (1891) and by subsequent popes in documents such as *Dignitatis Humanae* (1965), which nuanced the question of religious liberty while building on Leo's fundamental principles.
