## Summary

*Le Pèlerinage de Lourdes* is a papal encyclical issued by Pope Pius XII on July 2, 1957, marking the centenary of the Marian apparitions at Lourdes. The document commemorates the Virgin Mary's appearances to Bernadette Soubirous in 1858, reviews the long history of papal support for the Lourdes shrine, and issues a vigorous call for personal conversion and the Christian renewal of a society threatened by materialism.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was published at the height of the Cold War, in a period when the Church saw itself confronting both Communist materialism in the Eastern bloc and consumerist materialism in the West. It follows a century of intense Marian devotion, including the 1854 definition of the Immaculate Conception (which Pius XII presents as prophetically linked to the Lourdes apparitions four years later), the spread of the Miraculous Medal, and the canonization of Bernadette Soubirous in 1933. The document also builds on Pius XII's own earlier encyclical *Fulgens corona* (1953) on the Immaculate Conception.

## Key Themes

- **Marian devotion as a path to Christ** — Mary always directs the faithful to her Son, especially in the Eucharist; Lourdes is uniquely both a Marian shrine and a "throne of the Eucharistic mystery."
- **The call to penance** — The central message of Lourdes is the Virgin's threefold appeal: "Penance, penance, penance!" This is not a private piety but a summons to conversion of heart and public Christian witness.
- **Confronting materialism** — The encyclical denounces materialism in both its Communist form and its Western manifestations: greed, hedonism, the cult of the body, contempt for unborn life, and social selfishness.
- **The social dimension of conversion** — Individual repentance is necessary but insufficient; the encyclical calls for a Christian renewal of society through families, Catholic Action, and the integration of faith into professional and civic life.
- **The dignity of suffering** — The sick and afflicted are honored at Lourdes as "the suffering members of our Lord," and their trials, united to Christ's, contribute to the redemption of the world.
- **The continuity of papal teaching** — Pius XII traces a line of papal favor from Pius IX through Leo XIII, St. Pius X, Benedict XV, and Pius XI, presenting Lourdes as a sanctuary divinely linked to the See of Peter.

## Significance

The encyclical is the definitive papal statement on Lourdes and its message, synthesizing a century of Marian theology with a sharp critique of modern secularism. It remains a key reference point for Catholic teaching on the relationship between Marian piety, Eucharistic devotion, and social justice, and it helped cement Lourdes's status as one of the world's foremost pilgrimage sites. Its warning against materialism — both atheist and consumerist — resonates as a major theme of Pius XII's later social teaching.
