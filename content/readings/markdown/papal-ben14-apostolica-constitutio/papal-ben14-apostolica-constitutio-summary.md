## Summary

*Apostolica Constitutio* (1749) is a lengthy pastoral letter from Pope Benedict XIV preparing the Church for the upcoming Holy Year (Jubilee) of 1750. It defends the practice of pilgrimages to Rome, especially to the tombs of Sts. Peter and Paul, instructs clergy on how to prepare the faithful for the Jubilee, and lays out the theological framework for indulgences, repentance, and confession.

## Historical Context

The document was issued on June 26, 1749, six months before the Jubilee began on Christmas Eve. Benedict XIV (Prospero Lambertini), one of the most learned popes of the 18th century, wrote at a time when Catholic piety was challenged by Jansenist rigorism (which downplayed indulgences and frequent communion) and by Protestant critiques of pilgrimages and the indulgence system. The document responds to that climate by reasserting traditional Catholic practice while urging pastoral prudence and reform of abuses — reflecting the Enlightenment-era call for order, clarity, and moderate devotion.

## Key Themes

- **Defense of pilgrimages** — traces the practice from the Old Testament through the Church Fathers, rebutting Protestant and Jansenist objections.
- **Theology of indulgences** — explains that indulgences remit temporal punishment through the Church's treasury of merits, but require true repentance and confession; they do not replace conversion.
- **Pastoral care in confession** — extensive guidance to confessors: they must be diligent, well-instructed, not give absolution too readily, and impose fitting penances.
- **Proper dispositions for the Jubilee** — pilgrims should make a general confession, receive the Eucharist, and undertake the prescribed visits and works with humility and devotion, not mere outward observance.
- **Role of bishops** — bishops are to instruct their flocks before departure, ensure no scandal arises, and see that the pilgrimage bears lasting spiritual fruit.

## Significance

This is one of the fullest magisterial treatments of pilgrimages and indulgences between the Council of Trent and the Second Vatican Council. It shaped the typical structure of Jubilee preparation (missions, general confessions, catechesis on indulgences) that continued into the 20th century. Benedict XIV's balanced tone — defending traditional piety while demanding pastoral vigilance against abuse — exemplifies the Catholic Reform's mature discipline and remains a reference point for understanding the Church's teaching on Jubilee indulgences and the spiritual value of pilgrimage to Rome.
