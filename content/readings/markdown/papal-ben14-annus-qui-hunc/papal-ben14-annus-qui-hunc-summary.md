## Summary

*Annus Qui Hunc* (1749) is a papal encyclical by Pope Benedict XIV addressed to bishops in the Papal States, written in anticipation of the Jubilee Holy Year of 1750. The document stresses three main areas of ecclesiastical discipline: the physical cleanliness and decent upkeep of churches (even in poverty), the reverent celebration of the Divine Office (the Liturgy of the Hours), and the regulation of sacred music — particularly the prohibition of theatrical, profane, or secular styles in polyphonic chant and instrumental accompaniment.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was issued just after the conclusion of a major European war (likely the War of the Austrian Succession, 1740–1748), and Benedict hoped the Jubilee Year of 1750 would draw large numbers of foreign pilgrims to Rome. He was concerned about the impression these pilgrims would take home: if churches were dirty, neglected, or if worship featured frivolous or operatic music, the Church's reputation would suffer. The document reflects the broader Counter-Reformation and 18th-century Catholic concern for decorum, catechetical impact, and the visual and auditory dignity of worship.

## Key Themes

- **Church cleanliness and decency** — even poor churches can and must be neat and clean; shabby or soiled vestments and vessels are an offense to the sacred mysteries.
- **Reverence in the Divine Office** — the canonical hours must be sung or recited attentively and devoutly, not negligently; bishops are responsible for enforcing this.
- **Sacred vs. theatrical music** — polyphonic (figured) chant and instruments are permissible, but only if they are grave, devout, and nothing like stage performance; singing must serve prayer, not display.
- **Instrumental regulation** — only certain instruments (organ, violone, cello, bassoon, violas, violins) are acceptable; trumpets, oboes, flutes, timpani, and theatrical instruments are banned.
- **Music must serve the text** — instruments should support voices and reinforce the meaning of the words; trills, hockets, and overly elaborate ornamentation are abuses.
- **Seasonal propriety** — elaborate orchestral music is inappropriate during penitential seasons like Holy Week, especially when the prescribed texts (e.g., the Lamentations) are sorrowful.
- **Rome as the standard** — the Roman Pontifical Choir's practice (vocal polyphony without organ) is held up as the model; the encyclical insists that liturgical uniformity flow from Rome outward.

## Significance

*Annus Qui Hunc* is one of the most important 18th-century papal documents on sacred liturgy and music. It anticipates many of the principles later codified in Pope Pius X's *Tra le sollecitudini* (1903) and in the Second Vatican Council's *Sacrosanctum Concilium*: that sacred music must be holy, true to the liturgy, free of theatricality, and ordered toward the participation and devotion of the faithful. It also offers a fascinating window into the practical, pastoral concerns of the pre-modern papacy — a bishop worried that dirty churches and operatic choirs would scandalise pilgrims and undermine Catholic discipline.
