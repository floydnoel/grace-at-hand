## Summary

*Haurietis Aquas* (Latin for "You shall draw waters," from Isaiah 12:3) is an encyclical by Pope Pius XII on devotion to the Sacred Heart of Jesus, issued on May 15, 1956. The document provides the most comprehensive theological treatment of the devotion ever written by a pope. Pius XII grounds the devotion in Scripture, the Church Fathers, and Catholic doctrine, presenting it not as a sentimental or optional piety but as a profound act of worship directed to the love of God incarnate in Christ. He defends it against critics who dismiss it as materialistic, passive, or unworthy of modern Christians, and presents it as a remedy for the spiritual ills of the age.

## Historical Context

The encyclical marked the centenary of Pope Pius IX's 1856 extension of the feast of the Sacred Heart to the Universal Church. It appeared in the mid-20th century, a period of post-World War II recovery, the Cold War, rising secularism, and the spread of atheistic materialism. Within the Church, devotion to the Sacred Heart was widespread — through the Apostleship of Prayer, consecrations of families and nations, and popular piety — but it also faced criticism from some Catholics who saw it as sentimental, overly emotional, or unsuitable for "educated men" and the active apostolic needs of the time. Pius XII, who had consecrated the world to the Immaculate Heart of Mary in 1942, wrote to give the devotion a firm theological foundation and to counter these misunderstandings.

## Key Themes

- **The threefold love of Christ** — the Sacred Heart symbolizes Christ's divine love (shared with the Trinity), His spiritual love (infused into His human soul), and His sensible bodily love (the natural human affections), all united without contradiction in the one Person of the Word.
- **The hypostatic union** — because Christ's physical heart is united to the divine Person, it is worthy of the same worship of adoration (*latria*) given to Christ Himself. This is an article of faith defined at Ephesus and Constantinople II.
- **Biblical foundation** — traces God's love from the Old Covenant (fatherly and spousal imagery in the prophets, the Song of Songs) to its perfect fulfillment in Christ's life, Passion, and the institution of the Eucharist.
- **Defense against critics** — refutes charges of naturalism, sentimentalism, and materialism, arguing that the devotion is fully spiritual and theological, directed through the physical symbol to the Person of Christ and the love of God.
- **Reparation and expiation** — acts of love and reparation for sin are essential, not optional, elements of the devotion.
- **Connection to the Eucharist, the priesthood, and the Church** — these are all gifts flowing from the Sacred Heart; the Church herself is born from Christ's pierced side.
- **The Immaculate Heart of Mary** — closely joined to the Sacred Heart by God's design in the work of redemption.
- **Social remedy** — the devotion is presented as the most effective "school of divine love" to counter the evils of secularism, materialism, and hatred of God, bringing the law of the Gospel into private and public life.

## Significance

This is the definitive magisterial document on devotion to the Sacred Heart. It elevated what had sometimes been viewed as a popular or private devotion to its proper place at the center of Catholic spirituality, showing that it encapsulates the whole mystery of the Incarnation and Redemption. The encyclical gave lasting theological vocabulary for the devotion — especially the concept of the "threefold love" — and confirmed its importance for both personal holiness and the renewal of society. It remains the authoritative reference point for all subsequent Church teaching on the Sacred Heart, and it paved the way for the continued emphasis on divine mercy and the connection between the Hearts of Jesus and Mary in later pontificates.
