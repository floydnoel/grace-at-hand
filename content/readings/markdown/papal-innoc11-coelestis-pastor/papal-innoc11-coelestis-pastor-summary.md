## Summary

*Coelestis Pastor* (1687) is Pope Innocent XI's apostolic constitution condemning 68 propositions drawn from the writings and teachings of Miguel de Molinos, a Spanish priest who promoted Quietism — a heretical form of mysticism that held that spiritual perfection consists in the total annihilation of the human will and the passive abandonment of the soul to God, to the point where external acts (even sinful ones) become matters of indifference.

## Historical Context

The document was issued in the aftermath of the Catholic Reformation, a period when the Church closely policed orthodoxy in mystical theology. Molinos had attracted a wide following in Rome with his 1675 book *Guía Espiritual* (The Spiritual Guide). By the 1680s, the Spanish Inquisition and then the Roman Inquisition investigated him for teachings that seemed to license moral passivity and to undermine the sacraments, prayer, and ecclesiastical authority. After a lengthy trial, Molinos was convicted and sentenced to life imprisonment. The condemnation also served as a broader warning against Quietist tendencies spreading in France and Italy.

## Key Themes

- **Annihilation of the will** – The soul must reduce its own powers to nothingness and abandon itself passively to God, becoming like an inanimate body.
- **Rejection of active spiritual practice** – Meditation, vocal prayer, thanksgiving, examination of conscience, and even the desire for heaven or fear of hell are presented as imperfections.
- **Antinomianism** – Propositions 41–52 suggest that demonically induced sinful acts committed against one's will are not sins, and that confessing them is discouraged.
- **Denigration of external discipline** – Vows, penances, confession, obedience to bishops, and devotion to the humanity of Christ, the Blessed Virgin, and the saints are dismissed or minimized.
- **Sola interiority** – The traditional three ways (purgative, illuminative, unitive) are called "absurdities"; only the interior way matters.
- **Elitist epistemology** – Advanced souls are said to reach a state beyond theology, reason, and even the need for sacraments.

## Significance

*Coelestis Pastor* is the definitive magisterial condemnation of Quietism and established clear boundaries for Catholic mystical theology. It affirmed that authentic contemplation does not dispense with the sacraments, the moral law, the active pursuit of virtue, or the Church's hierarchical governance. The document influenced later condemnations of Quietist ideas (e.g., Fénelon's *Maxims of the Saints*, condemned in 1699 by Innocent XII). It remains a reference point for distinguishing legitimate contemplative prayer from heterodox passivity.
