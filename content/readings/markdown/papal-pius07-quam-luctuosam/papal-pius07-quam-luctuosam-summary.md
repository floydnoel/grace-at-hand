# Quam luctuosam · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VII celebrates the public promulgation of the French concordat and the reopening of Catholic religious life. He recalls the negotiations with Napoleon, ratification of the seventeen agreed articles, reorganization of dioceses, and Cardinal Caprara's mission. Reopened churches, restored sacraments, legitimate pastors, and renewed communion with Rome are presented as the fruits of the settlement and grounds for solemn thanksgiving.

The address nevertheless distinguishes the concordat from additional articles published alongside it without the pope's knowledge. He intends to seek the necessary changes to those provisions while urging clergy to respect civil authority and avoid interference outside their religious duties. He also explains the composition of the renewed episcopate: faithful former bishops who had resigned, newly selected pastors, and some former unauthorized bishops whose reconciliation with Rome preceded their canonical appointment. He defends this last accommodation as a lawful exercise of charity to end schism, without abandoning essential requirements. Finally he announces a jubilee of thanksgiving and prayer for the settlement's continued success and the Church's needs.

## Key themes

- Thanksgiving for restored public Catholic life in France
- Distinction between the negotiated concordat and additional articles
- Reconciliation before appointment of former unauthorized bishops
- Religious duties and obedience to civil authority
