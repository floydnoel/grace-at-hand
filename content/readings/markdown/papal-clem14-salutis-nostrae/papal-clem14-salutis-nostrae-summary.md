## Summary

*Salutis Nostrae* is a papal bull issued by Pope Clement XIV proclaiming a universal jubilee (Holy Year) to be celebrated in Rome beginning at Christmas Eve 1774 and lasting through 1775. It grants a plenary indulgence to all faithful who confess, receive Communion, and visit the four major Roman basilicas, and it exhorts bishops, princes, and pilgrims alike to participate in this year of repentance, reconciliation, and grace.

## Historical Context

The bull was issued in 1774, the final year of Clement XIV's pontificate. Clement reigned during a turbulent period: the Church faced growing pressure from Enlightenment secularism, regalist movements, and the suppression of the Jesuits (which Clement famously executed in 1773 under political pressure from Bourbon courts). The jubilee was a traditional twenty-five-year cycle (following Benedict XIV's 1750 jubilee), offering a moment of spiritual renewal and unity amid external threats to papal authority and Catholic cohesion across Europe.

## Key Themes

- **Plenary indulgence and the treasury of merit** — The bull articulates a robust theology of indulgences, grounding them in the Communion of Saints and the superabundant merits of Christ, Mary, and the martyrs.
- **Pilgrimage as penance** — The physical labor and hardship of pilgrimage are presented as both reparatory and spiritually formative, not merely logistical conditions.
- **Universal call to repentance** — The jubilee is framed as a special "time of salvation," echoing the Old Testament jubilee tradition, and is addressed to all the faithful regardless of status.
- **Unity of the Church** — Prayers are prescribed for the exaltation of the Church, the destruction of heresies, peace among rulers, and the return of those who have strayed.
- **Pastoral solicitude** — Provisions are made for those who cannot complete the visits due to death or illness, reflecting a merciful application of disciplinary norms.
- **Cooperation between spiritual and temporal powers** — Bishops and Catholic princes are urged to work together to facilitate safe travel and lodging for pilgrims.

## Significance

*Salutis Nostrae* is a classic example of the pre-modern jubilee bull, combining juridical precision with elevated theological reflection on indulgences, penance, and ecclesial communion. It represents one of the last major papal acts of Clement XIV before his death later in 1774, and it stands as a witness to the Church's effort to maintain spiritual vitality and unity during a period of intense political and ideological pressure. Its theology of the treasury of merit and the Communion of Saints remains a touchstone for Catholic teaching on indulgences.
