## Summary

*Praestantia Scripturae* is a motu proprio issued by Pope Pius X in 1907 that reinforces the authority of the Pontifical Biblical Commission, requiring Catholics to submit to its doctrinal decisions as they would to decrees of Roman congregations. It also reaffirms two earlier documents—the decree *Lamentabili sane exitu* and the encyclical *Pascendi dominici gregis*—and imposes automatic excommunication (*latae sententiae*) on anyone who defends the modernist propositions condemned therein.

## Historical Context

The document was published on November 18, 1907, during the pontificate of Pius X (1903–1914), a period of intense conflict between the Catholic Church and theological modernism. The previous year, Pius X had issued *Pascendi dominici gregis* (September 1907), which famously characterized modernism as "the synthesis of all heresies," and the Holy Office had published the syllabus *Lamentabili sane exitu* (July 1907), condemning 65 modernist propositions. The Church was responding to the rise of historical-critical biblical scholarship, rationalism, and the broader intellectual currents of the late 19th and early 20th centuries that challenged traditional doctrines on Scripture, revelation, and faith.

## Key Themes

- **Binding authority of the Biblical Commission**: Decisions of the Pontifical Biblical Commission on doctrinal matters are to be obeyed in conscience, on par with decrees of other Roman congregations approved by the pope.
- **Condemnation of modernism**: The document reinforces the condemnations of *Lamentabili* and *Pascendi*, reiterating that modernism is a grave threat to Catholic faith.
- **Penalty of excommunication**: Anyone defending condemned modernist propositions incurs automatic excommunication (*latae sententiae*), reserved to the Roman Pontiff, under the constitution *Apostolicae Sedis*.
- **Vigilance over teaching**: Bishops and religious superiors are exhorted to remove modernist teachers from seminaries, exclude suspect candidates from holy orders, and suppress publications promoting condemned opinions.

## Significance

*Praestantia Scripturae* tightened disciplinary measures in what became known as the "anti-modernist crackdown" under Pius X. It effectively made dissent from the Biblical Commission's rulings a grave sin and imposed severe ecclesiastical penalties on modernist sympathizers. Together with the Oath Against Modernism (*Sacrorum antistitum*, 1910), it formed part of a broader campaign to enforce doctrinal uniformity that shaped Catholic biblical scholarship for decades, until the relaxation of anti-modernist disciplines around the Second Vatican Council (1962–1965).
