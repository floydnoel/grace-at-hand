## Summary

*Centesimus Annus* (1991) is Pope John Paul II's encyclical marking the hundredth anniversary of Leo XIII's *Rerum Novarum* (1891), the founding document of modern Catholic social teaching. The letter re-reads *Rerum Novarum* in light of the dramatic events of 1989 — the fall of Communist regimes in Central and Eastern Europe — and offers a Christian analysis of the post-Cold War world, addressing the proper relationship between free markets, the State, human dignity, work, private property, and the common good.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was published on 1 May 1991, less than two years after the fall of the Berlin Wall and the collapse of Soviet-backed governments across Europe. The Cold War was ending, "Real Socialism" was disintegrating, and many wondered whether liberal capitalism had won an unchallenged victory. John Paul II — himself a Polish pope who had lived under Communist rule — wrote to assess what had happened, why, and what Christians should propose in the new era. The encyclical also responds to rising consumerism, ecological concerns, and the persistent poverty of the Third World.

## Key Themes

- **Right to private property** is affirmed but subordinated to the universal destination of goods — ownership has a social function.
- **Critique of both Marxism and unbridled capitalism**: Marxism fails anthropologically (it reduces the person to a molecule of the collective); capitalism fails when it absolutizes the market, ignores the poor, and reduces freedom to mere economic license.
- **Dignity of work and the worker** — work is personal and necessary, not a commodity; a just wage, humane hours, and the right to form unions are fundamental.
- **Principle of subsidiarity** — the State should not absorb functions that smaller communities (family, associations, businesses) can perform themselves.
- **Preferential option for the poor** — society and State must defend the weakest.
- **Authentic democracy requires a moral foundation** — without transcendent truth, democracy degenerates into totalitarianism or mere power politics.
- **Human ecology** — respect for the natural environment is inseparable from respect for the human person, the family, and life from conception to natural death.
- **Alienation** persists even in wealthy societies through consumerism, individualism, and the reduction of persons to producers/consumers.

## Significance

*Centesimus Annus* is one of the definitive social encyclicals of the late 20th century. It provided a Catholic framework for understanding the post-1989 world without triumphalism, warning that the West must not mistake its economic system for a complete model of human flourishing. It integrated earlier social teaching into a coherent response to both state socialism and laissez-faire capitalism, reaffirmed the Church's "yes" to the market economy and its "no" to the market society, and has been widely cited in subsequent Catholic thought on globalization, development, and the moral limits of economic freedom.
