## Summary

*Ad Quo Die* (1758) is Pope Clement XIII's first encyclical, addressed to his fellow bishops at the start of his pontificate. The document is a pastoral exhortation on the duties of the episcopacy, calling bishops to unity, humility, diligent preaching, careful selection of priests, and faithful governance of their dioceses. It draws heavily on the analogy of Moses and the seventy elders, presenting bishops as co-workers with the Pope in shepherding the Church.

## Historical Context

Clement XIII was elected Pope on July 6, 1758, in a period when the Church faced growing pressure from Enlightenment rationalism, regalist movements (particularly in Portugal, Spain, and France), and the looming suppression of the Jesuits. The encyclical was written shortly after his election, reflecting a new pope's desire to set the tone for his reign by reaffirming episcopal identity, unity with Rome, and spiritual priorities amid external threats and internal laxity.

## Key Themes

- **Episcopal unity and fraternal charity** — bishops must preserve the bond of peace among themselves and with the Apostolic See, rooted in Christ's prayer "that they may all be one."
- **Bishop as servant, not lord** — the bishop governs by humility and example, imitating Christ who came not to be served but to serve; ambition, arrogance, and the desire to rule are called dangerous poisons.
- **Preaching as the primary duty** — following the apostles, bishops must personally preach the word of God; if unable, they must still ensure it is taught through others, especially to children.
- **Careful selection and formation of priests** — priests are mirrors for the laity; unfit or vicious clergy cause immense harm, so candidates must be tested thoroughly before ordination.
- **Residence and visitation** — bishops must not be absent from their dioceses and should visit all parts to correct abuses and maintain discipline (citing the Council of Trent).
- **Care for the poor** — Church goods are the inheritance of the poor; almsgiving, along with prayer and the Eucharist, is a chief means of obtaining grace.
- **Opposition to error** — bishops must have the fortitude to confront threats to orthodox faith and morals, trusting in God's strength rather than fearing human opposition.
- **Papal primacy and recourse to Rome** — serious disputes should be referred to the See of St. Peter, from which episcopal authority flows as from its source.

## Significance

*Ad Quo Die* is Clement XIII's foundational statement on the episcopal office, setting the pastoral and spiritual agenda for his pontificate. Its emphasis on episcopal unity, humble service, and meticulous priestly formation echoes the reforming spirit of the Council of Trent. The encyclical also strongly asserts papal primacy — the Roman See as the source and apex of episcopal authority — anticipating the more fully developed ecclesiology of the First Vatican Council (1869–1870). It remains a classic expression of the Tridentine ideal of the bishop as teacher, shepherd, and servant.
