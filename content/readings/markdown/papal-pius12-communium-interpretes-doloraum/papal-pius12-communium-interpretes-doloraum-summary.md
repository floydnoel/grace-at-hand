## Summary

*Communium Interpretes Doloraum* (Interpreter of the Universal Anguish) is an apostolic exhortation issued by Pope Pius XII on April 15, 1945, calling on the faithful — especially children — to pray fervently throughout the month of May for an end to the Second World War. The Pope urges not only prayer and Marian devotion but also a deep moral renewal of public and private life, insisting that true peace can only be built on justice, charity, and a return to Christian principles.

## Historical Context

The letter was written in the final weeks of World War II in Europe. Germany would surrender less than a month later, on May 7–8, 1945, but the fighting was still ongoing, and immense destruction, displacement, and suffering continued across the continent. Pius XII, who had maintained a delicate diplomatic posture throughout the war, here speaks as a pastor calling for prayer, penance, and a just peace — one that would not sow the seeds of future conflicts. The approach of May, traditionally dedicated to the Virgin Mary, provides the liturgical setting for the appeal.

## Key Themes

- **Prayer and intercession of Mary** — The month of May is presented as a privileged time to seek peace through the Blessed Virgin's intercession, especially by the innocent prayers of children.
- **Moral renewal as prerequisite for peace** — Outer devotion is not enough; a conversion of heart, rooted in Christian morality, is required for any lasting social or political order.
- **Justice and charity as foundations of peace** — True peace must be "tempered by the impartial scale of justice" and "embrace in fraternal charity all peoples and all nations," avoiding hidden seeds of future discord.
- **Concern for all victims** — The Pope specifically prays for refugees, prisoners of war, the wounded in hospitals, and all those "afflicted by this monstrous conflict."
- **Role of Christian doctrine** — Private and public salvation must be sought from the precepts of Christian teaching, which alone can renew minds and souls.

## Significance

*Communium Interpretes Doloraum* stands as one of the last papal appeals for peace before the actual cessation of hostilities in Europe. It encapsulates Pius XII's wartime pastoral strategy: urging prayer, avoiding direct political condemnation of either side, and insisting on a just and charitable peace rather than a punitive settlement. The document is also a notable example of the Pope's consistent promotion of Marian devotion as a spiritual response to global crisis. While not one of his major encyclicals, it offers a window into the Church's voice at the close of the most destructive war in human history.
