**La Santa Sede**

BRIEF OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV QUAM EX SUBLIMI To the Venerable Brothers and Beloved Sons, Metropolitans, Archbishops, Bishops, and other Apostolic Vicars. Pope Benedict XIV. Venerable Brothers and Beloved Sons, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. From this sublime vantage point of the most holy apostolate, in which we reside not by any merit of our own but by the ineffable abundance of divine goodness, and in view of the heavy responsibility for all the Churches entrusted to us and imposed upon us for the purpose of governing all souls redeemed by the Blood of Jesus Christ, at another time, specifically on January 26, 1753, we adopted measures of Apostolic providence for the East Indies. Even in those regions, venerable Brothers and beloved Sons, where you are assigned and established and where, with praiseworthy perseverance, you carry out your apostolic ministry—not without grave hardships and constant toil— we cannot bear the thought that such measures might be lacking, just as the immense charity of Jesus Christ, the Supreme Prince of Pastors, strongly impels Us to devote all Our activity and zeal to this end—and all the more earnestly, the farther those regions are from Us. 1. It was not without grave distress to Our pontifical soul that We learned, directly from the Apostolic Vicars of the East Indies, that a certain individual, having been conferred the title and office of Bishop (but without a coadjutor designated to succeed him), upon the conclusion of his earthly life, had ascended to heaven to receive the crown he had earned through his apostolic labors, yet no one had been chosen or appointed to exercise ordinary jurisdiction in place of the deceased Apostolic Vicar. For these reasons, we enjoined and ordered all Apostolic Vicars, individually and collectively, that those who had neither a coadjutor bishop designated to succeed them nor a Vicar General drawn from the clergy—whether secular or religious—should, as soon as each of them received certain news ofOur other Letter, previously sent in the form of a Brief, they should appoint a Vicar belonging to the clergy—whether secular or regular—who is capable and suitable, so that, should the death of an Apostolic Vicar occur without a Coadjutor with future succession, as a delegate of this Holy Apostolic See, would be required to assume the governance of the Apostolic Vicariate until a new Apostolic Vicar, appointed by this same Holy See, takes possession of the Apostolic Vicariate, or until the Holy See has proceeded with another ordination. Furthermore, as has already been stated, after the Vicar General has chosen an Apostolic Vicar, We, in accordance with the spirit of our esteemed Letter and by Apostolic Authority, granted and conferred all the individual faculties that shall remain in force perpetually in the future and that are recognized as pertaining to and being the responsibility of the capitular vicars of every cathedral church when the see is vacant. Furthermore, to the same Vicar General, following the death of the Apostolic Vicar, We granted and conferred the use and exercise of those faculties which the deceased Apostolic Vicar Bishop had exercised and enjoyed during his lifetime, with the exception of those powers that require episcopal ordination and qualifications or that cannot be exercised without the use of the sacred oils. Whenever the need arose, we also granted and conferred upon the Vicar General the power to consecrate chalices, patens, and portable altars with the sacred oils blessed by the Bishop. 2. Furthermore, as we have likewise learned, the same circumstances and precisely the same situations arise in those regions where you strive to fulfill the office entrusted to you. At the same time, it has come to light that in certain places, several Apostolic Vicars, who have not been conferred the title and office of Bishop, but only by virtue of an Apostolic Letter—whether sealed with lead or sent in the form of a Brief—are appointed to hold that office in place of the Apostolic Vicar following the death of the incumbents. For these reasons, we extend the same measures and the aforementioned powers adopted and granted for the East Indies, in the same manner and form—and in no other way—to all Apostolic Vicars of these regions, whether they hold the title and character of a bishop or possess only priestly dignity. And to these Apostolic Vicar Priests (as stated above) we likewise enjoin and order that each one, mindful of his own death, be required to appoint a pro-vicar—provided he is experienced and suitable—who, after the death of his superior, shall assume the office of Apostolic Vicar, and who shall remain in the office of that same vicariate and act using only those faculties which the deceased vicar used, exercised, and enjoyed, until a new Apostolic Vicar designated by the Holy See takes possession of and assumes the governance of that same vicariate. 3. Furthermore, since it has recently become known and established that in Ireland, Albania, Macedonia, Serbia, Bulgaria, the Aegean Islands, Persia, and Mesopotamia (and perhaps elsewhere as well) there are certain Venerable Brother Archbishops and ordinary Bishops of those places, some of whom have a Chapter of Canons under their authority, while others lack such a Chapter of Canons, therefore, with equal measure and authority, we have established, prescribed, and ordained that, upon the death of a superior, the election of the Capitular Vicar shall proceed immediately, in accordance with the custom, practice, and tradition observed thus far. This is to be done either where the Chapter of Canons assembles or solely with the participation of the Canons, if this has already been agreed upon and is considered customary, or with the participation of the Canons together with other clergy, when it is evident that the latter have always taken part on such occasions and have cast their votes in such an election. When, however, the Chapter of Canons is not convened, the parish priests—whether alone or together with other clergy—shall participate in the election of the Capitular Vicar in accordance with the same norms and procedures and in observance of the custom handed down from the past. However, in these elections of the capitular vicars, we decree, ordain, and command that the Constitutions of the Council of Trent be fully observed in all respects, especially Section Twenty-Four, Chapter Sixteen. 4. Furthermore, we decree and order that in those places where Venerable Brothers who are Ordinary Superiors, residing in those same places, have neither a Chapter of Canons nor parish priests under their authority in their cities and dioceses, but where there are only a few priests and missionaries scattered throughout the countryside and villages, so that when any superior dies, they are unable to convene together, then the Vicar General, who has already been appointed by the deceased superior, shall immediately be recognized, shall become, and shall be elected Chapter Vicar, endowed with the aforementioned powers that rightfully belong to Chapter Vicars, and shall hold the office of Chapter Vicar until a new superior designated by this Holy See arrives at that place and assumes its governance. In truth, it is not easy to find, in those places, capable clergy who have earned a doctorate or licentiate in canon law from a university, as is required to hold the office of Vicar, whether capitular or general. While we sympathize with the calamities afflicting those regions, we wish to declare with equal measure and dignity that whoever is to be elected as a capitular vicar in the aforementioned cases, provided he is experienced and qualified for governance and leadership, shall, for the time being and for the time to come, be considered as if he had earned a doctorate or licentiate in canon law. 5. We have decreed by this present Letter that every provision contained herein shall remain firm, valid, and effective at all times, and that it shall achieve and obtain its full and complete effect; and that obedience is due from all those to whom it pertains, in accordance with the circumstances, as and not otherwise stated in the preamble, on the part of each of the ordinary judges, the delegates, as well as the auditors of cases at the Apostolic Palace and the Cardinals of the Holy Roman Church and the Congregations of those same Cardinals, and anyone else who holds or will hold a preeminent office or exercises power: and that anything done otherwise, outside the scope of this Letter, by any authority—whether knowingly or through ignorance—shall be null and void. 6. This is to be understood notwithstanding the Apostolic Constitutions and those special constitutions decreed in the Universal, Provincial, and Synodal Councils, as well as the Rules and any other Decree—whether general or specific—that has been granted, confirmed, or amended, which in any way conflicts with the foregoing. For the rest, Venerable Brothers, Beloved Sons, We address you with the same words and apostolic exhortations with which We greeted the Apostolic Vicars of the East Indies. Therefore, in accordance with the principle of Apostolic wisdom and Our Authority—a principle consistent above all with the care for the salvation of souls, which We have embraced and prescribed to each of you—recognize how great is the responsibility of the apostolic mission entrusted to each of you: Strive for this, so that through constant toil, vigilance, and pastoral care, you may ensure that none of the faithful of Christ entrusted to you—for whom you will have to give an account to the Supreme Prince of Shepherds and eternal Judge—perish; that all may attain eternal salvation; and that God, the just Rewarder, may grant you the reward due to your merits. Meanwhile, Venerable Brothers, beloved Sons, with the help of heavenly protection, we impart with great affection the Apostolic Blessing also to the people of your dioceses. 7. We further desire that, upon receipt of this letter or even printed copies thereof, signed by the Prefect and the Secretary (in office as the circumstances may require) of the Congregation of Our Venerable Brothers the Cardinals of the Holy Roman Church, who are in charge of the offices of the Propagation of the Faith, and bearing the seal of that same Congregation, the same faith be given to them as would be given to this very letter itself if it were presented or shown. Given in Rome, at Santa Maria Maggiore, under the Fisherman’s Ring, on August 8, 1755, in the fifteenth year of Our Pontificate.
