# Quemadmodum Nihil

**La Santa Sede**

---

> *Note: This document was translated from the original Italian into English. Translation provided by DeepL (July 2026).*

BRIEF OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV QUEMADMODUM NIHIL To the Venerable Brothers, Patriarchs, Primates, Metropolitans, Archbishops, Bishops, and all Ordinary Prelates of those places that are at peace and in communion with the Apostolic See. Pope Benedict XIV. Venerable Brothers, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. Just as for men who are estranged from the family of God and from communion with Him, and incapable of receiving His divine radiance, nothing is more fitting, salutary, and necessary than prayer to secure eternal salvation and union with God; for prayer is like the soul’s ascent and elevation from earthly to heavenly things, a search for heavenly goods, a longing for the invisible, union with the Holy Spirit, and communion with God. Thus—lest the whole earth become desolate because no one meditates in their heart—all Christians and every individual, everywhere and always, must be exhorted with wholesome precepts: one must always pray and never grow weary, so that in all our prayers and supplications offered with thanksgiving, our requests may be presented to God. He, who is generous toward all who call upon Him, does not turn away our prayers, nor does He withhold His mercy from us. For this reason, our predecessor Roman Pontiffs, in accordance with divine institution, in order to encourage the Christian faithful to undertake and practice prayer unceasingly—whether vocal or —as is commonly said—silent, sought to inspire them, both by inflaming their hearts with exhortations and by bestowing upon them the treasures of the gifts that the Most High has entrusted to them. In fact, they granted indulgences, remission of sins, and the remission of penances imposed in any manner, both to those who recited vocal prayers and to those who, each day for a certain period of time, meditated on the Law of the Lord. We, therefore, inspired by the most praiseworthy examples of our Predecessors, not only confirm with the same formalities all the individual indulgences—including the remission of sins and the dispensation from penances—that have been granted to this day by our Predecessors to those who recite any vocal prayer —provided that such a concession has never been revoked—or to those who pray in any manner—whether ad tempus or in perpetuum—but by virtue of Apostolic Authority and in accordance with the present decree, we confirm them anew if necessary; indeed, we grant even more, especially to those who devote themselves to mental exercises or meditation. First of all, to all and to each individual, whether in churches or elsewhere, or in any public or private place, who teach people who are uneducated and inexperienced in prayer and meditation how to pray and meditate, and to those who participate in these pious institutions, and whenever they do so, We grant an indulgence of seven years and seven quarantenas, provided that, having truly repented, they receive Holy Communion in the customary manner of the Church. We also graciously grant, once in a lifetime, a Plenary Indulgence for all their sins to both the teachers and the disciples who have assiduously performed the aforementioned prayers and engaged in meditation, provided that, likewise repentant and having received Holy Communion, they offer pious supplications to God for the harmony of Christian princes, for the eradication of heresies, and for the exaltation of Holy Mother Church. The Indulgence may be applied as a suffrage for the souls of the faithful Christians who have departed this life united with God in charity. Likewise, by Our Authority and in the same manner, We grant a Plenary Indulgence once in a lifetime, with the remission of all sins, to all those who, for a continuous half-hour or at least a quarter of an hour each day, devote themselves to mental prayer throughout the month, provided that, having repented and been confessed, they receive the Most Holy Sacrament of the Eucharist and offer pious prayers to God for the harmony of Christian rulers, for the eradication of heresies, and for the exaltation of Holy Mother Church. The indulgence may be applied as a suffrage for the souls of the Christian faithful who, united to God through charity, have departed this life. And although anyone who intends to pray according to the divine precept of our Lord Jesus Christ must not act like the hypocrites, seeking to be seen by others, but must instead enter his own room, close the door, and pray to the Father in secret—nevertheless, since the Lord Himself has stated that where two or more are gathered in His name, He is in their midst; for St. John Chrysostom teaches that in prayer together with one’s brothers there is something more, namely, harmony, agreement, the union of charity, and the witness of sincere friendship—through which even the most humble prayers, when united together are more effectively carried to heaven—it would truly be best if what takes place in some institutes were to become the custom in all dioceses; namely, that on certain days—upon a specific ringing of the bells—in the various churches where the multitude of the people can easily gather to pray (men and women separated so that there may be no confusion or disorder), as well as heads of households in their own homes, gathering the entire family before a sacred image, may pray devoutly at the same time or at another more suitable time. Therefore, Venerable Brothers, we beg and exhort you in the Lord to order all Superiors or Rectors of churches or places of worship—in the cities of the cathedral and other principal churches, and in the towns of the parish churches—that – following the ringing of the bell – on those days and at those hours they deem most appropriate, arrange for all the faithful entrusted to their care to gather together and be instructed and introduced to the practice of mental prayer – or have them instructed by others, provided they are experts – and to inspire them to practice it, emphasizing its necessity and usefulness, explaining to them the treasures of indulgences that We bestow upon them through Our Apostolic benevolence, and earnestly exhorting them to gain these indulgences with the necessary reverence and piety. And, as we have stated previously, if it can be done conveniently, care should be taken to train them in the practice of mental prayer in a communal setting, as is commonly said. This shall apply both now and in the future. We further desire that the same faith and authority be accorded to this Letter, even if transmitted in copy or in print, provided it is signed by a public notary and bearing the seal of a person holding ecclesiastical office, as if it were presented or shown directly. Meanwhile, we impart to you, Venerable Brothers, with the utmost affection, the Apostolic Blessing. Given in Rome, at Saint Mary Major, under the Fisherman’s Ring, on December 16, 1746, the sixth year of Our Pontificate.
