## Summary

This 1948 encyclical by Pope Pius XII addresses the war in Palestine and calls for prayers for peace, protection of the Holy Places, and an internationally guaranteed status for Jerusalem. The Pope expresses profound sorrow over the violence in the land of Christ's redemption, condemns recourse to armed force, and urges the faithful to pray for a just settlement that respects the rights and traditions of all parties.

## Historical Context

Written on October 24, 1948, during the Arab-Israeli war that followed the establishment of the State of Israel that same year. The encyclical comes in the aftermath of World War II and the Holocaust, with Pius XII having already issued *Auspicia Quaedam* earlier that year urging prayers for peace in Palestine. The document reflects both the humanitarian crisis (thousands of refugees, destruction of sacred buildings) and the Vatican's concern for Christian Holy Places caught in the conflict.

## Key Themes

- **Sorrow over war in the Holy Land** — the profound irony that blood continues to flow where Christ shed His blood for salvation
- **Papal impartiality** — the Holy See stands above political conflicts while actively working for justice and peace
- **Protection of the Holy Places** — particular concern for Jerusalem's sanctuaries and the buildings around them
- **Humanitarian action** — the Holy See sent material aid to victims and encouraged Catholic relief organizations
- **Prayer as primary response** — human means are insufficient; constant recourse to prayer is essential
- **Internationalization of Jerusalem** — the Pope proposes an internationally guaranteed status for Jerusalem and its outskirts to protect the sanctuaries and ensure free access and freedom of worship
- **Respect for rights and traditions** — peace must be built on truth, justice, and respect for each group's acquired rights and obligations

## Significance

*In Multiplicibus Curis* established the modern Vatican position on the status of Jerusalem — advocating for its internationalization — a stance that would be reaffirmed by subsequent popes for decades. It represents one of the earliest and most authoritative Catholic responses to the Arab-Israeli conflict, framing the issue not merely as a political dispute but as a matter of profound religious concern for all Christianity. The encyclical also exemplifies Pius XII's diplomatic approach: publicly impartial yet privately active in humanitarian relief and behind-the-scenes peace efforts. Its call for free access to Holy Places and religious freedom remains a cornerstone of Vatican policy in the Middle East to this day.
