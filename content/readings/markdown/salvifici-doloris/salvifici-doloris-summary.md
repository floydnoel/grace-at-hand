# Salvifici Doloris · English editorial summary

## Summary

John Paul II approaches bodily and spiritual suffering with compassion and respect for the mystery of each person. Surveying Scripture and human experience, he rejects the assumption that every sufferer is being punished for personal wrongdoing: Job’s innocence exposes the inadequacy of that explanation. The fullest Christian response to suffering comes through divine love revealed in Christ, who freely enters human anguish and, through his Cross and Resurrection, overcomes sin and death. This victory gives suffering a new horizon without removing it from earthly life.

United with Christ, a suffering person can participate in the fruitfulness of his redemption for others and the Church. Paul’s language about completing Christ’s afflictions does not imply an incomplete sacrifice: Christ’s redemptive work is fully accomplished and inexhaustible, yet remains open to human participation in love. John Paul presents Mary’s compassion beneath the Cross as an exceptional witness. He acknowledges that recognizing this vocation may involve protest, difficulty, and a long interior journey; it is an encounter with the suffering Christ rather than a merely abstract explanation.

The Good Samaritan supplies the corresponding obligation toward others: stop, show compassion, and give effective help. The letter commends healthcare, voluntary service, family support, and education in solidarity, while insisting on personal attentiveness within institutions. Discovering spiritual meaning in suffering therefore accompanies active work to relieve it. Christ identifies himself with those who suffer and calls both sufferers and caregivers into self-giving love.

## Key themes

- Innocent suffering and the rejection of automatic blame
- Christ’s completed redemption and human participation through love
- Patient spiritual accompaniment and Mary’s compassion
- The Good Samaritan, effective care, and solidarity
