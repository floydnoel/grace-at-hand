## Summary

*Diuturnum* (1881) is Pope Leo XIII's encyclical on the origin of civil power. It argues that all political authority derives from God, not from the people or any social contract, and that rulers are ministers of God. The pope warns that modern philosophies — particularly popular sovereignty, communism, socialism, and nihilism — have eroded respect for authority and destabilized society, and he calls both rulers and subjects back to the Catholic teaching on the divine source of political power.

## Historical Context

Written just months after the assassination of Tsar Alexander II of Russia by nihilists (March 1881), the encyclical responds to a wave of anti-monarchist violence across Europe. Leo XIII was also contending with the legacy of the French Revolution, the rise of liberal nationalism, and the spread of Enlightenment ideas about popular sovereignty (Rousseau's social contract). Within the Church, the pope was navigating the challenge of defining Catholic political teaching in an era when the Papal States had recently been lost (1870) and the Church's own temporal authority was in question.

## Key Themes

- **Divine origin of authority**: All political power comes from God, not from the people or any human pact (paras. 8–11).
- **Legitimacy of diverse forms of government**: The Church does not prescribe monarchy, aristocracy, or democracy — any just form is acceptable (para. 7).
- **Consent vs. delegation**: The people may choose their rulers, but this choice designates the person, not the source of authority, which remains God (para. 6).
- **Duty of obedience**: Citizens must obey rulers for conscience' sake, not merely from fear of punishment (paras. 13–14).
- **Limited obedience**: When rulers command what is contrary to divine law, Christians must obey God rather than men (para. 15).
- **Rulers' accountability**: Princes will be judged by God and must govern for the common good, not their own advantage (para. 16).
- **Critique of modern errors**: Attacks popular sovereignty, the social contract, the Reformation's political consequences, and contemporary ideologies like communism and nihilism (paras. 5, 12, 23).
- **Church's civilizing role**: The Church strengthens civil authority, promotes social harmony, and has consistently taught obedience even under pagan persecutors (paras. 18–22, 26).

## Significance

*Diuturnum* is the first of Leo XIII's great political encyclicals, laying the groundwork for later documents like *Immortale Dei* (1885) on the Christian constitution of states and *Libertas praestantissimum* (1888) on human liberty. It represents the definitive 19th-century Catholic response to Enlightenment political thought, reasserting the Thomistic doctrine that authority flows from God while accommodating modern forms of government. The encyclical remains a key reference point for Catholic teaching on the state, the limits of civil disobedience, and the proper relationship between spiritual and temporal power.
