## Summary

Unam Sanctam is a papal bull issued by Pope Boniface VIII in 1302 that asserts the absolute supremacy of the spiritual authority (the papacy) over all temporal power. It declares that outside the Catholic Church there is no salvation, that the Church has both spiritual and temporal swords at its disposal, and that every human creature must be subject to the Roman pontiff for salvation.

## Historical Context

Boniface VIII issued Unam Sanctam amid a bitter conflict with King Philip IV of France over the king's authority to tax clergy and try bishops in royal courts. Philip had arrested a papal legate, and Boniface responded with escalating bulls, culminating in this document. The broader backdrop includes the rise of centralized national monarchies challenging the medieval papacy's claim to universal temporal supremacy, as well as ongoing tensions with the Eastern Church (the "Greeks" referenced in the text).

## Key Themes

- **Extra ecclesiam nulla salus** — Outside the one Church there is neither salvation nor remission of sins
- **One body, one head** — The Church cannot have two heads (like a monster); Christ and his vicar (the pope) are its singular head
- **Two swords doctrine** — Both spiritual and temporal power belong to the Church; the temporal sword is exercised on behalf of the Church, the spiritual by the Church, and the spiritual judges the temporal when it errs
- **Papal supremacy** — The pope, as successor of Peter, holds supreme authority over all Christians; "it is altogether necessary for salvation that every human creature be subject to the Roman pontiff"

## Significance

Unam Sanctam is the most extreme medieval statement of papal supremacy over temporal rulers. It became a foundational text for ultramontane Catholicism and was frequently cited in later debates about Church and state, including at the First Vatican Council (1869–1870), which defined papal infallibility. Its stark dual-swords framework—while never formally adopted as dogma — shaped Catholic ecclesiology for centuries and remains a landmark in the history of Western political thought.
