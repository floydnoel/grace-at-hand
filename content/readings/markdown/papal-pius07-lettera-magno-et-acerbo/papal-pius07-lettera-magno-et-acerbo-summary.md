# Lettera Magno et acerbo · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VII rebukes a prelate for encouraging indiscriminate reception and reading of recent vernacular Bible translations that did not follow the Church's prescribed safeguards. He defends the existing rules requiring suitable Catholic annotations and argues that unregulated translations and interpretation can introduce doctrinal errors. His concern extends beyond translation: he accuses the recipient of quoting the Council of Trent and Pius VI selectively, omitting passages that affirm tradition and explain why an approved translation was commended.

The pope presses for correction of the resulting scandal. While urging a formal retraction, he explicitly commands at least a new pastoral letter reproducing the relevant texts fully, affirming that Christian doctrine is contained in Scripture and tradition and that their authoritative interpretation belongs to the Church. The recipient must clarify that he did not commend unauthorized translations or indiscriminate reading by every believer, but addressed clergy and suitably instructed laity judged capable by their pastors. The letter presents these requirements as necessary protection of faith and episcopal responsibility under the discipline then in force.

## Key themes

- Historical restrictions on unapproved vernacular Bible translations
- Scripture, tradition, and ecclesiastical interpretation
- Correction of misleading quotations from authoritative texts
- Episcopal responsibility for repairing public scandal
