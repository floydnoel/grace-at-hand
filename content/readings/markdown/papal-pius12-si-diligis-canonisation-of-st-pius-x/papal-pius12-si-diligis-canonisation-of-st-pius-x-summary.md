## Summary

"Si Diligis" is a 1954 allocution delivered by Pope Pius XII to cardinals, archbishops, and bishops gathered in Rome for the canonization of St. Pius X. Taking its title from Christ's command to Peter — "If you love me, feed my sheep" — the document focuses primarily on the nature and authority of the Church's teaching office, warning against trends that undermine the hierarchy's divinely instituted role as authentic teacher of faith and morals.

## Historical Context

The allocution was delivered on May 31, 1954, during the canonization celebrations of St. Pius X (who had himself fought the Modernist crisis). The post-war period saw significant intellectual ferment in Catholic theology, with movements like the *nouvelle théologie* seeking to renew Thomism through historical and patristic sources. Pius XII had already addressed these currents in his 1950 encyclical *Humani generis*, warning against doctrinal relativism. "Si Diligis" extends that concern, reflecting anxiety about the rise of "lay theology" and a perceived erosion of deference to the Church's living Magisterium.

## Key Themes

- **The triple office of bishops** — The document frames episcopal identity through the threefold role of teacher, priest, and ruler, though it limits itself to the teaching office.
- **Exclusive divine constitution of teaching authority** — Only the Pope and bishops are, by divine right, authentic teachers in the Church; others teach only by delegation and remain always subject to hierarchical oversight.
- **Warning against independent "lay theology"** — Pius XII criticizes the emergence of lay theologians who claim a teaching authority (*sui juris*) independent of and sometimes opposed to the hierarchy, rejecting appeals to New Testament charisms as justification.
- **Confirmation of *Humani generis*** — The allocution reiterates the 1950 encyclical's condemnation of those who neglect conformity with the living Magisterium.
- **Lay apostolate welcomed but supervised** — The document praises lay catechetical and apostolic work but insists it remain under the authority, leadership, and watchfulness of bishops.
- **Pastors as "patterns of the flock"** — Drawing on 1 Peter, Pius XII urges bishops to lead by example, not constraint, and to actively guard against what he calls a "spiritual contagion."

## Significance

"Si Diligis" is a significant but less-cited companion to *Humani generis*, sharpening the practical implications of that encyclical for episcopal governance. It is one of the clearest mid-20th century statements on the hierarchical nature of teaching authority in the Church, anticipating later debates about the role of the laity in theology that would intensify during and after the Second Vatican Council. Its warnings about lay teaching authority remain relevant in contemporary discussions about academic freedom in Catholic institutions and the relationship between the hierarchy and Catholic intellectuals.
