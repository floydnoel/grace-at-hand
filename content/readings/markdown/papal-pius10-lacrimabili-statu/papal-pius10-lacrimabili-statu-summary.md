## Summary

*Lacrimabili Statu* (1912) is an encyclical by Pope Pius X addressed to the archbishops and bishops of Latin America, denouncing the ongoing enslavement, exploitation, and brutal treatment of Indigenous peoples of South America. Written nearly 170 years after Benedict XIV's similar letter *Immensa Pastorum* (1741), Pius X acknowledges that formal slavery has been legally abolished in most regions (largely due to Church advocacy), but reports that de facto slavery, forced labor, human trafficking, and violent abuses persist, especially in remote areas. The pope calls on the Church in Latin America to redouble its efforts to protect Indigenous peoples through missions, charitable works, and moral instruction.

## Historical Context

By the early 20th century, the independent republics of Latin America had formally abolished the slave trade, yet in practice Indigenous communities in frontier and jungle regions continued to suffer forced labor, land dispossession, and violent abuse. Pius X writes after receiving corroborated reports from bishops, apostolic delegates, and missionaries confirming these ongoing atrocities. The encyclical reflects both the limits of state authority in remote territories and the Catholic Church's role as the primary institutional advocate for Indigenous rights in a region where civil governance was weak, corruption was rampant, and economic greed drove the exploitation of native peoples.

## Key Themes

- **Continuity with papal tradition**: The encyclical explicitly recalls and reaffirms Benedict XIV's *Immensa Pastorum* (1741), placing itself in a long line of papal defense of Indigenous peoples.
- **Denunciation of de facto slavery**: Though formal slavery has been abolished, Pius X condemns the ongoing practices of capturing, selling, trading, and holding Indians in servitude, as well as separating families and depriving them of property.
- **Root causes**: The pope identifies greed ("lust of lucre") as the primary driver, alongside the isolation of remote regions from both civil authority and religious formation, which fosters moral corruption among settlers.
- **Collaboration of Church and State**: Pius X praises civil authorities for their efforts but notes that government action alone is often ineffective in remote areas; Church missions working alongside the State offer the best hope for meaningful protection.
- **Duty of the faithful**: Bishops are urged to promote charitable works for Indigenous peoples, to encourage financial support and prayers for missions, and to preach Christian charity that recognizes all people — regardless of nation or color — as true brethren.
- **Condemnation and penalties**: The pope explicitly condemns as a grave sin anyone who enslaves, sells, buys, traffics, or deprives Indigenous people of their liberty, invoking apostolic authority to reinforce this prohibition.
- **Expansion of missions**: Pius X announces the establishment of additional missionary stations to provide safe havens for Indigenous people, emphasizing the Church's role as a refuge.

## Significance

*Lacrimabili Statu* stands as one of the strongest 20th-century papal statements on Indigenous rights and remains a key reference in Catholic social teaching on the protection of native peoples. It demonstrates the Church's willingness to confront systemic injustice even where civil authorities are unable or unwilling to act, and it reaffirms the principle that evangelization cannot be separated from the defense of human dignity. The encyclical also illustrates a shift from the older, more paternalistic missionary model toward a fuller assertion of the Church's role as an independent moral authority advocating for the most vulnerable, a theme that would recur in later papal teaching on Indigenous rights, economic justice, and the critique of colonial exploitation.
