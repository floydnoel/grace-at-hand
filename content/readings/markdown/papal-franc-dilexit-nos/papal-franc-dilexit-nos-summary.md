## Summary

*Dilexit Nos* ("He Loved Us") is Pope Francis's fourth encyclical, issued on October 24, 2024. It calls the Church and the world to rediscover the theological and spiritual significance of the Sacred Heart of Jesus as the symbol of Christ's human and divine love, arguing that devotion to the heart of Christ is a synthesis of the Gospel and a necessary antidote to the superficiality, individualism, and "heartlessness" of modern society.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was published in the twelfth year of Francis's pontificate (2024), during a period marked by ongoing wars (notably in Ukraine and Gaza), deepening social polarization, the rise of artificial intelligence, and a consumerist culture that Francis describes as "liquid" and rootless. It follows and complements his social encyclicals *Laudato Si'* (on creation care, 2015) and *Fratelli Tutti* (on fraternity, 2020), completing a trilogy by focusing on the interior, spiritual foundation for social transformation. It also marks the 350th anniversary of the apparitions of the Sacred Heart to Saint Margaret Mary Alacoque at Paray-le-Monial (1673–1675).

## Key Themes

- **The heart as the unifying centre of the person** — Francis defines the heart not sentimentally but as the deep core where intellect, will, emotions, and identity converge, and which alone can integrate a fragmented life.
- **Rediscovering interiority in a superficial age** — The encyclical diagnoses modern culture as "heartless," dominated by algorithms, consumerism, and haste, and argues that returning to the heart is essential for personal and social renewal.
- **The threefold love of Christ's heart** — Drawing on Pius XII, Francis distinguishes and unites Christ's divine love, the infused spiritual love of his human soul, and his sensible (emotional, bodily) love, insisting that all three are inseparable in the one Person of the Word.
- **A clear Christological and Trinitarian focus** — Devotion to the Sacred Heart is not worship of a body part but adoration of the living Christ, which leads through the Spirit to the Father.
- **Reparation as building a civilization of love** — Francis reinterprets reparation not as appeasing an angry God but as cooperating with Christ's merciful love to heal social wounds and build fraternity.
- **Mission and service as the fruit of devotion** — Authentic devotion to the heart of Christ necessarily overflows into love of neighbour, especially the poor, the suffering, and the marginalized.
- **The witness of the saints** — The encyclical draws extensively on Saint Margaret Mary Alacoque, Saint Claude de La Colombière, Saint Francis de Sales, Saint Charles de Foucauld, Saint Thérèse of Lisieux, Saint John Henry Newman, Saint Ignatius Loyola, and Saint John Paul II, among others.

## Significance

*Dilexit Nos* is the first magisterial document in decades to offer a comprehensive treatment of devotion to the Sacred Heart, reaffirming its centrality after a period of decline in popular piety. By rooting this traditional devotion in Scripture, patristic theology, and modern philosophy (Heidegger, Guardini, Rahner), Francis gives it renewed intellectual depth while linking it directly to his signature social concerns. It completes his so-called "social trilogy" by arguing that care for creation (*Laudato Si'*) and universal fraternity (*Fratelli Tutti*) require an interior conversion grounded in the experience of Christ's love — a love "that has so greatly loved" and that alone can give our world back its heart.
