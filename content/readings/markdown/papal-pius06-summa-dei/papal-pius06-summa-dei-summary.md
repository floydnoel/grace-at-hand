# Summa Dei · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VI gives thanks for the Jubilee celebrated in Rome during his first pontifical year, completing the observance announced by Clement XIV. He recalls the opening and closing of the holy doors, pilgrims' penitential devotion, Roman hospitality, and the work of confessors. He explains the jubilee's purpose as reconciliation with God and participation in the remission of punishment through Christ's merits and the communion of saints.

The bull extends the jubilee to Catholics throughout the world, including those who had already observed it in Rome. During a locally published six-month period, participants are to confess, receive Communion, make the prescribed church visits on fifteen days, and offer the designated prayers. Adaptations are provided for travelers, enclosed religious, prisoners, the sick, and children, with further provisions for those dying before completing the visits. Approved confessors receive exceptional faculties subject to stated limits. Bishops must explain and promote the observance, and Catholic rulers are asked to assist. The extension emphasizes lasting conversion and charitable participation rather than pilgrimage alone.

## Key themes

- Thanksgiving for the Roman Jubilee of 1775
- Worldwide extension through local church visits
- Sacramental reconciliation and penitential renewal
- Accommodations for impeded pilgrims and other participants
