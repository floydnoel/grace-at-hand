## Summary

*Rerum Orientalium* is a 1928 encyclical by Pope Pius XI on the promotion of Oriental studies. It calls for greater knowledge of Eastern Christian languages, liturgies, histories, and traditions among Catholic clergy and seminarians, and reaffirms the Church’s commitment to the Pontifical Oriental Institute in Rome as a center for fostering reunion with Eastern Orthodox and other separated Eastern churches.

## Historical Context

Written seven years into Pius XI's pontificate and less than a decade after Benedict XV had founded the Pontifical Oriental Institute (1917), the encyclical reflects the aftermath of World War I and the collapse of the Ottoman and Russian Empires, which opened new possibilities for engagement with Eastern Christians. The emerging ecumenical movement (addressed the year before in *Mortalium animos*) and growing Catholic interest in the Christian East prompted the Pope to systematize Oriental studies, transfer the Institute to the Jesuits, relocate it to a permanent home near St. Mary Major, and urge every seminary to appoint at least one professor in Eastern sciences.

## Key Themes

- **Knowledge as a path to unity** — mutual ignorance and contempt are the root causes of schism; study dissolves prejudice and fosters reunion.
- **Papal continuity** — the encyclical traces a long line of predecessors (Adrian I, Clement V, Gregory XVI, Leo XIII, Benedict XV) who supported Oriental learning and Eastern Christians.
- **The Pontifical Oriental Institute** — its founding, Jesuit administration, move to a permanent site, and its dual mission of teaching and publishing (*Orientalia Christiana*).
- **Every diocese needs an Orientalist** — seminary professors should teach the elements of Eastern history, liturgy, and canon law; Catholic universities should establish dedicated Oriental faculties.
- **Respect for Eastern rites** — diversity of rites reveals the beauty of the Church; preservation of liturgy within Catholic unity is paramount.
- **Cooperation with separated brethren** — the Institute welcomes both Eastern Catholics and those separated from Rome; scholarship serves charity.

## Significance

*Rerum Orientalium* remains a foundational text for Catholic engagement with the Christian East. It institutionalized the study of Eastern Christianity in Catholic seminaries and universities, anticipated the Second Vatican Council's affirmation of Eastern traditions (*Orientalium Ecclesiarum* and *Unitatis Redintegratio*), and shaped the modern Pontifical Oriental Institute as a lasting hub of scholarship and dialogue between Latin and Eastern Churches.
