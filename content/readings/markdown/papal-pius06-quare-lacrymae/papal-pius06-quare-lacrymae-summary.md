# Quare lacrymae · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VI laments the execution of Louis XVI and denounces the revolutionary tribunal and political order responsible for it. He praises the king's patience, Catholic devotion, testament, and repentance for measures harmful to the Church. In the pope's interpretation, hostility to Catholic religion was the fundamental cause of the king's death, despite the political charges brought against him. He connects the event with revolutionary persecution and with the influence he attributes to anti-Catholic writers and religious opponents.

Comparing Louis with Mary Stuart and other cases discussed in the Church's treatment of martyrdom, Pius argues that a political occasion does not exclude martyrdom when hatred of the faith is decisive. He also answers the objection that Louis had signed the Civil Constitution of the Clergy, declining responsibility for competing accounts of the signature and emphasizing the king's subsequent retraction. Crucially, the address distinguishes the pope's expressed conviction about martyrdom from a formal declaration by the Apostolic See. It therefore ends by ordering public funeral suffrages for Louis rather than treating his status as canonically established.

## Key themes

- Papal denunciation of Louis XVI's trial and execution
- The document's religious interpretation of revolutionary violence
- Martyrdom, motive, repentance, and disputed political acts
- Distinction between personal judgment and formal ecclesiastical recognition
