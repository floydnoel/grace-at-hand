## Summary

*In Plurimis* is an 1888 encyclical by Pope Leo XIII celebrating the impending abolition of slavery in Brazil while condemning the slave trade globally. It traces the Church's historical role in opposing slavery, affirms the God-given dignity of every human person, and calls for the complete eradication of the institution everywhere, especially in Africa.

## Historical Context

Brazil was the last Western Hemisphere nation to maintain legal slavery. By 1888, mounting pressure from abolitionist movements, the imperial family (Emperor Pedro II and Princess Isabel), and Church figures had led to gradual emancipation laws. The encyclical was published just days before the *Lei Áurea* (Golden Law) of May 13, 1888, which abolished slavery outright in Brazil. Pope Leo XIII was also responding to reports of ongoing slave raids in Africa and the broader "scramble for Africa" then underway among European colonial powers.

## Key Themes

- **Slavery contradicts natural law** — God created humans to rule over beasts, not over one another; slavery is a consequence of sin, not nature.
- **The Church as liberator** — From the Apostles through the Church Fathers to modern popes, the Church consistently taught the spiritual equality of slaves and free persons and worked to mitigate and ultimately abolish slavery.
- **Conversion as the engine of freedom** — The spread of Christianity is the surest path to the abolition of slavery; faith transforms how masters and slaves relate.
- **Papal continuity against the slave trade** — The encyclical cites a chain of predecessors (Pius II, Paul III, Urban VIII, Benedict XIV, Pius VII, Gregory XVI) who condemned slavery and the slave trade.
- **Condemnation of the African slave trade** — Leo XIII specifically denounces the ongoing capture and sale of Africans, calling it "base and wicked."
- **Liberty is not license** — Freed slaves are exhorted to use their liberty virtuously, as responsible citizens and faithful Christians.

## Significance

*In Plurimis* is Leo XIII's strongest magisterial condemnation of slavery and represents the culmination of a long trajectory of Catholic teaching against the institution. It aligned the Church squarely with the abolitionist cause at a decisive historical moment, reinforced the theological foundation for universal human dignity, and served as a precursor to later social encyclicals. Its canonization of St. Peter Claver (the "slave of the Negroes") on the eve of its publication symbolically sealed the message. Though it did not stop the slave trade outright, the document permanently committed the papacy to the principle that slavery is intrinsically evil and incompatible with Christian faith.
