## Summary

*Fausto Appetente Die* is an apostolic letter issued by Pope Benedict XV on June 29, 1921, to mark the seventh centenary of the death of St. Dominic (1221). The document celebrates Dominic's holiness, his founding of the Order of Preachers (Dominicans), and the Order's enduring contribution to the Church through preaching, doctrinal fidelity, Marian piety, and loyalty to the papacy. Benedict XV calls on the Dominicans—and the whole Church—to renew their devotion to St. Dominic's example.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written shortly after World War I (1914–1918) and amid widespread social upheaval, secularism, and religious indifference in Europe. Benedict XV had spent his pontificate pursuing peace and humanitarian relief during and after the war. The 700th anniversary of Dominic's death (1221–1921) provided an opportunity to recall the Church's intellectual and spiritual heritage. The pope also had a personal connection: before his election, he had been Archbishop of Bologna, the city that preserves Dominic's remains.

## Key Themes

- **Preaching and doctrinal solidity**: Dominic insisted his friars be thoroughly grounded in Scripture and theology before preaching, a model Benedict XV upholds as urgently needed in the modern age.
- **Fidelity to the Apostolic See**: Dominic's unwavering loyalty to the papacy—symbolized by the vision of him holding up the Lateran Basilica—is presented as a hallmark of the Dominican charism, inherited by later figures like Catherine of Siena and St. Pius V.
- **Marian piety and the Rosary**: Dominic is credited with receiving the Rosary from the Virgin Mary and promoting it as a weapon against heresy. Benedict XV urges its continued use, echoing Leo XIII's Marian devotion, especially in "troublous times."
- **The Dominican intellectual tradition**: The letter highlights Dominicans such as Albert the Great, Thomas Aquinas, and Vincent Ferrer, and notes that the Church has declared Thomas's teaching her own—a reference to *Aeterni Patris* and the Thomistic revival.
- **Relevance to contemporary needs**: Benedict XV insists the Dominican mission is as vital in 1921 as it was in the 13th century, addressing ignorance, error, and disaffection from the Church.

## Significance

*Fausto Appetente Die* is one of several early-20th-century papal documents reinforcing the centrality of St. Dominic and the Dominican Order in Catholic life. It reaffirms the Rosary as a lay devotional staple, ties Dominican identity to papal loyalty (a pointed message in an era of anti-clericalism and nationalism), and underscores the Church's commitment to Thomistic philosophy as a bulwark against modern errors. Its appeal to the Dominican Third Order (lay tertiaries) reflects a broader papal strategy of engaging the laity in the Church's mission. Though less cited than Leo XIII's Rosary encyclicals or *Aeterni Patris*, it remains a concise, affectionate portrait of the Dominican vocation from a pope who knew the Order well.
