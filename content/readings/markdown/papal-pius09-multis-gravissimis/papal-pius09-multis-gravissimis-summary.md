# Multis gravissimis · English editorial summary

## Summary

Following the abolition of Sicily’s claimed Apostolic Legation, the so-called Monarchy, and associated tribunals, Pius IX establishes procedures for ecclesiastical cases on the island. Ordinary cases begin before local ecclesiastical authorities, with specified routes of appeal to metropolitan or delegated bishops and ultimately to Rome. Greater causes remain reserved, direct recourse to the Holy See is protected, and local judges may not assume authority over papally reserved censures.

The brief gives detailed rules for marriage-nullity proceedings, requiring a defender of the bond, proper participation in each stage, and review before remarriage under the conditions then prescribed. It also regulates challenges to the validity of religious profession, balancing diocesan and religious superiors’ roles, requiring legally gathered evidence and a defender, and establishing review procedures. Particular marriage dispensations are made available without charge to the genuinely poor. The special faculties and concessions are limited to ten years. Pius asks bishops to implement this historical judicial framework faithfully as a means of protecting souls, episcopal jurisdiction, and ecclesiastical discipline.

## Key themes

- Replacement of abolished Sicilian tribunals by canonical procedures
- Appeals, reserved causes, and direct recourse to Rome
- Defenders and review in marriage and religious-profession cases
- Temporary faculties for free marriage dispensations to poor petitioners
