## Summary

*Appetente Sacro* (1759) is Clement XIII's exhortation to reinvigorate the Lenten fast. It warns against abuses that had weakened fasting discipline — particularly the mistaken belief that a dispensation to eat meat also permitted dairy-based drinks — and insists that true penance requires not only confession but active satisfaction through fasting, almsgiving, and prayer.

## Historical Context

Written near the end of the 18th century, the document reflects a period when laxity in fasting observance had become widespread. Clement's predecessor Benedict XIV had already issued two briefs attempting to restore the discipline; Clement XIII follows up to combat "new corruptions" and "perverse customs" that continued to erode it.

## Key Themes

- **Penance requires satisfaction**: Confession alone is insufficient; the penitent must also punish his own sins through fasting and other ascetical works, lest God do so.
- **The Lenten fast is a participation in Christ's passion**: Fasting prepares the faithful to die with Christ and rise with Him at Easter.
- **Abuse of dispensations**: Those granted permission to eat meat cannot extend that permission to milk-based drinks; the integrity of the fast must be preserved.
- **Role of bishops**: Pastors must teach, inspire, and exemplify the love of penance so that the faithful embrace fasting willingly.

## Significance

The encyclical is a minor but pointed document in the Church's perennial effort to maintain penitential discipline against encroaching laxity. It reaffirms the traditional Catholic doctrine that penance is a necessary part of conversion, not merely an interior sentiment, and it illustrates the pastoral vigilance expected of bishops in safeguarding liturgical and ascetical norms.
