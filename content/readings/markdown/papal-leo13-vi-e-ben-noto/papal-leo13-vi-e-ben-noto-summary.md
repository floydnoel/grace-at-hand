## Summary

*Vi E Ben Noto* (1887) is an apostolic letter from Pope Leo XIII to the bishops of Italy, urging a revival of devotion to the Rosary, especially during the month of October, as a spiritual remedy for the Church's trials. The pope places his confidence in the Virgin of the Holy Rosary to protect Christendom, restore faith and morals in Italy, and counter the work of anti-Catholic sects — particularly active in Rome itself.

## Historical Context

Written on 20 September 1887, the letter reflects the turbulent situation of the Church after the unification of Italy (the *Risorgimento*). The Papal States had been lost in 1870, and the pope was effectively a "prisoner in the Vatican," unable to exercise temporal power. Leo XIII saw Italy's faith under attack by secularist movements, secret societies, and what he calls "the sects." The letter is part of his wider campaign to promote the Rosary as a weapon of spiritual warfare and public renewal.

## Key Themes

- **The Rosary as spiritual weapon** — Leo presents devotion to Mary under the title Queen of the Rosary as the Church's surest recourse in times of crisis, invoking historical victories (e.g., over the Albigenses) as precedents.
- **Italy's special vocation** — Because God chose Italy as the seat of the papacy, the Italian people are called to a particular closeness to the Holy Father and a corresponding responsibility to defend the faith.
- **Attack on faith and morals** — The pope laments that faith and Christian morality are undermined "artfully and in covert ways, or even openly," especially in Rome, by anti-Christian forces.
- **Marian intercession** — Mary, "in whose hands are all graces," is invoked as a merciful mother whose heart can be moved by persistent prayer ("loving violence to her mother's heart").
- **Liturgical promotion** — Leo elevated the Feast of the Rosary to a double of the second class for the universal Church, cementing its importance.
- **October devotion** — The month of October is reaffirmed as a special period of daily Marian prayer for the whole Church.

## Significance

*Vi E Ben Noto* is one of several Rosary encyclicals by Leo XIII that helped shape modern Catholic Marian devotion. By linking the Rosary to the pressing political and social issues of his day — the loss of papal temporal power, the rise of secularism, and the unification of Italy — Leo modeled how popular piety could serve as a form of public witness and spiritual resistance. The letter reinforced the October Rosary tradition that remains a fixture of Catholic practice and contributed to the theological development of Mary as Mediatrix of grace and Queen of the Rosary.
