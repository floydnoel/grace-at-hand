## Summary

*Charitas* is a papal brief issued by Pope Pius VI on April 13, 1791, condemning the Civil Constitution of the Clergy enacted by the French Revolutionary government. The document declares the Constitution heretical, sacrilegious, and schismatic, and warns that anyone who swears the required civil oath, participates in the election or consecration of bishops under its provisions, or accepts appointment through its procedures is suspended from office and cut off from communion with the Church.

## Historical Context

The document was written during the French Revolution, after the National Assembly had seized control of Church governance through the Civil Constitution of the Clergy (1790). This law required clergy to swear an oath of loyalty to the state, restructured dioceses, and made bishops and priests elective by local citizens — including non-Catholics and Jews. King Louis XVI reluctantly approved the measure under pressure. By early 1791, several bishops had taken the oath, and on February 24, 1791, the first "constitutional" bishops were consecrated without papal mandate, formalizing a schism between the "jurors" (those who swore the oath) and the "non-jurors" (those who refused). Pius VI had previously pleaded with the king and consulted French bishops before issuing this definitive condemnation.

## Key Themes

- **Primacy of the Apostolic See** — No bishop may be lawfully consecrated without mandate from the Pope; the right to appoint bishops resides in the Holy See alone.
- **Heresy and schism** — The Civil Constitution is not merely a political arrangement but is "composed of principles derived from heresy" and overturns the divinely instituted hierarchy of the Church.
- **Illegitimacy of lay-controlled elections** — Entrusting episcopal elections to laity, heretics, unbelievers, and Jews violates immemorial Church practice and the canons of ecumenical councils.
- **The oath as perjury** — Swearing to observe the Constitution is itself a grave sin that incurs automatic suspension; all subsequent acts (consecrations, appointments, sacraments) are null and void.
- **Unity with the visible head** — No one can be in the Church of Christ without being in unity with the See of Peter; the constitutional clergy have cut themselves off.
- **Pastoral firmness with mercy** — The Pope imposes canonical penalties but leaves a 40-day window for retraction; he pleads with offenders to repent before incurring heavier sanctions.

## Significance

*Charitas* is the definitive papal response to the French Revolution's assault on the Church's institutional independence. It formalized the rift between the Revolutionary state and Catholicism, forcing every French cleric to choose between loyalty to the Pope and loyalty to the Revolution. The document's condemnation of the Civil Constitution of the Clergy contributed to the de-Christianization campaign that followed and deepened the counter-revolutionary movement in regions like the Vendée. It stands as a landmark assertion of papal authority over episcopal appointments and Church governance, anticipating later ultramontane teaching and the broader 19th-century conflict between the Church and secular liberalism.
