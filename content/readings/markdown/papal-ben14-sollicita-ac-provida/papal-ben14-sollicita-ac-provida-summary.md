# Sollicita ac provida · English editorial summary

## Summary

Benedict XIV regulates how the Roman Inquisition and Congregation of the Index were to examine books for prohibition, correction, or permission. Answering complaints that Catholic works were condemned hastily, he describes the successive work of qualified reviewers, consultors, cardinals, and the pope. A Catholic author's book ordinarily required another independent assessment before condemnation, with further review when judgments differed. For reputable Catholic authors whose works could be corrected, he provides for communicating objections before publishing a conditional prohibition and encourages a hearing or an appointed defender when the corrected work could benefit readers.

These safeguards have defined limits: the constitution maintains the legitimacy of prohibitions issued without hearing authors and allows immediate prohibition in clear cases of deliberate heretical teaching or encouragement of immorality. Reviewers must know their subject, read the entire work in context, interpret ambiguities fairly, and distinguish settled Catholic doctrine from permissible disputes between schools. Benedict also requires secrecy and condemns abusive theological controversy, presenting scholarly moderation and charity as obligations alongside concern for doctrinal truth.

## Key themes

- Successive expert review and papal oversight of book censorship
- Correction, author representation, and limits on procedural safeguards
- Contextual reading and freedom within legitimate theological disagreement
- Confidentiality and charity in scholarly controversy
