## Summary

*Sempiternus Rex Christus* is a 1951 encyclical of Pope Pius XII commemorating the 1500th anniversary of the Council of Chalcedon (451). The document defends the Chalcedonian Definition — that Christ is one person with two distinct, unconfused natures, divine and human — and uses the occasion to affirm the primacy of the Roman pontiff (as demonstrated by Pope St. Leo the Great's role at Chalcedon), condemn the ancient heresies of Eutyches (Monophysitism) and Nestorius, and extend an ecumenical invitation to the separated Eastern churches.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was issued on 8 September 1951, fifteen centuries after the Council of Chalcedon. In the Church, the memory of the great Christological controversies had fresh relevance: the Eastern churches (Coptic, Ethiopian, Syrian, Armenian) remained separated from Rome largely over the phrasing of the one-nature/two-natures question, which the encyclical treats as largely a terminological misunderstanding traceable to Cyril of Alexandria's ambiguous use of "nature" to mean "person." In the wider world, the encyclical was written only six years after the end of World War II; Pius XII references the recent war's devastation, the persecution of Christians under hostile regimes, and the urgent need for Christian unity against "the confederated ranks" of secular and godless forces.

## Key Themes

- **Chalcedonian Christology:** The definitive teaching of two distinct natures (divine and human) in the one person of Christ, without confusion, change, division, or separation.
- **Primacy of the Roman See:** Leo the Great's delegates presided at Chalcedon; his *Tome to Flavian* was accepted as the rule of faith ("Peter has spoken through Leo"). The encyclical also recounts Leo's annulment of the "Robbers' Council" of Ephesus (449) and his rejection of Canon 28 (which would have elevated Constantinople).
- **Rejection of Eutyches (Monophysitism) and Nestorius:** These are the two poles of Christological error — either absorbing the human into the divine or dividing Christ into two persons — both refuted by the Chalcedonian formula.
- **Kenoticism as a modern error:** The encyclical condemns a then-contemporary "kenotic" doctrine that imagines the divinity was stripped from the Word in the Incarnation.
- **Ecumenical appeal to Eastern Christians:** The encyclical argues that the Monophysite separation is largely a semantic misunderstanding, and it urges the return of the Oriental Orthodox churches to communion with Rome, promising respect for their rites and traditions.
- **Marian and moral dimensions:** Mary is affirmed as *Theotokos* (Mother of God); the faithful are called to live out the Incarnation in holiness.

## Significance

*Sempiternus Rex Christus* is one of the major Christological encyclicals of the 20th century, standing alongside Pius XI's *Lux Veritatis* (on Ephesus) and *Quas Primas* (on Christ the King). It binds together doctrinal precision, papal authority, and ecumenical outreach in a single document. It remains a key reference for Catholic teaching on the hypostatic union and for understanding the Church's approach to the Oriental Orthodox churches in the mid-20th century. Its patient treatment of the semantic root of the Monophysite schism foreshadows the more irenic, dialogue-based ecumenism of the Second Vatican Council.
