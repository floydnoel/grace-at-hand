## Summary

*In Amplissimo* (1902) is Pope Leo XIII's letter of gratitude and encouragement to the Catholic Church in the United States, written near the end of his twenty-fifth year as pope. He praises the American bishops for their zeal in fostering unity with the Holy See, expanding Catholic schools and seminaries, evangelizing non-Catholics through public missions, and ministering to African Americans and Native Americans. He also thanks American Catholics for their financial support of the Vatican.

## Historical Context

Leo XIII wrote this encyclical in 1902, the twenty-fifth anniversary of his pontificate. The late 19th and early 20th centuries saw the U.S. Catholic Church growing rapidly through immigration, while simultaneously navigating a Protestant-majority culture that afforded religious liberty but no special legal privileges. In Europe, the pope was witnessing the secularization of traditionally Catholic nations — a contrast he explicitly notes. The letter reflects the Church's *Americanist* moment, when Leo was cautiously encouraging the U.S. hierarchy's energetic, voluntary approach to evangelization and institution-building.

## Key Themes

- **Unity with Rome** — Leo praises the American bishops for their loyalty to the Apostolic See, calling it "the apex and centre of government, of teaching and of the priesthood."
- **Religious liberty as opportunity** — The pope notes that while U.S. law shows "no special favor" to the Church, it grants "just liberty," and Catholics should use this favorable moment energetically.
- **Education** — He commends the bishops' work in establishing Catholic schools, academies, and seminaries, in line with the decrees of the Council of Baltimore.
- **Evangelization** — The encyclical applauds the appointment of traveling clergy to preach publicly to non-Catholics ("dissidents") and resolve their objections.
- **Care for the marginalized** — Leo highlights work among African Americans and Native Americans as a sign of the bishops' charity.
- **Generosity to the Holy See** — American financial contributions to the Vatican are praised as "an exercise and a testimony of your faith."

## Significance

*In Amplissimo* stands as Leo XIII's formal endorsement of the distinctive American Catholic model: a Church thriving without state establishment, relying on voluntary associations, schools, and energetic evangelism. It anticipates later 20th-century teachings on religious freedom and the proper relationship between Church and state. The encyclical is also a valuable primary source for understanding the state of U.S. Catholicism at the turn of the 20th century and the Vatican's warm regard for the American episcopate.
