# I Gloriosi Nostri · English editorial summary

## Summary

Leo XII reorganizes Rome's charitable institutions for poor girls following an apostolic visitation intended to correct abuses and restore their founding purposes. A permanent deputation receives unified responsibility for spiritual discipline, administration, income, expenditure, and productive work, while local deputies oversee individual houses. Several institutions are brought into a common system, with distinct arrangements for specified foundations and a separate fee-paying educational house.

The regulations reserve ordinary admission to poor, healthy, Rome-born girls aged six to twelve, giving priority to orphans, and abolish contributions formerly required from supported pupils. The institutions must provide maintenance, clothing, religious and domestic education, and training in a trade. The decree envisages marriage, religious life, teaching, domestic service, or return to relatives as possible departures, with dowries and savings from a share of the girls' work proceeds assisting their placement.

The act also establishes rules for work, illness, burial, inheritance, and changes in family means. It sets an initial supported population of about seven hundred, pools assets and accounting, and provides a fixed annual treasury subsidy. Profits from productive activity are first to strengthen that activity and later contribute to maintenance. A general regulation is to complete the reorganization in light of experience.

## Key themes

- Unified oversight of Rome’s charitable institutions for girls
- Historical admission conditions and abolition of entry contributions
- Religious education, trade training, dowries, and placement
- Pooled finances, productive work, and public subsidy
