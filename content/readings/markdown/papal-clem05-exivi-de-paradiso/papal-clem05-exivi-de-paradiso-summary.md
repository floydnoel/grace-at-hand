## Summary

*Exivi de paradiso* (1312) is a papal constitution issued by Pope Clement V at the Council of Vienne, providing an authoritative clarification of the Rule of the Friars Minor (the Franciscans). It resolves widespread doubts among the friars about which parts of the Rule bind under obedience, which evangelical counsels are obligatory, the proper interpretation of Franciscan poverty (especially regarding money, property, inheritances, and the use of goods), and the procedures for electing provincial ministers.

## Historical Context

The document was issued during the Council of Vienne (1311–1312), a major ecumenical council of the Catholic Church convened by Clement V. The Franciscan Order was internally divided between the "Spirituals," who insisted on the strictest possible observance of St. Francis's ideal of absolute poverty, and the "Conventuals," who favored a more moderate interpretation adapted to the Order's institutional needs. Earlier popes (Nicholas III, Boniface VIII) had issued clarifications, but these had not quieted consciences — controversies continued in consistories and in daily life over money, property use, inheritance, and governance. Clement V, who professed a lifelong devotion to the Franciscans, sought to settle these disputes definitively.

## Key Themes

- **Obligation and counsels** — Friars are bound by vow only to the three evangelical counsels (obedience, poverty, chastity) and to those things expressed in the Rule under preceptive or equipollent words; they are not bound to every evangelical counsel.
- **Poverty and use** — The friars are obliged to the "poor or strict use" of things as contained in the Rule, though holding that poor use is a matter of heresy is declared "presumptuous and temerarious," not heretical.
- **Money** — The absolute prohibition on receiving or handling money is reaffirmed; recourse to spiritual friends for necessities is restricted to the sick, clothing, and urgent needs when alms have failed; alms boxes and money collections in churches are forbidden.
- **Property and dominion** — The Roman Pontiff receives dominion over all goods granted to the Order; the friars have only simple factual use; they may not inherit, receive annual revenues, hold vineyards or gardens for sale, or maintain granaries and cellars except in clear necessity.
- **Governance** — Detailed rules are given for the election of provincial ministers (by provincial chapters, confirmed by the minister general), with special provisions for distant provinces (Ireland, Greece, Romania).

## Significance

*Exivi de paradiso* is a landmark document in Franciscan history, forming — along with *Exiit qui seminat* (Nicholas III) and later *Quia vir reprobus* (John XXII) — the core of the papal legislation on the Franciscan Rule. It attempted to steer a middle course between rigorist and lenient interpretations, affirming the特殊性 of Franciscan poverty while rejecting extreme positions. Its clarifications on the binding force of the Rule's language and on the distinction between dominion and use became foundational for subsequent canon law on religious vows. The document's failure to fully satisfy either faction, however, foreshadowed the deeper conflicts that would erupt later in the 14th century under John XXII.
