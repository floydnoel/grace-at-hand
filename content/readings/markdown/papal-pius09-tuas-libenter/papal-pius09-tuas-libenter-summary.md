# Tuas libenter · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius IX responds to the Munich gathering of Catholic scholars, welcoming its declared aim of defending Catholic doctrine while questioning its initial organization by private persons without hierarchical authorization. After learning of the archbishop’s permission and the participants’ professions of fidelity, he expresses hope for its usefulness. He defends established theological learning and rejects claims that supernatural mysteries can be comprehended or demonstrated entirely by natural reason.

The letter carefully extends the scope of scholarly obedience beyond solemnly defined dogmas. Truths taught as divinely revealed by the ordinary magisterium of the Church throughout the world also require divine faith. Pius then identifies further obligations for Catholic scholars: adherence to doctrinal decisions of pontifical congregations and to theological truths and certain conclusions held by common Catholic consent. Opinions opposing this latter teaching may be censurable without being heretical; these categories are not simply equated with solemn dogmatic definitions.

Pius urges the archbishop to explain these requirements to the participants and promote scholarship that respects both reason’s proper principles and revelation’s authority. Scientific and theological progress, he maintains, should deepen knowledge of nature and grace while remaining in communion with the Church’s teaching office.

## Key themes

- Hierarchical oversight and the legitimate aims of Catholic scholarship
- Divine faith in revealed teaching beyond solemn definitions
- Further doctrinal assent distinguished from dogmatic definition
- Reason’s contribution and the transcendence of revealed mysteries
