# Avendoci ella · English editorial summary

## Summary

Responding to Cardinal Tommaso Bernetti's account of excessive workload and ill health, Gregory XVI separates the management of internal state affairs from the existing Secretariat of State. He approves this division after consultation with a commission of cardinals, presenting it as an administrative adjustment within the established forms of papal government.

The secretary of state retains diplomatic correspondence, relations with nuncios and consuls, matters linking domestic and foreign affairs, high policing, passports, troop movements, and newspaper censorship. A new cardinal secretary for internal state affairs is to supervise domestic administration, provincial authorities, ministries, military discipline and administration, and specified congregations. The document also assigns the two secretariats to the Quirinal and Vatican palaces and directs Bernetti to propose their staffing and arrangements. Its detailed allocation of functions aims at more orderly, prompt, and considered public administration.

## Key themes

- Division of diplomatic and domestic responsibilities
- Allocation of policing, military, and censorship functions
- Staffing and locations of the two secretariats
