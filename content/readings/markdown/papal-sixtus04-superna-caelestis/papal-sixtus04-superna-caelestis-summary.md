## Summary

This is the apostolic bull *Superna Caelestis*, issued by Pope Sixtus IV on April 14, 1482, canonizing St. Bonaventure of Bagnoregio (the "Seraphic Doctor") and enrolling him in the catalogue of saints. The document recounts Bonaventure's life, virtues, doctrinal writings, miracles, and the rigorous canonical process that preceded the canonization.

## Historical Context

The bull was issued in the late 15th century, a period of intense theological development and ecclesiastical reform. Bonaventure (1221–1274) had been a key figure at the University of Paris, Minister General of the Franciscan Order, and Cardinal-Bishop of Alba. His canonization was sought by Emperor Frederick III, Kings Louis XI of France, Ferdinand I of Naples, and Matthias Corvinus of Hungary, along with numerous Italian city-states. Sixtus IV, himself a former Franciscan Minister General, had a personal devotion to Bonaventure and took extraordinary care in the process, ordering the investigation of miracles to be conducted twice to ensure canonical rigor.

## Key Themes

- **The criteria for canonization**: the bull emphasizes threefold testimony — the power of miracles (attributed to the Father), the wisdom of doctrine (attributed to the Son), and the holiness of life (attributed to the Holy Spirit)
- **Bonaventure as Doctor of the Church**: his writings are described as inspired by the Holy Spirit, and he is granted liturgical honors as a Confessor, Pontiff, and Doctor
- **Franciscan identity**: Sixtus IV highlights his own Franciscan formation and Bonaventure's role in organizing the Order into provinces and custodies
- **Parallel with St. Thomas Aquinas**: the bull notes the fraternal charity between Bonaventure and Thomas and grants equal liturgical honors
- **Indulgences**: plenary and partial indulgences are granted for visiting the saint's church and for attending Franciscan liturgies
- **Papal authority and due diligence**: the lengthy process, secret consistory, public consistory, triduum of prayer and fasting, and consultation of prelates all underscore the solemnity of canonization

## Significance

*Superna Caelestis* is the formal act by which St. Bonaventure was inscribed in the Roman Martyrology and declared a saint for universal veneration. It is notable for the theological framework it employs — treating Bonaventure's miracles, doctrine, and life as a trinitarian witness — and for establishing the feast of St. Bonaventure on the second Sunday of July. The bull also reflects the close bond between the Franciscan Order and the papacy in the late Middle Ages. Bonaventure would later be declared a Doctor of the Church by Pope Sixtus V in 1588.
