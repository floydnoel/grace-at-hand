## Summary

*Laudato si'* ("Praise Be to You") is Pope Francis's 2015 encyclical on care for creation, addressed not only to Catholics but to every person on the planet. It diagnoses the ecological crisis as a symptom of deeper moral and spiritual failures — a "throwaway culture," unchecked technocratic power, and a loss of the sense that all creatures are interconnected. Francis calls for an "integral ecology" that unites concern for the environment with justice for the poor, and he insists that technological fixes alone are insufficient without a profound conversion of hearts and lifestyles.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was published on 24 May 2015, during a period of accelerating climate change, biodiversity loss, and rising inequality. Pope Francis, the first pontiff from the Global South, drew on the scientific consensus (especially around anthropogenic global warming) and on the teachings of his predecessors — John XXIII (*Pacem in Terris*), Paul VI, John Paul II, and Benedict XVI — as well as the reflections of the Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew. It was also shaped by the lead-up to the 2015 Paris Climate Agreement (COP21) and by the concerns of bishops' conferences from developing nations, particularly in Latin America, Africa, and Asia.

## Key Themes

- **Integral ecology** — environmental degradation, social injustice, and spiritual poverty are interconnected and must be addressed together; "everything is connected."
- **Critique of the technocratic paradigm** — the reduction of all reality to an object for manipulation by technology and markets is a root cause of the crisis.
- **Option for the poor** — the worst effects of ecological damage fall on the world's poorest and most vulnerable people; "the cry of the earth and the cry of the poor" are one.
- **Throwaway culture** — consumerism, waste, and the treatment of both people and things as disposable are symptoms of a deeper moral disorder.
- **Common destination of goods** — private property is subordinate to the universal destination of the earth's goods; the earth is a shared inheritance for all.
- **Ecological conversion** — authentic change requires a spiritual transformation: gratitude, sobriety, humility, and a contemplative openness to creation as a gift from God.
- **Dialogue** — the encyclical appeals for a new global dialogue involving science, politics, culture, and religion, rejecting both blind technological optimism and anti-human environmentalism.

## Significance

*Laudato si'* is the first papal encyclical devoted entirely to ecology, firmly embedding environmental concern within Catholic social teaching. It extended the Church's moral framework to encompass the non-human world while insisting that care for creation cannot be separated from care for the poor. It was widely cited in climate policy debates and helped shape the 2015 Paris Agreement. The encyclical's influence extends beyond Catholicism, as it was explicitly addressed to "every person living on this planet" and has been taken up by secular environmental movements, other Christian denominations, and interfaith initiatives on climate justice.
