## Summary

*Lux Veritatis* ("The Light of Truth") is a 1931 encyclical by Pope Pius XI commemorating the 1,500th anniversary of the Council of Ephesus (431 AD). The document defends the Council's condemnation of Nestorianism, affirms the hypostatic union (one divine person in Christ), proclaims Mary as *Theotokos* (Mother of God), and asserts the supreme authority of the Roman Pontiff over the universal Church — all three dogmas the Council upheld.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was issued on Christmas Day 1931, the tenth year of Pius XI's pontificate. The Council of Ephesus had been convoked in 431 AD to address the heresy of Nestorius, Patriarch of Constantinople, who taught that Christ's divine and human natures were only morally united (not hypostatically), which logically denied Mary's divine maternity. Pius XI wrote amid a modern context of rising secularism, ecumenical dialogue with separated Eastern churches, and a perceived crisis of faith and family life — themes he had already addressed in *Mortalium animos* (1928), *Casti connubii* (1930), and *Divini illius Magistri* (1929). He also faced attempts by some contemporary historians to rehabilitate Nestorius and to argue that the Council of Ephesus, rather than Pope Celestine I, had the final authority.

## Key Themes

- **Hypostatic Union:** Christ is one divine person with two natures, human and divine, united substantially — not merely morally or by "indwelling."
- **Mary as Mother of God (*Theotokos*):** Because the person born of Mary is God, she is truly the Mother of God, a source of her unique dignity and intercessory power.
- **Papal Primacy:** Pope Celestine I had already condemned Nestorius before the Council; the Council Fathers themselves executed that sentence, acknowledging they were "compelled" by his judgment. The encyclical insists this demonstrates the Pope's supreme, infallible teaching authority over the whole Church.
- **Unity of the Church:** The hypostatic union is presented as the mystical model for Church unity — "one body, compacted and fitly joined together" under one head, the successor of Peter.
- **Marian Devotion and the Family:** Mary as Mother of all Christians, the model for mothers and families; the encyclical links devotion to Mary with renewal of Christian family life, especially against contraception and divorce (themes from *Casti connubii*).
- **Call to Separated Brethren:** A pastoral appeal to Eastern Christians and Protestants to return to the one fold under the successor of Peter, with Mary as a unifying figure.

## Significance

*Lux Veritatis* solidified the Church's interpretation of the Council of Ephesus as a decisive demonstration of papal supremacy, not merely a conciliar judgment. It rejected revisionist historical claims that diminished papal authority and reaffirmed Ephesus as an ecumenical council whose authority derived from its unity with Rome. The encyclical also introduced a new liturgical feast — the Divine Maternity of Mary — which was to be celebrated by the universal Church, a lasting liturgical contribution. Theologically, it remains a key reference for Catholic teaching on Christology, Mariology, and the relationship between ecumenical councils and papal authority.
