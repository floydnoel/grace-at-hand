## Summary

*Divini Redemptoris* — "On Atheistic Communism" — is Pope Pius XI's 1937 encyclical condemning bolshevistic and atheistic Communism as a mortal threat to Christian civilization. It systematically exposes the errors of dialectical materialism, affirms the Church's social teaching on the dignity of the human person, private property, the family, and the state, and calls for a spiritual, pastoral, and practical response to halt Communism's global spread.

## Historical Context

Written on 19 March 1937, the encyclical responds to the consolidation of the Soviet Union under Stalin's terror, the violent persecution of the Church in Mexico, and the outbreak of the Spanish Civil War (1936–39), in which Republican and Communist forces massacred thousands of clergy and destroyed churches. The Great Depression had destabilised economies worldwide, making Communist propaganda more attractive to the working class. Pius XI had already condemned Communism repeatedly (e.g., in *Quadragesimo Anno*, 1931) but judged that the crisis now demanded a dedicated encyclical.

## Key Themes

- **Denunciation of atheistic Communism** — Communism is "intrinsically wrong," based on dialectical materialism that denies God, the soul, and any spiritual reality; it reduces man to a cog in an economic machine and uses class warfare and terror as instruments of progress.
- **Defence of the human person** — Man is created in God's image with a spiritual and immortal soul; he possesses God-given rights to life, property, association, and religious practice that no state may abolish.
- **The family and marriage** — Matrimony is of divine origin and indissoluble; Communism's attack on the family, its emancipation of women from the home, and its denial of parental educational rights are contrary to natural law.
- **Private property and social justice** — Private property is a natural right, but its use carries social obligations; wages must support the worker and his family, and the state must foster economic conditions that serve the common good — neither liberal individualism nor collectivist terrorism is acceptable.
- **The state's role** — Authority derives from God, not the collectivity; the state must protect rights, not absorb them. Communism's claim that the state will "wither away" is a fantasy; in practice it becomes a terrorist instrument.
- **Spiritual and pastoral remedies** — Renewal of personal holiness, detachment from material goods, the precept of Christian charity, and a crusade of prayer and penance are the deepest remedies; priests are urged to "go to the workingman," and Catholic Action is called to lead a lay apostolate.
- **No collaboration with Communism** — Paragraph 58 is emphatic: "Communism is intrinsically wrong, and no one who would save Christian civilization may collaborate with it in any undertaking whatsoever."

## Significance

*Divini Redemptoris* is one of the most forceful social encyclicals of the 20th century, completing Pius XI's trilogy of social teaching alongside *Quadragesimo Anno* (economic order) and *Casti Connubii* (marriage). It shaped Catholic anti-Communist identity for decades, was cited frequently during the Cold War, and remains the Church's most comprehensive doctrinal rejection of Marxist-Leninist ideology. Its insistence that the root of the crisis is spiritual — not merely economic or political — and its call for a united front of all believers against atheism have had lasting influence on Catholic social thought.
