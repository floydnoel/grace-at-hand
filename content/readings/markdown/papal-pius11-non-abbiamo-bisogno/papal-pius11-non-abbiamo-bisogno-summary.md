## Summary

*Non Abbiamo Bisogno* ("We Do Not Need") is a papal encyclical written by Pope Pius XI in 1931 to denounce the Fascist regime in Italy for its violent suppression of Catholic Action — especially its youth organizations — and to defend the Church's right to educate young people in the faith, rejecting the regime's claim of total control over youth formation.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was published on June 29, 1931, following months of escalating tension between Mussolini's Fascist government and the Holy See. After the Lateran Treaties of 1929 had normalized relations, the regime soon began consolidating power, demanding total loyalty from all citizens — especially the young — through its own party youth organizations. In May 1931, the government ordered the police to dissolve hundreds of Catholic Action youth circles, accusing them of engaging in political activity. Associations were raided, property seized, and members harassed, often violently, with little police protection. This encyclical was Pius XI's response to what he called "a real and true persecution."

## Key Themes

- **The nature of Catholic Action** — It is "the participation and collaboration of the laity with the Apostolic Hierarchy," strictly religious and non-political, by definition and by papal directive.
- **The right of the Church to educate youth** — The Church has a divine mandate, rooted in Christ's command, to form young people in the faith; this cannot be superseded or monopolized by the State.
- **Condemnation of "Statolatry"** — The Fascist ideology demanding total allegiance of the young is a form of pagan state-worship irreconcilable with Catholicism.
- **The oath to the Fascist party** — Any oath that requires service to a cause that disregards God's law and the rights of the Church is "unlawful" for Catholics.
- **Defense against political accusations** — The Pope refutes claims that Catholic Action was political, producing evidence of its purely religious character and warning that the real target was not politics but the Church's hold on the young.
- **The unity of bishops and faithful** — Pius XI thanks the bishops for their solidarity and underscores that they are appointed by the Holy Spirit, not servants of the State.

## Significance

*Non Abbiamo Bisogno* is one of the boldest papal documents of the twentieth century, offering a direct and public confrontation with a totalitarian regime. It was the first time a pope explicitly condemned Fascist ideology as incompatible with Catholicism, drawing a clear line on the Church's exclusive rights over youth education and religious formation. While it stopped short of excommunicating Mussolini or condemning the Fascist Party as such, it set the theological and moral framework for later Catholic resistance to totalitarianism, influenced subsequent papal teaching on religious freedom and the rights of the family, and remains a landmark in the Church's witness against state overreach.
