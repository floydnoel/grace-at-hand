## Summary

*Iamdudum* (1911) is an encyclical of Pope Pius X condemning the Portuguese Republic’s Law of Separation of Church and State. The Pope denounces the law as a systematic assault on the Catholic Church — stripping it of property, banning religious orders, suppressing clerical authority over worship, prohibiting public expressions of faith, and subjecting ecclesiastical affairs to state control. He declares the law null and void, praises the Portuguese bishops and clergy for their resistance, and calls them to unity and fidelity to the Holy See.

## Historical Context

Portugal’s monarchy fell in the republican revolution of October 1910. The new government, driven by anticlericalism, quickly enacted measures targeting the Church: expelling religious orders, closing convents, banning religious instruction in schools, introducing divorce, and suppressing feast days. The Law of Separation, promulgated on 20 April 1911, was the culmination of this campaign. Pius X, who had already condemned similar secularization in France (the 1905 French Law of Separation), saw the Portuguese law as an even more aggressive attack on the Church’s liberty and divine constitution.

## Key Themes

- **Denial of the social kingship of Christ** — The Republic’s claim to have “no religion” is condemned as absurd, since both individuals and nations depend on God.
- **Defence of Church property rights** — The law expropriates all Church holdings, forbids future acquisition, and even diverts a third of voluntary offerings to civil purposes.
- **Rejection of lay-dominated worship** — The law entrusts the organization of public worship to civil associations and excludes clergy from any role in their administration.
- **State usurpation of ecclesiastical authority** — The Republic claims power to regulate clerical training, ban bishops’ pronouncements, prohibit religious processions, and suppress clerical dress.
- **Attack on Catholic unity** — The law blocks publication of papal decrees without state permission and discourages clergy from studying in Rome, aiming to sever the Portuguese Church from the Holy See.
- **Nullity of unjust civil laws** — Pius X invokes his apostolic authority to declare the law null and void insofar as it violates the Church’s divine rights.

## Significance

*Iamdudum* is a forceful assertion of the Church’s spiritual sovereignty against a modern secular state. It belongs to a series of early-20th-century papal documents (e.g., *Vehementer Nos* on France, *Gravissimo Officii Munere*) that resist the Laïcité model of aggressive state secularism. The encyclical is often cited in Catholic social teaching on religious liberty, Church–State relations, and the right of the Church to govern her own affairs without civil interference. It also strengthened the resolve of Portuguese Catholics during the anti-clerical First Republic, which lasted until 1926.
