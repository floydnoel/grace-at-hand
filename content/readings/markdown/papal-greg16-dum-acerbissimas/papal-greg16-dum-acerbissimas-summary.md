# Dum acerbissimas · English editorial summary

## Summary

Gregory XVI condemns specified theological works of Georg Hermes after an extended examination by theologians and cardinals. He places the case within his concern that religious writers can undermine Catholic doctrine while appearing to defend it. The central criticism is that Hermes makes positive doubt the starting point of theology and treats reason as the principal norm and sole means for attaining knowledge of supernatural truths.

The brief describes the review process, including examination by German-speaking theologians, translation and annotation of relevant passages, further theological scrutiny, and consideration by the cardinals of the Inquisition. It identifies disputed teachings concerning faith and its sources, divine attributes, grace, original sin, and fallen humanity. Acting on the resulting judgment, Gregory orders the named works entered in the Index of Forbidden Books in every language and edition. He asks bishops and other ordinaries to remove them from schools and protect the faithful from the doctrines he condemns.

## Key themes

- Positive doubt and reason as contested foundations of theology
- Several stages of doctrinal examination
- Historical prohibition of Hermes’s specified works in schools and publications
