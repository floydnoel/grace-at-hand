# Ad gravissimas · English editorial summary

## Summary

Writing to the archbishop of Malta, Gregory XVI expresses alarm at reports that clandestine societies are recruiting members, especially young people. He portrays their secret meetings, promises of pleasure or intellectual distinction, and opposition to religious and civil authority as grave dangers to Catholic faith and morals. He recalls earlier papal condemnations, particularly Leo XII's renewal of them, and his own first encyclical.

The pope urges the archbishop to respond through pastoral letters, public warnings, and the assistance of parish priests. He directs him to explain the societies' alleged dangers and the ecclesiastical penalties then attached to membership or support. Alongside prevention, Gregory insists on patient pastoral care for those already involved: paternal admonition, counsel, and instruction should seek their return to Christ. The letter presents vigilance and the recovery of those led astray as inseparable duties of episcopal responsibility.

## Key themes

- Secret societies and the recruitment of Maltese youth
- Earlier papal prohibitions and historical ecclesiastical penalties
- Parish cooperation in warning and instructing the faithful
- Patient pastoral efforts to recover society members
