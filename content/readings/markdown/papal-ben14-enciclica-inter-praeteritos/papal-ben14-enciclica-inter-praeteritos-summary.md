## Summary

*Inter Praeteritos* (December 3, 1749) is an encyclical by Pope Benedict XIV addressed to the penitentiaries and confessors of Rome, providing a detailed theological and canonical instruction for the proper administration of the Holy Year Jubilee of 1750. The Pope walks through each of his three recent constitutions — *Peregrinantes*, *Cum Nos nuper*, and *Convocatis* — resolving longstanding controversies about confession, the suspension of indulgences, the commutation of vows, absolution of censures, and the practical duties of confessors, all with the aim of ensuring the faithful can validly obtain the Jubilee indulgence.

## Historical Context

Benedict XIV wrote in the run-up to the 1750 Holy Year, which would be the third Jubilee celebrated in Rome during his lifetime. The mid-18th-century Church was navigating post-Tridentine moral theology, growing jurisdictional complexity (especially around regular clergy privileges), and an increasingly detailed casuistic tradition. Benedict, a renowned canon lawyer and one of the most learned popes of the era, sought to cut through centuries of academic dispute by codifying clear rules in his constitutions. The document also reflects the practical challenge of managing a massive influx of pilgrims to Rome and the need to equip confessors with unambiguous faculties.

## Key Themes

- **Actual confession required for the Jubilee** — Benedict confirms that sacramental confession *in re* (not merely *in voto*) is necessary, settling a debate reaching back to Boniface VIII.
- **Definition of "day" for basilica visits** — The Pope allows visits to count either from midnight to midnight or from First Vespers to the following evening twilight, ending a long controversy.
- **Suspension of indulgences and faculties** — During the Holy Year, all plenary and partial indulgences are suspended unless expressly preserved; the same applies to faculties to absolve reserved cases, including those granted for reasons unrelated to indulgences.
- **Pastoral care for the dying and the impeded** — Provisions are made for foreigners who cannot complete their visits, those who die en route, and those prevented by illness or poverty, following precedent from Clement VI and others.
- **Complicit confessor rule** — Benedict reaffirms his own constitution *Sacramentum Poenitentiae*, which nullifies absolution given by a priest who is an accomplice in a sin against the sixth commandment, even during a Jubilee.
- **Vows and oaths** — Penitentiaries are granted the faculty to commute all simple vows (even those reserved to the Holy See) into other pious works, including vows confirmed by oath.
- **Heresy absolution** — Following Alexander VII's decree, Benedict insists that the faculty to absolve from heresy must be *expressly* granted; general language does not suffice.
- **Repeatability of the Jubilee** — Benedict allows the Jubilee indulgence to be gained multiple times during the year by repeating the prescribed works.
- **State of grace during visits** — Visits made while in mortal sin are accepted, provided the final act by which the indulgence is obtained is performed in a state of grace.

## Significance

*Inter Praeteritos* is a masterclass in 18th-century papal governance: a pope-legist resolving centuries of scholastic and canonical debate through precise legislative drafting. It demonstrates Benedict XIV's characteristic method — reviewing the full tradition of authors (from St. Thomas and St. Antoninus to contemporaries like Viva and Passerino), identifying the root of each dispute, and issuing a definitive ruling. The encyclical remains a valuable case study in how the Holy See balanced doctrinal rigor with pastoral flexibility, and it cemented Benedict's reputation as a reformer who brought clarity to the Church's penitential discipline during Jubilee years.
