## Summary

*Rite Expiatis* is a 1926 encyclical by Pope Pius XI commemorating the seventh centenary of the death of St. Francis of Assisi. It presents St. Francis not as a romanticized or secular figure, but as a true imitator of Christ whose poverty, humility, obedience, and apostolic zeal were providentially deployed to reform a corrupt and war-torn medieval society, and whose example remains urgently relevant for the modern Church.

## Historical Context

Written in 1926, the encyclical coincides with the Holy Year jubilee and the 700th anniversary of St. Francis's death (1226). Pius XI's predecessor had recently named Francis the patron of "Catholic Action," a lay apostolate movement. The world was still recovering from World War I; Europe faced political instability, secularism, and social upheaval. Pius XI draws explicit parallels between the 13th-century crises—heresy, class conflict, clerical laxity, grasping wealth—and the 20th-century situation, presenting Francis as a model for a new evangelization.

## Key Themes

- **Evangelical poverty and humility** — Francis embraced Lady Poverty and Holy Humility as the foundation of Christian perfection, directly countering the greed and pride of his age (and ours).
- **Obedience to the Church** — The encyclical vehemently rejects any portrayal of Francis as a proto-rebel or proto-Protestant; he sought papal approval for his Rule, insisted on submission to bishops and the Holy See, and required his friars to be "truly Catholic and apostolic."
- **Total conformity to Christ** — Francis is described as "another Jesus Christ"; his stigmata, his recourse to the Gospels as an oracle, and his life of penance all reflect a radical imitation of the Crucified.
- **Social reform through the Third Order** — The Franciscan Third Order is praised as a brilliant innovation: a lay religious community without vows that transformed civic life by forbidding arms, oaths of vassalage, and luxury, while protecting the poor and weak.
- **Rejection of secularized admiration** — Pius XI warns against admirers who praise Francis's poetry, love of nature, or patriotism while ignoring his penance, austerity, and obedience, insisting that true devotion means imitation.

## Significance

The encyclical firmly anchors St. Francis in Catholic orthodoxy against contemporary attempts to secularize or distort his legacy. It connects the Franciscan charism to the Church's social teaching and to the lay apostolate of Catholic Action, emphasizing that the saint's reforms were not merely personal but structural—rebuilding both the Church and civil society. *Rite Expiatis* remains a key reference for understanding how the magisterium interprets St. Francis as a model of evangelical renewal rather than a counter-cultural figure apart from the Church.
