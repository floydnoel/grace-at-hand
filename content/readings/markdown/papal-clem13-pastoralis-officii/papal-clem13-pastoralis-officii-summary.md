# Pastoralis officii · English editorial summary

## Summary

Responding to King Charles Emmanuel of Sardinia, Clement XIII revises ecclesiastical sanctuary privileges to address serious crime while preserving what he considers the Church’s rightful immunity. The brief excludes from asylum perpetrators of specified offenses: intentional homicide, with exceptions for accident and legitimate defense; deliberate arson; commissioned attacks involving actual violence and injury; and highway robbery, even without injury to the victim.

It also defines which buildings and adjoining spaces retain protection. Many rural chapels, private or fortress chapels, detached bell towers, commercial premises, and unenclosed land are excluded, while pastoral churches and certain clergy residences remain protected under stated conditions. Bishops must publish the changes and arrange the transfer of those already in places losing immunity, first securing the necessary assurances from secular authority.

## Key themes

- Specified serious crimes excluded from sanctuary
- Precise boundaries of protected churches and residences
- Episcopal implementation with safeguards for existing occupants
