# In Suprema Catholicae

**La Santa Sede**

---

> *Note: This document was translated from the original Italian into English. Translation provided by DeepL (July 2026).*

BRIEF OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV IN SUPREMA CATHOLICAE Pope Benedict XIV. To all Christians who will read this Letter, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. In the supreme governance of the Catholic Church, which Almighty God has entrusted to Us through the ineffable generosity of His Divine Goodness, We trusted that We would find no small aid to Our weakness in the general harmony among Christian Princes. Hence, Our spirit—almost prostrate and terrified by the magnitude and difficulty of such a great undertaking—had drawn great hope from the prospect of living in times when, the fires of war would be extinguished, the kingdoms united, the fear of enemies dispelled, and peace secured through Him who commands the winds and the sea, We might more easily fulfill the duties of Our pastoral office and the care of the Lord’s flock. But after a relatively smooth start to the office We had undertaken, the situation of Christendom gradually became increasingly bleak and tragic, to the point that, with all of Europe in flames, as if the torches of war had been lit everywhere, we are now tossed about by the most turbulent waves of the storm, and are nearly overwhelmed; so that We are compelled to denounce to you more openly, with groans and lamentations, alongside Our Predecessor Saint Gregory the Great, the end of a now-aging world and the approach of the day of wrath and vengeance. Indeed, We have learned that one kingdom rises up against another kingdom and one people against another people, even though they are all honored with the name of Christians; their aggression afflicts the lands, and what happens elsewhere We, shedding many tears, see taking place right here before Our very eyes. Various earthquakes in different places had previously served as a warning for us to flee the danger zone: fields and crops swept away by floods; a plague of the worst kind has decimated populations far and wide across various regions; fields abandoned by farmers; countless men killed by thirst and hunger. Cities emptied of their inhabitants; streets covered with corpses; unburied bodies piled upon other bodies, and blood spilled like water: clear signs of divine wrath appeared everywhere and even today reveal the hand of avenging justice; indeed, lest it seem that anything is lacking in these most severe calamities that have befallen Italy in particular, We ourselves live amidst the swords and almost fear that, once the Shepherd is struck down, the sheep will scatter. 1. But rightly recalling that the Lord, when He is angry, remembers His mercy and does not desire the death of the sinner but rather that he may be converted and live, bearing worthy fruits of penance, we have implored the Lord through private and public prayers to turn His indignation away from us; nevertheless, so that He may pour out the riches of His mercy more abundantly upon us who have recourse to the throne of Grace, following the ancient custom of the Roman Church, we have decreed to open the treasuries of heavenly graces and to dispense them generously so that, in this propitious time, when the humanity of Our God and Savior appears, an abundance of peace may arise in His days, and He, causing wars to vanish to the ends of the earth, may break the bow He has drawn, destroy the weapons, and burn the shields with fire. 2. Therefore, trusting in the mercy of Almighty God, and by the authority of His Holy Apostles Peter and Paul, and by that power to loose and bind which the Lord has granted to Us, though unworthy, to all faithful Christians of both sexes who dwell both in this Our Beloved City and throughout Italy and the adjacent islands, and to those who, in the aforementioned Our Beloved City, will devoutly participate in the solemn procession on the feast of Saint Andrew the Apostle—which will proceed from the aforementioned church of Saint’Andrea della Valle, known as the Church of the Theatine Clerics Regular, to the Church of the Blessed Virgin Mary above Minerva, of the Friars of the Order of Preachers, with the participation of the entire clergy and the people, We grant and bestow the fullest indulgence and remission of all sins, as in a Jubilee year. The indulgence is also granted for a period of two weeks, beginning on the feast of Saint Andrew and ending on the third Sunday of Advent, inclusive, to those who have visited at least once the churches or basilicas of St. John Lateran, the Prince of the Apostles, or St. Mary Major, and there have offered pious prayers to God for a certain time; and who, on the Wednesdays, Fridays, and Saturdays of one of these weeks, have fasted and then, after confessing their sins, have devoutly received the Sacrament of the Most Holy Eucharist on one of the aforementioned Sundays or on another day during these weeks, and have given alms to the poor as their own devotion dictates. Furthermore, to all those who live outside Rome, as we have previously stated, the plenary indulgence for their sins is likewise granted, if they visit the churches designated by the local bishops or by their vicars or officials, or by their mandate, or, in their absence, by those who minister to the souls there, once they have received notice of this letter of ours. This applies to those who, during the two weeks following the publication of the designation of these churches, visit one of them, pray there as described above, and on Wednesday, Friday, and Saturday, have fasted and also confessed their sins, and on the Sunday immediately following or on another day within the same week receive Holy Communion and give, as above, alms to the poor. We grant and bestow upon these persons a most plenary Indulgence and Remission of their sins, in accordance with this Letter, just as it is customary in the Jubilee Year to grant to those who visit the designated churches both within and outside the City. 3. We likewise grant that seafarers and those who are traveling, upon their return to their homes, may, under the aforementioned conditions, gain the Indulgence after visiting the parish or principal church of the place where they reside. As for members of religious orders of both sexes and cloistered monks and nuns, and all others, whether laypeople or clergy, whether secular or religious, as well as to those in prison, prisoners, and those unable—due to some infirmity or other impediment—to observe what has been stated above, we grant to confessors—who have already been approved for this purpose by the local Ordinaries prior to the publication of this document, or who are to be approved— the authority to substitute the aforementioned conditions with other works of piety and to prescribe those that the penitents themselves are able to perform. 4. Furthermore, to all Christian faithful of both sexes, whether laypeople or secular clergy, as well as to members of religious orders, congregations, or institutes of any kind, residing both in Rome and outside of it throughout Italy and the adjacent islands, we grant the authority and permission to choose any priest confessor, whether secular or belonging to any Order or Institute, provided that, as we have already stated, he is approved by the respective Ordinary of the various localities. The Confessor may absolve them in the forum of conscience from all ecclesiastical censures—whether de jure or ab homine—of excommunication or suspension, imposed for any cause, as well as from all sins, excesses, crimes, and offenses, even those that are grave and heinous; including those reserved to the local Ordinary or to Us, or to the Apostolic See, and even those that are customarily read in the letters of Holy Thursday. The Confessor may commute any vow (except vows of religious life and chastity) into other pious and salutary works after having imposed a salutary and appropriate penance, for any and all of the aforementioned cases, at the discretion of the Confessor himself. 5. Therefore, in accordance with this Letter and by virtue of Holy Obedience, we strictly order and command all our Venerable Brothers, Patriarchs, Archbishops, and other Ecclesiastical Prelates, as well as all Ordinaries residing in every place and their Vicars or Deputies, or, in their absence, to all those who exercise care of souls, that, as soon as this Letter is received—whether transmitted in copy or in printed form—it be made public immediately, without delay, hesitation, or impediment, in their Churches and Dioceses, throughout the Provinces, Cities, Towns, and Villages; and that they have it published and designate the church or churches to be visited for the purpose of obtaining the indulgence. 6. However, we do not intend, by means of this Letter, to grant a dispensation from public or hidden irregularities, incurred in any manner, whether through fault, incapacity, or inability; nor do we intend to grant any authority to dispense, rehabilitate, or restore to the original state, even if only in the forum of conscience. Nor may those who have been excommunicated, suspended a divinis, or interdicted by Us, by the Apostolic See, or by any other Prelate or ecclesiastical judge be included in the indulgence, even if their names have not been expressly mentioned, nor those who have incurred publicly denounced sentences or censures, unless within the aforementioned two-week period they have satisfied the requirements or reached an agreement with the parties. 7. Notwithstanding the Constitutions and Apostolic provisions, according to which the faculty to absolve, in certain specific cases, is expressly reserved to the Roman Pontiff pro tempore, so that no one may presume to grant indulgences or the like, unless they are expressly mentioned or a specific exception is made; notwithstanding Our rule against granting indulgences ad instar; notwithstanding the oath and Apostolic confirmation of any Religious Order, Congregation, or Regular Institute; notwithstanding any other concessions, Statutes, Customs, Privileges, Indults, and Apostolic Letters that may have been in any way granted, approved, and renewed to the same Orders, Congregations, Institutes, and their members, We hereby intend to revoke all and each of these concessions, even if specific, express, and individual mention had been made of them and their content—and not merely by virtue of the general clauses set forth herein—and even if some other special form had been used, or should another expression have been used (while maintaining that their content and form are expressed in this Letter), this time, in a special, express, and specific manner, We intend to revoke them, setting them aside, rejecting any contrary exception. 8. Furthermore, so that this Letter may more easily come to the attention of all—should it not be delivered to individual locations —We decree that it shall have the same authority and authenticity in every locality, even if presented in manuscript or printed form, provided it is signed by a public notary and affixed with the seal of a person holding ecclesiastical office. Given in Rome, at Santa Maria Maggiore, under the Fisherman’s Ring, on November 20, 1744, the fifth year of Our Pontificate.
