# Celebrationem Magni

**La Santa Sede**

---

> *Note: This document was translated from the original Italian into English. Translation provided by DeepL (July 2026).*

ENCYCLICAL CELEBRATIONEM MAGNI OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV To all Patriarchs, Archbishops, Bishops, and other local Ordinaries who are in peace and communion with the Apostolic See. Pope Benedict XIV. Venerable Brother, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. With God’s help, we have concluded the celebration of the Great Jubilee in this beloved City. In accordance with ancient custom, we have closed in the Vatican the Holy Door of the Basilica of the Prince of the Apostles, which we ourselves had opened with a solemn rite at the beginning of the Holy Year. Likewise, the doors of the other basilicas—namely, St. Paul’s on the Ostiense Road, St. John Lateran, and St. Mary Major—were opened and closed, at Our behest, by the Cardinal Legates of the Holy Roman Church, whom We had appointed for this rite. Indeed, We have many and just reasons for deep satisfaction and for offering endless thanks to Almighty God, from whom all good things come, since everything We had hoped for on this occasion has been fulfilled, and what We ordered to be done has been carried out with the utmost zeal. We had ordered in due time that throughout our Domain the roads be repaired and leveled for the convenience of the pilgrims, and all this was carried out thoroughly and promptly. We had ordered that food supplies be stockpiled, both throughout the territory of the aforementioned dominion and especially in the City, so that pilgrims traveling to receive the Jubilee—and who therefore had to stay in the City—might find, at a fair price, what was needed to meet their needs, unlike what usually happens where the necessary provisions are not stockpiled in anticipation of an extraordinary influx of people; this directive of ours was also promptly obeyed. Furthermore, in the year preceding the one in which the Jubilee was proclaimed, We strongly urged those responsible to restore the churches of Rome and to enhance their splendor and decorations, as far as possible and to the extent required by divine worship: We cannot fully express all the satisfaction We have felt in this regard and all the joy in Our heart when We saw the splendor of the House of God shining forth everywhere. Indeed, Our call—in which We urged all Christians scattered throughout the Earth to venerate the dwellings of the Holy Apostles and to take advantage of the Treasure of Indulgences—was answered by an incredible throng of people, who came here even from the most distant regions, from Armenia, Syria, Egypt, and elsewhere, so much so that the turnout far exceeded that recorded during several other Jubilees, as We ourselves witnessed in Rome during the two previous Jubilee years. We ourselves had not failed to send out exhortations far and wide so that pilgrims might be welcomed kindly not only within the territory subject to Us, but also in the domains of other Princes and Republics, and be hosted in such a way that it could be said the ancient examples of charity had been renewed, for which pilgrimages to the holy tombs of the Apostles are still celebrated today: in this regard, too, We were left wanting for nothing. Furthermore, through Apostolic letters, We have urged Our Venerable Brother Bishops of all the Churches to take an active role, so that the faithful under their care, who on that occasion were undertaking the journey to Rome, might be fully informed both about the nature and purpose of the Holy Jubilee and about the proper way to benefit from it. That this directive was faithfully carried out is evident from the following: never before have visits to the Holy Basilicas been so frequent, with such large crowds and such clear displays of devotion and piety, as in the past year. Furthermore, as we have learned from the report of the Penitentiaries, countless faithful devoted themselves to the purification of their souls through general confessions of their sins and sincere expressions of repentance and contrition; while a great many others, having honestly renounced the heresy they had professed, have happily returned to the bosom of the Holy Roman Catholic Church. 1. Truly, the devotion of the Romans proved no less than that of the foreigners. In fact, having ordered that, as the Holy Year approached and throughout its duration, the Sacred Missions be celebrated in the squares and churches of Rome, it is incredible with what enthusiasm and with what devout fervor the Roman people turned their hearts to listening to the preaching of the Word of God. And it is from this, above all, that those magnificent examples of penance and piety offered by citizens of every social class have sprung, as they vied with one another in practicing the devotions of the Holy Year. Certainly, those who were called to shine with the brightest light of good works, like candles placed on a candelabrum, responded so fully to Our wishes and expectations that We take pride in having drawn from their piety the greatest consolation and immense joy. In fact, not only did We witness with Our own eyes Our Venerable Brother Cardinals of the Holy Roman Church, the Bishops and Prelates of Our Curia, the other notables of the City, and the nobles of both sexes assiduously attending the sacred services of the aforementioned Missions; but also, throughout the Holy Year, We saw in part—and were told of the rest—that they devoutly made the prescribed visits to the churches; they humbly served at the pilgrims’ tables, washed their feet in the public hospices, and at times instructed them more by example than by words; they assiduously attended the public services of the Church; through generous alms they alleviated the miseries of the poor; they covered the expenses of the places of worship from their own funds (expenses that the assets of those places could not have covered), as they considered it their duty to offer, with generous liberality, a roof, a bed, and a meal to the countless multitude of pilgrims. Since these events took place in full view of nearly the entire Catholic world, will surely silence those most shameless men who, having no connection whatsoever with the Church, have dared to slanderously assert that the celebration of the Jubilee in Rome can be described as nothing other than a heap of scandals and a scheme to make money. 2. In truth, already at the beginning of the Holy Year, We—following the examples of Our Predecessors—through the publication of the Constitution beginning with \*Paterna Charitas\* granted this privilege—not only to monks, to anchorites, hermits, and those held in prison, but also to those who, due to some physical infirmity, were unable to come to Rome —the opportunity to obtain the sacred Indulgence and the other benefits associated with the Universal Jubilee outside Rome, substituting other acts of devotion for visits to the Roman Basilicas, in accordance with the wise prescriptions laid down by their prelates and superiors. As for those who were not prevented from making the journey to Rome either by the law of enclosure or by physical infirmity, it is well known that the Apostolic See did not always follow a single policy in granting or denying them the right to obtain theIndulgence and the other benefits associated with the Jubilee, even outside Rome, during the Holy Year; even in the case of persons of high rank, whom it is fitting to take into special consideration. As for Our Predecessor Pope Clement VI, it is recorded that he could not bring himself to grant the Indulgence of the Holy Year—which was then being celebrated in Rome—to Ugone, King of Cyprus, even though the latter ardently desired it and asserted that certain important public affairs prevented him from making the journey to Rome. The Pope replied: “Although several other princes, like you, have made the same request, our Venerable Brothers, considering that the Indulgence is granted both for the salvation of souls and for the honor and veneration of the Saints, have in no way wished to grant it to anyone who had not visited the Basilicas and Churches.” On the other hand, our other predecessor, Pope Boniface IX, during the Holy Year, granted the Catholic Prince Richard of England and King John of Portugal permission to benefit from the Jubilee Indulgence outside Rome, granting their confessors the authority to prescribe other virtuous acts for them to perform in lieu of the journey to Rome and the visit to the basilicas of Rome itself. We also have records that Pope Sixtus V, also one of Our Predecessors, while celebrating the Jubilee in Rome, permitted the King and Queen of Castile and León and their relatives, generals, and barons, to obtain the Jubilee Indulgence by performing other acts of devotion without having to come to Rome to visit the Sacred Basilicas. We have also learned that similar concessions were granted by Pope Julius III, also Our Predecessor (who likewise celebrated the Holy Year), in favor of Charles V, Emperor of the Romans, his son Philip, proclaimed Prince of Spain, and other princes. Therefore, following in the footsteps of the aforementioned Pontiffs, We have by no means refused to grant this to Our Most Beloved Sons in Christ, the Catholic Monarchs, who requested it, and to the other Princes who, were prevented from traveling to Rome—not for reasons of health or age but for reasons of state—might, by virtue of other salutary works of piety appropriately prescribed for them, in lieu of such a journey and their presence, be able to obtain the indulgence of the Holy Jubilee even during the Holy Year. 3. In truth, in granting these exceptions, We have not in any way departed from the custom and rules of Our Predecessors, as is evident from what has been said thus far; indeed, within the variety of such regulations, We have chosen the most lenient and benevolent course; thus, it is now Our intention to maintain the same course of action. In fact, once the Holy Year has concluded, We intend to extend and expand the Indulgence and the benefits of the Jubilee to other parts of the Catholic world, for the good of all the faithful of Christ, including those who did not come to Rome during that Year. Some time ago, our praised predecessor Pope Sixtus IV, having observed that in 1475—the Jubilee Year he celebrated—the number of pilgrims who had come to Rome was small due to fear of war and unsafe roads; in order to provide for the welfare of the faithful of Christ (especially those from distant lands), he decided and permitted that those who, in the following year, from May 1 until the end of the year, traveled to Bologna and there visited the four churches designated by the Pontiff himself, might gain the Jubilee Indulgence, which the previous year had been granted to those who had traveled to Rome; Consequently, it is said that an extraordinary multitude of pilgrims flocked to Bologna from all over the world on that occasion. From the other Pope already mentioned, Gregory XIII, there remains the Apostolic Letter by which, after the celebration of the Jubilee in Rome in the year 1575, and in response to the prayers of Saint Charles Borromeo, then Archbishop of Milan, he extended the same Jubilee to the city and people of Milan, leaving it to the discretion of that same most holy Prelate to determine the manner and time for taking advantage of the Jubilee, as well as to designate the churches that were to be visited for that purpose. That the Archbishop carried this out to the great edification of the people is amply attested to by the Acts of the Church of Milan and the biographers of St. Charles himself. But even the aforementioned Clement VI, who had refused to grant the Jubilee Indulgence to those who had not traveled to Rome during the Holy Year, granted—after the Holy Year of 1350 had ended—to the Friars of the Order of Saint Augustine (who had gathered in Basel on the Feast of Pentecost immediately following to hold the General Chapter of their Order) the same indulgence that each of them would have earned by coming to Rome the previous year. It is also recorded that the Jubilee was similarly extended for the benefit of certain individuals or communities, after the Holy Year 1400, by our predecessor Pope Boniface IX. Furthermore, there are several examples (left by our Predecessors Alexander VI, Gregory XIII, Clement VIII, and also by others closer to our own time) of universal grants by which the benefits were extended to the entire Catholic world after the conclusion of the Jubilee in Rome. 4. Encouraged by such examples, We have decided to promulgate the full extension of the Jubilee for the benefit of all the faithful of Christ living in every part of the world. And since in times past it was customary to apply the following criterion—namely, that an Apostolic Letter in the form of a Brief was sent to every Bishop who requested such an extension, by which the Jubilee of the Holy Year was extended only to the city and diocese of the petitioner himself; and since it often happened that some, either inexperienced in such matters or not inclined to reflect, neglected to disseminate this Letter (even though it was usually sent to everyone at no additional cost), and thus many cities and dioceses were deprived of such a great spiritual benefit, We have judged it best to extend, by means of a single general Constitution, the Indulgence of the Universal Jubilee—along with the other privileges and benefits specified in that Constitution—to the entire Church, as vast as it is; and so that the opportunity to obtain such a treasure may be made available to all who rightfully request it, on your part, Venerable Brothers, or on the part of your representatives in Rome, there should no longer be any difficulty in distributing the specific letter mentioned above. 5. We have issued several Constitutions or Apostolic Letters on the occasion of the Jubilee of the Holy Year. The first Constitution, which begins with \*Peregrinantes\*, addressed to all the children of the Catholic Church, contains the proclamation of the Universal Jubilee to be celebrated in Rome during the recently concluded Holy Year. In another, which begins with \*Cum Nos Nuper\*, in accordance with the custom of Our Predecessors, We established which indulgences and which faculties were to remain suspended and which were to remain in force during that same Holy Year. Subsequently, We sent an Encyclical Letter to all Patriarchs, Archbishops, and Bishops, which begins with the words \*Apostolica Constitutio\*; in it, We set forth the means by which each person must prepare to obtain the Jubilee Indulgence. Then We issued another Constitution beginning with \*Convocatis\*, in which We clearly set forth the faculties We intended to grant during the Holy Year, both to the Penitentiaries to be assigned to the Basilicas and Churches of Rome and to the Confessors whom the Cardinal of the Holy Roman Church, Our Vicar in Rome, may wish to designate; for this reason, several Constitutions issued in the past on these matters were recently abrogated. We have also added certain binding provisions for those We have mentioned above, so that they may make good use of the powers granted to them. Furthermore, upon consulting (not without long and arduous effort) the works of theologians known as “moral theologians,” we have learned of the countless controversies that they address in various ways, both regarding the use of the faculties granted to the Penitentiaries designated for the Holy Year and concerning the best way to gain the Indulgence; we have thus realized that in their treatment of these matters, those theologians take entirely different approaches; and therefore we thought it worthwhile to write another letter to the aforementioned Penitentiaries appointed in Rome and to the Confessors appointed by the Cardinal Vicar, which was published in Italian and begins with \*Fra le fatiche\* and which has now perhaps been translated into Latin. \[See: \*Inter praeteritos\*, December 3, 1749\]. In it, I demonstrated that a large part of the difficulties that arose in the past had been foreseen by Us and immediately overcome through the appropriate wording used in Our Constitutions promulgated up to that day; and since these were matters that depended solely on Our will, We did not hesitate to express it openly and to remove every cause for doubt, also pointing out the validity of the reasons upon which each of Our measures is based. Furthermore, it occurred to Us (and We realized that Our Predecessors had also wisely considered this) that many would have gladly come to Rome to obtain the Jubilee during the Holy Year if their particular circumstances or some other reason of force majeure had not prevented them from making this pilgrimage; such is the case for the nuns, the oblates, and the other virgins and women living in monasteries and religious houses; and also for the anchorites, the hermits, the sick, the elderly, and those confined in prison: for all of them, so as not to deprive them of the fulfillment of their pious desires, We made appropriate provision in Our other aforementioned Constitution, which begins with \*Paterna Charitas\*. 6. Finally, only this remained. The Jubilee of the Holy Year, recently decreed in Rome for the past year 1750, had been extended with liberal Apostolic generosity to all Christians who had been unable to travel to Rome the previous year and who could not leave the borders of their regions. Now, We have addressed this matter in Our recent Constitution \*Benedictus Deus\*, to which We deemed it appropriate to add this Encyclical Letter addressed to You, Venerable Brothers. 7. In that Constitution, therefore, the works that each person must perform to obtain the Indulgence are prescribed; the time within which these works must be performed is specified; and it sets forth the powers granted to confessors so that they may absolve the faithful who confess their sins in sacramental confession, remitting all sins—even the most grave and heinous ones reserved to the local Ordinaries or to the Apostolic See. It is entrusted to the prudent judgment of the Ordinaries to grant appropriate modifications and reductions of these works to the people of their dioceses who, in legitimate cases, have been unable to perform those prescribed. 8. Finally, as we extended the grace of the Jubilee—which had been granted to Christians present in Rome last year—to the entire Catholic world for certain months of the coming year, as indicated in the Bull, we took care to maintain the greatest possible uniformity, as far as circumstances permitted, both in prescribing the obligations and in granting the faculties of which confessors may make use. This also applies to what was stated in the aforementioned Encyclical Letter beginning with \*Apostolica Constitutio\* regarding the necessity of sacramental Confession and Communion and the instructions that must be given so that these sacraments may be received in a worthy and fruitful manner; as well as the norms found in the Bull beginning with \*Convocatis\* and in the other Letter beginning with \*Fra le fatiche\*, regarding the manner of visiting churches and the prayers that must be offered to God during such visits to obtain the Jubilee then proclaimed in Rome; all these provisions must be kept in mind during the duration of the Jubilee itself. Although the powers usually granted to the Penitentiaries appointed in Rome for the Holy Year have never been extended, not even during the Jubilee, to all confessors outside Rome, nevertheless, in the Constitution beginning with \*Benedictus Deus\*, the confessors outside Rome were granted those broader faculties that the assigned purpose seemed to require and that the tradition of the Apostolic See permitted. If any doubt should arise regarding the understanding or application of these provisions, one may consult Our Letter \*Convocatis\* and the other, in Italian, \*Fra le fatiche\*; in them, one will find all the answers to such doubts. Although, in fact, the Bull \*Benedictus Deus\* did not address all matters concerning these faculties, they are nonetheless contained in the two aforementioned Letters; in this latest Constitution, there is no topic that has not been addressed in those same Letters. 9. Furthermore, in the Constitution \*Convocatis\*, mentioned several times above, the Penitentiaries appointed for the Churches of Rome were granted the authority to absolve even the Regulars of those Orders in which there is a prohibition against confessing one’s sins to anyone other than priests recognized by their Order. In the other Constitution, \*Paterna Charitas\*, by which nuns, oblates, tertiaries, anchorites, and the disabled were granted the opportunity to obtain the Jubilee during the Holy Year, even outside Rome, we authorized the aforementioned nuns and their novices to choose (in order to fulfill the sacramental confession that grants access to the benefits of the Jubilee) any confessor—whether secular or religious—provided that he is “authorized by the local Ordinaries to hear the confessions of nuns.” Furthermore, for the other Oblates, the Tertiary Sisters, and other women living in monasteries or religious and pious houses, as well as for prisoners, the sick, and other infirm persons—whether laypeople or clergy, secular or regular members of any Order, even those worthy of special mention— we granted that, for the same purpose, they might choose any secular or regular confessor of any Order—even a different one—provided he is authorized by the Ordinaries to hear the confessions of laypeople in “whose cities, dioceses, and territories such confessions are to be accepted.” We wished this criterion to be confirmed also in the most recent Constitution, by which we extended the Jubilee of the Holy Year to other parts of the Catholic world, as can be seen in the paragraph “Insuper,” where nuns and their novices are granted the right to choose “any confessor admitted by the current local Ordinary of the place where their monasteries are located to hear the confessions of the nuns.” And for other Christians, whether laypeople or clergy, whether Secular or Regular, of any Order, Congregation, or Institute—even those to be specified by name—are permitted to choose for this purpose “any Confessor Priest, whether Secular or Regular, of any Order or Institute, even a different one, equally authorized by the current Ordinaries (in whose cities, dioceses, or territories such confessions are to be received) to hear the confessions of laypeople.” Furthermore, in the paragraph “Praesentes,” any contrary law, constitution, or custom is set aside, especially those that prohibit members of certain religious orders from going to confession outside their own order. 10. You are certainly not unaware, Venerable Brothers, of the countless controversies raging among moral theologians, now concerning the confessor whom anyone may choose during the Jubilee, when such a choice is permitted in the Letter proclaiming the Jubilee itself; and now also regarding the local Ordinary, whose consent is essential for anyone to be appointed as a confessor. Some, in fact, held that even one who had been admitted to hear confessions solely by the penitent’s Ordinary could be appointed as a confessor. Others did not hesitate to assert that there were apostolic privileges granted to certain persons, by virtue of which those privileged individuals—provided they are recognized by some Ordinary Bishop, even if not the Ordinary of the place where confessions are to be heard—may nevertheless be elected as Confessors anywhere. Still others, arguing more subtly, said that approval must come from the Ordinary of the place where confessions are heard, but that the authorization the priest once obtained from the Bishop responsible for ordinary jurisdiction in that particular place is sufficient, even if that Prelate has been taken by death or transferred by the Apostolic Authority to govern another Church. Such sophistical aberrations, often attributed to the Congregations of Cardinals of this Holy Roman Church, were just as often rejected; nor did the Roman Pontiffs themselves fail to condemn them in their Apostolic Constitutions with contrary decrees, as can be seen in the Bull of Our Predecessor of recent memory, Pope Clement X, which begins “Superna,” and inthat of Pope Innocent XIII of blessed memory, which begins with \*Apostolici Ministeri i\*, and then in Ours, which begins with \*Apostolica Indulta\* (printed in Our Bullary, Volume I, No. C). In the aforementioned Constitutions of Our Predecessors, an exception was introduced to any contrary privilege, even if it had been granted after the Council of Trent; in Our Bull, however, this derogation was extended to any other privilege, even if not expressly mentioned by the aforementioned Predecessors or by Ourselves in those Constitutions. But while the preamble made no mention of the Universal Jubilee—which was not then under discussion—in order to prevent those reprehensible opinions from being raised again, now, however, since it might perhaps be argued that such opinions coincide perfectly with the Church’s greater generosity—which, throughout the world, grants her children the Universal Jubilee, occurring only once every twenty-five years, opens spiritual treasures, and desires that they be lavishly bestowed upon penitents— In Our recent Constitution beginning with \*Benedictus Deus\*, We have included provisions such that all contrary opinions must be considered rejected even during the current Jubilee; these same provisions, on the occasion of another Jubilee—whether Universal or particular—will have the same analogous effect. 11. Since in this latest Constitution, We—speaking further of the confessors whom the nuns and novices may choose—have permitted them to elect “any confessor authorized by the current local Ordinary of the place where their monasteries are located to hear the confessions of the nuns” (words that have already been quoted in this Letter), some have asked whether it is permissible for the nuns, on this occasion, to choose as their confessor a priest whom the Ordinary has not expressly designated for their monastery, but for some other one. In this regard, We, noting that if it were an obligation for the nuns to choose a confessor designated solely for their monastery, the privilege granted to them in this circumstance would be almost meaningless, were naturally led to declare that it was lawful for the nuns and their novices, in order to obtain the benefits of the current Jubilee, to choose a Confessor approved by the current local Ordinary—even if he is designated for another monastery or “for the nuns in general”—and who has never been openly censured for any demerit; In short, we declare ourselves as far removed from inconsistent opinions, lacking in rigor, as from those that lead to excessive and intolerable severity. 12. We have also deemed it appropriate, Venerable Brothers, to set forth these matters to you in this Encyclical Letter of ours. Other responsibilities must be addressed by each of you in your own cities and dioceses. Each of you, that is to say, must take care—through persistent prayer and exhortation—that the people entrusted to your guidance be led to sincerely detest their sins and prepare themselves to receive the benefits of the Jubilee after having approached the Sacrament of Penance devoutly and in the prescribed manner. Certainly, if the Jubilee is proclaimed in the diocese without first invoking the help of Divine Providence through public prayers and without the people being urged to penance—either verbally or in writing—by the Shepherd; if, after prior and appropriate instruction, the Christian people are not informed about how to confess their sins effectively, nor are they made aware of the value of general Confession and, in certain cases, of its necessity; if, finally, the confessors do not take care to administer the proper remedy for the wounds of souls, then we greatly fear (and we cannot write or even think of such a possibility without tears) that the celebration of the Jubilee will appeal only to frivolous minds and that the people will derive no substantial benefit from it. While we trust that many sinners, formerly steeped in iniquity, have emerged from the mire during the recent Holy Year through theinfinite mercy of Our God, and that many of the faithful have reaped the fruits of the Holy Jubilee; we are at the same time convinced that such an outcome must be attributed above all to that heavenly ray of divine light from which we drew the impetus both to stimulate the zeal of individual bishops, so that each, in his own diocese, might prepare—by the means mentioned above—those who were about to go to Rome to receive the Jubilee, and also to perform in Rome all those acts which, in the preceding year and during the Holy Year of the Jubilee itself, we performed and took care to have others perform, so that, through appropriate instructions and admonitions, all might be disposed to true penance and submit to it. 13. And let none of you, Venerable Brothers, think that your duty has already been fulfilled simply because you arranged for public prayers to be offered in the previous year, or because you delivered—or had delivered—instructions, teachings, and discourses to prepare those who were about to come to Rome to benefit from the Jubilee; and that it is by no means necessary to perform all those acts again, now that the benefit of the Jubilee itself is extended to the city and diocese of each of you. The conduct of Saint Charles Borromeo was quite different; he was a model for bishops and a radiant light who, having first prepared and ardently exhorted his people through sermons, instructions, and discourses to undertake the Sacred Pilgrimage to Rome; and having come to Rome himself, he offered such outstanding examples of religious piety there that merely seeing them led many sinners to do penance. Then, upon returning to his diocese and having obtained—as we have already mentioned—from Pope Gregory XIII, after the Holy Year, the extension of the Jubilee to his diocese of Milan, he was by no means satisfied with his efforts in preparing his faithful to undertake the journey to Rome and to reap the benefits of the Jubilee by fulfilling the obligations prescribed in Rome; but, having to make this extension public, driven by the same religious zeal as always, he once again organized public prayers, addressed the people, and disseminated many teachings full of doctrine and piety that are preserved in the Acts of the Church of Milan; finally, he neglected none of those works which he deemed useful or necessary to lead his people to penance and to prepare them to reap the fruits of the Jubilee. 14. This is a matter of no small importance, Venerable Brothers. The Treasury of the Church is made available to all the faithful, to whom the way to obtain a plenary indulgence is opened; at the same time, the ministers of the Sacrament of Penance are granted broad powers to remit sins. And although these benefits—which were initially granted only once every hundred years—are now granted every 25 years, nevertheless, as is the nature of human beings, those for whom a single lifetime will not suffice to enjoy them again will be far more numerous than those who, still alive after another twenty-five years, will be able to hope to receive them. The works prescribed are such that even to the most feeble and weak they cannot seem too harsh or burdensome; especially if they consider that about two centuries before the pontificate of Boniface VIII—who conferred the status of Holy Year upon the Jubilee—those who wished to obtain a plenary indulgence were required to travel to Palestine to fight to liberate that region from the yoke of the infidels: “For anyone who, out of devotion alone—and not for the sake of honor or money—sets out for Jerusalem to liberate the Church of God, that journey shall be considered the highest form of penance.” These are the words of our predecessor, Pope Urban II, spoken at the Council of Chiaromonte in the year 1096. Regarding these words, the Venerable Servant of God Cardinal Giuseppe Maria Tommasi—whom we gladly cite as an authoritative figure on the subject, and with whom we were once colleagues in the role of Consultor to the Congregation of Cardinals responsible for Indulgences—wrote as follows: “This plenary indulgence (which imposes a heavy burden in terms of expenses, hardships, exhausting journeys, and imminent dangers to life, so as to appear more like an exchange for penance than a total remission of it) this plenary indulgence, I say, was subsequently confirmed by other Supreme Pontiffs for those who journeyed to the Holy Land. Nor do I recall any other plenary indulgence until the beginning of the 14th century, when Boniface VIII granted it for the Jubilee Year of 1300.” Thus spoke that learned and pious Cardinal in a certain handwritten treatise that may soon see the light of day among his other works, a most meticulous edition of which is now being prepared. 15. We have deemed it appropriate to bring all this to your attention to stir up your zeal, so that you may neglect nothing that might lead the peoples entrusted to your care and faith to sincere repentance, a complete confession of sins, and an honest reexamination of their lives, so that they may be prepared to obtain the Plenary Indulgence of the Jubilee in their hometowns, even if they did not go to Rome in the previous year for whatever reason. Let us finally bring this already overly lengthy Letter to a close, asking and imploring you, in the name of Our Lord Jesus Christ, to reflect often on the rank granted to you by the Emperor of the Heavenly Militia in that great Legion which He Himself prepared and deployed to eradicate errors from the world, to defeat all the enemies of His glory, to establish the Kingdom of justice and truth, and to save souls. Remember that this Legion, with the help of Almighty God, has always won glorious victories whenever its Commanders and Soldiers fought with steadfast resolve and the strenuous virtue proper to the ecclesiastical spirit. Once, the regions of France were freed from the contagion of Arianism by the hand of Hilary alone. Spain, which for nearly three hundred years had been wretchedly overwhelmed by the same errors, finally rose again thanks to Leander and Isidore. Augustine, in Africa, suppressed and crushed the attempts of the Manichaeans, the Donatists, and the Pelagians. Ambrose preserved Italy from the pestilential infamy of heresies. Athanasius, Basil, and Gregory, through their labors, their writings, and at their own risk, preserved the Catholic Faith in Egypt and the East, from where heretics, arising in great numbers, were preparing to corrupt the entire world. Chrysostom purified Greece and Asia of the filth of bad faith; and to turn to more recent examples, consider the great deeds accomplished by Turribius in India, how much in our own regions Charles Borromeo and Francis Xavier have accomplished; and how much the purity of the Catholic Faith, the splendor of ecclesiastical discipline, and the practice of Christian piety have been strengthened through their merit and that of other men endowed with priestly virtue. Admiring their examples, be virtuous, show the zeal for pastoral charity that is within you, and joyfully seize the opportunity offered to you to save souls from perdition, to improve the morals of the people, and to instill the habit of performing pious works. 16. Be assured, Venerable Brothers, that among Christian peoples the power of the Divine Religion is great, and that the ardent devotion spread in the hearts of the faithful by the power of the Holy Spirit, if it is supported and nourished appropriately by the priests, and stimulated at the right moment, will burst forth in flames of charity. By divine inspiration, this was well understood by many Catholic kings and princes who, taking to heart the pious desires of their subjects, offered up humble prayers to Us that We might extend this Jubilee to the provinces of their domains. We certainly did not fail to grant their request with great eagerness, after having praised their outstanding piety; therefore, it is your duty, Venerable Brothers, to combine your efforts with their zeal and also with Our own wishes and those of the Church. Especially at this time, as each of you raises public prayers to Almighty God, within His Church, for the prosperity and health of these same Christian Princes, it is your duty to promote, through your sermons, your examples, and your efforts, the sanctification of your flock, which has always been entrusted to your care but is now also entrusted to you through their special concern. Finally, Venerable Brothers, we invoke the grace of Divine Providence to assist your precious work, and the peace of God upon the peoples you govern; and to you and to them, with all our affection, we impart the Apostolic Blessing. Given in Rome, at Saint Mary Major, on January 1, 1751, the eleventh year of Our Pontificate. BENEDICT XIV
