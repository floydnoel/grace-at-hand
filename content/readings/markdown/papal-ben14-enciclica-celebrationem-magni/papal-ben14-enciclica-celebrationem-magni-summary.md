## Summary

*Celebrationem Magni* is an encyclical issued by Pope Benedict XIV after the close of a Great Jubilee (Holy Year) in Rome. It recounts the successful completion of the Jubilee's sacred rites — including the opening and closing of the Holy Doors at the four major basilicas — and reviews the practical preparations that made the event orderly: road repairs for pilgrims, stockpiling of food supplies at fair prices, and the restoration and decoration of Rome's churches. The pope expresses deep satisfaction and gratitude to God that the Jubilee's spiritual and logistical goals were fully realized.

## Historical Context

Benedict XIV reigned from 1740 to 1758, a period of relative stability for the Church. The Great Jubilee (likely the 1750 Holy Year) drew large numbers of pilgrims to Rome. The document reflects the Church's effort to accommodate mass pilgrimage in an age before modern infrastructure — managing roads, food supply, and church decoration were major undertakings. The encyclical was written in the wake of the Jubilee's conclusion, both to thank participants and to memorialize the event for the universal Church.

## Key Themes

- **Jubilee as a central devotional practice** — The opening and closing of Holy Doors at St. Peter's, St. Paul's, St. John Lateran, and St. Mary Major follows ancient custom, emphasizing Rome's role as a pilgrimage destination.
- **Pastoral and practical governance** — The pope takes personal pride not only in the spiritual dimensions but in the concrete preparations (roads, food, church restorations), showing that temporal care for pilgrims is a pastoral duty.
- **Unity with the Apostolic See** — The address to "all Patriarchs, Archbishops, Bishops, and other local Ordinaries in peace and communion with the Apostolic See" underscores the universal scope of the Jubilee and the pope's role as shepherd of the whole Church.
- **Gratitude to God** — The encyclical opens and returns to thanksgiving, framing the Jubilee's success as a gift from "Almighty God, from whom all good things come."

## Significance

The encyclical stands as a administrative and pastoral capstone to one of the 18th century's major Church events, illustrating Benedict XIV's reputation as a meticulous, learned, and practically minded pontiff. It provides a window into how the Church managed large-scale pilgrimages before the modern era, balancing spiritual solemnity with logistics — a model that would influence later Jubilee preparations, including those of the 20th and 21st centuries. While not a doctrinal landmark, it reflects the continuing importance of the Holy Year tradition in Catholic life.
