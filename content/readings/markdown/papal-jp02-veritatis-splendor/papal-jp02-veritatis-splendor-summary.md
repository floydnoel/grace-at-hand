## Summary

*Veritatis Splendor* ("The Splendor of Truth") is Pope John Paul II's 1993 encyclical on the foundations of moral theology — the first papal document ever devoted entirely to this subject. Structured as a meditation on Jesus' conversation with the rich young man (Matthew 19:16–22), it addresses a crisis in Catholic moral teaching after the Second Vatican Council, reaffirming the intrinsic bond between human freedom and truth, the universality of the natural law, the role of conscience as a witness to truth rather than a creator of it, and the existence of intrinsically evil acts that can never be justified by good intentions or circumstances.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was issued on 6 August 1993, fifteen years into John Paul II's pontificate, amid widespread theological dissent following Vatican II. New currents in moral theology — proportionalism, consequentialism, and theories of a "fundamental option" separable from particular acts — had gained traction in seminaries and faculties, challenging traditional teaching on the natural law, conscience, and moral absolutes. The document was preceded by the *Catechism of the Catholic Church* (1992) and was first announced in 1987. It responds to what the Pope diagnoses as a "genuine crisis" rooted in the detachment of human freedom from its essential relationship to truth.

## Key Themes

- **Freedom and truth** are not opposed but intrinsically linked; freedom achieves itself only in obedience to truth, and the exaltation of freedom as an absolute leads to moral relativism
- **Natural law** is universal, immutable, and accessible to reason, grounded not in mere biology but in the dignity of the human person as a unity of body and soul
- **Conscience** does not create moral truth but discerns and applies it; an erroneous conscience retains dignity only when its error is invincible, and it must be continually formed by the Church's teaching
- **Intrinsically evil acts** (*intrinsece malum*) exist — acts that are always wrong by their object, regardless of intention or circumstances, such as homicide, adultery, and theft
- **The moral act** is judged by three sources: the object (primary), intention, and circumstances; proportionalism and consequentialism are rejected as inadequate
- **The fundamental option** is real but cannot be separated from particular choices; a mortal sin in grave matter with full knowledge and consent can revoke it
- **Christian morality** is discipleship — following Christ, not mere rule-keeping — made possible by grace, especially the gift of the Holy Spirit
- **The Church's Magisterium** has the authority and duty to teach moral truth with finality, and theologians serve this teaching office in ecclesial communion

## Significance

*Veritatis Splendor* is the definitive magisterial statement on the foundations of Catholic moral theology. It set the terms for subsequent debate by firmly rejecting proportionalism and the separation of fundamental option from concrete acts, and by reasserting negative moral absolutes as essential to human dignity. The encyclical shaped John Paul II's later social and moral encyclicals (*Evangelium Vitae*, *Fides et Ratio*) and remains a reference point for theological reflection on freedom, conscience, and the moral life. Its diagnosis of the link between ethical relativism and the crisis of truth in modern culture continues to resonate.
