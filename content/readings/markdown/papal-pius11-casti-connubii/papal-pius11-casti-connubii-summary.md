## Summary

*Casti Connubii* ("Of Chaste Wedlock") is a papal encyclical issued by Pope Pius XI on December 31, 1930, reaffirming the Catholic Church's teachings on Christian marriage. The document defends the sanctity, indissolubility, and sacramental nature of matrimony, while condemning a range of contemporary errors including contraception, divorce, eugenics, "companionate" marriage, and the broader secularization of the marital bond. It presents marriage as a divinely instituted covenant ordered toward the procreation and education of children, mutual fidelity, and sacramental grace.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written in the wake of the 1920s and early 1930s, a period of rapid social change in the Western world. The Church was responding to several developments: the rise of secularism and eugenics movements; increasing acceptance of contraception and divorce legislation in many countries (including the 1930 Lambeth Conference, where the Anglican Communion conditionally approved contraception); the spread of Communist ideologies that sought to weaken the traditional family; and the growing influence of companionate marriage ideals that downplayed permanence. Pius XI explicitly situates the encyclical as a reaffirmation and expansion of Leo XIII's *Arcanum Divinae* (1880), updating its teaching to address new challenges.

## Key Themes

- **Divine institution of marriage** — Marriage was not invented by man but established by God, and its essential laws cannot be altered by human decrees or private consent
- **Primary end of marriage** — The procreation and education of children is the chief purpose of the conjugal act, with secondary ends (mutual aid, love, remedy for concupiscence) subordinate to it
- **Condemnation of contraception** — Any act that deliberately frustrates the natural power of the marital act to generate life is intrinsically evil and gravely sinful (citing the Onan narrative)
- **Indissolubility** — A valid, consummated sacramental marriage cannot be dissolved by any human power; divorce is unequivocally rejected as contrary to divine law
- **Conjugal faith** — Marriage demands complete unity, mutual fidelity, chastity, and a hierarchical order in the home with the husband as head and the wife as heart
- **Sacramental grace** — Christian marriage is a sign and source of grace, conferring supernatural strength to fulfill the duties of the married state
- **Rejection of abortion and eugenics** — Direct killing of the unborn, sterilization, and eugenic legislation are condemned as violations of natural law and the right to life
- **Social justice for families** — The State has a duty to provide economic conditions (just wages, housing, healthcare) that enable married couples to fulfill their responsibilities
- **Mixed marriages** — The Church strongly discourages marriage between Catholics and non-Catholics due to spiritual dangers

## Significance

*Casti Connubii* is one of the most authoritative magisterial documents on marriage in the modern era. It provided the definitive Catholic response to the early twentieth-century birth control movement and set the theological foundation that was later reaffirmed and expanded by Paul VI's *Humanae Vitae* (1968). The encyclical remains a cornerstone of Catholic sexual ethics, cited frequently in subsequent documents on marriage, family, and bioethics. Its comprehensive treatment — spanning theology, law, economics, and pastoral care — also marks an important moment in the Church's engagement with modern social questions.
