# Hoc ipso · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VII explains his decision to travel to France after Napoleon requests papal anointing and an imperial coronation. He recalls the concordat's restoration of public worship, reopened churches, and legitimate pastors, expressing gratitude for Napoleon's role in those results. The journey also offers, he believes, an opportunity for direct discussion of unresolved ecclesiastical questions and further benefits to the Church.

The pope acknowledges the objections presented by distance, season, age, health, and matters that had troubled his conscience. He says imperial assurances, consultation with the cardinals, reflection, and prayer have persuaded him to proceed. His stated purposes are God's glory, the progress of religion, and the salvation of souls. He also announces safeguards for government during his absence: the Curia, tribunals, and officials will continue in their existing arrangements, and provisions have been made for a papal election in Rome should he die away from the city. He asks the cardinals to pray that the planned journey fulfill its religious aims.

## Key themes

- Papal participation in Napoleon's imperial coronation
- Hopes for resolving ecclesiastical affairs through personal negotiation
- Consultation, assurances, and discernment before departure
- Continuity of Roman government and provision for a future election
