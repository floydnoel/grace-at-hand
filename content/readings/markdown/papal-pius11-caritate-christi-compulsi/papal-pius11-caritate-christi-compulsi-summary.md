## Summary

*Caritate Christi Compulsi* (Constrained by the Charity of Christ) is a 1932 encyclical in which Pope Pius XI calls the Church and all people of goodwill to prayer and penance as the primary remedies for the economic depression, social unrest, and rising atheism gripping the world. He diagnoses the root cause of these evils as greed and the rejection of God, and urges a return to the Sacred Heart of Jesus through reparation, mortification, and trust in divine providence.

## Historical Context

Written on May 3, 1932, at the height of the Great Depression, the encyclical follows Pius XI's earlier social encyclicals *Quadragesimo Anno* (1931) and *Nova Impendet* (1931). Mass unemployment, economic collapse, and the rise of militant atheism — particularly communism — were creating what the Pope saw as an existential crisis for civilization. The document was issued on the feast of the Invention of the Holy Cross and was timed to precede the Feast of the Sacred Heart, which the Pope had recently elevated to an octave.

## Key Themes

- **Greed as root of all evil:** The economic crisis is traced to the "insatiable greed for earthly goods" and the concentration of wealth in few hands.
- **Atheism as the central danger:** The rise of organized, militant atheism (communism) is portrayed as a satanic attack on God and the moral order, far more dangerous than the isolated unbelief of earlier ages.
- **Prayer and penance as the primary weapons:** Human means and social reforms are insufficient; only prayer and voluntary mortification can defeat the "mystery of iniquity."
- **Reparation to the Sacred Heart:** The Feast of the Sacred Heart is proposed as a moment for the whole Church to unite in supplication and acts of reparation for sins against God.
- **Universal solidarity:** The Pope calls not only Catholics but all who acknowledge God to unite against the enemies of religion.
- **Peace through moral order:** True peace cannot come from treaties or economic plans alone, but only from the restoration of God's law in public and private life.

## Significance

The encyclical is a bridge between Pius XI's social teaching and his devotional theology, linking concrete economic analysis with a call to supernatural means. It reinforced the importance of the Sacred Heart devotion in Catholic life and set the stage for subsequent papal condemnations of communism and secularism. Its insistence that the crisis was fundamentally spiritual — a choice between God and the rejection of God — anticipated the Church's later confrontations with totalitarian ideologies.
