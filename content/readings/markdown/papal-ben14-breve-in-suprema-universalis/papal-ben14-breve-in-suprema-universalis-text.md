# In Suprema Universalis

**La Santa Sede**

---

> *Note: This document was translated from the original Italian into English. Translation provided by DeepL (July 2026).*

BRIEF OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV IN SUPREMA UNIVERSALIS To the Venerable Brothers, Patriarchs, Primates, Metropolitans, Archbishops, and to all Bishops who are in peace and communion with the Apostolic See. Pope Benedict XIV. Venerable Brothers, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. We, though without any merit of our own, have been raised—by the ineffable richness of divine goodness—to the highest office of governing the Universal Church, as defenders of the true faith and guardians and guarantors of ecclesiastical discipline. We desire above all to protect, against the laxity of those who fast, Lenten fasting, and to restore, as far as possible with God’s blessing, the original observance of this fast, which—and no Catholic can dispute this—has always and everywhere, from the very beginnings of the Church, counted among the fundamental points of proper discipline. In Our letter in the form of a Brief addressed to Your Fraternities on the 30th of the month of May just past, We took care to stir up the zeal of Your Fraternities to ensure that, on this point, neither the desired relief from the anxieties of Our apostolic concern nor a salutary remedy against the decline of the most sacred fast would be lacking. Mindful, moreover, of that paternal pontifical charity which knows how to support the frailty of the weak and to become weak with the weak, since We have judged that, at times, for just cause or when a most grave and urgent necessity requires it, one must, with apostolic generosity, grant a dispensation from fasting, so we have also prescribed (among other things) that only one meal be observed, without any additional meals consisting of permitted or prohibited foods. 1. In this regard, we have learned that some, using a wholly human logic worthy only of those who shun Christian penance, seek to persuade themselves and others that the single meal, without any additional meals consisting of permitted or prohibited foods, must be observed above all when, due to an urgent and very grave necessity, a multitude of people are dispensed from it without distinction, and not when, for just cause and upon the advice of one or another physician, individuals are dispensed. 2. To completely remove from everyone’s mind this conviction, which is foreign to our thinking, We declare and establish that all those who happen to be dispensed—for whatever reason, whether indiscriminately as a large group due to an urgent and very grave necessity, or as individuals for a just reason and upon the advice of both physicians, during Lent and at other times of the year, and on days when the consumption of meat, eggs, and dairy products is prohibited—all of them, without exception, must observe a single meal without any additional meals, consisting of both permitted and non-permitted foods, unless there is a certain and serious risk of illness that requires otherwise. We expressly prescribe and command the same to individual members of the faithful of both sexes through individual letters—always in the form of a brief—which grant them, due to the illness from which they suffer, permission to eat during Holy Lent and on other days set aside for fasting. 3. Therefore, we pray and implore Your Fraternities in the Lord to admonish the people entrusted to your care with care and paternal concern, urging them to take with diligence the medicine of holy fasting, so well suited to healing those wounds from which every person suffers due to human weakness. Warn them that this is not a matter of little consequence, but of the utmost gravity—namely, to observe and maintain the fast that purifies both soul and body. Encourage them, therefore, with the hope of heavenly blessings, to which the sufferings of the present moment are incomparable; indeed, the momentary and slight discomfort—not of tribulation, but of a small act of self-denial—brings an immeasurable amount of glory in heaven. Athletes who compete exercise self-control in everything in order to obtain a perishable crown: may the soldiers of the Christian army, who are so inept and delicate, be ashamed if they shun communion with the cross of Christ, who offers the hope of an imperishable crown, prepared for those who struggle as they ought. As a sign and pledge of this crown, we bestow upon Your Fraternities, with great affection, the Apostolic Blessing, to be imparted in Our name to your people as well. 4. We further decree that to extracts or copies of this Letter—including printed versions—signed by hand before a notary public and bearing the seal of a person holding ecclesiastical office, be accorded everywhere the same faith that would be given—and could be given—to this Letter if it were presented or shown. Given in Rome, at Saint Mary Major, under the Fisherman’s Ring, on August 22, 1741, in the second year of Our Pontificate.
