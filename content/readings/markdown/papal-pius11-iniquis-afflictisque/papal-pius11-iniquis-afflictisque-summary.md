## Summary

*Iniquis Afflictisque* is a 1926 encyclical by Pope Pius XI denouncing the severe persecution of the Catholic Church in Mexico under the anti-clerical policies of the Mexican government. It protests laws that stripped the Church of legal status, expropriated its property, banned religious orders, restricted the number of priests, outlawed religious education, and subjected clergy to civil registration and surveillance. Pius XI commends the bishops, priests, and laity for their heroic passive resistance and calls on Catholics worldwide to pray for divine intervention.

## Historical Context

Written in the wake of the Mexican Revolution (1910–1920), the 1917 Constitution of Mexico enshrined sweeping restrictions on the Church — including state ownership of all church buildings, prohibition of religious orders, a ban on clerical political rights, and state control over worship. President Plutarco Elías Calles, a vehemently anti-clerical secularist, enforced these provisions with escalating severity through 1925–26, culminating in the "Calles Law" of July 1926, which imposed draconian penalties on religious exercise. In response, the Mexican bishops suspended all public worship on July 31, 1926, and the Cristero War — a popular uprising of Catholic peasants — would erupt shortly after. This was also the era of Church-state conflicts in France, Portugal, and the Soviet Union, making it a global period of Catholic struggle against secularist regimes.

## Key Themes

- **The Church as a perfect society** — Pius XI insists the Church possesses God-given rights (jure divino) that no state may legitimately override, and that the Mexican laws are not true laws because they contradict divine and natural law.
- **Religious liberty and the primacy of conscience** — The faithful must "obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29) when civil law commands what is sinful; passive resistance is a positive moral duty.
- **The unity and authority of the hierarchy** — The encyclical repeatedly praises the bishops' united leadership and the clergy's near-unanimous fidelity (only one or two priests defected out of roughly four thousand).
- **The laity's active role** — Organizations like the Knights of Columbus, the National Sodality of Fathers of Families, the Federation for the Defense of Religious Liberty, and the Union of Catholic Women of Mexico are celebrated as models of organized Catholic Action.
- **Redemptive suffering and martyrdom** — Persecution is framed as a participation in Christ's cross; the blood of martyrs strengthens the Church and testifies to the faith.
- **Historical gratitude** — The Church is the source of Mexico's civilization and culture; persecution by an "ungrateful government" is a betrayal of this legacy.
- **Our Lady of Guadalupe** — Pius XI entrusts the resolution of the conflict to Mary, the patroness of Mexico, and calls for prayer rather than violent reprisal.

## Significance

*Iniquis Afflictisque* is the definitive papal condemnation of the Calles-era persecution and served as a rallying point for Catholics worldwide. It articulated a robust theology of Church-state relations at a moment when the modern secular state was aggressively encroaching on religious life. Along with Pius XI's later encyclicals *Quadragesimo Anno* (1931) on social order and *Mit brennender Sorge* (1937) on Nazism, it forms part of his body of teaching defending the Church's freedom against totalizing state ideologies. The encyclical also provided moral and spiritual support to the Cristero movement, though it stopped short of endorsing armed resistance, and it remains a key reference in Catholic teaching on religious liberty and conscientious objection.
