## Summary

This papal brief (a less formal type of papal document) issued by Pope Pius V on July 25, 1571, confirms the royal foundation of the University of Lima (later known as the Universidad Nacional Mayor de San Marcos) and grants it all the privileges, exemptions, and immunities previously enjoyed by the University of Salamanca. The document notes that the university was originally established by Emperor Charles V and subsequently upheld by King Philip II of Spain, with the aim of educating the population of the New World in the Catholic faith.

## Historical Context

Written during the height of the Spanish colonization of the Americas, this breve reflects the Church's missionary priorities in the post-Tridentine period. The Council of Trent (1545–1563) had recently concluded, reinforcing Catholic doctrine and the importance of clerical education in response to the Protestant Reformation. In Peru, Dominican friars and other religious orders had been evangelizing the indigenous population, and Lima — the "City of Kings" — had grown into one of the principal cities of the Spanish viceroyalties. The pope references the conversion of "the greater part" of the native peoples and the need to confirm them in the faith through formal education.

## Key Themes

- **Education as evangelization**: The university is explicitly established to instruct a people "ignorant of letters" in both the humanities and sacred sciences, and to confirm them in the Catholic faith.
- **Papal confirmation of royal patronage**: Pius V ratifies the foundation already made by the Spanish Crown, reflecting the patronato real (royal patronage) system under which the Spanish monarchs exercised extensive control over Church affairs in the Americas.
- **Privileges of Salamanca**: By extending the rights of the University of Salamanca — one of Europe's most prestigious medieval universities — to Lima, the pope places the New World institution on equal footing with the Old World centers of learning.
- **The Dominican role**: The university was initially established in a house of the Dominican Order dedicated to Our Lady of the Rosary, highlighting the religious orders' central role in early American higher education.

## Significance

*Exponi nobis nuper* is a foundational document for higher education in the Americas. Together with the earlier royal decree of Charles V (1551), this papal confirmation established the University of Lima as the first officially constituted university in the New World (though it was preceded in operation by the earlier studia of Santo Domingo and Mexico City). By granting it the privileges of Salamanca, Pius V ensured that degrees earned in Lima would carry the same canonical weight as those from Europe, integrating the Americas into the medieval university system and shaping the intellectual and clerical elite of the Spanish Empire for centuries.
