# Multiplices inter · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius IX renews earlier papal condemnations of Freemasonry and related secret societies. He recalls the measures of Clement XII, Benedict XIV, Pius VII, and Leo XII, including penalties for membership and assistance. He attributes revolutionary upheavals and attacks on the Church partly to these organizations and criticizes rulers and churchmen whom he considers insufficiently vigilant.

The allocution disputes the claim that such societies are harmless charitable associations. Pius points to secrecy, religious diversity, binding oaths, and threatened penalties, contrasting them with the publicly stated rules and works of Catholic associations. He also rejects the argument that civil toleration renders ecclesiastical prohibitions ineffective, asserting that papal pastoral authority does not depend on secular permission. He confirms the prohibitions and their existing penalties for Catholics everywhere, urges enrolled members to leave, and prays for their return.

## Key themes

- Reaffirmation of previous prohibitions and penalties
- Critique of secrecy and the claim of merely charitable purposes
- Ecclesiastical authority independent of civil toleration
