## Summary

*Humani Generis Redemptionem* (1917) is Pope Benedict XV's apostolic letter on the preaching of the Word of God. It laments that despite the high number of preachers, Christian morals are in decline, and traces the problem to three root causes: unworthy preachers, wrong intentions (vainglory or greed), and faulty methods. The pope calls bishops to vet preachers rigorously for both learning and holiness, insists that no one preach without a bishop's mandate, and holds up St. Paul as the model of selfless, prayerful, and uncompromising proclamation of Christ crucified.

## Historical Context

The document appeared during the turmoil of World War I (1917), a period Benedict XV called "the suicide of civilized Europe." The Church faced rising secularism, the spread of modernism (which the pope's predecessor Pius X had combated), and a drift away from supernatural faith even among the clergy. Many pulpits had become platforms for rhetorical display or political commentary rather than sound doctrine. Benedict XV, elected in 1914, sought to reform preaching as a key remedy for the crisis of faith and morals.

## Key Themes

- **Preaching is the bishop's paramount duty** — Bishops are the primary preachers; others may preach only with a lawful mission from the bishop.
- **The preacher must have a divine vocation** — Neither natural talent nor desire suffices; the candidate must possess proven virtue and solid learning, akin to the standard for confessors.
- **The right intention** — Preachers must seek not applause, popularity, or money, but the salvation of souls and the glory of God.
- **The right method** — Preaching should be simple, scriptural, and fearless. Preachers must not avoid hard truths (sin, judgment, eternity) for fear of offending hearers.
- **St. Paul as the model preacher** — He was prayerful, conformed to God's will, patient in hardship, and preached "Jesus Christ and Him crucified" without rhetorical tricks.
- **Holiness over eloquence** — A saintly life is more powerful than studied delivery; grace won through prayer, not technique, gives preaching its effect.

## Significance

The encyclical reaffirmed the Tridentine and patristic vision of preaching as a pastoral rather than a rhetorical art, and influenced subsequent magisterial teaching on homiletics. It is cited in later documents such as *Evangelii Nuntiandi* (Paul VI) and the *Catechism of the Catholic Church*. Its insistence that preaching be rooted in Scripture, sound doctrine, and personal holiness remains a standard for Catholic preaching to this day.
