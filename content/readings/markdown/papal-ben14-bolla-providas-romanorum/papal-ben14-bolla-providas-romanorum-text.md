# Providas Romanorum

**La Santa Sede**

---

> *Note: This document was translated from the original Italian into English. Translation provided by DeepL (July 2026).*

BULL OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV PROVIDAS ROMANORUM Bishop Benedict, servant of the servants of God. For perpetual remembrance. We deem it our duty, through a new exercise of Our authority, to uphold and confirm—since just and serious reasons so require—the prudent laws and penalties of Our Predecessors, the Roman Pontiffs: not only those laws and sanctions whose force, whether through the passage of time or human neglect, we fear may be weakened or extinguished, but also those that have recently come into force and full effect. 1. In fact, Clement XII, Our Predecessor of blessed memory, in his Apostolic Letter of April 28 in the year of the Incarnation of the Lord 1738, the eighth year of his pontificate —a letter addressed to all the faithful and beginning with the words “In eminenti”—permanently condemned and prohibited certain societies, unions, gatherings, assemblies, conventicles, or associations commonly called the Freemasons or des Francs Maçons, or known by other names, which were already widespread in certain countries at that time and are now increasing ever more. He forbade all Christians, individually and collectively (under penalty of excommunication incurred ipso facto without any declaration, from which no one could be absolved by others, except on their deathbed, except by the Roman Pontiff pro tempore) from attempting or daring to join such societies, to propagate them, to lend them favor or shelter, to conceal them, to enroll in them, to associate with them, or to participate in them, and anything else as is set forth more fully and extensively in the same letter. Here is the text. 2. “Clement, Bishop, servant of the servants of God. To all the faithful, greetings and Apostolic Blessing. Placed, by the will of Divine mercy, though unworthy, in the eminent See of the Apostolate, in order to fulfill the duty of pastoral care entrusted to Us with assiduous diligence and care, to the extent permitted by Heaven, We have turned our thoughts to those matters by which—having closed the door to errors and vices—the integrity of the Orthodox Religion may be preserved above all, and in these most difficult times the dangers of disorder may be removed from the entire Catholic world. It is already known to Us through public report that certain societies, unions, gatherings, assemblies, secret meetings, or associations—commonly called the Freemasons or des Francs Maçons, or by other names depending on the variety of languages, in which, through a strict and secret alliance, according to their own laws and statutes, men of every religion and sect unite, content with a certain affected appearance of natural honesty. Such societies, through a solemn oath taken on the Holy Scriptures and under the threat of severe penalties, are bound to maintain an inviolable silence regarding the matters they carry out in secret. But since it is the nature of crime to reveal itself and generate the commotion that exposes it, it follows that the aforementioned societies or secret groups have aroused such suspicion in the minds of the faithful, such that, for honest and prudent men, joining these groups is tantamount to staining oneself with the infamy of wickedness and perversion: if they did not act wickedly, they would not hate the light so decisively. This reputation has grown to such an extent that these societies have already been outlawed by secular rulers in many countries as enemies of the kingdoms, and have been providentially eliminated. We, therefore, reflecting on the most grievous harm that such societies or secret gatherings generally cause not only to the peace of the temporal Republic but also to the spiritual health of souls—since they are in no way in accordance with either civil or canon law— instructed by the divine words to watch day and night, as a faithful and prudent servant entrusted with the Lord’s household, so that this class of men may not plunder the House like thieves, nor ruin the Vineyard like foxes; that is to say, so that they may not corrupt the hearts of the simple nor secretly harm the innocent; with the aim of closing the path which, if left open, could allow crimes to be committed with impunity; for other just and rational reasons known to Us, on the advice of some of Our Venerable Brother Cardinals of the Holy Roman Church, and also motu proprio, with certain knowledge, mature deliberation, and the fullness of Our Apostolic authority, We decree that the aforementioned Societies, Unions, Gatherings, Assemblies, Associations, or Conventicles of Freemasons must be condemned and prohibited, as We hereby condemn and prohibit, by this Our Constitution, to be valid in perpetuity, the aforementioned Societies, Unions, Gatherings, Assemblies, Associations, or Secret Societies of Freemasons or des Francs Maçons, or whatever other name they may be called. Therefore, strictly and by virtue of holy obedience, We command all and each of the faithful, of whatever state, rank, condition, order, dignity, or preeminence, whether Laity or Clergy, both Secular and Regular, even if worthy of special and individual mention and citation, that no one dare or presume, under any pretext or guise, to establish, propagate, or promote the aforementioned Societies of Freemasons or des Francs Maçons or otherwise named; to harbor or conceal them in their own homes or elsewhere; to enroll in or join them; to provide them with the means, authority, or opportunity to convene in any place; to supply them with anything or even to offer in any way advice, assistance, or favor, whether openly or in secret, directly or indirectly, on one’s own behalf or on behalf of others, as well as to urge, induce, provoke, or persuade others to join or participate in such societies, or in any way to benefit or favor them. Indeed, everyone must absolutely abstain from the aforementioned societies, unions, gatherings, meetings, groupings, or secret societies, under penalty of excommunication for all offenders, as stated above, to be incurred ipso facto and without any declaration, from which no one may be absolved—except on the point of death—by anyone other than the Roman Pontiff pro tempore. We further decree and command that both the bishops, the higher prelates, and the other local ordinaries, as well as the Inquisitors appointed in any place to combat heretical wickedness, proceed to conduct inquisitions against offenders of any status, rank, condition, order, dignity, or preeminence, and that they repress and punish them with the same penalties with which they punish those suspected of heresy. Therefore, we grant and confer full authority upon them, and upon each of them, to proceed and conduct an inquisition against the aforementioned offenders, and to imprison and punish them with the appropriate penalties, invoking, if necessary, the assistance of the secular authorities. We further decree that copies of this document—even if printed—signed by a public notary and bearing the seal of a person holding ecclesiastical office, shall be accorded the same credibility as would be accorded to the original letter if it were presented or shown. Therefore, no one, under any circumstances, is permitted to violate or, with reckless audacity, contradict this page of Our declaration, condemnation, commandment, prohibition, and interdiction. If anyone should dare to do so, let them know that they will incur the wrath of Almighty God and of the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul.” 3. But since, as far as We have been informed, some have had no qualms about publicly asserting and spreading the claim that the aforementioned penalty of excommunication imposed by Our Predecessor is no longer in effect because the relevant Constitution was not subsequently confirmed by Us—as if it were necessary for Apostolic Constitutions to remain valid to require the explicit confirmation of the successor; 4. and since it has been suggested to Us by certain pious and God-fearing persons that it would be most useful to eliminate all the subterfuges of the slanderers and to declare the harmony of Our mind with the intention and will of that same Predecessor, by adding to his Constitution the new declaration of Our confirmation; 5. We certainly, up to now, when We have graciously granted absolution from the excommunication incurred—often, first and foremost, during the past Jubilee Year— to many of the faithful who were truly repentant and sorrowful for having transgressed the laws of that very Constitution, and who assured Us from the heart that they would completely distance themselves from such societies and secret gatherings, and that they would never return to them in the future; or when We granted the Penitentiaries delegated by Us the authority to grant absolution in Our name and by Our authority to those who appealed to the Penitentiaries themselves; and when, with diligent vigilance, We did not fail to ensure that the competent judges and courts would proceed in proportion to the crime committed against those who violated the Constitution itself—which was indeed carried out on several occasions; We have certainly provided arguments that are not only plausible but entirely evident and beyond doubt, through which the dispositions of Our mind and Our firm and deliberate will—in accordance with the censure imposed by the aforementioned Clement Predecessor—should have been understood. If a contrary opinion were to spread concerning Us, We could certainly dismiss it and entrust Our cause to the just judgment of Almighty God, uttering those words that were once recited during sacred services: “Grant, O Lord, we beseech You, that We may not heed the slanders of perverse minds, but having trampled that very perversity underfoot, we implore You not to allow Us to be afflicted by unjust calumnies or ensnared by cunning flattery, but rather that We may love what You command.” This is recorded in an ancient Missal attributed to Saint Gelasius, Our Predecessor, and which the Venerable Servant of God, Cardinal Giuseppe Maria Tommasi, inserted into the Mass entitled “Against Slanderers.” 6. However, lest it be said that We had imprudently omitted anything, in order to easily eliminate the pretexts for false calumnies and silence their voices; having first heard the counsel of some of Our Venerable Brother Cardinals of the Holy Roman Church, We have decreed to confirm the very same Constitution of Our Predecessor, word for word, as specifically set forth above, which is to be considered the most comprehensive and effective of all: We confirm, validate, and renew it, and We will and decree that it shall have perpetual force and effect by Our certain knowledge, in the fullness of Our Apostolic authority, in accordance with the terms of the said Constitution, in every respect, as if it had been promulgated by Our own initiative and under Our authority, and had been published by Us for the first time. 7. Indeed, among the most serious reasons for the aforementioned prohibitions and condemnation set forth in the Constitution cited above, there is one by virtue of which men of any religion or sect may associate with one another in such societies and secret gatherings; it is clear what harm this may cause to the purity of the Catholic Faith. The second reason is the strict and impenetrable vow of secrecy, by virtue of which what takes place in these gatherings is concealed; to this, one may rightly apply the maxim that Cecilius Natalis, in the case of Minucius Felix, invoked in a very different matter: “Honest deeds always seek the light of day; wicked deeds are kept secret.” The third reason is the oath by which they pledge to observe this secrecy inviolably, as if it were permissible for anyone, when questioned by a legitimate authority, to use the excuse of some promise or oath to evade the obligation to confess everything that is sought, in order to ascertain whether anything contrary to the stability and laws of Religion and the Republic is being done in such secret gatherings. The fourth reason is that these societies oppose civil sanctions no less than canonical ones, given that, under civil law, all associations and gatherings formed without public authority are prohibited, as stated in the Pandects (Book 47, Title 22, De Collegiis et corporibus illicitis), and in the famous letter (No. 97 of Book 10) by C. Pliny Cecilius, who reports that it was prohibited by his edict, in accordance with the Emperor’s command, for the Eteriae to be held—that is, for societies and assemblies to exist and meet without the Prince’s authorization. The fifth reason is that in many countries the aforementioned societies and associations have already been outlawed and banned by laws enacted by secular rulers. Finally, the last reason is that prudent and honest men condemned the aforementioned societies and associations: in their judgment, anyone who joined them incurred the stigma of wickedness and perversion. 8. Finally, the same Predecessor, in the aforementioned Constitution, exhorts the Bishops, the Higher Prelates, and the other local Ordinaries not to neglect to invoke the assistance of the secular authorities whenever necessary for the enforcement of this provision. 9. All these matters, even individually, are not only approved and confirmed by Us, but are also recommended and enjoined upon ecclesiastical superiors; but We ourselves, out of due Apostolic solicitude, by this Our Letter invoke and earnestly seek the support and assistance of Catholic Princes and secular authorities —since these same Princes and Rulers are chosen by God as defenders of the faith and protectors of the Church—so that they may take it upon themselves to act in the most effective manner to ensure that the Apostolic Constitutions are accorded due reverence and the most absolute obedience. This was recalled by the Fathers of the Council of Trent (session 25, chapter 20), and long before that, Emperor Charlemagne had admirably declared it in his Capitulations (title I, chap. 2), in which, after commanding all his subjects to observe ecclesiastical sanctions, he added these words: “We cannot in any way know how those who show themselves to be unfaithful to God and disobedient to His priests can be faithful to us.” Consequently, he ordered all the governors and ministers of his provinces to compel everyone to render due obedience to the laws of the Church. Furthermore, he imposed very severe penalties on those who failed to do so, adding, among other things: “Those who are found to be negligent and in violation of these matters (which must not happen) should know that they will not retain their honors in our Empire, even if they are our own children; nor will they have a place in the Palace, nor will they have any association or fellowship with us or with our faithful, but rather they will suffer punishment in distress and hardship.” 10. We further decree that copies of this document—even if printed—signed by a public notary and bearing the seal of a person holding ecclesiastical office, shall be accorded the same credibility as would be accorded to the original letter if it were presented or shown. 11. Therefore, no one, under any circumstances, shall be permitted to violate or, with reckless audacity, contradict this document of Our confirmation, innovation, approval, command, invocation, request, decree, and will. If anyone should dare to do so, let him know that he will incur the wrath of Almighty God and of the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul. Given in Rome, at Santa Maria Maggiore, on March 18 in the year of the Incarnation of the Lord 1751, the eleventh year of Our Pontificate.
