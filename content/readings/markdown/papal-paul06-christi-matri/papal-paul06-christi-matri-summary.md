## Summary

*Christi Matri* ("Mother of Christ") is an encyclical letter issued by Pope Paul VI on September 15, 1966, calling on the faithful to pray the Rosary during October for peace in the world. Written amid the escalation of the Vietnam War and the nuclear arms race, the document blends a fervent plea to world leaders to lay down arms with a spiritual appeal to the Virgin Mary, invoked as the Queen of Peace and Mother of the Church, to intercede for humanity.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written in the shadow of the Vietnam War, which by 1966 had intensified into a bloody and protracted conflict. It also reflects Cold War anxieties: the nuclear arms race, nationalism, racism, and revolutionary upheavals. Paul VI had personally addressed the United Nations on peace in October 1965, and *Christi Matri* was issued exactly one year later. The document also follows the conclusion of the Second Vatican Council (1962–65), during which Paul VI had formally declared Mary the Mother of the Church.

## Key Themes

- **Peace as an urgent moral imperative** — Paul VI pleads directly with world leaders to negotiate before events overtake the possibility of peace, warning of "immense harm and enormous slaughter."
- **Mary as Queen of Peace and Mother of the Church** — The encyclical grounds its Marian devotion in the council's teaching and in patristic tradition, emphasising Mary's role as intercessor for the whole human family.
- **The Rosary as a weapon for peace** — Paul VI recommends the Rosary as the primary prayer for October, describing it as effective in warding off evils and fostering Christian life.
- **A universal call to prayer** — The pope designates October 4, 1966, as a worldwide Day of Prayer for Peace, asking especially for the participation of children, the sick, and the suffering.
- **Conscience of leaders** — Rulers are reminded of their "very grave obligation in conscience" before God and history to pursue a peace built on justice and freedom.

## Significance

*Christi Matri* is one of the most pointedly political encyclicals of the 1960s, showing Paul VI's commitment to using the papal office as a moral voice in international affairs — a role he had already dramatised at the UN. It also solidifies the title "Mother of the Church," which Paul VI had proclaimed at Vatican II, and reinforces the Rosary as a prayer for peace, a theme later popes (especially John Paul II) would take up in the face of global conflict. The encyclical stands as a document of its anxious moment — the Cold War and Vietnam — while offering a lasting pastoral model: prayer and public exhortation in tandem.
