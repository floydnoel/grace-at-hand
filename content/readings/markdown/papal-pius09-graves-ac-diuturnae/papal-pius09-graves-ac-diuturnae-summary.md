## Summary

*Graves Ac Diurnae* is an 1875 encyclical by Pope Pius IX addressed to bishops, clergy, and the Swiss people, warning against the "Old Catholic" schism. The Pope condemns the sect's rejection of Vatican Council dogmas (especially papal supremacy), exhorts the faithful to shun their worship and ministers, decries anti-Catholic marriage laws enacted by Swiss civil authorities, and calls for steadfast adherence to legitimate bishops and the Apostolic See.

## Historical Context

Written in the wake of the First Vatican Council (1869–1870), which defined papal infallibility, a group of dissenting Catholics — mostly German- and Swiss-speaking theologians and laity — broke away, forming the "Old Catholic" Church. They rejected the new dogmas and sought state support. In Switzerland, the *Kulturkampf* between the Church and liberal civil authorities was intense: the government favored the Old Catholics, granted them use of churches and universities (notably the University of Bern), and enacted civil marriage laws contrary to canon law. Pius IX writes to reinforce Catholic identity under political and ecclesiastical pressure.

## Key Themes

- **Warning against schism and false shepherds** — Old Catholics are condemned as heretics who use deceit, claiming to preserve ancient faith while rejecting papal primacy and Vatican I.
- **Duty to shun** — The faithful must avoid Old Catholic worship, ministers, writings, and any association, treating them as thieves and wolves.
- **Defense of marriage** — Swiss civil marriage laws are attacked as contrary to Church teaching and jurisdiction; bishops are urged to instruct the faithful on the sacrament's sanctity.
- **Unity and obedience** — Clergy and laity are called to rally behind their legitimate bishops, who hold mission from the Apostolic See, and to persevere in faith amid persecution.
- **Papal authority and care** — The Pope exercises his supreme apostolic office to protect the universal flock from error, reiterating earlier condemnations (1873 letter).

## Significance

The encyclical is a pointed expression of Pius IX's post-Vatican I ecclesiology: an uncompromising defense of papal supremacy, rejection of state interference in Church affairs, and a call to separatism from schismatic bodies. It reflects the broader *Kulturkampf* struggles across Europe and illustrates the hardening of Catholic identity around ultramontanism in the late 19th century. While primarily a pastoral and disciplinary document for Switzerland, it stands as a strong reaffirmation of the binding authority of ecumenical councils and the necessity of communion with Rome.
