## Summary

Inter Caetera is a papal bull issued by Pope Alexander VI in 1493 that divided the non-Christian world between Spain and Portugal. It granted Spain sovereignty over all lands discovered and yet to be discovered west and south of a line drawn 100 leagues west of the Azores and Cape Verde, and authorized the Spanish crown to conquer those territories and convert their inhabitants to Catholicism.

## Historical Context

Written on May 4, 1493, just months after Christopher Columbus returned from his first voyage to the Americas, the bull reflects the Age of Exploration and the competition between Spain and Portugal for overseas territories. The "Reconquista" of Granada from Muslim rule had just been completed in 1492, and the papacy saw itself as the arbiter of Christian princes' claims. The document assumes papal temporal authority over non-Christian lands (the "Donation of Constantine" tradition) and frames colonization as a missionary enterprise. It was followed by the Treaty of Tordesillas (1494), which moved the demarcation line further west.

## Key Themes

- **Papal supremacy over temporal rulers**: The pope claims authority to "give, grant, and assign" undiscovered lands to monarchs "by the fullness of our apostolic power."
- **Missionary justification for colonization**: The expansion of Spanish rule is explicitly tied to evangelization — bringing "barbarous nations" to the Catholic faith.
- **The Doctrine of Discovery**: Lands not already under Christian rule are considered empty for the taking; prior Indigenous sovereignty is not recognized.
- **Excommunication as enforcement**: Anyone who trades with or visits these lands without Spanish permission faces automatic excommunication.
- **Providentialism**: Columbus's discovery is portrayed as divine reward for Spain's defeat of the Muslims in Granada.
- **Racial and cultural hierarchy**: Indigenous peoples are described as "living in peace, going unclothed, not eating flesh" and "sufficiently disposed to embrace the Catholic faith."

## Significance

Inter Caetera is one of the most consequential documents in world history. It established the legal and theological framework for European colonization of the Americas, later cited in U.S. and international law as the "Doctrine of Discovery." The bull shaped centuries of Spanish colonial policy and Indigenous dispossession. In the modern era, it has been sharply criticized; Pope Francis formally repudiated the Doctrine of Discovery in 2023, stating that it was "not a teaching of the Catholic Church."
