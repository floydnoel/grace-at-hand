ENCYCLICAL CERTIORES EFFECTI OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV To the Venerable Brothers, Patriarchs, Primates, Archbishops, and Bishops of Italy. Pope Benedict XIV. Venerable Brothers, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. Having been informed that in certain dioceses of Italy a controversy is spreading regarding the obligation by which priests who celebrate Mass are compelled, between one service and the next, to administer the Eucharist to those faithful who present themselves prepared to receive it, and who ask to participate in the Sacrifice at which they are present; consequently, and in good conscience, we have deemed it appropriate to address this growing evil through the teaching contained in this Apostolic letter, so that this evil may not progress further and may not be a cause of scandal for the faithful. Therefore, Venerable Brothers, we address these words to you so that, in this matter as well, you may not neglect any part of your pastoral duties. 1. First of all, we must state that it should not occur to anyone that private Masses, in which the priest alone receives the Sacred Host, thereby lose the nature of the true, perfect, and complete bloodless sacrifice instituted by Christ the Lord and must therefore be considered unlawful. For the faithful are well aware that the Most Holy Council of Trent, relying on the doctrine preserved by the Church’s perennial tradition, condemned Luther’s new, false, and contrary opinion: “If anyone has said that Masses in which the priest alone receives Sacramental Communion are unlawful, and therefore to be abolished, let him be excommunicated” (Council of Trent, sess. 22, chap. 6, and can. 8). 2. However, since the ancient practice and discipline of the Church—according to which the faithful present at Masses were accustomed to participate without distinction in the Most Holy Sacrifice in public churches—are in every respect in keeping with the teaching and example of Christ the Lord, we repeat the words of the Council itself in the same spirit in which they were uttered by it: “The Most Holy Synod desires that the faithful present at individual Masses receive Communion not only with spiritual affection but also through the sacramental act of approaching the Eucharist, so that they may derive more abundant fruit from that Most Holy Sacrifice.” And may heaven grant that the people of our time, too, might be inflamed with the same fervor of Christian piety that burned within the faithful of the early centuries, and that they might eagerly flock to the public Sacred Table—not merely being present at the solemnity of the Holy Mysteries, but being devoutly eager to participate in them. Certainly, there is no act in which Bishops, parish priests, and confessors can devote their efforts more fruitfully than in encouraging the faithful to cultivate that purity of mind by which they are made worthy to approach the Sacred Table frequently and to participate not only in spirit, but also by approaching the Sacraments and the Sacrifice which the Priest, as a public minister of the Church, offers not only for himself but for them and in their name. 3. Although those who participate in the same Sacrifice include not only those to whom the celebrating priest distributes, during the same Mass, a portion of the Victim he offers, but also those to whom the priest offers the Eucharist that he usually reserves for himself, nevertheless, the Church has never forbidden, nor does she now forbid, the priest from granting the devout and just request of those who, while attending Mass, ask to receive the comfort of the same Sacrifice that they offer in the manner appropriate to them; on the contrary, she approves of and desires that this detail not be overlooked, and she should rebuke those priests who, through their own fault or negligence, deny the faithful such participation. 4. But since it is necessary that everything in the Christian Church be in order and properly arranged, pastors should exercise their care and vigilance to ensure, on the one hand, that the devotion of the faithful is not deprived of this privilege of approaching and participating in the Sacrifice; but that, on the other hand, both of these faculties be distributed in such a way that no disorder arises in the other praiseworthy arrangements, and that no confusion or even scandal ensues. Therefore, Pastors must admonish the faithful: those who wish to partake of the Holy Table (and this must be warmly encouraged, as we have said) should seek to find the time, place, and circumstances in which they may fulfill their desires without hindering the rites of devotion. The faithful, by showing themselves docile to these exhortations from their Pastors, will avoid complaining, as if they had suffered an injustice, if at times—given the moment, the place, and the people— the Bishop does not deem it appropriate for the celebrating Priest to distribute the Eucharist to those present; for certainly at that very moment they can easily approach the same Table prepared for all in many other places. 5. Bishops and parish priests will easily persuade the faithful of this, helping them understand that, under the Church’s current discipline, the participation they desire will certainly be not more difficult but easier for them. In fact, while the ancient custom required that only one Mass be celebrated in each church, at which the faithful attended and participated (and indeed wished to receive the Eucharist and the other sacraments only from their own pastors), in these times, however, given the large number of officiating priests, places, and altars where the rite is celebrated publicly, anyone can easily gain access to the Holy Table and be admitted to the Sacred Banquet. If, moreover, those same faithful, despite admonitions, were to insist inappropriately on receiving the Eucharist under circumstances of time, place, and persons that the Bishop had objected to, based on the authority of the Roman Ritual, then such a request on their part—neither just nor reasonable—would reveal a rebellious and unyielding spirit, eager for disorder and therefore entirely unprepared to receive the Eucharist with due devotion. 6. As long as the Pastors behave in this way toward the Faithful, and as long as the Faithful lend such a compliant ear to the words of the Pastors, there will undoubtedly arise that perfect peace and harmony by which the head and the members must be firmly united; and those ill-advised controversies—which serve only to stir up turmoil and scandal, to the detriment of the true fruit of souls, to which nothing should be dearer to the Shepherd—will come to an end. Let us therefore turn to the words of the Apostle Paul to the Corinthians: “I urge you, brothers, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, to speak the same language and to avoid divisions among yourselves; and furthermore, to be of one mind and one heart” (2 Cor 13:11). Venerable Brothers, as we wish these words to reach you through this Apostolic Letter, as a wish for happiness and a pledge of paternal love, we impart to you the Apostolic Blessing. Given in Rome, at Saint Mary Major, on November 13, 1742, the third year of Our Pontificate.
