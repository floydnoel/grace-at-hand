# Ad gravissimum · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius IX protests uprisings in Bologna, Ravenna, Perugia, and other parts of the Papal States during war between Catholic nations. He recounts the removal of papal emblems, the expulsion of representatives of his government, and appeals for the king of Sardinia to assume authority. He attributes these events to organized conspiracy, outside assistance, and agitation rather than legitimate popular action.

The allocution defends the papacy’s temporal sovereignty as the providential means of securing the pope’s independence in governing the universal Church. On that basis, Pius declares the revolutionary acts invalid and states that those who participated in usurping papal jurisdiction have incurred ecclesiastical censures. At the same time, he prays for their repentance and restoration to communion. He looks to European rulers, including the French emperor, to help preserve papal territory and restore public order.

## Key themes

- Revolutionary removal of papal authorities and symbols
- Temporal sovereignty as a safeguard of papal independence
- Condemnation of usurpation alongside prayer for repentance
