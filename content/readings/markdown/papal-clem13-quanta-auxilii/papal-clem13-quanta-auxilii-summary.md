# Quanta auxilii · English editorial summary

## Summary

Clement XIII addresses the French bishops’ general assembly as an opportunity for concerted action on the Church’s difficulties. Their firsthand knowledge of their provinces should guide practical decisions to protect religion, restore ecclesiastical life, preserve public tranquility, and serve the salvation of souls. He leaves detailed remedies to their judgment while assuring them of his prayers and support.

The pope urges a combination of humility, courage, and firmness in defending episcopal authority. Patience must neither become timidity nor strength become arrogance. He encourages the bishops to lay the Church’s grievances before the king, whom he expects to remedy abuses once properly informed. He also explains his restraint toward the bishops of Alès and Angers: rather than immediately proceeding further against them, he hopes their colleagues’ counsel will bring them back into ecclesiastical concord. The letter thus joins defense of authority with a preference for reconciliation within the episcopate.

## Key themes

- Collective episcopal judgment grounded in local knowledge
- Courage joined with humility and patience
- Appeal to royal protection and reconciliation of bishops
