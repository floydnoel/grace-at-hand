## Summary

*Divini Illius Magistri* ("Of the Divine Master") is Pope Pius XI's 1929 encyclical on Christian education. It lays out the Catholic Church's teaching on who has the right and duty to educate children — the Church, the family, and the State — and in what order of priority. The document argues that all true education must be directed toward humanity's supernatural end (God) and that the Church, by divine mandate, holds the primary and supreme role in education, especially in matters of faith and morals.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was published in 1929, a period of intense educational debate worldwide. Secular and nationalist ideologies were on the rise, with states increasingly asserting control over schooling (e.g., Mexico's Calles government, Fascist Italy's education reforms, anti-Catholic secularization in France). Pius XI wrote partly in response to these encroachments on Church and family educational rights. The document also coincided with his Sacerdotal Jubilee, and he explicitly dedicates it to youth. It is considered the first magisterial document to systematically treat the Church's teaching on education.

## Key Themes

- **Three societies, three rights**: Education belongs primarily to the Church (by divine mandate), then to the family (by natural law), and finally to the State (for the common temporal good). Each has distinct but harmonious roles.
- **The Church as supreme teacher**: The Church has an exclusive, infallible teaching authority in faith and morals and an independent right to establish schools and supervise all education of the faithful.
- **Family rights are prior to the State**: Parents hold an inalienable, God-given right to educate their children, which predates and limits civil authority. The State may supplement but not supplant the family.
- **Rejection of naturalism and laicism**: The encyclical condemns pedagogical systems that rely solely on human nature, exclude supernatural grace, or deny original sin — including "sex education," coeducation, and exaggerated nationalism in physical training.
- **The proper end of education**: Education must form the whole person (body and soul, natural and supernatural) for his or her ultimate end: God.
- **Harmony of Church and State**: Drawing on Leo XIII, Pius XI affirms the two powers are supreme in their own spheres and must cooperate, not conflict.

## Significance

*Divini Illius Magistri* remains the foundational Catholic document on education. It was cited extensively during the Second Vatican Council (especially in *Gravissimum Educationis*) and continues to shape Catholic educational philosophy, canon law, and the Church's advocacy for parental rights in international forums. Its teaching on the priority of the family over the State in education is invoked in debates over school choice, religious liberty, and the limits of government authority in schooling worldwide.
