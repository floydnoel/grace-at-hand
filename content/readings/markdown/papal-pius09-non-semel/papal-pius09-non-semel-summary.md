# Non semel · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius IX denies accusations that his reforms encouraged revolutionary movements or that he secretly fomented Italian unrest. He explains his amnesty and administrative changes as measures for his subjects’ welfare, consistent with reforms European rulers had themselves recommended to the Holy See. Public enthusiasm and subsequent disorder, he argues, do not establish that he intended revolution.

The allocution declares that papal troops were sent to defend the security and integrity of the Papal States and that the pope does not intend to join a war against the German peoples. His universal pastoral office requires equal paternal concern for every nation. He also rejects proposals to make him president of a new Italian republic and denies ambition for expanded temporal rule. Instead, he urges loyalty to legitimate rulers, reconciliation, and peace. The address ends by deploring publications that attack religion, damage reputations, or inflame civil conflict, and by asking for prayer for peoples and rulers.

## Key themes

- Explanation of early reforms and rejection of revolutionary intent
- Defensive purpose of papal troops and refusal of war
- Universal pastoral responsibility above competing national claims
