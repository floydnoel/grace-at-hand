## Summary

*Pergrata* is an encyclical from Pope Leo XIII to the bishops of Portugal, written to affirm the recent concordat between the Holy See and the Portuguese kingdom. The pope praises Portugal's historical fidelity to the Catholic faith, especially its role in spreading Christianity through its overseas empire, and outlines the pressing reforms needed to restore the Church's strength in Portugal — particularly the revival of seminaries, the formation of a virtuous clergy, and the fight against harmful printed media.

## Historical Context

Issued in 1886, *Pergrata* reflects the aftermath of decades of political turbulence in Portugal: the liberal revolution of 1820, the suppression of religious orders (1834), and the wider European trend of anti-clericalism that had left the Portuguese Church weakened — with a shortage of priests, closed seminaries, and diminished religious life. The encyclical follows a diplomatic agreement (concordat) between the Holy See and the Portuguese crown to regularise Church affairs, especially in the Portuguese colonies (India, Africa, Oceania). Leo XIII, pursuing a broader diplomatic strategy of *rapprochement* with modern states, seeks to rebuild the institutional Church in Portugal through episcopal unity, clerical education, and lay Catholic action.

## Key Themes

- **Clerical formation and seminaries:** The pope insists that each diocese must have a properly functioning seminary, with priests formed in sound doctrine, sacred and canonical studies, and genuine holiness of life.
- **Unity among Catholics:** Leo XIII strongly urges the bishops to overcome partisan political divisions; he warns against conflating the Catholic faith with any particular political party.
- **The role of the laity and the press:** The encyclical addresses the danger of bad literature and calls for the creation of Catholic newspapers to counter the spread of impiety and corruption.
- **The vocation of Portugal as a Catholic nation:** The pope presents Portugal's imperial history as providential — its explorers and kings were motivated by zeal to spread the faith, not merely by ambition.
- **Episcopal authority and obedience:** Clergy are reminded that they remain under the bishop's authority at all times, whether in ministry or teaching.
- **The Church and the state:** Leo XIII affirms that concord between spiritual and civil power is the foundation of good governance, and that the Catholic religion deserves legal protection as the legitimate faith of Portugal.

## Significance

*Pergrata* exemplifies Leo XIII's characteristic strategy of engaging modern states through diplomacy rather than confrontation. It applies the principles of his earlier encyclicals — especially *Immortale Dei* (1885) on the Christian constitution of states — to a specific national context. While not among his most famous documents, it is a valuable case study in how the late-19th-century papacy sought to rebuild local Churches after decades of revolutionary upheaval, by insisting on clerical education, episcopal unity, and lay mobilisation through a virtuous press.
