# Multis gravibusque · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius IX surveys several threats to Catholic life. He first protests Baden’s unilateral abandonment of a recently ratified concordat and replacement of it with legislation he considers hostile to Church freedom. Against treating the Church as an association possessing only rights granted by the state, he asserts its divinely founded authority and worldwide pastoral mission. He then rejects a French pamphlet proposing a national church separated from papal authority and praises bishops’ attachment to Rome.

The allocution also condemns restrictions and confiscations in Italian territories, interference with education and marriage, and the imprisonment or displacement of clergy. Turning eastward, Pius recounts persecution of Christians in Korea, China, Cochinchina, and Tonkin, and the continuing insecurity of Syrian Christians after massacres. He praises their constancy and the charitable assistance sent to Syria, including funds entrusted to him by Catholics. His conclusion joins resistance to ecclesiastical oppression with prayer for relief, perseverance, and the conversion of opponents.

## Key themes

- Baden’s concordat and the Church’s independent rights
- Rejection of a national church separated from Rome
- Persecution in East Asia and charitable relief for Syria
