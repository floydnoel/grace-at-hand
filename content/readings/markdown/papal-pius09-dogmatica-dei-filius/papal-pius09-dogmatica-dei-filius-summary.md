# dogmatica Dei Filius · English editorial summary

## Summary

Promulgated by Pius IX with the approval of the First Vatican Council, Dei Filius sets out Catholic teaching on God, revelation, faith, and reason. God is distinct from the world, freely creates from nothing, and sustains creation by providence. Human reason can know God with certainty through created things; supernatural revelation discloses God’s saving purpose and mysteries beyond reason’s unaided reach. Revelation is transmitted in Scripture and apostolic tradition, with the Church entrusted to guard and interpret it.

Faith is a supernatural gift and a free assent to God’s authority as revealer, enabled by grace. It is neither simply a conclusion of natural reasoning nor a blind impulse: miracles, prophecy, and the Church provide grounds of credibility. Divine and Catholic faith embraces revealed truths proposed by solemn definition or the ordinary and universal magisterium.

The constitution distinguishes faith and reason by their principles and objects while denying any real contradiction between them, since both come from God. Reason can deepen understanding without exhausting divine mysteries. The sciences retain their own methods within their proper domains; growth in theological understanding must preserve the meaning of dogma. Corresponding canons reject materialism, pantheism, rationalism, and other positions opposed to these teachings.

## Key themes

- Free creation and the natural knowledge of God
- Revelation in Scripture and apostolic tradition
- Grace, freedom, and rational grounds for faith
- Distinct but harmonious orders of faith and reason
