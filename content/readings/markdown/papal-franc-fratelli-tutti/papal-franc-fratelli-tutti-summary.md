## Summary

*Fratelli Tutti* (Italian for "Brothers All") is Pope Francis's third encyclical, a sweeping meditation on human fraternity and social friendship addressed to all people of good will. Taking its title and inspiration from St Francis of Assisi, the document draws on the parable of the Good Samaritan to argue that every person is called to become a neighbour to others, transcending borders, cultures, and beliefs in the pursuit of a more just, inclusive, and peaceful world.

## Historical Context

Released on 4 October 2020 (the feast of St Francis), the encyclical was signed at the tomb of St Francis in Assisi and written against the backdrop of the Covid-19 pandemic. It follows Pope Francis's 2019 meeting with Grand Imam Ahmad Al-Tayyeb in Abu Dhabi, where they jointly signed the *Document on Human Fraternity*. The letter responds to rising nationalism, xenophobia, economic inequality, digital polarisation, climate degradation, and the fragmentation of global cooperation — all laid bare by the pandemic's revelation that "no one is saved alone."

## Key Themes

- **The Good Samaritan as a social paradigm** — neighbourliness is not about proximity but about choosing to care for the wounded, whoever they are, regardless of ethnicity, religion, or nationality.
- **Critique of a "throwaway culture"** — societies discard the unborn, elderly, poor, disabled, and migrants; true human dignity demands that no one be treated as expendable.
- **Universal destination of goods** — private property is a secondary right subordinate to the principle that the earth's goods exist for everyone; private ownership carries a social mortgage.
- **Solidarity and political charity** — love must take institutional form; politics is a "lofty vocation" and "one of the highest forms of charity" when directed to the common good.
- **Dialogue as the path** — authentic social dialogue, grounded in truth and open to difference, is the alternative to ideological polarisation, media manipulation, and fanaticism.
- **Rejection of war and the death penalty** — war is declared a "failure of politics and of humanity"; the death penalty is "inadmissible"; nuclear deterrence is condemned as unsustainable.
- **Openness to migrants** — nations are called to "welcome, protect, promote and integrate" migrants; the right not to emigrate is affirmed alongside the obligation to receive the stranger.
- **Religions at the service of fraternity** — the deepest source of fraternity is the transcendent dignity of the human person as a child of God; religious leaders must be "artisans of peace."

## Significance

*Fratelli Tutti* is the first papal encyclical co-authored in spirit with a Muslim leader and the first to declare the death penalty "inadmissible" in a magisterial document of this rank. Together with *Laudato Si'*, it forms a dyad of social encyclicals addressing the twin crises of ecological and social breakdown. Its insistence that "everything is connected" — that peace, justice, migration, economics, and the environment are inseparable — extends Catholic social teaching's horizon beyond intra-ecclesial concerns to a genuinely global, interreligious vision of the human family's common destiny.
