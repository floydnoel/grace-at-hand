## Summary

*Respicientes*, issued by Pope Pius IX on November 1, 1870, is a vehement protest against the seizure of the Papal States by the Kingdom of Piedmont (Italy). The Pope recounts the long history of Piedmontese aggression, rejects offers to surrender temporal power, and formally declares the occupation of Rome (September 20, 1870) and all accompanying usurpations null and void. He also pronounces excommunication upon the invaders and their supporters.

## Historical Context

The document was written at the culmination of the Italian *Risorgimento*, the movement for Italian unification. After decades of gradual annexation of papal territories, King Victor Emmanuel II's forces breached Rome's walls at Porta Pia on September 20, 1870, taking advantage of the Franco-Prussian War (which forced the withdrawal of French troops protecting the Pope). The Pope withdrew to the Vatican, refusing to accept the loss of temporal sovereignty — a stance that became the "Roman Question," unresolved until the Lateran Treaty of 1929.

## Key Themes

- **Temporal power as divinely ordained**: The Pope argues that the Papal States were granted by God to guarantee the freedom and security of his spiritual office.
- **Injustice of usurpation**: All acts of conquest are declared null, void, and without legitimacy before God and man.
- **Excommunication**: Invoking the authority of Peter and Paul, the Pope imposes excommunication on the invaders, commanders, and collaborators.
- **Pope as a "prisoner"**: The document establishes the enduring motif of the Pope as a captive unable to freely exercise his pastoral office.
- **Rejection of false conciliation**: The Pope vows never to accept any agreement that diminishes the Church's rights, even under duress.
- **Suffering Church, trusting in God**: Despite persecution, the Church is assured of divine deliverance.

## Significance

*Respicientes* is one of the most important nineteenth-century assertions of papal temporal sovereignty. It condemned the liberal-nationalist unification of Italy from the papal perspective and formally inaugurated the "Prisoner in the Vatican" period (1870–1929). Its excommunications, rejection of the plebiscite, and refusal to negotiate set the terms of Catholic opposition to the Italian state for nearly sixty years. Though the Lateran Treaty eventually resolved the territorial question, *Respicientes* remains a landmark expression of the papacy's resistance to the secularization and dispossession of Church property in the modern era.
