# Hanno sempre · English editorial summary

## Summary

Gregory XVI transfers the custody and ordinary administration of the Capitoline Museum's sculpture collection to Rome's civic conservators while reserving higher papal oversight through the prefect of the apostolic palaces. He recalls earlier popes' contributions to the collections, their educational value, and Rome's loyalty to the Holy See as reasons for this grant.

The act gives the civic magistracy authority to appoint the antiquarian president and other staff, while protecting existing employees' salaries and privileges and reserving final dismissal to the pope. It requires a legal handover of objects and keys, careful observance of museum regulations, and continued care for public usefulness and access. Egyptian objects are excluded because they are to be transferred to a new Vatican museum, with other works gradually substituted at the Capitol. Arrangements are also made to cover expenses beyond the civic administration's available funds.

## Key themes

- Civic custody under continuing papal oversight
- Staff appointments and protection of existing employees
- Transfer of Egyptian objects to a new Vatican collection
- Preservation, public benefit, and museum funding
