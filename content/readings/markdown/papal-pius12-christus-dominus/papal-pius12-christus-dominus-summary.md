## Summary

*Christus Dominus* (1953) is an apostolic constitution by Pope Pius XII that substantially mitigates the Church's traditional law on the Eucharistic fast — the requirement that both priests and the faithful abstain from food and drink from midnight before receiving Holy Communion. While reaffirming the importance of the fast, the document introduces several relaxations: natural water no longer breaks the fast; the sick may take liquid or medicine; priests facing late hours, arduous ministry, or long journeys may take non-alcoholic drink up to one hour before Mass; and local ordinaries may permit evening Masses (after 4 p.m.) with a reduced three-hour fast from solids and one hour from non-alcoholic beverages.

## Historical Context

The document was issued in the aftermath of World War II and amid rapid social and economic change. Industrial shift-work, longer commutes, crowded school schedules, and the increased burdens on an undersized clergy made the traditional "fast from midnight" rule unworkable for many. Bishops from around the world petitioned Rome for relief, and the wartime indults (special permissions) had shown that a more flexible approach was both possible and pastorally necessary. Pius XII sought to regularise these scattered concessions into a single, coherent discipline.

## Key Themes

- **Pastoral adaptation to modern conditions**: The document explicitly acknowledges new social realities — factory shifts, night work, mothers' household duties, school schedules, and the strain on a clergy stretched thin by mission work and postwar needs.
- **The primacy of Eucharistic piety**: Every relaxation is framed not as a dilution of reverence but as a means to *increase* frequent communion and devotion to the Blessed Sacrament.
- **Gradual rather than total abrogation**: The fast is *mitigated*, not abolished. The midnight-fast rule stays in force for those who can keep it; natural water is permitted for all; the sick, the overworked, and those with serious inconvenience receive specific dispensations.
- **Confessor-guided discretion**: The faithful may not self-dispense — a prudent confessor must judge the gravity of the need and advise accordingly.
- **Evening Mass as a pastoral tool**: Local ordinaries gain the faculty to permit evening Masses on specified days, with reduced fasting requirements, primarily for workers and large gatherings.

## Significance

*Christus Dominus* marked a major turning point in Latin-rite Eucharistic discipline, shifting from a rigid universal norm to a more flexible, pastorally attuned framework. It paved the way for the further relaxations that followed the Second Vatican Council (notably *Sacrosanctum Concilium* and the 1964 instruction *Inter Oecumenici*, which generalised evening Masses and reduced the Eucharistic fast to one hour). The document is a landmark example of the Church adapting immemorial liturgical law to changing circumstances while insisting on the underlying theological value of fasting as an expression of reverence and preparation.
