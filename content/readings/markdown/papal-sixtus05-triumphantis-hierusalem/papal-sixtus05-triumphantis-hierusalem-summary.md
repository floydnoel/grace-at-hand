## Summary

This papal bull from Pope Sixtus V (1588) formally declares St. Bonaventure a Doctor of the Church, ranking him among the preeminent theological doctors alongside St. Thomas Aquinas. It orders the establishment of a college named after St. Bonaventure in Rome, mandates the publication of his complete works from the Vatican press, and prescribes that his feast be celebrated as a double feast on July 14. It also grants a plenary indulgence to the faithful who honor his feast in Bagnoregio, Lyons, or Rome.

## Historical Context

The document was issued on March 14, 1588, during the post-Tridentine period when the Catholic Church was consolidating doctrine in response to the Protestant Reformation. Sixtus V, a Franciscan educated in the Seraphic Order, sought to elevate St. Bonaventure (a 13th-century Franciscan theologian and Cardinal) to the highest rank of theological authority. The bull references the Council of Lyons (1274), where Bonaventure played a key role in the temporary reunion of the Greek and Latin Churches, and builds on earlier efforts by Popes Clement IV, Gregory X, and especially Sixtus IV, who had canonized Bonaventure in 1482. The document also reflects the Church's vigorous defense of scholastic theology against Protestant critiques.

## Key Themes

- **Elevation of Bonaventure as a Doctor of the Church** — ranking him explicitly alongside St. Thomas Aquinas as "two olive trees and two candlesticks" lighting the house of God.
- **Defense of Scholastic Theology** — the bull presents Bonaventure's work as a bulwark against heretical errors and calls for scholastic theology to be "retained, explained, and propagated."
- **Bonaventure's holiness and learning** — detailed account of his life: his entry into the Franciscan Order, his studies under Alexander of Hales, his teaching at Paris, his refusal of the Archbishopric of York, his service as Minister General of the Franciscans, and his pivotal role at the Second Council of Lyons.
- **Papal authority and continuity** — Sixtus V explicitly confirms and extends earlier papal acts (Sixtus IV's canonization, Pius V's honors to Aquinas), presenting an unbroken chain of magisterial teaching.
- **Indulgences and devotional practice** — grants plenary indulgence for those who honor Bonaventure's feast with confession and communion; mandates observance of his feast with a "double" rite.

## Significance

_Triumphantis Hierusalem_ is the papal act that formally recognized St. Bonaventure as a Doctor of the Church (the "Seraphic Doctor"), cementing his place among the Church's foremost theological authorities. By pairing Bonaventure with Thomas Aquinas as complementary lights of scholastic theology, Sixtus V affirmed that the Franciscan intellectual tradition holds equal standing with the Dominican. The bull also provides a window into post-Tridentine papal priorities: the promotion of scholasticism, the centralization of liturgical authority, and the use of indulgences to encourage popular devotion. Bonaventure remains one of the 37 Doctors of the Catholic Church, and his writings continue to be studied as classics of medieval theology and spirituality.
