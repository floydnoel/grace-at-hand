## Summary

*Satis Vobis Compertum* (Latin: "We Have No Doubt") is a 1741 encyclical of Pope Benedict XIV addressing the growing problem of clandestine or "secret" marriages (marriages of conscience) in the Church. The pope recalls the Tridentine decrees requiring public banns, celebration before a parish priest and witnesses, and proper registration, then laments that these provisions are falling into disuse. He catalogues the grave evils that flow from secret marriages — including polygamy, deception of innocent parties, children deprived of legitimacy and inheritance, filial disobedience, and clerics clinging to benefices while married — and gives detailed pastoral instructions to bishops on how to handle requests for secret marriage dispensations, how to maintain sealed registers for such unions, and how to protect the rights of children born from them.

## Historical Context

Benedict XIV was elected pope in 1740, a period when the Church was still implementing the disciplinary reforms of the Council of Trent (1545–1563). Trent's decree *Tametsi* had transformed marriage law by requiring the presence of a priest and witnesses for validity, but clandestine marriages persisted as a widespread abuse across Catholic Europe. The 18th century also saw cultural shifts toward individual autonomy, making secret unions more common. This encyclical, issued just over a year into Benedict's pontificate, reflects his characteristically scholarly and pragmatic approach to governance — he was one of the most learned popes of the early modern period, deeply concerned with both doctrinal integrity and pastoral practice.

## Key Themes

- **Dignity of Matrimony as a public sacrament** — Marriage is not a private arrangement but a sacrament *coram Ecclesia*, to be celebrated openly before the Christian community.
- **Pastoral concern for the vulnerable** — The encyclical dwells at length on harm to children: those born of secret marriages lose legitimacy, inheritance, and even proper parental care.
- **Clandestine marriage as a source of grave sin** — The pope identifies polygamy, deception, concubinage disguised as marriage, and clerical abuse of benefices as direct consequences of secret weddings.
- **Episcopal vigilance and discretion** — Bishops are urged to be prudent in granting dispensations from banns, to investigate the character and circumstances of petitioners, and to weigh whether the couple has legitimate and urgent cause.
- **Administrative rigour with pastoral flexibility** — The encyclical innovates by prescribing a sealed register for secret marriages kept in the episcopal chancery, balancing the need for secrecy with the need for a permanent, legally valid record.
- **Legal protection of children** — Parents of secret marriages must report baptised children to the bishop within 30 days; failure to do so can lead the bishop to make the marriage public for the child's sake.

## Significance

*Satis Vobis Compertum* stands as Benedict XIV's most substantial contribution to marriage discipline. It anticipates later canonical reforms — particularly the 1907 decree *Ne Temere* and the 1917 Code of Canon Law — which would further tighten marriage formalities to eliminate clandestinity, and the 1983 Code's canons on the form of marriage and secret marriages (Canons 1108–1123, 1130–1133). Its lasting innovation, the sealed register for marriages of conscience, remains part of Catholic marriage law to this day. The encyclical also exemplifies Benedict XIV's distinctive governing style: rigorous in doctrine, attentive to human weakness, and practical in its pastoral solutions.
