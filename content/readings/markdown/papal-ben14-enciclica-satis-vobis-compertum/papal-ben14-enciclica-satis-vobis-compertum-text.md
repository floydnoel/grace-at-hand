**La Santa Sede**

ENCYCLICAL SATIS VOBIS COMPERTUM OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV To the Venerable Brothers, Patriarchs, Primates, Archbishops, and Bishops. Pope Benedict XIV. Venerable Brothers, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. We have no doubt, Venerable Brothers, that you are well aware of how the Holy Mother Church has always been vigilant in ensuring that the Sacrament of Matrimony—proclaimed “great” by the Apostle—be celebrated publicly and in the presence of the faithful. To ensure that this would henceforth be observed by all with even greater diligence than in the past, the Holy Council of Trent, following in the footsteps of the Lateran Council held under Innocent III, decreed that from that time forward, prior to the celebration of the marriage, the parish priest of the betrothed couple should proceed, three times over three consecutive feast days, to make the banns in church during Mass; thereafter, in the absence of any legitimate impediment, the ritual celebration should take place in the presence of the congregation, before the parish priest or another priest duly delegated by the parish priest or the Ordinary, and in the presence of two or three witnesses. The Holy Synod also decreed that the parish priest diligently keep a register recording the names of the spouses and witnesses, as well as the date and place of the marriage. 1. These providential provisions, appropriately enshrined by such great authority, seem to be gradually falling into disuse due to the wicked state of the times and have almost lost their force because of the all-too-widespread practice of marriages celebrated in such secrecy as to erase, if possible, even the memory of them and relegate them forever to the darkness of oblivion. It has become customary to celebrate them without the required public banns, in the presence of only the parish priest—or another priest delegated by him—and with only two witnesses summoned by the contracting parties themselves, whose complicity is not suspected by any of them. Often, the ceremony takes place outside the church; sometimes even inside, but with the doors closed, or at a time when no one else is present: knowledge of the marriage, apart from the parish priest, the couple, and the witnesses, is completely unknown to others. 2. Anyone can easily realize—if they focus on the harmful consequences—how far these marriages, commonly called “marriages of conscience,” deviate from the dignity of the sacrament and the provisions of ecclesiastical law, so as to oppose them in the most decisive manner. For it is from this that grave sins arise, especially for those who, setting aside the prohibition of the Divine Judge, after leaving their first wife and marrying secretly, promise to marry another publicly, deceiving her with the hope of a future marriage and inducing her to live together in reprehensible debauchery. Wicked lust so blinds the minds of some that it leads them to enter into a new secret marriage after a first, equally secret marriage that has not yet been dissolved by the death of the previous spouse, thereby committing the grave crime of polygamy. Still others go so far as to commit such grave impudence, in contempt of this great Sacrament, that they do not hesitate to bind themselves shamelessly through polygamy, proceeding to new marriages—whether in public or in private—after the first ones were performed in secret. Consider, then, what grave evils arise from these marriages, to the point that they cannot be tolerated in any way! If, therefore, removing every sign of marriage results in a man living apart from a woman, the consecrated way of life in unity is immediately abolished, and the word of the Lord is nullified: “A man shall be united to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh” (Gen 2:24). If this way of life is maintained, no one can fail to condemn it as reprehensible and scandalous. Nor can the harm caused by the scandal be remedied by celebrating a secret marriage, for the sin is shrouded in darkness and goes unnoticed by all. 3. The harm inflicted upon the resulting children is no less serious. For it often happens that children, separated from their parents and especially from their mother, cannot be raised either in virtue or in a decent manner, but remain abandoned to the whims of chance—unless the parents, contrary to the laws of nature, go so far as to attempt to take their lives with criminal intent. Even if such a heinous crime were to deter the parents, and nature itself were to compel them to nurture and educate their children, other heartbreaking consequences loom over the offspring born of a secret marriage, due to the loss of the family’s assets and property, given the impossibility of taking possession of them—even though the rights of blood demand it—because the secret marriage deprives them of legitimacy and proof of filiation. 4. Secret marriages contracted by children—who are still subject to paternal authority—against their father’s rightful will must also be traced back to this same source of evils, and no one is unaware of the grave inconveniences that result from them. What else? Wickedness has reached such a point that at times people, having been ordained to the minor orders, have dared to retain stipends and benefices intended for divine worship and ecclesiastical offices, even after entering into a clandestine marriage, thereby securing for themselves, in a base manner, a tomb with their ill-gotten wealth. 5. This long series of evils would be better lamented with an abundance of tears than described at greater length. But since, from this vantage point of the Apostolic See, our vigilance demands our full attention, we cannot refrain from appealing to your compassion and zeal by calling upon you, Venerable Brothers, to share in Our anxiety, so that even at night you may watch over the flock entrusted to you, which the sad state of these times is leading to ruin. First of all, therefore, may the not infrequent occurrence of risk make you more cautious in granting dispensations from the banns, which are often requested by those intending to marry for wicked purposes. The Council of Trent provides fully evident reasons for how cautiously and carefully Bishops must act in such circumstances. For in fact (as the Holy Council itself states), if there is any possibility that the marriage might be fraudulently impeded were the banns to be published first, in this case at least one bann should be published, or the ceremony should be conducted in the presence of the parish priest and two witnesses, and subsequently, before the marriage is consummated, the banns should be published in the church, in order to more easily uncover the existence of any impediments. Although the Bishop retains the authority to grant a dispensation from the publication of banns in every case, this authority does not depend exclusively on the will of the one granting it, but is limited by the Council of Trent through laws of prudent discretion and careful judgment: this means requiring a legitimate cause for the dispensation. 6. It is also necessary for you to exercise equal—and perhaps even greater—vigilance to ensure that, after the dispensation is granted, the marriage is not celebrated before the parish priest or before another priest delegated by the parish priest or by you, in the presence of two or three witnesses of proven trustworthiness, for the sole purpose of preventing any news or rumor about the celebration from leaking out. In fact, for this to be accomplished in accordance with the prescriptions of the Sacred Canons, what is required is not just any simple or trivial cause, but a grave and most pressing one. According to the Sacred Tribunal of Our Penitentiary, the circumstance that permits the celebration of such a marriage is recognized first and foremost in the fact that the man and woman, living publicly as husband and wife without anyone suspecting their guilt, persist in a state of secret cohabitation. Anyone can easily see how inappropriate it would be, in order to free them from the snare of perdition, to compel them to publicly contract marriage after the banns have been read. We have deemed it appropriate to propose this practice to you, not because the dispensation is appropriate only in the case mentioned—since there are also other similar and perhaps even more urgent cases in which a dispensation must be granted— but because the duty of your pastoral office is to carefully examine the legitimate and urgent causes for a dispensation, so as to prevent marriages celebrated in secret from having the heartbreaking consequences that we have listed with deep sorrow. 7. For this reason, therefore, we urge you—and we strongly urge you—to conduct a thorough investigation of the individuals requesting a secret marriage. You must ascertain whether their character, social standing, and circumstances are such as to justify it; whether they are acting of their own volition or under the influence of others. If they are children of a family whose marriage is rightly opposed by their father, it would not be fitting for the episcopal office you hold to offer these children an easy opportunity for disobedience. If they are clerics who have been ordained to minor orders and have received pensions or ecclesiastical benefices, their detestable possession of such benefits while married can be curbed by appropriate measures. First and foremost, however, your care must ensure—before a license for a secret marriage is granted—that the contracting parties present clear, reliable documents attesting to their marital status, free from any fraud, in order to avert the danger of polygamy on the part of those with dishonest intentions. 8. As for the minister of the secret marriage, we wish that this duty be entrusted to the parish priest of whichever of the two contracting parties whose knowledge of the individuals, experience, and long practice are presumed to have made him more competent than any other priest from outside the parish. If, however, specific circumstances arise that seem to require a priest other than the parish priest—in the presence of an urgent and serious cause—you are to choose one who is recommended for his integrity, doctrinal knowledge, and ability to fulfill the duty. 9. Whoever is appointed to officiate at the marriage must be strictly admonished not to do so unless he has first exhorted the spouses, in the Lord and with paternal love, the spouses, in the Lord and with paternal love, on the necessity of having their unborn children reborn as soon as possible through holy Baptism, and on the fact that they will have to give a strict account to Christ the Judge if they do not acknowledge the legitimacy of their children, fail to instill in them piety and good morals, and fail to allow them to enjoy the temporal goods left by their ancestors in their wills or transmitted by the provident authority of the law. 10. After the celebration of the marriage, the written document—recording the place, date, and those present at the celebration—shall be forwarded without delay to the Bishop by the parish priest or the priest in whose presence the marriage took place. It will then be your specific responsibility to ensure that the document is transcribed into a special register, distinct from the one in which marriages contracted in public are normally recorded, so that it may serve as a lasting record of the event. This register, specially prepared for secret marriages, closed and sealed, is to be carefully kept in your Episcopal Chancery. It may be unsealed, and you shall permit it to be opened only with your consent in the event that another marriage of the same type must be transcribed into it, or if the administration of justice so requires, or if a truly necessary document is requested by someone who cannot obtain supporting documents elsewhere. You must, however, remember that, once the matter has been resolved, it must be closed and sealed again as before. The certificates and attestations of secret marriages, drawn up by the parish priest or the priest acting in his stead and submitted for your review, shall be transcribed into the aforementioned register exactly as they are, word for word, by a person appointed for this purpose who enjoys a clear and universal reputation for integrity and esteem. You are to keep these certificates and attestations, stored in a secret and secure place. 11. In the event that a child is born of a marriage of the type described, the child is to be cleansed by the saving waters of Baptism in the same church where Baptism is administered to other children without distinction. And since it is obvious that, in order to keep the secretly contracted marriage hidden, no mention may be made of the parents and their names must be deliberately omitted from the baptismal register, we decree and expressly order that, through the efforts of the baptized child’s father—or, in the event of his death, through the efforts of the mother—the child be reported to you. This declaration shall be made directly by the parents themselves, or by means of a letter written in their own hand, or through a trustworthy person chosen by them, in order to ensure with absolute certainty that the child baptized in that place and on that date, even if the parents’ names were omitted or fictitious names were used, is legitimate even if born of a secret marriage. To ensure that this is not forgotten once it has been brought to your attention, it must be faithfully recorded in a register by the person delegated to record secret marriages. The register in which the baptized and the names of both parents are to be recorded—even if separate from the marriage register—must be kept in the Bishop’s Chancery with the same care and precautions, locked and sealed, in accordance with the same procedures prescribed for the register of secret marriages. 12. Since there will inevitably be those who do not heed the voice of their own conscience and fail to comply with these Our injunctions, they shall be punished with penalties proportionate to their offenses. Indeed, since We know well from experience that in these particular cases people, lowering their gaze to the ground, become more reluctant out of human deference and are led astray from the path of righteous conduct, We command that you disclose and make known such secret marriages, if you have certain knowledge that a child born of a secret marriage has been baptized without the parents’ names being mentioned, and no notification has been given to you—as was required—by the parents within thirty days from the date of birth. 13. So that they may not accuse their pastors of abuse of power, defamation, and breach of confidentiality, you must take the utmost care to ensure that the parish priest or other priest delegated to celebrate the secret marriage clearly warns them that the celebration of such a marriage is granted to them under these conditions and by this specific agreement: that the children to be born must not only be reborn through Holy Baptism, but must also be reported to the Bishop immediately afterward, with a record of the place and date of the administration of the Sacrament and a precise indication of the parents from whom they were born, as indicated above. Otherwise, even if the marriage was contracted with the Bishop’s promise of secrecy, it will be made public for the sake of the children, to spare them a grave and unacceptable misfortune. 14. Finally, we decree that the documents and certificates pertaining to secret marriages and the children born of them—extracted from these registers entrusted to your custody as specified above—shall have the same legal validity as those derived from other parish registers of Baptisms and Marriages. 15. We decree, Venerable Brothers, that Our prescriptions, in these adverse circumstances, be scrupulously observed by you for the salvation of souls and the safeguarding of ecclesiastical discipline, which, due to the growing wickedness of men, must always endure and fear new harm. Moreover, with this letter of Ours, We do not intend to set aside those more effective remedies which your prudence recognizes as suitable for this ever-resurgent evil, to be combated by every means in accordance with your pastoral duty. At the same time, as a sign of paternal love and benevolence, We bestow upon you the Apostolic Blessing. Given in Rome, at Saint Mary Major, on November 17, 1741, in the second year of Our Pontificate.
