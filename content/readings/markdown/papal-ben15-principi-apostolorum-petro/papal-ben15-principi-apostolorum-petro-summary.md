## Summary

This encyclical, issued by Pope Benedict XV on October 5, 1920, formally declares St. Ephrem the Syrian — a fourth-century deacon, poet, theologian, and Scripture scholar from Edessa — a Doctor of the Universal Church. The document presents St. Ephrem as a model of sanctity, learning, and pastoral charity, and offers him as a special patron and exemplar for the Eastern Churches in the aftermath of World War I.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written shortly after the end of World War I, a period of profound political and social upheaval, especially in the East, where many communities were emerging from Ottoman rule and striving to re-establish national and religious identity. Benedict XV had already issued an encyclical marking the fifteenth centenary of St. Jerome, and he draws a parallel between Jerome (as a light of the West) and Ephrem (as a light of the East). The document is also a direct response to petitions from the Eastern Catholic patriarchs — the Syrian, Maronite, and Chaldean patriarchs — who urged the Pope to bestow the title of Doctor on Ephrem, seeing it as a gesture of honor and unity for the Eastern churches.

## Key Themes

- **Primacy of Peter and the Roman See**: The encyclical opens with an extended meditation on Peter's primacy and the authority of the Apostolic See, emphasizing that the ancient Eastern Fathers (Basil, Athanasius, Chrysostom) appealed to Rome in times of crisis.
- **St. Ephrem as a model of orthodox teaching and biblical exegesis**: Ephrem is praised for grounding his preaching and teaching in Sacred Scripture and for never departing from the Church's constant interpretation.
- **Use of poetry and music for evangelization**: Ephrem composed hymns to counter heretical teachings, earning the title "lyre of the Holy Spirit," and his liturgical innovations influenced both Eastern and Western traditions.
- **Charity and service to the poor**: The encyclical highlights Ephrem's dramatic intervention during a famine in Edessa, where he used his eloquence to persuade the wealthy to feed the starving, and personally nursed the sick.
- **Marian devotion and Eucharistic faith**: Ephrem's writings on the Virgin Mary's sinlessness and on the Real Presence in the Eucharist are quoted at length.
- **Unity of Eastern and Western Churches**: The Pope expresses his desire for the reunion of separated Eastern churches with Rome, grounded in the common patristic heritage exemplified by Ephrem.

## Significance

The encyclical is the formal act by which St. Ephrem the Syrian was declared a Doctor of the Universal Church, making him one of the few Eastern Doctors and a bridge figure between Eastern and Western Christianity. It reflects Benedict XV's broader irenic policy toward the Eastern churches — affirming the legitimacy of their liturgical customs and canonical traditions while extending the papal hand of unity. The document also serves as a pastoral exhortation for the post-war reconstruction of Christian society, urging fidelity to Scripture, devotion to the Eucharist and the Virgin Mary, and the integration of patriotism with the universal call of the Church.
