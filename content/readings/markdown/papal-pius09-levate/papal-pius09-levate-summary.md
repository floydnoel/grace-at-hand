## Summary

*Levate* (1867) is an encyclical in which Pope Pius IX laments the persecution of the Church in Italy under the Piedmontese government and in the Russian Empire and Polish Kingdom, where Catholic dioceses were being suppressed, bishops exiled, and ecclesiastical authority trampled. He calls on bishops worldwide to organize public prayers and grants plenary indulgences to those who participate.

## Historical Context

Written in 1867, the encyclical reflects the mounting pressure on the Papal States from the Italian unification movement (Risorgimento), led by the Kingdom of Piedmont-Sardinia, which was encroaching on Rome itself. Simultaneously, after the failed January Uprising (1863–64), the Russian Empire intensified its suppression of Catholic institutions in Poland and the western provinces, dissolving dioceses (Podlachia, Kamieniec), replacing them with a state-controlled "Roman Catholic Ecclesiastical College" in St. Petersburg, and forcing clergy into schism. Pius IX, having already issued the *Syllabus of Errors* (1864), saw these events as part of a global assault on the Church's freedom.

## Key Themes

- **Persecution of the Church by civil powers** — both the Piedmontese government in Italy and the Russian Empire in Poland are accused of systematically undermining Catholic hierarchy, seizing property, and suppressing dioceses.
- **Defense of papal primacy and episcopal jurisdiction** — Pius IX condemns the Russian-established "Ecclesiastical College" as a schismatic instrument that subordinates Church governance to a secular minister, violating the constitution of the Church.
- **Unity and communion with the Holy See** — the Russian government's effort to sever communication between Polish Catholics and Rome is described as an attempt to drive the faithful into schism.
- **Prayer as the Church's weapon** — in the face of overwhelming military and political force, the pope exhorts the faithful to three days of public prayer, attaching indulgences (including plenary indulgence) to encourage participation.
- **Suffering of ordinary faithful** — the encyclical highlights the plight of laypeople, widows, orphans, and the poor caught in these conflicts.

## Significance

*Levate* is a snapshot of the Church under siege in the mid-19th century, illustrating the papacy's increasing reliance on spiritual authority (prayer, indulgences, appeals to bishops worldwide) in the face of territorial and political losses. It foreshadows the final collapse of the Papal States (1870) and the subsequent *Roman Question*, while also documenting the longer arc of Catholic–Orthodox conflict under the Russian Empire. The encyclical's emphasis on papal jurisdiction and its rejection of state interference in Church governance anticipates the more developed ecclesiology of the First Vatican Council (1869–70), particularly *Pastor Aeternus* on papal primacy.
