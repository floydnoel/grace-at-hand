## Summary

*Commissum Divinitus* is an encyclical issued by Pope Gregory XVI in 1835, addressed to the clergy of Switzerland, condemning the resolutions of the Baden Conference of 1834. The document rejects attempts by civil authorities to assert control over ecclesiastical matters, including doctrine, discipline, the sacraments, and the governance of the Church. It upholds the exclusive spiritual jurisdiction of the Church and the primacy of the pope.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written in response to a gathering of lay and civil officials held in Baden, in the Swiss canton of Aargau, in January 1834. This assembly produced articles that sought to subordinate the Catholic Church in Switzerland to state authority — granting secular powers oversight of synods, seminaries, clerical appointments, and even aspects of sacramental discipline. These actions reflected the broader 19th-century trend of Josephinism and state-controlled church reform movements across Europe. Gregory XVI, a staunch defender of papal authority and traditional ecclesiology, reacted forcefully to prevent the spread of these ideas in Switzerland and beyond.

## Key Themes

- **Church and State**: The Church is divinely instituted and entirely independent of civil government; secular authorities have no jurisdiction over spiritual matters.
- **Ecclesiastical Power**: The Church holds both a teaching power (*magisterium*) and a governing power, given directly by Christ to the apostles and their successors (bishops and the pope), not to lay rulers.
- **Papal Primacy**: The pope is the visible head of the Church, possessing not merely primacy of honor but full jurisdictional authority over the universal Church; bishops are subject to him.
- **Condemnation of Indifferentism**: The document rejects mixed marriages and the notion that religious differences are unimportant, calling the underlying attitude "indifferentism" — absurd and impious.
- **Sacramental Independence**: Civil authority must not interfere in the administration of the sacraments, especially marriage, which is a divinely instituted sacrament governed solely by the Church.
- **Unity and Obedience**: All Catholics must remain united with the Holy See; separation from Peter's successor is separation from Christ.

## Significance

*Commissum Divinitus* stands as a forceful 19th-century assertion of the Church's spiritual sovereignty against the encroachment of state power — a key theme that would be developed further in Pope Gregory XVI's *Mirari Vos* (1832) and later in Pius IX's *Syllabus of Errors* (1864) and *Quanta Cura* (1864). It reaffirmed papal primacy and the hierarchical constitution of the Church at a time when liberal and nationalist movements across Europe sought to reduce ecclesiastical influence. While addressed to a specific local conflict in Switzerland, its principles became part of the broader magisterial defense of Church freedom in the modern era.
