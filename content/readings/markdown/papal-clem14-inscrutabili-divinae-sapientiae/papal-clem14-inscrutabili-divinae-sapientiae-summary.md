## Summary

This encyclical, *Inscrutabili Divinae Sapientiae* (By the Inscrutable Counsel of Divine Wisdom), issued by Pope Clement XIV in 1769, proclaims a universal Jubilee at the outset of his pontificate. It grants a plenary indulgence to all the faithful who fulfill specified spiritual works — visiting designated churches, fasting, confession, Communion, and almsgiving — within a two-week period. The document also authorizes confessors to absolve even reserved sins and commute vows (except religious vows and chastity), and provides accommodations for the sick, imprisoned, and otherwise impeded.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was issued on December 12, 1769, in the first year of Clement XIV's pontificate. It follows the ancient custom of popes opening their reign with a Jubilee year, drawing on the treasury of grace. Clement XIV reigned during a turbulent period: tensions between Catholic monarchies and the Holy See were high, and the question of the Suppression of the Jesuits (which Clement would eventually decree in 1773) was already a pressing issue. The encyclical reflects the Church's traditional penitential and indulgence practices on the eve of major structural changes.

## Key Themes

- **Papal consciousness of unworthiness:** Clement humbly acknowledges his elevation as due to God's inscrutable wisdom, not his own merits, expressing fear at the gravity of his task.
- **Plenary indulgence as a spiritual gift:** The indulgence is offered broadly — to laity and clergy, religious and secular, men and women — as a means of grace for the faithful.
- **Works of piety as conditions:** The indulgence requires concrete acts: church visits, fasting (Wednesday, Friday, Saturday), confession, Communion, and almsgiving, embedding forgiveness in sacramental and charitable practice.
- **Broad confessional faculties:** Confessors are empowered to absolve even sins and censures ordinarily reserved to the Holy See, and to commute vows, reflecting the extraordinary grace of a Jubilee.
- **Pastoral solicitude for the impeded:** The sick, prisoners, cloistered religious, and others unable to meet the conditions receive commutation through their confessor — a hallmark of pastoral equity.
- **Reservation of certain matters:** Despite the broad faculties, the pope explicitly does not dispense from irregularities, defects of character, or censures of those unreconciled, preserving ecclesiastical discipline.

## Significance

*Inscrutabili Divinae Sapientiae* stands as Clement XIV's opening act as pope, establishing a tone of humility, generosity of grace, and careful pastoral accommodation. While not one of the more doctrinally landmark encyclicals, it exemplifies the traditional Jubilee indulgence mechanism — a practice rooted in medieval Catholic piety — and shows how the popes adapted it to the needs of the universal Church. It also foreshadows the tension between reform and tradition that would define Clement's brief pontificate.
