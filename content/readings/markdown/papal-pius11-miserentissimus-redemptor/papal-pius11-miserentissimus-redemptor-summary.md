## Summary

*Miserentissimus Redemptor* ("Our Most Merciful Redeemer") is a papal encyclical issued by Pope Pius XI in 1928 that establishes and mandates the annual observance of the Feast of the Sacred Heart with a solemn act of **reparation** — making amends for sins committed against Christ's love. Building on the earlier consecration to the Sacred Heart and the Feast of Christ the King, the Pope commands that a specific Prayer of Reparation be recited in every church on the Feast of the Sacred Heart. The document presents reparation not merely as an optional devotion but as a duty flowing from justice and love, rooted in the mystical body of Christ and our union with His ongoing passion.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written in the aftermath of World War I and during a period the Pope saw as marked by rampant secularism, persecution of the Church (particularly in Mexico and later in Russia), rising totalitarian ideologies, and moral decay. It draws on the earlier legacy of devotion to the Sacred Heart, especially the revelations to St. Margaret Mary Alacoque in the 17th century, and continues the program of Pius XI's own *Quas Primas* (1925), which instituted the Feast of Christ the King. The Pope was responding to what he considered a crisis of faith, lawlessness, and the marginalization of Christ from public life — "the public sins of nations which resist the rights and the teaching authority of the Church."

## Key Themes

- **Reparation as a corollary of consecration** — consecration gives love back to God; reparation compensates for love that has been withheld or violated.
- **The Mystical Body of Christ** — Christ's passion continues in His Church; the faithful can "fill up what is lacking in the sufferings of Christ" and console His Sacred Heart even now.
- **Sin as a present wound to Christ** — every sin, past or present, renews the Passion "in a certain manner," and likewise our reparation was foreseen by Christ and gave Him solace in Gethsemane.
- **The necessity of expiation** — rooted both in justice (satisfying for offenses against God) and in love (suffering with Christ and offering Him comfort).
- **The "Holy Hour" and Communion of Reparation** — specific devotional practices recommended by Christ to St. Margaret Mary, approved by the Church and enriched with indulgences.
- **Universal scope** — reparation is not just personal but corporate, encompassing the sins of society, the unfaithful, and public authorities.
- **Connection to the Eucharist** — the sacrifice of the Mass is the same as Christ's sacrifice on Calvary, and the faithful must join their own oblation to it.

## Significance

*Miserentissimus Redemptor* elevated the practice of reparation to the Sacred Heart from a popular devotion to a **universally mandated liturgical act**, giving the whole Church a unified voice of expiation on the Feast of the Sacred Heart. It remains a key text for Catholic teaching on the theology of reparation, the Sacred Heart, and the mystical body. Alongside *Quas Primas* (on Christ the King), it forms part of Pius XI's project of re-asserting Christ's sovereignty over all aspects of life — personal, domestic, and civil — against the secularizing currents of the early 20th century. The Prayer of Reparation appended to the encyclical is still recited in many parishes today.
