## Summary

*Etsi Multa* (Latin for "Although Many") is an encyclical issued by Pope Pius IX on November 21, 1873, addressing the persecution of the Catholic Church across Europe — specifically in Italy, Switzerland, and Germany. The Pope catalogues a series of anticlerical laws, expulsions of bishops, suppression of religious orders, and seizures of Church property, presenting them as a coordinated assault on the Church's divine constitution and the papal primacy.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written during the *Kulturkampf* ("culture struggle") in the newly unified German Empire under Otto von Bismarck, where state authorities sought to subordinate the Catholic Church to civil power through a series of restrictive laws. In Switzerland, liberal cantonal governments passed schismatic legislation, expelling Bishop Eugène Lachat of Basel and imposing state control over clerical appointments. In Italy, the capture of Rome in 1870 by the Kingdom of Italy had stripped the Pope of his temporal sovereignty, and the subsequent suppression of the Gregorian University was seen as an attack on the Church's intellectual life. The rise of the "Old Catholic" schism — formed by those who rejected the First Vatican Council's definition of papal infallibility — added an internal front to these external pressures. Freemasonry and other secret societies are identified as the masterminds behind these coordinated attacks.

## Key Themes

- **Church and State:** The encyclical articulates a two-kingdoms theology — civil authority is legitimate in temporal matters but must not usurp the Church's divinely given jurisdiction over faith, morals, and ecclesiastical governance.
- **Papal Primacy:** Pius IX forcefully defends the Petrine office as the foundation of Church unity, insisting that no bishop is legitimate unless in communion with Rome. The Old Catholic "pseudo-bishop" Reinkens is excommunicated for receiving consecration from the schismatic Jansenists of Utrecht.
- **Religious Liberty as a Sham:** Governments that promised religious freedom in treaties are condemned for betraying their word through laws that effectively strangle Catholic practice.
- **Martyrdom and Resistance:** The faithful in Switzerland and Germany are praised for their constancy under persecution, likened to the early martyrs; obedience to unjust laws that violate divine law is not required.
- **Secret Societies:** Freemasonry and similar groups are denounced as a "synagogue of Satan" waging a global war against the Church, and pastors are urged to warn the faithful against their influence.

## Significance

*Etsi Multa* is one of the most forceful papal condemnations of the *Kulturkampf* and remains a key document for understanding the 19th-century struggle between the Catholic Church and the rising liberal-secular state. It crystallizes Pius IX's uncompromising ecclesiology — a Church that does not negotiate its divine constitution, even under state persecution. The encyclical also prefigures later magisterial teaching on the limits of civil law and the duty of conscientious resistance (echoed in documents like *Dignitatis Humanae*). Its condemnation of Old Catholics reinforced the post-Vatican I boundary around papal infallibility, while its warnings about secret societies continued to shape Catholic anti-Masonic teaching well into the 20th century.
