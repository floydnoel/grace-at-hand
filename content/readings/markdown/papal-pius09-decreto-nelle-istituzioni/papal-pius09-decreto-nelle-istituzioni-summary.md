# Decreto Nelle istituzioni · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius IX replaces a merely consultative representation of the papal provinces with two deliberative councils: an appointed upper council and an elected council of deputies. The statute sets rules for membership, restricted electoral qualifications, public sessions, legislation, taxation, budgets, ministerial responsibility, and petitions. It also provides protections for personal liberty and property, equality before ordinary courts, and judicial independence in applying the law. Preventive political censorship of print is abolished, while ecclesiastical censorship remains.

These representative institutions operate within expressly reserved papal sovereignty. Legislation requires approval by both councils and papal sanction after consultation with the cardinals. The councils may not legislate on ecclesiastical or mixed affairs, contradict Church discipline, amend the statute, or debate the Holy See’s foreign religious relations. Catholic profession is required for political rights. The decree also regulates administration during a papal vacancy, guarantees specified ecclesiastical expenditures, and establishes a Council of State.

## Key themes

- Two legislative councils with restricted electoral eligibility
- Civil safeguards, public finance, and ministerial responsibility
- Reserved papal authority and limits on parliamentary competence
- Government during a vacancy of the Apostolic See
