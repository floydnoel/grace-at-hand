## Summary

*Redemptoris Nostri Cruciatus* is a 1949 encyclical of Pope Pius XII concerning the Holy Places in Palestine. Written during Holy Week, it addresses the precarious situation in the region following the 1948 Arab-Israeli war, calling for an internationally guaranteed legal status for Jerusalem and its surroundings to protect Christian sacred sites, and appealing for justice and relief for the many refugees displaced by the conflict.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was issued on Good Friday, April 15, 1949, just months after the 1948 Arab-Israeli war ended with armistice agreements. The newly created State of Israel controlled much of Palestine, including western Jerusalem, while Jordan held the Old City and the West Bank. Thousands were displaced, and many Christian holy sites had been damaged or profaned. Pius XII had already addressed the crisis in his earlier encyclical *In Multiplicibus* (1948), and *Redemptoris Nostri Cruciatus* reiterates and intensifies his call for internationalization of Jerusalem, reflecting the Vatican's longstanding concern for the freedom and security of Catholic institutions and pilgrims in the Holy Land.

## Key Themes

- **Protection of the Holy Places**: Pius demands that all Christian sacred sites in Palestine — not only in Jerusalem — receive guaranteed immunity and protection under an international statute.
- **Internationalization of Jerusalem**: The Pope insists that Jerusalem and its vicinity be given a special "international" juridical status, which he believes is the only reliable way to safeguard access to the holy sites amid competing national claims.
- **Justice for Refugees**: The encyclical decries the suffering of the thousands forced from their homes, appeals for humanitarian aid, and urges political leaders to render justice so the displaced can return to peaceful lives.
- **Right of Pilgrimage**: Pius expresses the desire that the faithful be able to visit the Holy Land freely, openly, and without fear, and laments worldly entertainments that profane sacred spaces.
- **Preservation of Catholic Rights**: The document insists that the historic rights Catholics have acquired and defended over centuries in the Holy Places must remain inviolate.
- **Pastoral Exhortation to Prayer and Advocacy**: The Pope calls on bishops to encourage the faithful both to pray for peace and to make their lawful requests known to the rulers of nations.

## Significance

*Redemptoris Nostri Cruciatus* is one of several mid-twentieth-century papal interventions on the status of Jerusalem and the Holy Places, reinforcing the Vatican's consistent position that Jerusalem requires a special internationally guaranteed status. Along with *In Multiplicibus* (1948) and the later encyclical *Peregrinatio Religiosa* (1952), it forms part of Pius XII's sustained diplomatic effort to shape the post-war political settlement in Palestine. While the call for full internationalization was never realized, the encyclical stands as an important witness to the Holy See's commitment to the protection of Christian heritage in the Middle East and to its broader humanitarian engagement with the region's conflicts.
