## Summary

*Depuis Le Jour* (1899) is Pope Leo XIII's encyclical to the French clergy on the education and formation of priests. It calls for rigorous intellectual training (Latin, scholastic theology, Thomistic philosophy), warns against modernist philosophical trends and lax discipline, and exhorts priests to holiness, obedience to bishops, and prudent zeal in their ministry.

## Historical Context

Written near the end of Leo XIII's long pontificate (1878–1903), the encyclical addresses the Church in France amid the rising tide of secularism, naturalism, and anti-clericalism following the French Third Republic's laicization policies. Leo was responding to worrying trends: the spread of rationalist philosophy (especially Kantian subjectivism), the neglect of Latin and classical studies in seminaries, and what he saw as imprudent innovations by some priests — mixing too freely with the world, adopting indiscreet speech and manners, and undermining episcopal authority under the banner of modern zeal.

## Key Themes

- **Priestly formation:** Seminaries must preserve classical Latin and Greek studies, Thomistic philosophy (per *Aeterni Patris*), and the scholastic method; the *Summa Theologica* and the Roman Catechism are essential texts.
- **Against false philosophy:** A strong condemnation of "doctrinal skepticism" and subjectivism of Protestant origin that denies reason's ability to know God, the soul, and objective reality.
- **Hierarchical obedience:** Priests must submit to their bishops; zeal without discipline and discretion is dangerous, as illustrated by the Maccabean priests who rushed into battle without orders.
- **Priestly holiness:** Purity of life, gravity of conduct, and separation from worldly levity are indispensable; the priest who mingles indiscriminately returns "less a priest."
- **Prudent engagement:** While priests should engage social problems (workers, the poor, economic life), they must do so under the direction of their bishops, avoiding the errors of naturalism and the "Americanist" innovations Leo had recently condemned.

## Significance

*Depuis Le Jour* is a key text for understanding late-19th-century Catholic seminary reform and the Church's defensive posture against secular modernity. It reinforces the Thomistic revival Leo XIII championed and foreshadows the anti-modernist campaign of his successor, Pius X (who would issue *Pascendi Dominici Gregis* eight years later). The encyclical remains a touchstone for traditional Catholic teaching on clerical formation, the importance of Latin in seminaries, and the proper relationship between priestly zeal and hierarchical discipline.
