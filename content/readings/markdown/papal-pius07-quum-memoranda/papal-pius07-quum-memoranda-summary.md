# Quum memoranda · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VII protests the French occupation of Rome and the seizure of papal territory, interpreting them as an assault on the Holy See's spiritual freedom as well as its temporal rights. He recounts restrictions on communication and government, removal of his guards and advisers, interference with religious communities, and violations of the French and Italian concordats. He argues that the territorial sovereignty entrusted to him must be defended because it protects the independent exercise of his ministry.

The brief declares the authors, commanders, advisers, supporters, and executors of the specified invasions and violations subject to major excommunication and other canonical penalties, renewing the censure where necessary. It identifies those responsible by their actions rather than naming an individual ruler. Absolution is reserved to the pope, apart from the stated deathbed exception, and requires public retraction and restitution or adequate satisfaction. Pius nevertheless expressly forbids his subjects and all Christians to injure the censured persons, their property, or their rights. The penalty is presented as an ecclesiastical measure seeking repentance and reconciliation. Arrangements for posting and authenticated copies are intended to give legal notice despite impediments to publication.

## Key themes

- Occupation of Rome and violations of papal sovereignty
- Spiritual independence as the stated ground for temporal claims
- Defined responsibility, reserved absolution, and required reparation
- An explicit prohibition of violence against the censured
