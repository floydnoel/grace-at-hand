## Summary

*Cum Semper Oblatas* (August 19, 1744) is an encyclical by Pope Benedict XIV addressed to the bishops of Italy, clarifying three interrelated obligations of clergy discipline: the duty of pastors to offer the parish Mass *pro populo* (for the people) on Sundays and feast days; the duty of chapters in cathedral, metropolitan, and collegiate churches to offer the daily conventual Mass for all benefactors in general; and the duty of canons to actually chant the Divine Office in choir rather than merely attending in silence. Drawing heavily on Benedict's own experience as secretary of the Congregation for the Interpretation of the Council of Trent, the encyclical aims to settle longstanding disputes among canonists and to provide uniform norms for episcopal governance.

## Historical Context

Benedict XIV reigned from 1740 to 1758, a period sometimes called the "Augustan age" of papal learning. A renowned canon lawyer and former Archbishop of Bologna, he was one of the most scholarly popes of the early modern period. The eighteenth-century Church was still implementing the disciplinary reforms of the Council of Trent (1545–1563), and disputes persisted over the precise scope of clerical obligations — especially regarding the application of Mass intentions, the receipt of stipends, and the liturgical duties of cathedral chapters. Benedict wrote this encyclical to end the "variety of opinions and judgments" that had divided canonists and to provide bishops with a clear rule for their synodal constitutions.

## Key Themes

- **Divine precept of the *pro populo* Mass:** Citing Trent (Session 23, cap. 1), Benedict affirms that pastors are bound by divine command to offer the "medium" fruit of the Mass for their flock; they may not apply it to others or accept a stipend for it.
- **Applicability to all with care of souls:** The obligation extends not only to parish priests but also to vicars (both secular and regular), temporary administrators (*economi*), and even regular clergy serving parishes — no contrary custom, even immemorial, excuses them.
- **Frequency of the obligation:** Pastors must celebrate *pro populo* on all Sundays and feast days of precept, including those on which servile work is permitted but Mass attendance remains obligatory.
- **Conventual Mass for all benefactors:** The daily conventual Mass in cathedral and collegiate churches must be applied for *all* benefactors in general, not for particular individuals; a Mass applied for a specific donor does not satisfy this duty.
- **Chanting obligation of canons:** Canons and dignitaries must actually sing the psalmody and participate vocally in choir — silent presence alone does not fulfill their duty, and those who persist may forfeit their prebends and distributions.
- **Pastoral solicitude for poor clergy:** Bishops may dispense impoverished pastors to accept stipends on feast days in certain circumstances, provided the *pro populo* obligation is made up during the week. Bishops are also empowered to assign a congruous portion of fruits to underpaid vicars.

## Significance

*Cum Semper Oblatas* is a landmark disciplinary encyclical that codified and definitively settled the Tridentine norms around the application of Masses — a subject that had generated centuries of canonistic controversy. It reinforced the pastoral character of the priesthood by grounding the *pro populo* obligation in divine law, while also tempering rigor with pastoral solicitude for poor clergy. Later popes, including Pius IX in *Amantissimi Redemptoris* (1858), would explicitly cite Benedict XIV's teaching as authoritative. The encyclical remains an important reference point in Catholic canon law regarding the obligations of pastors and cathedral chapters.
