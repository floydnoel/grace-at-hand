**La Santa Sede**

ENCYCLICAL CUM SEMPER OBLATAS OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF BENEDICT XIV To the Venerable Brothers, Patriarchs, Archbishops, and all the Ordinaries of the various regions of Italy. Pope Benedict XIV. Venerable Brothers, greetings and Apostolic Blessings. We always gladly take advantage of every opportunity offered to Us to address You, Venerable Brothers, so that the proof of Our sincere love for You may shine ever more brightly; and now We do so with even greater eagerness of spirit, to stir up the zeal of your brotherhood for the preservation of proper discipline in the governance of the clergy entrusted to you in these particular circumstances of time and need. We could not hope to bear the heavy burden of caring for all the Churches, imposed upon our weakness, without recommending and instilling an increase in Divine Worship, the observance of ecclesiastical sanctions in individual dioceses, and the particular and vigilant care of the pastors. 1. First and foremost, this Letter provides Us with the opportunity to address You regarding the responsibility assumed by all those who care for souls, namely, to celebrate the Parish Mass for the people entrusted to their care; as well as the celebration of the Conventual Mass for the benefit of benefactors in general, which must be performed by those who sing at Mass in Patriarchal, Metropolitan churches, cathedrals, and collegiate churches; and finally, the obligation to chant, to which the canons who assist from the choir in said churches are bound. Our discussion concerns this last topic, which is not new but has, in fact, always been addressed by the writers. This duty was discussed and defined many times in the Congregation of Our Venerable Brethren, the Cardinals of the Holy Roman Church, interpreters of the Council of Trent, ever since We ourselves, having been ordained to the minor orders, served for many years as Secretary of that same Congregation. And although the decrees of this Sacred Congregation have almost always been uniform, consistently receiving the approval of Our Predecessors, it is not surprising that news of them has not yet reached all of you and each individual. For this reason, We have deemed it not only opportune but necessary to write this Encyclical Letter to you, so that the constant opinion and directive of this Apostolic See on these matters may be made known, thereby putting an end to the variety of opinions and judgments among which the writers have been divided. This, therefore, will serve as a guideline for your brotherhoods, so that you may direct all your synodal constitutions and rescripts—which we hereby order you to publish—in accordance with this rule. You will therefore see to it—and We have no doubt that you will—that the prescriptions set forth in this Letter, which are to be preserved and observed, are carried out with the utmost diligence and vigilance. It will be up to you to ensure that any likely appeals to the Tribunals of Our Curia against your Rescripts do not constitute an obstacle or hindrance, since We have prescribed and ordered that they must all be rejected. For this reason, We wish this Letter of Ours to be preserved in the collections and archives of Our Tribunals, and We order that both the rulings of the Tribunals and the Rescripts you issue in accordance with it be observed. 2. What We have now stated—namely, that the holy Sacrifice of the Mass must be offered by the shepherds of souls on behalf of the people entrusted to their care—is clearly set forth by the Holy Council of Trent in these important words, as a consequence of the divine command: “For it is a divine precept that all who have charge of souls must carefully distinguish their flock and offer the Sacrifice for them” (Council of Trent, sess. 23, chap. 1), and although there have been those who, through ridiculous or unfounded interpretations, have sought to eliminate this obligation recalled by the Holy Synod or at least to mitigate it; nevertheless, since the aforementioned words of the Council are sufficiently clear and precise, and furthermore, since the aforementioned Congregation, specifically charged with interpreting the Council itself, has consistently declared that those entrusted with the care of souls must not only celebrate the Sacrifice of the Mass, but must also apply its “middle” fruit for the benefit of the people entrusted to them, and may not apply it for the benefit of others, nor may they receive alms for such application; and since, finally, —and this is most important—this intention has been approved and confirmed by Our Roman Pontiff Predecessors, none of you can but desire to embrace it, to carry it out, and to ensure with every zeal that it is promptly implemented in your respective dioceses. 3. We, too, who, as we have already mentioned, when We were still engaged in lesser duties, served for many years as Secretary of the aforementioned Congregation for the Interpretation of the Council of Trent, and during the not insignificant number of years We spent governing the Diocese of Ancona and part of the Metropolitan See of Bologna, our beloved homeland, which we still love—we are, as it were, well aware of all the various loopholes through which many seek to evade fulfilling this obligation, the enforcement of which we are specifically charged to ensure. 4. The Holy Council of Trent frequently instructs bishops that, wherever necessary, so that the care of souls may not be neglected, they should choose vicars suitable for exercising this care of souls, assigning them a suitable stipend or benefice, as can be read in Session 6, Chapter 2; session 7 and chapters 5–7; session 21, chapter 6; session 25, chapter 16. It is not uncommon that, during the vacancy of a parish, the Bishop must appoint a Vicar (spiritual steward) to fulfill the duties of that parish until the election of the new Rector, again in accordance with the provisions of the Council of Trent (De Reformatione, sess. 24, chap. 18). Many of these Vicar-General then seek to evade this obligation, either because they already have a regular pastoral charge elsewhere and are serving in this capacity only temporarily, or because they are removable at the Bishop’s discretion and exercise that parish ministry for only a short time; not to mention the Regular Parish Priests, who often claim they are not required to celebrate the festive Mass pro populo. Instead, it is Our will and command that, as has already been established on other occasions by the aforementioned Congregations, all those who exercise pastoral care—not only parish priests or secular vicars, but also regular parish priests or vicars; in short, all those mentioned above— all those who have been deemed worthy of this specific mention—all are equally bound to celebrate the parish Mass for the people entrusted to their care. 5. Some, to avoid fulfilling this obligation, are in the habit of objecting that the revenues of their parish are insufficient; others hide behind a long-standing custom, claiming that this obligation was never in practice—neither for themselves nor for their predecessors—for a long time, indeed, from time immemorial. We, however, reaffirm the aforementioned prescriptions issued by the Congregation of the Council, and to the extent necessary, by Our Apostolic Authority, in accordance with this Letter, We decree and declare that this provision must be enforced, even if parish priests or others, as we have seen above, who have the care of souls lack the appropriate established income, and despite the fact that, by custom from time immemorial, the opposite had been practiced in their dioceses or parishes; all are equally bound to celebrate the parish Mass henceforth. 6. When we stated that all those who have the care of souls must offer the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass for the people entrusted to them, we did not thereby intend to establish that they are bound to do so daily, or whenever they celebrate. Indeed, the Holy Council of Trent (session 23, chap. 14) enjoins bishops to ensure that priests celebrate Holy Mass at least on Sundays and solemn feasts; if they are in charge of souls, they are to celebrate Holy Mass frequently enough to meet the needs of their flock. But already in many Synodal Constitutions, the bishops have providently established—as we well know—the days on which pastors of souls must celebrate Holy Mass for the people. We have undertaken to decree only when, without any doubt, Mass must be celebrated for the people. Indeed, we are aware of what had previously been decreed by the Sacred Congregation of the Council, namely, that a parish priest endowed with a substantial benefice should celebrate and offer Holy Mass for the people every day, and that one who does not enjoy such abundant income is required to do so only on feast days. But We, being well aware of the controversies that have arisen on this point—namely, what amount the revenues of the parish church must reach in order to be considered substantial and abundant— and since such revenues—even if copious—cannot be deemed substantial if they are burdened by numerous and severe obligations, and since We are aware of the many complaints that have arisen against this decree, which is considered too rigid, We deem it appropriate to declare to Your Fraternities that it is already satisfactory to Us and sufficient for you that those who exercise the care of souls celebrate the Sacrifice of the Mass every Sunday and on holy days of obligation, offering it for the people. Sundays and other holy days of obligation are those on which, according to the precept of the Council of Trent (session 5, chapter 2; session 24, chapter 4), all those entrusted with the care of souls must nourish the people entrusted to them with wholesome words, teaching those truths that everyone must know for their salvation; and these are the days regarding which the Holy Council decreed: “The Bishop shall admonish the people with great care, and everyone must be present at their parish, when convenient, to hear the Word of God.” On these days, parish priests must instruct their parishioners in Christian doctrine, in accordance with what the Council itself prescribes: “Bishops are to see to it that children, on Sundays and other feast days, are instructed in their respective parishes in the rudiments of the faith and in obedience to God and to their parents” (Council of Trent, sess. 24, chap. 4). 7. And since in some dioceses the number of holy days of obligation has been reduced by Our authority—so that on certain holy days the Christian faithful must attend Holy Mass and abstain from servile labor, while on other feast days servile labor is permitted, though the obligation to attend Mass remains, We—in order to dispel the doubts that have already arisen regarding the obligation to celebrate the parish Mass on these latter feast days— We hereby establish and declare that all pastors are required to celebrate and offer Mass for the people even on the aforementioned days, on which the faithful must attend Mass and may engage in menial labor. 8. We know quite well, however, having experienced it ourselves on several occasions, that there are parish priests so poor that they are almost forced to live on the alms they receive from the faithful for the celebration of Masses. Others, on the other hand, who are appointed as vicars or stewards to exercise pastoral care in the parish priest’s absence, are treated so miserably in certain places that the meager income granted to them and the scant and uncertain earnings they make are barely enough to meet their basic needs. This often happens as well to those priests who, in certain churches, exercise their ministry only on an interim basis, while it is permanently entrusted to others; consequently, it would seem overly harsh of us to forbid them from receiving alms for the celebration of Mass precisely on feast days, when the opportunity to receive them arises most readily. For this reason, We, moved by the deepest compassion for the poverty of both groups, and with the aim of coming to their aid within the limits of Our authority; although, as We have stated above, each and every one of the aforementioned priests is obliged on feast days to celebrate and apply the Mass pro populo; nevertheless, for the benefit of the aforementioned parishes in need, We grant each of you the authority to appropriately dispense those whom you have determined to meet the required conditions, so that they may freely and lawfully receive alms, even on feast days, from some pious benefactor, and offer the Sacrifice on his behalf, if the donor so requests: provided that this is done for the necessary convenience of the people, and on the condition that, during the week, they apply as many Masses on behalf of the people as they have celebrated on the feast days occurring in that week, according to the particular intention of another pious benefactor. 9. As regards the Vicars, that is, the Administrators, of vacant churches—since the Council of Trent (session 24, chap. 18), to appoint and establish them “with a suitable allocation of the fruits of the benefice at his own discretion,” it is up to you, Venerable Brothers, to deal with those who claim the fruits of that vacant church, so as to provide a certain appropriate increase to compensate for the burden of celebrating and administering the Mass for the people on feast days to that Steward in need who receives a meager allocation of assets and few and uncertain other sources of income. Furthermore, in those places where the revenues of vacant churches are collected for the benefit of Our Apostolic Chamber, We have sent appropriate orders to Our Treasurer General, which he will not fail to transmit to the local Collectors in these places: “The Bishops of Our jurisdiction and ecclesiastical region, and of other places where the revenues from vacant churches belong to the aforementioned Apostolic Chamber, shall be required to allocate a portion of these same revenues for the purpose mentioned above.” 10. Finally, with regard to those appointed as perpetual or temporary vicars who have the care of souls that habitually belongs to others—that is, the parish of some parish church united to their churches or monasteries, colleges, and pious institutions—although Our Predecessor of venerable memory, Pope Saint Pius V, established a certain portion to be assigned to these Vicars, as is clearly indicated in his Constitution beginning with the words \*Ad exequendum\*, dated November 1, 1567, however, if a predetermined portion of the revenues has not been assigned to these Vicars—either not at all or not in full— or even if the portion assigned to them by the aforementioned Constitution is deemed by you to be insufficient in light of current circumstances and especially for the fulfillment of the obligation to celebrate and administer the Mass pro populo on holy days of obligation; you may exercise this power granted by the Council of Trent to bishops according to their prudent judgment (Council of Trent, sess. 7, chap. 7), taking into account the needs of the times and the reason for the imposed obligation, and assign to these Vicars a suitable portion of the revenues. For this reason, We grant Your Fraternities, to the extent necessary, the necessary and appropriate faculties, abolishing any privilege, appeal, or enforcement—as established by the same Council—that might be opposed to the salutary provisions issued by You. 11. We have thus indicated to Your Fraternities the norms that must be established regarding the Parish Mass. Going a step further, the norms governing the Conventual Mass are so well known and clear that no doubt can arise: namely, that according to the provisions of the Sacred Canons, it is prescribed that every day in Patriarchal, Metropolitan, and Collegiate churches, the Canonical Hours must be recited in the proper manner and form; not only that, but the Conventual Mass must also be celebrated. Regarding these obligations as well, there are resolutions issued many times by this Congregation of Our Venerable Brethren, interpreters of the Council of Trent, which We, by Our Apostolic Authority, approve and confirm, particularly urging their implementation. Therefore, the Conventual Mass celebrated daily by the clergy of the aforementioned churches is to be offered daily for their benefactors in general, in the same manner as it is offered pro populo by those who care for souls every Sunday and on holy days of obligation, as We have stated above. 12. Endeavor, therefore, to dispel the false opinion held by some—which we know to be accepted in some of these churches either out of error or maliciously— This opinion is as follows: namely, that when the Conventual Mass is celebrated and applied for a particular benefactor of the Church—whether out of gratitude or due to an accepted or imposed obligation—the obligation of the celebration is thereby considered fulfilled. Instead, this duty and obligation do not pertain to certain specific benefactors, but to all benefactors in general of any Church in whose service the Dignitaries, Canons, Mansionaries, and those who receive choral benefices and celebrate the Conventual Mass according to their schedules are assigned. 13. You will understand that the claim made by others—that this obligation is sufficiently fulfilled when prayers are occasionally offered for benefactors in their churches, or when the Holy Sacrifice is celebrated for them on certain days or on anniversaries—is no less reprehensible. No one should presume to fulfill this obligation in any way other than that which has been repeatedly prescribed by ecclesiastical laws: namely, that the Conventual Mass must be celebrated daily for benefactors, applying it to all of them in general. 14. In the early centuries of the Church, but also in times not too distant from our own—and we have no doubt that you, too, have learned this from Church history—each church kept a detailed list of all those whose generosity had made the church’s construction possible; and their names were inscribed in the “Sacred Dyptychs” (as they were then called) so that their memory might never fade and so that prayers might be offered for them and the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass might be celebrated. For this reason, it was customary in many churches to place that register before the eyes of the celebrating priest, even though many pious benefactors had stated in their donations that they had not attached any condition regarding Holy Masses, but that they offered their goods to God solely for the remission of their sins; yet the bishops of the Churches decreed that prayers and intercessions be offered for them, even though these benefactors, in offering their goods, had made no mention of this. But gradually this practice of using the Sacred Dictyons fell into disuse, and for this reason the names of many benefactors have fallen into oblivion in so many places. Nevertheless, this does not mean that the practice and discipline of praying for them and offering the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass in their suffrage should be neglected. From these facts, then, arose (and finds its justification) the precept to apply the Conventual Mass for all benefactors. 15. Just as various excuses are offered—as mentioned above—to avoid celebrating the Parish Mass pro populo on holy days of obligation, so too is this the case regarding the celebration of the daily Conventual Mass for the Benefactors. And just as the former excuses were providentially removed by the appropriate resolutions of the Congregation of the Council of Trent—which We once again approve and confirm—so too have the latter been removed. 16. Some, however, citing the contrary custom—even “from time immemorial”—that prevails in their Church, have convinced themselves that they may exempt themselves from such an obligation. But it has already been stated many times that such a custom, even if “from time immemorial,” must more properly be called an abuse and a vice, and cannot in any way or by anyone be defended or accepted. 17. Others would like to be exempted from celebrating the Mass for Benefactors, either because they are subject to another obligation to celebrate Masses, or by reason of their canonry or other ecclesiastical benefice, which they have obtained through a canonical prebend; or because—in addition to their office as canon, beneficiary, or mansionar in the cathedral or collegiate church—when they celebrate the Conventual Mass on holy days of obligation, they must simultaneously offer the Mass “pro populo” and therefore cannot at the same time offer the Holy Sacrifice for their benefactors as well. But provision has also been made for these individuals, by ordering them to apply the Conventual Mass for their benefactors; as for the others, who are required to apply the Mass specifically for others, they are to be replaced by another priest, who, in their place, will celebrate that Mass to be applied pro populo. 18. Others point out that the Conventual Mass is not always celebrated by canons or dignitaries, but sometimes by beneficiates or mansionaries. It is not right that there should be no alms for that Mass, and they do not know from where that alms should be taken. Provision has been made for this as well, by ordering that it be deducted from the “Mass of Distribution.” 19. Still others have pointed out the meagerness of these distributions, which, if the daily alms for the Conventual Mass were deducted, would be reduced to almost nothing. Then there would be no one left to take care of it, to the grave detriment of service to the Church. The Council of Trent (session 24, chapter 15) sets forth the appropriate reasons for addressing the lack of resources and the poverty of certain canonical prebends. If, however, the path indicated by the Council cannot be followed—as often happens—then the only recourse is to file an appeal with the Congregation of the Council, which has the authority to limit the daily celebration of the Conventual Mass to feast days only. This is to be done after having duly examined your particular situation on the basis of your report, and with the Apostolic authority granted to it by Our Predecessors and confirmed by Us in this Letter. 20. We are aware that the singing of the Conventual Mass has been mandated daily in Patriarchal, Metropolitan, and Collegiate Churches, as prescribed in the General Rubrics, the preservation and observance of which we strongly recommend to your brotherhoods. However, on certain days, two or three Conventual Masses must also be celebrated. In such cases, as has been ordered from above, the first Mass must certainly be celebrated for the benefactors; but it remains to be decided whether the Chapters of the churches subject to your jurisdiction should be required to ensure that the other Masses—if they are to be celebrated—are likewise offered in suffrage for the benefactors. 21. This question has been brought before the Sacred Congregation by some of you, who are ardent in your zeal for the Church. But even before this inquiry, it was found that on other occasions the same Congregation of Our Venerable Brothers, Interpreters of the Council of Trent, had responded that an exemption should be granted from applying the second or third Conventual Mass for the benefit of the benefactors, whenever the meager endowment of the canonries or benefices so required. From this, one could infer the obligation to celebrate the Mass where the churches in question were not poor. 22. However, being well aware of the rule followed by the Sacred Congregation in defining this matter—namely, to refer the resolution of this question to Our judgment in cases of doubt—We therefore rule—and We wish this to be observed by You—as follows: All those who spontaneously apply the second or third Conventual Mass for benefactors in general are to be commended and encouraged; those who do so in accordance with the custom prevailing in their church must persevere in this custom; where, however, such a custom does not exist, celebrants must be given the freedom to celebrate the second and third Conventual Masses, provided that the benefactors of the Church are always remembered in the commemoration of the dead. 23. In concluding this Letter of Ours, We earnestly urge Your Fraternities to exercise the utmost care and vigilance so that in the choirs of Your Churches, in addition to the devout celebration and proper observance of the Conventual Mass, the Liturgy of the Hours may not be sung hastily, but rather with diligence, always observing the required pauses, and with all due respect and devotion. 24. We are well aware that in certain Metropolitan Churches and Cathedrals, an opinion has taken hold among the Canons according to which they claim to sufficiently fulfill their duty merely by their presence in the choir, even if they remain silent there and do not join in the singing of the Beneficiates and Mansionaries. To support this view, they usually cite ancient customs, specific statutes, and even alleged privileges of their churches. But since the Council of Trent, speaking of the dignitaries and canons who must be present in the choir, sets forth their duties in these terms: “Praise the Name of God with hymns and psalms, with reverence, a clear voice, and devotion in the choir established for this purpose to chant the psalms” (Council of Trent, sess. 84, chap. 12); and since there are currently few chapters in which the canons participate in the choir in the manner We deplore; and therefore there are few who oppose the discipline of the Church—as far as We know—; and since, moreover, this opinion (which was never put up for discussion in the Congregation of the Council of Trent) was immediately condemned and rejected as soon as it was examined—even though alleged customs and other grounds and reasons were adduced in its support—and despite the petition made by the canons of the patriarchal churches of this city of ours; since, finally, a judgment to this same effect was issued by many Provincial Synods—which were also approved and confirmed by this Apostolic See—there seems to be nothing left to prevent these few from conforming to the universal law. In truth, We do not see on what particular grounds the Canons of this or that Church can rely to convince themselves that they fulfill their obligation by their mere presence in the choir, without the singing of the Divine Psalms. Therefore, unless they possess an Apostolic Privilege or Indult—one that is neither presumed nor abrogated, but one that is legitimate and still in force—it is rightly and justly to be feared that, as long as they act in this manner, they cannot claim the fruits of their prebends and distributions, and that they are bound to return them. Therefore, it is your duty, Venerable Brothers, to explain all these responsibilities to them, lest we—together with you—by our concealment and silence, encourage and confirm the abuses and corruption that we were meant to eliminate, by courageously rebuking and warning, so as not to be found guilty before the Divine Judge in a matter of such great importance, one so closely related to the worship of God. Meanwhile, to your brotherhoods, whom we embrace with all our hearts, we impart the Apostolic Blessing with great affection. Given in Rome, at Saint Mary Major, on August 19, 1744, the fifth year of Our Pontificate.
