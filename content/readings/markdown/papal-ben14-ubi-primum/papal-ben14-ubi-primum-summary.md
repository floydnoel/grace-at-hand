## Summary

*Ubi Primum* (1740) is Pope Benedict XIV's first encyclical, addressed to the bishops of the Catholic Church, outlining the pastoral duties of the episcopacy. The document emphasizes the bishop's responsibility to ensure clergy are well-formed, resident in their dioceses, and diligent in preaching, teaching, and administering the sacraments, with particular attention to the establishment of seminaries and the catechetical instruction of the young.

## Historical Context

Benedict XIV wrote this encyclical at the beginning of his pontificate in 1740, a period when the Church faced challenges of clerical laxity, absentee bishops, and inadequate formation of priests. The document reflects the reform-minded spirit of the Council of Trent (1545–1563), which had called for seminaries, episcopal residency, and improved clerical discipline — reforms that were still being implemented unevenly across Europe nearly two centuries later.

## Key Themes

- **Clerical Formation**: Bishops must carefully select and train clergy, prioritizing virtue and learning over numbers; seminaries should be established or expanded.
- **Preaching and Catechesis**: Pastors have a grave duty to preach regularly and to teach children the basics of the faith.
- **Episcopal Residency**: Bishops must reside in their dioceses; absence requires a compelling reason, and delegating pastoral care to others is not a substitute.
- **Diocesan Visitation**: Bishops must personally visit their dioceses to know their flock, identify problems, and ensure that remedies are actually put into practice.
- **Personal Example**: Bishops are to lead by example in piety, liturgy, charity, and every good work, so that clergy and laity may imitate them.

## Significance

*Ubi Primum* established Benedict XIV as a reforming pope committed to Trent's vision of a disciplined, pastorally engaged episcopate. It served as a model for subsequent papal teaching on the bishop's office, influencing later documents such as Pius X's *Haerent Animo* and Vatican II's *Christus Dominus*. Its insistence on clerical formation, residency, and personal oversight remains foundational to Catholic episcopal theology.
