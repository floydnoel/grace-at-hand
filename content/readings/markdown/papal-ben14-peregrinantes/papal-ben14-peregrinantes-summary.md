## Summary

*Peregrinantes* (1749) is Pope Benedict XIV's formal proclamation of a Holy Year (Jubilee) for 1750, calling all Catholics to repentance, pilgrimage to Rome, and spiritual renewal. It announces a plenary indulgence for the faithful who confess, receive communion, and visit the four major Roman basilicas, and urges both clergy and civil authorities to support the observance.

## Historical Context

The document was issued on May 5, 1749, proclaiming the ordinary jubilee of 1750 — the standard twenty-five-year interval established by Paul II in 1470. The mid-18th century was a period of relative peace following the War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748), and Benedict refers to peace as a gift from God that should now lead to spiritual renewal rather than complacency. The Church faced challenges from Enlightenment rationalism, Jansenist rigorism, and Protestant division, and Benedict sought to reassert Catholic unity, piety, and papal authority through the traditional jubilee mechanism.

## Key Themes

- **Pilgrimage and penitence** — The Christian life is a pilgrimage; the Holy Year is a structured opportunity for conversion and return to God.
- **Plenary indulgence** — Grounded in the treasury of merit (Christ, Mary, and the saints) and the power of the keys (binding and loosing), the indulgence requires confession, communion, and visitation of the four patriarchal basilicas over 30 days (Romans) or 15 days (pilgrims).
- **Mercy balanced with discipline** — The Church is both mother offering mercy and teacher requiring penance; those physically unable to complete the visits are nonetheless included.
- **Rome as the New Jerusalem** — A sustained contrast between pagan Rome (Babylon, idolatry, circus spectacles) and Christian Rome (martyrs' tombs, papal basilicas, unity of nations).
- **Call to the separated** — A direct appeal to those who have left the Church to return, acknowledging that many know the truth but lack the will to act.
- **Spiritual warfare** — The jubilee is cast as a "new kind of war" against vice, luxury, impurity, hatred, and the enemies of salvation.
- **Unity of hierarchy and state** — Bishops are to preach and lead by example; Catholic princes are to protect pilgrims and support ecclesiastical discipline.

## Significance

*Peregrinantes* is the classic 18th-century formulation of the ordinary jubilee, articulating the theology of indulgences, the role of Rome as the center of Catholic unity, and the integration of popular piety with ecclesiastical authority. It reflects Benedict XIV's reputation as a learned, pastoral, and diplomatic pope — one who sought to renew the Church through traditional means while engaging the intellectual currents of his age. The document remains a reference point for understanding the Church's teaching on jubilees, indulgences, and the spiritual purpose of pilgrimage to Rome.
