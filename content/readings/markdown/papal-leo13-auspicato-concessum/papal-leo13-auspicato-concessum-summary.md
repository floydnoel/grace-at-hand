## Summary

*Auspicato Concessum* is a papal encyclical issued by Pope Leo XIII on September 17, 1882, to mark the seventh centenary of the birth of St. Francis of Assisi. The document celebrates the life and virtues of St. Francis, presents him as a model of Christian perfection, and urges the faithful — especially members of the Franciscan Third Order — to imitate his example of poverty, charity, and devotion to the Cross as a remedy for the social and moral ills of the modern age.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written in the aftermath of Italian unification, a period of intense anticlericalism that had seen the suppression of religious orders and the seizure of Church properties. Pope Leo XIII, who had a personal devotion to St. Francis and counted himself among the Franciscan family, wrote just months after similar centenary celebrations for St. Benedict. The 19th century was marked by rapid industrialization, the rise of socialism, class conflict, and widespread secularization — all of which Leo XIII saw as symptoms of a decline in Christian morals and charity.

## Key Themes

- **St. Francis as a model of Christian perfection**: Leo XIII presents Francis as the man God raised up to recall a corrupt age to the imitation of Christ, especially through radical poverty and embrace of the Cross.
- **The Third Order as a remedy for social ills**: The encyclical strongly promotes the Franciscan Third Order as a way for lay people to live Gospel values without leaving family or society, arguing it can heal class conflict, curb luxury, and restore peace.
- **Critique of contemporary society**: The Pope condemns the greed, violence, social division, and moral license of both the 12th century and his own time, drawing a direct parallel between the two eras.
- **The dignity of poverty and charity**: The document teaches that poverty is not undignified, that the rich should be merciful, and that social harmony depends on both classes accepting their respective duties.
- **Franciscan influence on culture**: Leo XIII highlights Francis's contributions to Italian literature, art, and architecture as part of his enduring legacy.

## Significance

*Auspicato Concessum* is one of Leo XIII's key social encyclicals, complementing *Rerum Novarum* (1891) by proposing a spiritual — not merely political or economic — solution to the "social question." It helped revive interest in the Franciscan Third Order in the late 19th century and remains an important statement on the role of religious lay movements in Catholic social teaching. The encyclical also reflects Leo XIII's broader strategy of drawing on the Church's medieval heritage to address modern challenges.
