## Summary

*Orientales Omnes Ecclesias* is a papal encyclical issued by Pope Pius XII on 23 December 1945, marking the 350th anniversary of the Union of Brest (1595–1596), which reunited the Ruthenian (Ukrainian) Church with Rome. The document recounts the history of the Ruthenian Church, defends the legitimacy and benefits of its union with the Apostolic See, and condemns the persecution of Ruthenian Catholics under Soviet domination, calling for prayer, endurance, and fidelity.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written at the close of World War II, as Soviet forces expanded into Eastern Europe and annexed Galicia (western Ukraine). Under Stalin, the Soviet state moved to suppress the Ruthenian Catholic Church (the "Uniate" Church), arresting its bishops and priests, confiscating its property, and forcing its faithful into the Russian Orthodox Church. The encyclical responds to the synod of 1945 in which the newly elected Russian Orthodox Patriarch Alexis excommunicated the Ruthenian Catholics and called for their absorption. It also situates this persecution within the longer history of the Ruthenian Church — its union with Rome in 1595/96, its flourishing in Galicia under Austrian rule, and its earlier suppression under the tsars in 1839 and 1875.

## Key Themes

- **Defence of Eastern-rite Catholicism**: The Pope insists that union with Rome does not require abandonment of Eastern liturgical rites, customs, or patriarchal privileges. He emphasises that successive popes protected the integrity of the Ruthenian rite against Latinisation.
- **The Union of Brest as a blessing**: The encyclical argues that the union brought genuine spiritual and moral renewal — clerical discipline, flourishing monasticism (especially the Basilian order), education, and cultural life — in contrast to the decay that preceded it.
- **Martyrdom and fidelity**: A prominent theme is the witness of Ruthenian martyrs, especially St Josaphat Kuntsevych (killed in 1623) and the countless clergy and laity who suffered under tsarist and Soviet persecution rather than abandon Catholic unity.
- **The duty of obedience to civil authority vs. religious freedom**: The Pope acknowledges the Church's teaching on obedience to lawful civil authority but sharply rejects the Soviet claim that religious persecution is justified for political reasons.
- **Exhortation to perseverance**: The final sections are a pastoral appeal to Ruthenian bishops, priests, and laity to remain steadfast in the faith, resist coercion, and trust in divine reward.

## Significance

The encyclical is a key document in Catholic teaching on Eastern-rite churches, reaffirming the principle that unity with Rome does not require liturgical or cultural uniformity. It also stands as a historical record of the Soviet persecution of the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church, which was forcibly liquidated in 1946 at the pseudo-synod of Lviv (just months after this encyclical was issued) and driven underground for decades. The document thus foreshadows the Church's experience of totalitarian oppression and its eventual re-emergence after the fall of the Soviet Union.
