## Summary

*Sollicitudo Rei Socialis* ("The Social Concern of the Church") is an encyclical by Pope John Paul II, published December 30, 1987, to mark the twentieth anniversary of Paul VI's *Populorum Progressio*. It re-examines the question of authentic human development in a world still marked by deepening inequality, Cold War division, and the emergence of new forms of poverty. John Paul II argues that development cannot be reduced to economic growth alone but must encompass the cultural, spiritual, and moral dimensions of the human person, and insists that genuine development is impossible without solidarity—a virtue rooted in the Christian understanding of interdependence, justice, and love of neighbour.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written near the end of the Cold War, at a moment when the division of the world into two ideological blocs (East and West) was still the dominant geopolitical reality. The gap between the developed North and the developing South was widening; the international debt crisis was intensifying; and the arms race continued to divert vast resources away from human needs. In the Church, the encyclical follows the Second Vatican Council (1962–65) and responds to currents within liberation theology in Latin America, offering a theological framework for development that avoids both Marxist collectivism and unrestrained liberal capitalism. It belongs to the tradition of Catholic social teaching stretching back to Leo XIII's *Rerum Novarum* (1891).

## Key Themes

- **Authentic human development** is integral—it must promote the good of every person and the whole person, not merely material accumulation.
- **The distinction between "having" and "being"**: development must serve the fullness of human vocation, not the mere multiplication of possessions.
- **Solidarity** as a Christian virtue and a moral category: the firm and persevering commitment to the common good, rooted in the interdependence of all peoples.
- **"Structures of sin"**: John Paul II introduces this concept to describe the institutional and systemic embodiments of greed, the thirst for power, and ideological conflict that obstruct development.
- **The preferential option for the poor**: love of neighbour must take concrete form in a priority for the hungry, homeless, and hopeless.
- **Critique of both liberal capitalism and Marxist collectivism**: neither system, as then constituted, adequately serves integral human development.
- **Peace and development**: echoing Paul VI, "development is the new name for peace"—underdevelopment, injustice, and arms spending are direct threats to peace.
- **Ecological responsibility**: development must respect natural limits and the integrity of creation.

## Significance

*Sollicitudo Rei Socialis* is a landmark document in Catholic social teaching. It introduced the concept of "structures of sin" and made "solidarity" a central category of Catholic moral and social thought. The encyclical deepened the Church's social analysis by linking the spiritual life to structural economic and political questions, insisting that conversion of heart and reform of institutions must go together. It also clarified the Church's social doctrine as a theological enterprise (not a "third way" ideology) and positioned the Church as a moral voice in the global debate on development, debt, and disarmament at the close of the Cold War.
