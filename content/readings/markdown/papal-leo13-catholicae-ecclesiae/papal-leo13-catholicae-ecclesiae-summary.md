# Catholicae Ecclesiae · English editorial summary

## Summary

*Catholicae Ecclesiae* is a papal encyclical addressed to Catholic missionaries in Africa, condemning the ongoing slave trade (particularly the estimated 400,000 Africans forcibly taken annually) and calling for renewed missionary efforts. Leo XIII decrees an annual Epiphany collection to fund both anti-slavery work and evangelisation, and tasks Cardinal Charles Martial Lavigerie with rallying European support.

## Historical Context

By 1890, European colonialism was accelerating after the Berlin Conference (1884–85). The transatlantic slave trade had ended, but slavery persisted in Africa through Arab and internal networks. Leo XIII had praised Brazil's emancipation (1888) and supported the Brussels Anti-Slavery Conference (1889–90). Cardinal Lavigerie was already campaigning against slavery, and this encyclical gave papal backing to that movement.

## Key Themes

- **Church's historic opposition to slavery**: Traces a papal lineage from St. Gregory the Great to Gregory XVI as consistent adversaries of slavery.
- **Human dignity and common origin**: All people share one creation, redemption, and calling — slavery violates both nature and Gospel.
- **Mission and liberation are inseparable**: Where the Gospel takes root, enslavement cannot survive.
- **Practical mechanism**: An annual worldwide Epiphany collection to fund missionaries and anti-slavery operations.
- **Solidarity with Africa**: A coordinated international Catholic effort to relieve African suffering.

## Significance

It is the first papal encyclical devoted almost entirely to the African slave trade, reaffirming Church teaching with a concrete action plan. The Epiphany collection it instituted funded missionary and anti-slavery work for decades. By linking evangelisation with the defence of human freedom, Leo XIII helped shape Catholic thinking on human dignity that would later inform the modern social magisterium.
