# Le più colte · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VII reforms the regulation of foodstuffs and related trades in the Papal States, arguing that restrictions have discouraged production, obstructed circulation, and favored scarcity and monopoly. He presents competition and voluntary exchange as means to encourage agriculture, attract suppliers, and moderate prices, extending principles already applied to the grain supply.

The decree removes many barriers to internal purchase, sale, transport, market location, and freely negotiated prices. It abolishes market-place charges, compulsory use of leased weights, preferential purchasing rights, transit duties, and the corporate privileges and compulsory fees of trades under the food-supply department. Certain legitimately acquired market rights may be compensated. The reform has important limits: domestic exports require permission based on available supply, customs requirements remain, and local consumption taxes are retained. Imported goods may be re-exported under safeguards against disguising domestic exports. A new food-supply deputation oversees enforcement, fraud, product quality, and certified weights and measures; punitive taxation discourages premature livestock slaughter. The measures therefore combine broad commercial freedom within this sector with fiscal, supply, and quality controls.

## Key themes

- Competition and voluntary pricing as remedies for scarcity
- Removal of market privileges and food-trade guild restrictions
- Export licensing and retained consumption duties
- Public supervision of quality, weights, and fraud
