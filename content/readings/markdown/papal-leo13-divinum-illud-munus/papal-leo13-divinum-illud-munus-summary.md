## Summary

*Divinum Illud Munus* ("That Divine Office") is Pope Leo XIII's 1897 encyclical on the Holy Spirit. It explains the Holy Spirit's role in the Church and in individual souls, exhorts devotion to the Third Person of the Trinity, and institutes a novena before Pentecost with attached indulgences.

## Historical Context

Written near the end of Leo XIII's long pontificate (1878–1903), the encyclical reflects his twin priorities: restoring Christian principles in civil society and promoting reunion with separated Christians. The 19th century saw rapid industrialization, the rise of secularism and liberal states, and the loss of the Papal States (1870). Against this backdrop, Leo sought to re-center Catholic life on the Holy Spirit as the soul of the Church and the source of renewal.

## Key Themes

- The Holy Spirit completes and perfects Christ's redemptive work, having been sent to the Apostles at Pentecost.
- The Holy Spirit is the soul of the Church — guiding it into all truth, constituting bishops, and empowering the sacraments (particularly Baptism and Confirmation).
- In individual souls, the Holy Spirit effects justification, adoption as sons of God, and the "indwelling" of grace.
- Christians ought to know, love, and pray to the Holy Spirit; pastors are urged to teach clearly about the Third Person.
- A nine-day novena before Pentecost is instituted and enriched with indulgences (applicable also to souls in Purgatory).
- Mary is invoked as the Spouse of the Holy Spirit, whose intercession accompanied both the Incarnation and Pentecost.

## Significance

*Divinum Illud Munus* remains one of the most authoritative papal teachings on the Holy Spirit prior to the 20th-century renewal of pneumatology (e.g., Vatican II's *Lumen Gentium* and *Ad Gentes*, and John Paul II's *Dominum et Vivificantem*). It revived popular devotion to the Holy Spirit, instituted the Pentecost novena still observed today, and offered a clear, accessible synthesis of Thomistic trinitarian theology for the modern era.
