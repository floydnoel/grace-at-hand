# Auctorem fidei · English editorial summary

## Summary

Pius VI condemns eighty-five propositions drawn from the Synod of Pistoia associated with Scipione de' Ricci. He describes successive episcopal, theological, and cardinalitial examinations and an unsuccessful invitation for Ricci to explain himself in Rome. Particular attention is given to ambiguous formulations: the constitution identifies the objectionable sense in which a statement is censured rather than treating every possible interpretation alike. The censures also differ, with some propositions judged heretical and others erroneous, rash, scandalous, or injurious to the Church.

The propositions concern ecclesiastical authority, papal primacy, bishops and diocesan synods, original justice and grace, sacramental doctrine, and disciplinary and devotional reforms. Pius rejects accounts that derive pastoral authority from the faithful, diminish papal jurisdiction, or assign final doctrinal authority to national councils. He also addresses transubstantiation, Mass intentions, penance, indulgences, marriage impediments, sacred images, the Sacred Heart, and religious institutes. His defense of established practices is directed against the synod's stated premises and prescriptions, while legitimate disputed opinions of Catholic schools are expressly protected from its blanket disparagement.

The conclusion condemns the synod's adoption of the Gallican declaration, corrects further theological formulations, and forbids its acts and defensive publications under stated ecclesiastical penalties. Condemning the selected propositions, Pius adds, does not imply approval of the remaining contents.

## Key themes

- Eighty-five individually qualified doctrinal and disciplinary censures
- Papal primacy and limits on diocesan or national autonomy
- Grace, sacramental efficacy, and established devotional practices
- Distinction between condemned meanings and legitimate Catholic disputes
