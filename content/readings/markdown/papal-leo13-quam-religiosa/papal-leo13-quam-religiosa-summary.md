## Summary

*Quam Religiosa* (1898) is an apostolic letter from Pope Leo XIII to the bishops of Peru expressing grave concern over a recently enacted civil marriage law. The Pope condemns the law for introducing civil marriage under the pretext of regulating non-Catholic marriages, and for permitting mixed marriages without Church authority. He reaffirms that Christian marriage is a sacrament under the sole jurisdiction of the Church, and urges the bishops to instruct the faithful and lobby Peruvian lawmakers to overturn or amend the law.

## Historical Context

Written in 1898, the letter reflects a recurring conflict in late-19th-century Latin America between the Catholic Church and liberal states seeking to secularize civil institutions — especially marriage. Peru had passed a law establishing civil marriage, which the Church saw as an encroachment on its sacramental authority. Leo XIII, who had already laid out Catholic teaching on marriage in his 1880 encyclical *Arcanum Divinae Sapientiae*, here applies that doctrine to a specific national crisis. The letter also references Peru's strong Catholic identity, invoking national saints Turibius of Mogrovejo and Rose of Lima as models of fidelity.

## Key Themes

- **Sacramental nature of marriage**: Christian marriage is a sacrament instituted by Christ and cannot be separated from religion or subjected to purely civil regulation.
- **Church jurisdiction over marriage**: The Church alone has authority over the marriages of Christians; civil authority is limited to the civil effects that flow from a valid marriage.
- **Condemnation of civil marriage laws**: Laws that establish civil marriage independently of — or in opposition to — Church teaching are a violation of divine law.
- **Mixed marriage restrictions**: Dispensations for mixed marriages are reserved to the Holy See; civil authorities may not permit them on their own.
- **Duty of bishops**: Bishops must instruct the faithful and actively engage lawmakers to ensure legislation respects Church teaching.

## Significance

*Quam Religiosa* is a notable example of Leo XIII's application of universal Catholic doctrine on marriage to a specific national situation. It illustrates the Church's firm opposition to the secularization of marriage in the modern state — a stance that would persist well into the 20th century. The letter also demonstrates the papacy's pastoral concern for Latin American churches and its expectation that Catholic politicians shape civil law according to Church teaching. Though addressed to Peru, its arguments reflect the broader Magisterial position articulated in *Arcanum Divinae Sapientiae* and later reaffirmed in canon law.
