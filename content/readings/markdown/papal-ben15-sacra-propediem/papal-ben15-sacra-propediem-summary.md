## Summary

*Sacra Propediem* is a 1921 encyclical in which Pope Benedict XV commemorates the seventh centenary of the Third Order of St. Francis (the Secular Franciscan Order). He presents the Third Order as a providential instrument for the renewal of Christian life in the modern world, calling Catholics to join or deepen their commitment to it as a means of personal sanctification and social restoration.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was issued on the Feast of the Epiphany, 1921, barely two years after the end of World War I. Europe lay devastated by war, class conflict was intensifying, and the Church faced the twin crises of rising secularism and moral decay. Benedict XV — who had spent the war years pleading for peace — saw in the Franciscan Third Order a model for rebuilding society on the foundation of the Gospel. The document also reflects the Church's ongoing response to the modernist crisis, insisting on a robustly orthodox, obedient, and ascetical Francis against sentimental or revisionist portraits.

## Key Themes

- **True image of St. Francis** — The Pope rejects modernist depictions of Francis as a vague, sentimental figure, insisting instead on his radical obedience to the Church, his spirit of penance, and his burning desire for the glory of Christ.
- **The Third Order as a universal path to perfection** — Francis uniquely made religious perfection accessible to laypeople living in the world, without monastic vows, through a rule of simplicity, penance, and charity.
- **Fraternal charity and peace** — The Third Order's characteristic mark is charity, which makes its members "messengers and apostles of peace" in a world torn by war and class hatred.
- **Countering worldliness** — The Pope identifies two dominant passions of the age — greed and lust for pleasure — and calls Tertiaries to embrace poverty of spirit, modesty, and self-denial as a counter-witness.
- **Moral reform of women** — A notable passage addresses women's immodest dress and "barbarous dances," calling Tertiary Sisters to be models of holy modesty.
- **The insufficiency of merely human peace** — True peace comes only from Christ and requires the pacification of hearts through the bridling of the passions; the Third Order is a school of this interior discipline.

## Significance

*Sacra Propediem* is one of the most important papal documents on the Secular Franciscan Order, reaffirming its lay character and its relevance beyond the medieval world. It connects the Franciscan charism directly to the social and moral crises of the early twentieth century — war, class struggle, secularism — and presents the Third Order not as a pious relic but as an urgent pastoral strategy. Benedict XV's insistence on the authentic, orthodox Francis also contributed to the Church's broader effort, continued by his successors, to reclaim the saint from ideological appropriation. The encyclical remains a key reference point for understanding the lay vocation to perfection in the Catholic tradition.
