## Summary

*Ex Quo* is a 1756 encyclical by Pope Benedict XIV addressed to Eastern Catholic clergy of the Greek Rite, announcing the completion of a long-awaited corrected edition of the Greek Euchologion (the liturgical book containing prayers, blessings, and sacramental rites). The Pope narrates the decades-long revision process—stretching back to Urban VIII in the 1630s—and explains four "Admonitions" prefixed to the new edition, addressing the commemoration of the Roman Pontiff in the liturgy, proper reverence during the Great Entrance, the administration of Extreme Unction and Confirmation, and observances around ritual purity.

## Historical Context

The document reflects the post-Reformation Catholic effort to reconcile Eastern churches with Rome. Eastern Catholic ("Uniate") communities existed alongside much larger Orthodox populations, and liturgical books printed by Orthodox presses often contained errors or schismatic interpolations. Benedict XIV, a towering 18th-century intellectual Pope and a noted canonist and liturgist, personally supervised the correction over a decade (c. 1744–1754), working through a dedicated Congregation of Cardinals and consultors. The background includes the Council of Florence (1439), which had briefly reunified East and West; the ongoing tensions with the Ottoman Empire, under which many Eastern Christians lived; and the perennial challenge of balancing Latin doctrinal norms with legitimate Eastern liturgical traditions.

## Key Themes

- **Papal primacy and liturgical commemoration**: The Pope insists that Greek priests must commemorate the Roman Pontiff by name in the Divine Liturgy, presenting this as both an ancient tradition (citing diptychs from the Acacian schism onward) and a non-negotiable sign of Catholic unity.
- **Distinguishing Latins and Greeks without coercing Latinization**: A central thread is that Greek rites should be preserved, not replaced by Latin ones. Benedict rejects the view that Greek customs are to be judged by Latin standards, and he retains Eastern practices (e.g., anointing with multiple priests for Extreme Unction, the Greater Entrance) unless they clearly contradict Catholic doctrine.
- **Adoration of the Eucharist vs. reverence for unconsecrated gifts**: The Pope carefully defends the Greek practice of bowing or prostrating during the Great Entrance (when bread and wine are carried but not yet consecrated), distinguishing external reverential acts from the *latria* due only to the consecrated species. He mandates catechesis to prevent confusion but refuses to abolish the rite.
- **Reception of Eastern sacramental theology**: On Confirmation (Chrismation), the Pope accepts the Greek practice of conferring the sacrament through chrism alone without the Latin imposition of hands, and defends its validity. On Extreme Unction, he reasserts that it is only for the seriously ill while clarifying that the oil of the lamp (not sacramental oil) may be used for other blessings.
- **Abrogation of Mosaic ceremonial law**: The fourth admonition makes clear that prayers in the Euchologion for purification after childbirth or contact with unclean things are matters of ecclesiastical custom and reverence, not obligations under the Old Law, which is abrogated. Benedict upholds the Greek discipline without treating it as binding on the Latin Church.

## Significance

*Ex Quo* is a landmark document in Catholic Oriental policy, embodying what would later be formalized as the principle that Eastern Catholic churches should preserve their legitimate rites, discipline, and patrimony rather than be Latinized. It is also a monument of 18th-century liturgical scholarship, reflecting the critical study of ancient manuscripts (e.g., the *Barberinum S. Marci* and the Grottaferrata codex) to produce an authoritative edition. The encyclical remains a key reference for understanding how the Catholic Church has historically navigated the tension between doctrinal unity and liturgical diversity in the East.
