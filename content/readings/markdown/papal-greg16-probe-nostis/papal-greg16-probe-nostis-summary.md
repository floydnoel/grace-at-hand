## Summary

*Probe Nostis* is an 1840 encyclical by Pope Gregory XVI addressing the threats facing the Catholic Church from heretics, unbelievers, and secularizing forces. It surveys the Church's afflictions — false teachings, anti-Catholic propaganda, unauthorized Bible translations, and the aggressive proselytizing of Protestant missionary societies — while also celebrating the Church's resilience, missionary successes, and the emergence of new pious associations, most notably the Society for the Propagation of the Faith.

## Historical Context

The document was written in 1840, a period of intense political and religious upheaval in Europe. The aftermath of the French Revolution and the Napoleonic Wars had weakened traditional Church–state ties, and liberal, rationalist, and anti-clerical ideas were spreading. Protestant and secular missionary societies were active across the globe, competing directly with Catholic missions. Gregory XVI, a conservative pope wary of modern innovations, saw the Church under siege on multiple fronts — from state interference, from heretical propaganda, and from the loss of the faithful to indifferentism and unbelief. The encyclical is part of a broader pattern of 19th-century papal responses that sought to reassert Catholic identity and centralize missionary work under papal authority.

## Key Themes

- **The Church under attack** — Gregory catalogues the methods of heretics and unbelievers: entering cities and countryside, distributing vernacular Bibles and pamphlets, using flattery and material gifts to draw the poor and youth away from the faith.
- **The Church's unconquerable constancy** — Despite persecution, the Church endures through Christ's promise to remain with her "until the end of the world." The recent martyrdoms of bishops, priests, and laity in Tonkin and Cochin (Vietnam) are cited as proof of this supernatural strength.
- **Praise for new societies** — The pope commends lay associations that combat bad books with good ones, women's sodalities (especially those under the rule of St. Vincent de Paul) devoted to charity and education, and above all the Society for the Propagation of the Faith (founded in Lyons, 1822), which channels the prayers and alms of the faithful to support worldwide missions.
- **The primacy of the Propagation of the Faith** — Gregory strongly urges bishops to promote this society in their dioceses, framing membership as a duty of the lay faithful in the "Christian battle line" against the devil.
- **Unity of clergy and laity** — The encyclical calls for a holy union of priests and people, combining prayer, almsgiving, and active defense of the faith as the surest way to obtain peace for the Church.

## Significance

*Probe Nostis* is an important milestone in the modern Catholic missionary movement. By giving papal endorsement to the Society for the Propagation of the Faith — which later became the Pontifical Mission Societies — Gregory XVI helped centralize and institutionalize global Catholic mission efforts under Rome's authority. The encyclical also illustrates the combative, defensive posture of the mid-19th-century papacy toward liberalism, Protestant proselytism, and secular culture, anticipating themes that would recur in the *Syllabus of Errors* (1864) and *Quanta Cura*. Its exaltation of lay associations presaged the later flowering of Catholic Action and the emphasis on lay apostolate that would culminate in the Second Vatican Council.
