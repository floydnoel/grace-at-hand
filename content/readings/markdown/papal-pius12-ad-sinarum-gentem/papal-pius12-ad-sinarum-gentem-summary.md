## Summary

*Ad Sinarum Gentem* ("To the Chinese People") is a 1954 encyclical by Pope Pius XII addressed to Chinese Catholics, reaffirming the Church's unity under the papacy and condemning the "Three Autonomies" movement — a state-sponsored effort to create a Chinese national church independent of Rome.

## Historical Context

Written in the wake of the Chinese Communist Revolution (1949), the encyclical follows escalating persecution of the Church in China: foreign missionaries were expelled, the Apostolic Nuncio was forced out, and Catholics were pressured to join state-controlled "patriotic" associations. Pius XII had previously addressed these issues in *Cupimus Imprimis* (1952), but conditions had worsened by 1954.

## Key Themes

- **Unity of the Church**: The Church is supranational by divine institution and cannot be broken into national churches; all bishops and faithful must remain in communion with the Pope.
- **Legitimate patriotism vs. religious fidelity**: Chinese Catholics are praised as exemplary citizens, but civil obedience cannot extend to violating divine law ("We must obey God rather than men").
- **Divine origin of hierarchy**: The Church's constitution — clergy/laity distinction, episcopal authority, papal primacy — is willed by Christ and cannot be reordered by the state.
- **Integrity of doctrine**: Bishops and priests are custodians, not inventors, of the Gospel; no nation may reinterpret Christian teaching to suit local political ends.
- **The Three Autonomies condemned**: The movement for "autonomy of government, finances, and teaching" is a cunning attempt to establish a merely national church, which would cease to be Catholic.

## Significance

The encyclical is a landmark magisterial statement on the Church's supranationality, cited in subsequent Vatican documents on church-state relations. It foreshadowed the decades-long suppression of the "underground" Church in China and remains relevant to ongoing Vatican-China diplomatic negotiations. Its clear rejection of "national churches" continues to inform Catholic ecclesiology regarding the limits of inculturation and state interference.
