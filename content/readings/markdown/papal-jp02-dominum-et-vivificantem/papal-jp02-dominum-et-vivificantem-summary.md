## Summary

*Dominum et Vivificantem* ("The Lord and Giver of Life") is Pope John Paul II's 1986 encyclical on the Holy Spirit — the third of his trilogy of Trinitarian encyclicals, following *Redemptor Hominis* (the Son) and *Dives in Misericordia* (the Father). It examines the person and mission of the Holy Spirit in the life of the Church and the world, drawing extensively on Scripture (especially John's Gospel), the Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed, and the teaching of the Second Vatican Council.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was published in 1986, near the close of the second Christian millennium. It responds to Paul VI's call, after Vatican II, for a "new study of and devotion to the Holy Spirit" as the complement to the Council's Christology and ecclesiology. It also follows the 1981 joint celebration with the Eastern Churches of the 1600th anniversary of the First Council of Constantinople (381 AD), which confirmed the divinity of the Holy Spirit. The document is part of John Paul II's broader project of Trinitarian catechesis and his preparation for the Great Jubilee of the Year 2000.

## Key Themes

- **The Holy Spirit as Person-Gift**: The Spirit is not merely a force but a divine Person — "Person-Love" and "Person-Gift" — who proceeds from the Father and the Son.
- **The Spirit and the Redemption**: Christ's "departure" (Cross and Resurrection) is the necessary condition for the sending of the Spirit; the Spirit "draws from" the Redemption and applies it to human hearts.
- **The Paraclete and Truth**: The Spirit is the "another Counselor" who teaches, reminds, bears witness to Christ, and guides the Church "into all truth."
- **The Spirit in the Old Testament**: Traces the presence of the *ruah Elohim* from creation through the messianic prophecies of Isaiah, where the Spirit rests upon the Anointed One.
- **Christ's Anointing**: Jesus is the Messiah — the One anointed with the Holy Spirit — and at his baptism the Trinitarian theophany reveals him as the beloved Son.
- **Pentecost and the Church**: The era of the Church begins with the sending of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost; the Spirit dwells in the Church as its soul, guiding, sanctifying, and renewing it.

## Significance

The encyclical completed John Paul II's Trinitarian trilogy and gave the post-conciliar Church a magisterial synthesis of pneumatology at a time when charismatic renewal, ecumenical dialogue with the East, and preparation for the Jubilee all demanded a deeper understanding of the Holy Spirit. It reaffirmed the Spirit's personal distinctness within the Trinity, his role in applying the fruits of the Redemption, and his ongoing mission as the invisible guide of the Church in history.
