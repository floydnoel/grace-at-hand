## Summary

*Editae Saepe* (1910) is Pope Pius X's encyclical on St. Charles Borromeo, written to commemorate the third centenary of Borromeo's canonization. Pius X presents Borromeo — the Cardinal-Archbishop of Milan and a central figure of the Catholic Counter-Reformation — as a model pastor and reformer for the modern Church, urging bishops and the faithful to imitate his zeal in restoring all things in Christ amid the challenges of the early 20th century.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was issued on May 26, 1910, during a period Pius X saw as deeply troubled: the rise of Modernism (which he condemned in *Pascendi Dominici Gregis* three years earlier), secularism in education and public life, the spread of religious indifferentism, and social unrest fueled by class conflict. Pius X draws an explicit parallel between the 16th-century crisis that produced the Protestant Reformation and the crisis of his own day, arguing that both stem from the same rebellion against Church authority and revealed truth. Borromeo's work implementing the Council of Trent's reforms is held up as a blueprint for the restoration of Christian society.

## Key Themes

- **True vs. false reform**: Pius X sharply distinguishes Borromeo's authentic Catholic reform — grounded in obedience to the Church, the Sacraments, and sound doctrine — from the "false reformers" of the 16th century and the Modernists of his own day, who seek to destroy rather than restore.
- **The primacy of faith and doctrine**: Pastors must guard Catholic doctrine inviolably; heresy is the gravest of evils, corrupting not only the Church but civil society itself.
- **Catechesis and preaching**: Christian instruction is urgently needed, especially given the rise of secular ("neutral") schools that exclude religion. Pius X echoes Borromeo's insistence that clergy and laity alike be grounded in the rudiments of the faith.
- **Frequent Communion with reverence**: The encyclical promotes frequent reception of Holy Communion (citing Borromeo and Pius X's own decree *Tridentina Synodus*) but insists on proper preparation and reverence.
- **The role of the bishop**: Borromeo is held up as the ideal shepherd — courageous, self-sacrificing, obedient to the Holy See, and tireless in reforming clergy morals, establishing seminaries, and caring for the poor and sick, especially during plague.
- **Catholic Action**: Pius X connects Borromeo's lay charitable works to the contemporary lay apostolate, calling the faithful to engage in the Church's mission under episcopal leadership.
- **Resistance to unjust civil authority**: Christians must obey just laws but resist unjust ones, without falling into either sedition or servile acquiescence to tyranny.

## Significance

*Editae Saepe* is a key document of Pius X's restorationist program, connecting his anti-Modernist campaign to the deeper tradition of Catholic reform exemplified by St. Charles Borromeo and the Council of Trent. It reinforces the integralist vision that defined his pontificate: the conviction that only a return to traditional Catholic doctrine, discipline, and devotion could cure the ills of modernity. The encyclical remains a reference point for understanding the Catholic Church's self-understanding in the face of secularization and remains frequently cited in discussions of authentic reform within the Church.
