## Summary

*Longinqua* (January 6, 1895) is Pope Leo XIII's encyclical addressed to the Archbishops and Bishops of the United States, offering praise for the growth of Catholicism in America while also issuing cautions about American-style separation of church and state. The Pope acknowledges the flourishing of Catholic institutions under American law's protection but warns against concluding that the American model of church-state separation should be universal, arguing the Church would bear even more fruit if she enjoyed the "favor of the laws and the patronage of the public authority."

## Historical Context

Written for the 400th anniversary of Columbus's voyage, the encyclical reflects Leo XIII's engagement with modern political questions (following *Rerum Novarum* in 1891 and the Americanist controversy brewing in the 1890s). The U.S. Church had grown rapidly through immigration and held the Third Plenary Council of Baltimore in 1884. Leo had established The Catholic University of America in 1889 and an Apostolic Legation to the U.S. in 1893 — both referenced in the document — signaling the Vatican's increasing attention to American Catholicism.

## Key Themes

- **Church and State**: America enjoys religious liberty, but this should not be mistaken for the ideal; the Church does best when law and public authority actively favor her.
- **Praise for American Catholic vitality**: Leo commends the growth of parishes, schools, universities, religious orders, and lay piety.
- **Unity and episcopal authority**: The Apostolic Legation strengthens, not undermines, bishops' authority; concord among bishops is essential.
- **Marriage and family**: Defends the indissolubility of marriage against divorce as foundational for society.
- **Catholic associations**: Encourages workers to form Catholic associations under priestly or upright lay leadership, following *Rerum Novarum*.
- **The press and intellectuals**: Urges Catholic journalists and writers to work in unanimity for the faith.
- **Evangelization**: Calls for continued mission to Native Americans and African Americans.
- **Education**: Champions Catholic higher learning and The Catholic University of America.

## Significance

*Longinqua* is a key text in Leo XIII's body of social teaching and a touchstone for understanding the Church's evolving relationship with modern democracy. It gave cautious approval to the American experiment while firmly rejecting a "separationist" model as universally desirable — a tension that would resurface in the Americanist crisis a few years later (*Testem Benevolentiae*, 1899). For American Catholics, it remains a classic statement of their place within both the universal Church and the American republic.
