## Summary

*Singulari Nos* (1834) is a papal brief by Pope Gregory XVI condemning the book *Paroles d'un croyant* ("Words of a Believer") by the French priest Félicité de Lamennais. The pope charges that the book uses Scripture deceitfully to undermine legitimate civil authority, incite rebellion, and promote freedom of conscience and speech — doctrines Gregory XVI had already rejected in his earlier encyclical *Mirari Vos* (1832). The document reproaches Lamennais personally for breaking his earlier pledge of obedience and calls on bishops to suppress the work.

## Historical Context

The document was issued on June 25, 1834, in the aftermath of the July Revolution in France (1830), which had toppled the Bourbon monarchy and installed a liberal constitutional regime. Lamennais, once a defender of ultramontanism, had moved sharply toward liberal Catholic positions — advocating separation of Church and state, freedom of the press, and popular sovereignty — and founded the newspaper *L'Avenir*. After Gregory XVI condemned these ideas in *Mirari Vos*, Lamennais published *Paroles d'un croyant*, a poetic, prophetic-style tract that became a best-seller across Europe. The pope saw it as a direct act of defiance and a dangerous fusion of religion with revolutionary politics.

## Key Themes

- **Condemnation of rebellion against civil authority** — The pope insists that loyalty and submission to rulers are binding, and that Lamennais's writings "break the bond of loyalty and submission."
- **Rejection of freedom of conscience and speech** — Gregory XVI reiterates the condemnation from *Mirari Vos* of "that freedom of conscience which should be thoroughly condemned" and the "spreading license of ideas and speeches."
- **Scriptural misuse** — The brief accuses Lamennais of twisting Holy Scripture to "release the people from the law of obedience," creating "a new Gospel."
- **Condemnation of indifferentism and secret societies** — The pope warns against the notion that truth can be found outside the Catholic Church and against groups that "enkindle destruction of sacred and state affairs."
- **Doctrinal continuity** — The errors in the book are linked to earlier heresies (Waldensians, Wycliffites, Hussites), placing Lamennais in a line of condemned innovators.
- **Paternal correction** — Despite the harsh condemnation, the pope expresses sorrow over Lamennais's error and prays for his return to the Church.

## Significance

*Singulari Nos* solidified the 19th-century papacy's opposition to liberalism, democracy, and religious pluralism — a stance that would define Catholic teaching until *Dignitatis Humanae* at Vatican II (1965). Together with *Mirari Vos*, it marks the definitive break between the Catholic hierarchy and the emerging liberal Catholic movement in Europe. The document also illustrates the papacy's deep suspicion of modern notions of human rights, popular sovereignty, and freedom of the press, setting the stage for the Syllabus of Errors (1864) under Pope Pius IX. Lamennais himself never reconciled with the Church and died outside its communion, though his questions about social justice and the relationship between Christianity and modernity would resurface in later Catholic social teaching.
