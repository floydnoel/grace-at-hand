## Summary

*Divino Afflatu* (1911) is a papal apostolic constitution by Pope Pius X that reformed the Roman Breviary, primarily by restoring the practice of reciting the entire Psalter (all 150 Psalms) within a single week. It replaced the existing psalter arrangement that had been diluted by the proliferation of feast-day offices for saints, which had crowded out the weekday and Sunday recitation of many psalms.

## Historical Context

The document was issued on November 1, 1911 (All Saints' Day), at the end of the ninth year of Pius X's pontificate. For centuries, the Roman Breviary had accumulated numerous feast-day offices for canonised saints, gradually displacing the ferial (weekday) and Sunday offices. By the time of the First Vatican Council (1869–1870), bishops from across the world petitioned the Holy See to restore the weekly recitation of the full Psalter without making the clergy's daily office more burdensome. Pius X, who had shared this desire before becoming pope, took up the reform after the council's interruption.

## Key Themes

- **Centrality of the Psalms**: The document vividly praises the Psalms as the "voice of the Church," a means of divine praise, a mirror for self-examination, and a source of spiritual renewal — drawing extensively on the Church Fathers (Athanasius, Basil, Augustine, Ambrose).
- **Distortion by accretion**: The multiplication of saints' feasts had effectively silenced the Sunday and ferial offices, causing many psalms to be neglected entirely — a loss the pope calls both a pastoral and liturgical impoverishment.
- **Balance between tradition and feasibility**: The reform restores the ancient weekly Psalter cycle but adjusts it so that clergy already burdened by pastoral work are not overburdened. The veneration of saints is simultaneously preserved.
- **A first step toward broader reform**: Pius X presents this Psalter reform as the initial phase of a larger project — a forthcoming revision of the entire Roman Breviary and Missal (which would culminate in his 1914 reform and later in the reforms after Vatican II).
- **Regulation and obedience**: The constitution imposes the new Psalter under canonical penalties, mandating its use in all Latin-rite churches, monasteries, and religious institutes from January 1, 1913.

## Significance

*Divino Afflatu* was the first major liturgical reform of the 20th century and a direct precursor to Pius X's 1914 Breviary reform and the broader Liturgical Movement that followed. It reasserted the primacy of the Psalter in Catholic daily prayer and attempted to recover the patristic ideal of the Divine Office as the prayer of the whole Church, not merely a series of saint commemorations. It also set a precedent for the kind of centralised, rubrical liturgical reform that would later characterise the work of Pius XII and the Second Vatican Council's *Sacrosanctum Concilium*.
