## Summary

*Ex Omnibus* is a papal encyclical issued by Pope Benedict XIV in 1756, addressed to the bishops of France, concerning the pastoral handling of those who publicly opposed the apostolic constitution *Unigenitus* (1713). The central question is whether viaticum (Holy Communion given to the dying) may be denied to such opponents. Benedict XIV affirms that it must be denied to public and notorious objectors, but he also lays down a careful pastoral rule of action for confessors and pastors, distinguishing between clear cases of public notoriety and mere conjecture, rumor, or partisan suspicion.

## Historical Context

The document was written during the protracted controversy surrounding *Unigenitus*, Pope Clement XI's constitution condemning Jansenism. By the mid-18th century, France was deeply divided between Jansenist-leaning clergy and their opponents, often called the "Constitutionnaires." The dispute had caused serious pastoral and political disruption, with some clergy refusing the sacraments to those who would not accept *Unigenitus*. King Louis XV and the French episcopal assembly sought papal guidance to resolve the crisis. Benedict XIV, known for his learned and conciliatory approach, wrote *Ex Omnibus* to provide a clear but pastorally nuanced directive.

## Key Themes

- **Authority of *Unigenitus***: The constitution is affirmed as binding on all Catholics; opposition to it risks eternal salvation.
- **Denial of viaticum to public sinners**: The long-standing canonical principle that a known public sinner may not receive the Eucharist is applied to opponents of *Unigenitus*.
- **Two kinds of notoriety**: A distinction is drawn between cases where guilt is evident from external acts (e.g., usury) and cases where guilt depends on interior disposition — the latter requiring more certain proof.
- **Rejection of conjecture and rumor**: Pastors must not act on mere presumption, hostility, or partisan gossip. Certainty is required before denying sacraments.
- **Pastoral rule of action**: When doubt remains, priests should approach the dying with gentleness, warn them of the danger of unworthy reception, and administer viaticum to avoid public scandal, while making clear the recipient's spiritual risk.
- **Role of civil authority**: The pope praises King Louis XV and calls on his support to protect episcopal rights and restore peace.

## Significance

*Ex Omnibus* is an important document in the history of sacramental discipline and the Jansenist controversy. It attempted to steer a middle course between rigorism (denying communion to all suspects) and laxity (ignoring the dispute entirely), insisting on both doctrinal fidelity and pastoral charity. It became a reference point for subsequent discussions on the denial of the Eucharist to public sinners. The encyclical also illustrates the close cooperation between the papacy and the French crown in managing ecclesiastical affairs in the ancien régime. Its principles — especially the distinction between types of notoriety and the warning against acting on rumor — remain relevant to Catholic sacramental practice today.
