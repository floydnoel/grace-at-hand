## Summary

This apostolic constitution from Pope Benedict XII (1336) formally defines the Catholic doctrine on the beatific vision — teaching that the souls of the righteous see God face to face immediately after death (or after any necessary purification), before the final resurrection and general judgment. It also affirms that souls dying in mortal sin descend immediately into hell.

## Historical Context

The document settled a long-running medieval theological dispute about whether the blessed departed see God before the Last Judgment. Some theologians (including Pope John XXII, Benedict's predecessor) had controversially taught that the vision of God was delayed until after the resurrection. The document was issued in 1336, shortly after Benedict's election, to definitively resolve the controversy.

## Key Themes

- **Immediate beatific vision:** The righteous see God's essence intuitively "face to face" immediately after death and any purification, not merely after the general judgment.
- **No intermediary:** The divine essence manifests itself directly, without any creature acting as a medium of vision.
- **Hell immediate:** Souls dying in unrepented mortal sin descend into hell immediately after death.
- **Faith and hope cease:** The beatific vision supersedes the theological virtues of faith and hope, which are no longer operative once God is seen directly.
- **General judgment preserved:** Despite immediate particular judgment, souls still appear before Christ's judgment seat at the end of time.

## Significance

_Benedictus Deus_ is one of the most important papal definitions on eschatology. It became the definitive Catholic statement on the "intermediate state" between death and the general resurrection, and it is regularly cited in later magisterial documents (including Vatican II's _Lumen Gentium_ and the Catechism of the Catholic Church) to affirm the immediate reward of the righteous and the immediate punishment of the damned.
