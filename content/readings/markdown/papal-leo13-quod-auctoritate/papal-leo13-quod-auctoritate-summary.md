## Summary

*Quod Auctoritate*, issued by Pope Leo XIII in December 1885, proclaims an extraordinary Jubilee year for 1886 throughout the Christian world, granting a plenary indulgence to the faithful who fulfill specified conditions of prayer, fasting, confession, almsgiving, and visitation of churches. The document ties the Jubilee to Leo's broader concern that civil society has drifted from Christian principles, and presents the spiritual renewal of individuals as the necessary foundation for the reform of public life and the State itself.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written during a period in which the Church faced mounting secularization across Europe — rising liberalism, anticlericalism, and the expansion of secret societies (which Leo had previously condemned). Leo XIII was deeply concerned that modern states were abandoning their Christian foundations. Earlier in 1885 he had issued *Immortale Dei*, his landmark encyclical on the Christian constitution of states, and *Quod Auctoritate* functions as its pastoral complement: a call to individual conversion as the remedy for societal ills. The reference to "domestic dissensions" within the Church also reflects internal tensions among Catholics over how to engage with the modern world.

## Key Themes

- **The link between personal virtue and the health of the State** — a State is only as good as the moral lives of its citizens, so reform must begin with the individual.
- **Penance and self-denial** — Leo warns against softness and indulgence, insisting that sin cannot be expiated without penance, including voluntary bodily mortification.
- **The necessity of persistent prayer** — especially the Rosary, which Leo had repeatedly promoted and here places as the patron devotion of the Jubilee.
- **Unity within the Church** — he condemns internal dissensions among Catholics as a grave obstacle to charity and evangelization.
- **The social mission of almsgiving** — he directs Jubilee alms toward two specific ends: primary schools for boys and seminaries for the clergy, seeing both as essential for Church and society.
- **The power of the Jubilee indulgence** — grounded in the Petrine power of binding and loosing, and applicable also to the souls in purgatory.

## Significance

*Quod Auctoritate* is a characteristic document of Leo XIII's long pontificate, blending traditional devotional practice (the Jubilee, the Rosary) with a sharp analysis of modern social and political problems. It exemplifies his strategy of meeting the challenges of the age not through political action alone but through the renewal of Catholic piety and discipline. By explicitly linking the Jubilee to the themes of *Immortale Dei*, it reinforced the idea that spiritual revival is the precondition for any lasting restoration of Christian civilization — a conviction that would shape Catholic social teaching well into the twentieth century.
