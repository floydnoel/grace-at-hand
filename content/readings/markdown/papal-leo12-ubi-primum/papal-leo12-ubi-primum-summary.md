## Summary

*Ubi Primum* (1824) is the first encyclical of Pope Leo XII, written shortly after his election to the pontificate. It addresses the world's bishops on the duties of the episcopal office — especially residence, visitation, and the careful selection of clergy — and warns against the rising threats of religious indifferentism, Bible societies distributing vernacular translations without Church oversight, and the broader contempt for ecclesiastical authority that Leo sees as the root of modern errors.

## Historical Context

Leo XII was elected in 1823 following the long pontificate of Pius VII, who had weathered the Napoleonic occupation of Rome and the suppression of the Jesuits. The post-Napoleonic period saw the spread of liberal and rationalist ideas across Europe, the growth of Bible societies (often linked to Protestant missionary movements), and a philosophical climate that championed toleration and private judgment in matters of faith. The Church was re-establishing itself after the disruptions of the French Revolution and faced what Leo saw as a coordinated assault on revealed religion and hierarchical authority.

## Key Themes

- **Episcopal duties** — Bishops must reside in their dioceses, personally perform visitations, and carefully vet candidates for Holy Orders, as decreed by the Council of Trent.
- **Indifferentism** — The encyclical condemns the view that any religion or sect is equally salvific, insisting on the uniqueness of the Catholic Church and the principle that there is no salvation outside her.
- **Bible societies** — Leo warns against the indiscriminate distribution of vernacular Bible translations, arguing that without ecclesial guidance they lead to misinterpretation, heresy, and the erosion of faith.
- **Church authority** — Contempt for the Church's teaching office — especially the primacy of the Roman Pontiff — is identified as the ultimate source of the era's religious and social disorders.
- **Role of civil rulers** — Leo appeals to secular princes to support the Church, since respect for ecclesiastical authority reinforces their own authority.

## Significance

*Ubi Primum* is a programmatic opening statement of Leo XII's pontificate, setting a firmly anti-liberal and restorationist tone that would characterize much nineteenth-century papal teaching. Its condemnation of indifferentism anticipates the more developed treatment in Gregory XVI's *Mirari Vos* (1832) and Pius IX's *Syllabus of Errors* (1864). Its polemic against Bible societies reflects a lasting Catholic suspicion of unguided lay scripture reading that would only begin to shift with *Divino Afflante Spiritu* (1943). The encyclical is a key document for understanding the post-Revolutionary Church's defensive posture toward modernity.
