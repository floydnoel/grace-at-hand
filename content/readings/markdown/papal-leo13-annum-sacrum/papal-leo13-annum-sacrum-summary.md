## Summary

*Annum Sacrum* is a papal encyclical issued by Pope Leo XIII on May 25, 1899, calling for the consecration of the entire human race to the Sacred Heart of Jesus. It establishes the theological basis for Christ's universal kingship and mandates that this act of consecration take place on June 9–11, 1899, in churches worldwide.

## Historical Context

The encyclical was written near the close of the 19th century, a period when secularism and the separation of Church and state were on the rise across Europe. Leo XIII was responding to what he saw as the deliberate exclusion of religion from public life and civil governance. The document also fulfilled a long-deferred proposal first raised 25 years earlier during the pontificate of Pius IX, following the revelations to St. Margaret Mary Alacoque, who had promoted devotion to the Sacred Heart. Leo himself had recently recovered from a serious illness, which he credits as a personal motive for the consecration.

## Key Themes

- **Christ's universal kingship** — Jesus has sovereign power over all people, not only Catholics but also non-Christians, by virtue of both His divine nature and His redemptive sacrifice.
- **Consecration as voluntary offering** — Though all things already belong to Christ, believers may nonetheless "consecrate" themselves to Him as a free act of love and allegiance.
- **The Sacred Heart as symbol** — The physical heart of Jesus represents His infinite love and serves as the focal point for this act of devotion.
- **Critique of secularism** — The encyclical laments the "wall" raised between Church and civil society and blames the exclusion of religion from public life for widespread social unrest.
- **Hope for restoration** — Consecration to the Sacred Heart is presented as the remedy for the world's evils, promising peace, justice, and renewed authority.
- **Unity of action** — Leo XIII insists the consecration be performed on the same day worldwide, so that the prayers of the faithful rise together.

## Significance

*Annum Sacrum* formalized the connection between devotion to the Sacred Heart and the doctrine of Christ's social kingship, a theme that would later culminate in Pius XI's encyclical *Quas Primas* (1925), which instituted the feast of Christ the King. The act of consecration mandated by this document has been repeated by subsequent popes, most notably by John Paul II in 1984. It remains a touchstone for Catholic teaching on the relation between Christ, the Church, and civil society.
